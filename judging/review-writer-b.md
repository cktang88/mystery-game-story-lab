# Review: Writer B v2, "The Jar That Was Never Opened"

Grader: independent; has not seen Writers A or C. Files reviewed: all of `outlines/writer-b/` (01 to 07), against `BRIEF.md` and `research/REPORT.md` (sixteen principles, checklist, trope table) and the round's banned list and setting requirements.

## Verdict in one paragraph

This is a serious, specific and mostly coherent piece of work. The setting (a city carved downward like Lalibela, living through a late-developmental "1987" moment) and the core mechanic (one legal person across 311 bodies, genome-addressed messages to the past paid for in memory, an alien that sees only genomes) produce scenes no other story could: 311 women standing up during a televised lottery, a court ruling that "nobody died", a blood sample that identifies everyone, a gate log that records a genome. The dates are right, including the Ethiopian calendar. The weaknesses are in the back half and in the rules under load. The record AI is close to all-seeing and the outline does not say why Tigist cannot simply listen to the killers' conversations. Three rules contradict each other or the hidden history. The midpoint and the second "frame-breaking" reveal are both things the player has already been made to state. Two committed choices are false or forced. And the election-night chapter packs about fifteen events into thirty-two minutes, with a judge, a winning candidate and a timed entrance that were never set up. The story also stays city-sized: there is no world-scale rung. All of this can be fixed without changing the premise, the founding crime or the ending.

**Overall: 7.0 / 10.** The premise and world could win the round. The structure and the second half are not yet at that level.

## Top strengths

1. **The premise generates scenes, as Pacific Rim's does.** The lottery count, "nobody died", Lemlem blood that "is all of theirs", a killer the alien cannot tell from her victim, a child who legally shares your guilt and vote: each comes from the rule, not from added scenery.
2. **Staging (chapter 11) is a new verb, and it is the ending in miniature.** You live a sixteen-minute room you have already seen in someone's fever, then decide who pays nine days of memory, and refusal has a named outcome (Etagegn). This is the report's "teach by experiment before it matters" done well.
3. **The founding lie is economical.** One act (pledging a baby copy and calling it self-pledge) explains the clone law, the vote rule, the Fig Guard's creed, the sealed jar, the Tsehafi's "Outstanding: one" and the Arho's silence. The two-column belief table is real work.
4. **The human anchor, and the payoff lines are earned.** Hiwot carries the fan, the figurine and the glass. "Someone set this well." / "You did." is set up by the 2160 forearm break and the rule that "the hands keep what the hands learned". "Outstanding: none" pays off a line planted in a ration tally.
5. **The cultural specificity is careful.** Amole (salt-bar money), wass (surety), wogesha (bonesetter), the third coffee round, the shola fig with its dead wasp, elders under the warka, Pagume as the word for non-persons, and the election falling in Pagume. The Lalibela technique is credited to humans and no real rite is staged. **Dates check out:** I recomputed Pagume 5, 2155 EC = 11 September 2163 Gregorian (correct, including the post-2100 one-day drift), and 1 Dec 2089 + 100 days = 11 Mar 2090, 3 Aug to 11 Sep = 39 days, 25 Aug to 3 Sep = 9 days, day 100 = 20 Dec 2163. All ages in 2163 are consistent.

## Seeds: do they shape the story?

All fourteen land, and most generate rules rather than decoration: bonesetter (the main deduction verb, the murder method and the final image), fig wasp (the alien's whole want), lottery ticket (the Act I climax), hourglass (the cap and the climax object), rust (rings in the iris; the public cost of waking a leaf), election (the climax and the clause), terracotta (jars and the fig). The weaker ones:
- **1980s Seoul** is structural and, in places, a near copy of history. A student dies in custody, protests force a first direct election, and a military general is the regime's candidate: that is the 1987 June Struggle (Park Jong-chul's death, the June 29 Declaration, Roh Tae-woo). The writer says "never in borrowed events", but this *is* the borrowed event sequence. It is not plagiarism of fiction, but it should be owned or bent (revision 12). The Korean strand (the Tunnelers) has a look and sound but no stake in the plot beyond Jae-won.
- **Stenographer** is real for Jae-won (the capture mini-game), but his payoff, playing a tape whose contents the player already knows, is mostly ceremonial.
- **Co-conspirators** is good (Aster, Sitota and Jae-won, mirrored by Almaz and Tsehay).

## Banned list

No breach. Inland highland city of 19 million; Ethiopian, Afar and Korean-Ethiopian naming; a broken neck rather than a drowning; the time rule has no fixed interval of years and no single site (plates in every glass room); the alien is silent. Two near-disguises to watch:
- **"Alien bound beneath a community that lives off it."** The city's economy (copies, the foresight trade) lives off the alien's gift; the alien's promised chamber sits sealed *under* the central record tower; and the alien waits, kept from its due, for 73 years. It is not imprisoned and the city is not small, so this is not a breach, but the moral skeleton (a community prospers on an alien's gift while withholding what it owes, with a sealed thing beneath its central building) is the banned shape's cousin. Keep the alien visibly free and mobile, as the giveaway already does, and do not add any scene in which the city draws power from the fig itself.
- **Voices of the dead** are the main lore channel: the founder in 2090 and 2131, Aster's tapes, the first book. This is legal, because it is the AI and cassettes, not the alien, but the climax leans on dead people's recorded speech three times. Consider moving one of these into an object or a bone.

## Clarity: the eleven Amharic terms and the 311

- **The term count is higher than eleven.** Player-facing terms include Amole, And Sew, Engida, Tsehafi, Bete Tsehafi, Shimagile, Chilot, Afe Chilot, Mircha, melikt, ra'iy, Pagume, Arho, Azaj, Fitawrari, wogesha, jebena, ferenj and Sokgi-1, plus coined English (the Narrow, the Spur, the fig, the Hand, the Fig Guard). The bigger problem is **synonyms for one thing**:
  - the alien: Guest, Engida, Foundress, Amole-Abat;
  - time: vision, fever, ra'iy, receipt, send, message, melikt;
  - the hostage: the Shadow, the hostage, the pledge, the first copy, Sitota, "Aster";
  - the AI: Tsehafi, the scribe, Sokgi-1, the record mind.

  The production fix in 07 (English gloss by default) is right. Go further: **one player-facing name per concept**, with the others heard only as faction voice.
- **"And Sew" will read as the English words "and sew".** Use a transliteration such as *Andu Sew* or *Ande Sew*, or "One Person" as the gloss.
- **The 311 is not the risk; the faces are.** The number is memorable and it does a job. The real hazard is that the principal cast includes **eight bodies with one face** (Hiwot 9, Mahlet 16, Tigist 34, Almaz 52, Aster 66, Etagegn 68, Tsehay 71, Sitota 73), and four of them are old women within seven years of each other. The swap needs Aster and Sitota to be close, but Tsehay and Etagegn must be readable at a glance through silhouette, prop, posture and gait. That is: the speaker's staff and ring; Etagegn's stone dust and carving hands; Sitota's carrier's lean, which is already planted. Decide also whether one voice actor plays every Lemlem, which is the honest choice for clones but hard on the player.
- **There are many rules.** Same genome; never received; not before the first jar; plate addressing; failed sends; the open-receipts register; memory cost with skills kept; party-waking; rust; the wake-register; clay line versus doubled line. Each is taught, but the climax uses nearly all of them at once.

## Hidden history and rule consistency (checked across files)

The dates and ages are consistent. The memory-cost arithmetic is consistent. The problems are logical:

1. **The Tsehafi is close to all-seeing, and nothing stops the player using it that way.** Lineage houses must have listening shafts by law; any Lemlem body is a party to every Lemlem record; Tigist is a Lemlem body. She could therefore wake the Lemlem house leaves of 3 to 21 August, when Almaz "tells Tsehay" about her vision and plans, and the house leaf of 23 August. Almaz and Tsehay could equally have woken the house leaf of 22 August and heard "Aster" come in. The outline never places the officers' conversations or the Aster and Sitota planning in unshafted space, and never says whether the Spur gate's shaft is in earshot of the murder at the Guest's door. As written, the AI short-circuits the mystery.
2. **"Outstanding: one" has two meanings.** In 02 it counts *the first book, unread to its subject*. That condition ends at 22:33, when the book is read to Sitota. Yet the line becomes "Outstanding: none" only at 22:52, after the payment, which makes it count *the founder's unpaid receipt*. Pick one meaning.
3. **What makes the alien start shedding contradicts its own perception.** 02 says it begins shedding because "the one who promised has died at its door". But it is genome-blind, so Lemlem bodies have died throughout 73 years, and **the founder herself, the actual promiser, died in 2131** without triggering anything. The trigger has to be something specific to the night, such as "the body of the pledge in my house has died".
4. **The founder's vision does not really narrow the payer to two people.** The exclusion rests on "my bare hand" (no office ring) and "clay to the wrist". Almaz can take off her ring and put her hand in the fig's clay. So the climax is not "genuinely between two people", which 07 says it is. This is actually an opportunity (see Options).
5. **Aster's robe versus identical election cloth.** Almaz's vision shows "a woman in Aster's robe" with her hand on the plate. Chapter 20 has Sitota passing among 310 sisters "in identical election cloth". Both cannot be true. Relatedly, nothing explains why Tsehay, on the dais and knowing exactly who "Aster" is, does not bar her at the roll.
6. **The officers' false belief needs a reason to survive.** Almaz and Tsehay believe "our mother saw her hand open the jar and never did", so the seen can be refused. The founder's last session (2131), a Lemlem leaf any Lemlem may wake, says the opposite. Tsehay was 39 in 2131 and the eldest registered Lemlem, so she may well have been in that room. "No one has dared wake it" needs an in-world taboo or a two-key rule, and Tsehay must be shown to have been absent.
7. **Smaller points:**
   - The first book's receipt appears on the register at 22:25 in section 8 of 02 but in the 22:39-22:41 row of the hall table.
   - Chapter 20 says "forty days ago"; it is 39.
   - 03 calls Hiwot "the city's first fluent speaker of exchange", but Sitota is fluent (perhaps intended, since Sitota is not counted, but state it).
   - The live city-wide count of 311 standing bodies has no mechanism: the Tsehafi counts gates by sweat on a brass bar, not standing people.
   - Trying Almaz for a killing that was legal when she did it is retroactive law; the court needs a line ("void from the start, because it was made in fraud").
   - Tigist serves as identifier in a case where she is legally the owner of the body, which is a conflict of interest worth one line of irony from the clerk.
8. **The rehearsal's cost is under-described.** If Tigist pays for Mahlet's receipt, she loses 25 August to 3 September: the fever-reading, the veil handoff, the medical leaves and the tailing, not just the lottery-night promise. The midpoint (4 September) then has her examining elders whose leaves she no longer remembers reading. That is a great cost, but it must be designed for, with her photographs and notes as her memory, and not ignored.

## Agency

- **Chapter 15 is a false choice.** The writer flags it. The proposed fix ("raid a night later, hand broken in the street") is still cosmetic.
- **The midpoint lie is forced.** "Tigist certifies 'Aster' (a scripted act the player performs by signing)" is the Spec Ops pattern the report warns against. Refusal must be a respected outcome at committed choices.
- **The election result is fixed in every branch.** Mekdes wins whatever the player does, so the player's testimony has no political consequence. This is acceptable only if the player has political choices elsewhere, and they do not.
- **What works:** the rehearsal (three payers, refusal means Etagegn), the payment (refusal means Sitota pays), and the naming (silence is an ending).

## The midpoint handoff and the "unpredictable" problem

The player learns that Aster's medical leaves match the body's lesions and her 2140 wrist break in chapter 10, along with "Aster's" carrier tells. Chapter 12 then *requires* the player to state "the dead woman is Aster" before it opens. Once the dead woman is Aster and "Aster" is walking around, "Aster" must be the extra body. The midpoint therefore delivers nothing the player does not already hold. Meanwhile the bigger reclassification, that **the Shadow is the first copy, older than the speaker**, was given away in chapter 4 (Aster's tape: "She has my face. She is older than any of us. They gave her as a pledge") and in chapter 9 (the paired objects and the plaque).

The back half has the same problem. R7 ("Tsehay sends Almaz's vision and betrays") is one of the four statements that gate the finale, so reveal 17, the "frame-breaking" one, is a conclusion the player has already typed in. That leaves the final act one real reveal, "She is not me", and a careful player will have inferred it from the pledge. The killer is also marked in the prologue: a new rust ring, ochre fingertips, the only ochre-style setter, and a "setter's hold reversed" two chapters later.

Dramatic irony is a legitimate choice (the report favours suspense over blindsides), but the brief asks for *lots* of unforeseen deaths, betrayals and reversals. Twenty days with one death (Aster) and one planned death (the Guest) is thin.

## The election-night climax

Chapter 20 packs these events into 21:00 to 22:52, plus the naming: roll, vote, seal, demand, Dawit standing aside, reading, testimony, ruling that voids the founding law, re-tally, the Arho entrance, Hiwot, Tsehay's send, exchange, the Guest's entry, the glass, the payment, the tape, the naming. Most beats are set up, which is better than season 8. Four are not:
- **The Afe Chilot** voids the city's founding law within five minutes of hearing testimony, with no face, no arc and no earlier ruling we watched them make.
- **Mekdes**, who wins the city, is a face on a fan.
- **The Arho walking the Guest in at 22:40** needs a cue someone arranged.
- **Tsehay "by reflex"** contradicts 07's own defence that "she chose to send". A reflex is not a choice, and the ending's moral hinge needs a choice.

The political climax and the personal climax are also compressed into one scene. The largest, most thematic beat, who pays, is correctly last, but there is no down beat between the fall of the law and the glass.

## Closeness to existing works

- **One person across many bodies, with the internal rift as the drama:** Ann Leckie's *Ancillary Justice* (Anaander Mianaai). This is the nearest work and it is not in the influence log. The difference is real (biological copies grown as babies, no shared mind, a legal fiction rather than a fact), but log it.
- ***Mickey7* / *Mickey 17*:** illegal "multiples" as non-persons is close to Pagume. It is logged, but under a different point.
- **Election thriller:** the historical 1987 sequence and the film *1987: When the Day Comes* (death in custody leads to a forced direct election). The vote wall flipping at the climax is a stock political-drama image; the per-body re-tally is what makes it this story's own.
- **Fixed-history loop caused by trying to prevent it:** Oedipus, *Twelve Monkeys*, *Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban*, Steins;Gate. The trope review keeps it with a reasoned note, but the fresher direction in the trope table was not really tried.
- **Ethiopian handling:** specific and respectful in its nouns. Two gaps:
  - A highland city carved like Lalibela with **no religious life at all** (no churches, no Orthodox calendar life, nothing of the Muslim Afar caravaneers' faith) turns the source culture into an aesthetic of carved stone, which is the decoration risk the brief warns about. Avoiding real rites in the plot is right; erasing faith from the world is not.
  - The Arho are close to "mystic nomads who serve the alien". They "lose nothing" if the truth comes out, and Dahir has no arc.

## Options in 01: strawmen, and where I would pick differently

Several rounds offer one developed option and two one-line strawmen: setting S4; central question Q3; T2 and T3; C2; A2 and A3; X2 and X3; ending E3 (which the writer itself calls the anti-model); hidden history H2. The premise-sentence round is three variants of one idea. The founding-crime and hidden-history rounds are genuinely different and well argued. All grading is self-grading.

Where I would choose differently:
- **Hidden history: promote H3 ("the spent children") from texture to the main path, as a second layer under H1.** It gives the story its missing world-scale rung (the foresight trade sells copies' receipts abroad through the ferenj houses), gives the Hand and the ferenj houses something to do, and makes voiding the "one person" law cost the world, not only Amole.
- **Ending: keep E1, but take the E2 instinct (a dark inheritor) as a third payer.** The ring exclusion fails anyway (rule problem 4). Let **Almaz** be able to pay: ring off, hand in the clay. The killer would forget the killing and the woman she killed it for. That is a real "yes, but" for a player who wants her punished, spared or redeemed, and it makes the naming choice afterwards much harder.
- **Premise sentence: merge options 1 and 3.** For example: "Where copies count as one person, someone must choose whose memories repay the founder's promise." Option 1's "a copy chooses who repays" hides the price.
- **Midpoint: keep M1 as the scene, but change what it reveals** (revision 3).
- **Opening, killer, anchor, AI, alien, time rule, clone rule:** I agree with the picks.

## Required revisions

1. **Limit the Tsehafi so that it cannot solve the case.** Define which spaces have no listening shaft (the Council Pit under the warka, which is open air; the Guest's house; Tunneler galleries; the Spur interior beyond a stated distance from the gate) and put every incriminating conversation there: Almaz and Tsehay on 3 to 21 August, the Aster and Sitota planning, the murder itself. Add a hard limit on waking, for example one wake per body per day, or wakes that require the Chilot's seal, so that each wake is a scarce, public move rather than a free search. State why the officers never wake the house leaf of 22 August (Sitota coming in): they are not looking, or the Tsehafi's audio cannot tell Lemlem voices apart.
2. **Repair the three rule contradictions in 02 and 03.**
   - (a) Redefine "Outstanding: one" as the founder's unpaid receipt, which the Tsehafi learned from the 2090 dictation and was ordered to keep off the public register. "None" at 22:52 then follows.
   - (b) Change the alien's trigger to "the body of the pledge in my house has died". Teach it once: the Arho know it sheds only if its surety dies.
   - (c) Replace the ring exclusion with something that cannot be taken off, or accept Almaz as a third payer (see Options).
3. **Make the midpoint and the final act surprising again.**
   - Cut "older than any of us" and "pledge" from the chapter 4 tape, and let the chapter 9 pairs establish only that the Shadow "came from a jar". The **doubled clay line at the wrist-setting** then lands three facts at once: "Aster" is the Shadow, she is the first copy, and she is older than the speaker who is officially "the first child of the jar".
   - Remove R7 from the finale gate. Gate instead on "the sender wore an office ring", so that Tsehay's send is a reveal and not a confirmation.
   - Add at least one unforeseen, fair shock in Acts III and IV, such as a second death caused by the investigation (Etagegn or Mahlet, planted early), or a reversal of trust from a character the player relies on (Dahir or Jae-won).
4. **Make every committed choice count.**
   - Chapter 15: telling the truth leads to the gallery raid and Jae-won's broken hand. Lying sends the raid to Tigist's flat, where Hiwot is; Hiwot is taken or hurt, and Jae-won keeps his hand and takes down the hall. The two routes converge in chapter 19.
   - Midpoint: let the player certify the truth ("not Aster"). The "yes, but" is that Sitota is declared Pagume and seized to be returned to clay, Act III becomes a rescue from the jar-house, and she reaches the hall without Aster's place on the roll.
   - Give the election one lever the player controls. For example, whether Tigist's testimony is given before or after the ballots are marked could change whether the per-body re-tally is lawful, so Mekdes wins only on one route. Or Dawit's concession depends on what he heard in chapter 18.
5. **De-rush the Mircha and seed what it uses.**
   - Split chapter 20 into two chapters: "The Book" (roll to ruling) and "The Glass" (the Guest to the payment), with a short playable down beat between them, for example Tigist finding Hiwot in the crowd.
   - Give the Afe Chilot a face and a line in chapter 2: the same judge who rules "nobody died" now voids the law, which is a growth arc in two scenes.
   - Give Mekdes one scene with Tigist in Act II or III (the existing *working* note), ideally one tied to the spent-receipts layer.
   - Set up the Arho's entrance as part of the plan in chapter 19: Dahir's cue is the seal breaking.
   - Replace "by reflex" with a visible decision: at 22:44 Tsehay sees the Guest reach for the child and sends to *warn Almaz to stop it*, still believing the seen can be refused because she never heard the second book. Plant earlier a reason she cares about the child, such as a scene of her with Hiwot in chapter 15.
6. **Fix the robe and roll contradiction, and the officers' ignorance of 2131.** Either the elders walk the roll in their own robes (and Sitota's passing is hiding *among elders* from Tsehay's eye), or change Almaz's vision to "a woman with an elder's sash". Establish a Lemlem rule that the saint's leaves need both the Azaj's and the speaker's hands, and that they never agreed to wake them. Make Tsehay absent from the 2131 session, at the Shimagile, so her belief is honest.
7. **Design the rehearsal's full memory cost.** If Tigist pays, she forgets the veil, the leaves and the tailing. Her photographs and the case board, shown as her own handwriting, carry the investigation, and the midpoint is played "from notes". Do the same check for Hiwot paying, who forgets the fan exchange: confirm that the hall hinge still works through the figurine, as a skill she keeps rather than a memory.
8. **Add a world-scale rung (see Options, H3).** The ferenj houses buy copies' receipts. Voiding "one person" makes every copy's receipt her own, which collapses a foreign foresight market, and the outside world reacts in the coda with concrete consequences. Promote "another Guest on another road" from rumour to one sighting, so the successor's walk has somewhere to go.
9. **Grow the side characters who decide the ending.** Mekdes, the Afe Chilot, Dahir (give the Arho a real loss, such as their role and trade as the Guest's road-keepers ending) and Mahlet (her receipt was spent by someone else; give her one act of her own in the hall).

Respell "And Sew". Write a one-page visual identity sheet for the eight Lemlem principals: silhouette, prop, gait, posture and costume colour.
11. **Treat the source cultures as living, not as a look.**
    - Put religious life back into the world without plot-critical rites: a carved church in a pit, a priest's voice in the faction chorus, the Afar caravaneers' prayers at a halt.
    - Own the 1987 Korean sequence in the influence log and bend it: for example, the regime's general behaves honourably (already true) while the opposition is the side that suppresses a death.
    - Give the Tunnelers a stake: their machine's 2090 leaf is in the fig, and what is in it implicates their founders too.
12. **Update the influence and trope logs.** Add *Ancillary Justice*, *1987: When the Day Comes*, "self-fulfilling prophecy" with a record of the alternatives tried, and "mentor or parent is the culprit".
13. **Guard the exchange verb against point-and-click.** Define its input as a constrained statement (choose an object to give, read the returned object as a sentence, and have the game confirm meaning in batches), never "use item X on the Guest to progress". Cap the main-path exchange pairs at the four already listed.
14. **Smaller fixes.**

    - "Forty days" should be 39.
    - Hiwot or Sitota as the "first fluent speaker".
    - A mechanism for the live 311 count, for example every body touching the nearest brass tally bar when her person's ticket wins.

    - A line acknowledging that the identifier is legally the owner of the body.
