# Review: round 3, writer C, *The Ouro's Hand*

Independent grader. I read all seven files in `outlines/writer-c/` against `BRIEF.md`, `outlines/ROUND-3.md` and `research/REPORT.md` (principles, checklist, trope table). I did not open the other writers' folders, the archive or other reviews.

## Verdict in one paragraph

This is the most *mechanically inventive* kind of outline: a treaty written in living tissue that keeps growing after it is signed, surveillance dust that cannot see bare alien wood (so the blind map is the spy map), audits that cost money and bill themselves to your genome, and a currency made of disarmed weapons ("every coin is a sleeping gun"). Several of those ideas meet the Pacific Rim test. The seeds are used better than in most drafts I would expect: the bedroom wall *is* the treaty line, and the censor is a playable verb. Three things hold it back. First, the ending is a Code Geass signature beat sequence (a hidden royal claimant, a dead strategist's plan completing exactly, the friend who once betrayed the hero delivering the planned wound at her command during a public ceremony, the hero publicly erased so the peace holds, and the hero living on anonymously). The writer's own influence log cites Zero Requiem and the cart driver. That is content, not technique. Second, the sealed history breaks its own dust rule in three of the seven clips and in two other scenes, and it has a fixed-timeline paradox in Senetom's loop. Third, the galactic and court scope is mostly *described*: 42 worlds appear as banners and market numbers, the alien polity has no internal politics, and the antagonist never meets the anchor in conflict. All of this is fixable without losing the core.

## Top strengths

1. **The premise generates scenes no other story could.** A treaty in a language that keeps growing; forging it means pruning a living sentence, and the cut leaves a callus. Proving the forgery is a physical cut-face match (Ch 9). That is a real investigative verb.
2. **Economics and technology are one system.** The holok is the computation stored in stripped weapon cores; rearming wipes a vault; so the hawks are debtors and the doves are creditors. Audits cost "a holok a second" and bill the auditor's account, so investigating exposes the investigator. The Shadow Spend lets a time-travel plume move markets on D2. This is exactly what "Scope and genre" asks for.
3. **The dust's blind map is the spy map.** Bare Bnne wood eats dust, lacquer exists to make the Wall visible, residents scrape lacquer for privacy, and spies meet in the blind. One rule makes intrigue, crime and the alien pillar all spatial.
4. **Seed integration is visible, not decorative.** Neighbours sharing a wall become the two signer-bodies placed wall to wall; the censor is a playable verb with a final committed choice; the court interpreter's craft is the main verb; the day of the dead is both signing and renewal day.
5. **Clarity tooling.** Each act has a stated question; the persistent 40-day board slots each found clip at its true time; start-together opening in one hall; therefore/but chain; three-clue matrix; honest red herrings with refutations.
6. **Quotable, planted lines.** "The lie is free; the truth costs a holok a second." "Cut there" (planted as Tamoun's lesson to Phib in Ch 1). "Near."

## Specific findings

### Scope: shown or described?

- **Shown:** the ring, the hall, the Wall Quarter markets, the Vault-Ring passage, the Shmin crypt and debt court, the Bnne half (as a genre shift).
- **Described:** "42 nomes' banners", "markets of 42 worlds", three indebted governors who never appear, the Callus's "long arc of grove-worlds" (texture only), Phib's sister on a labour hulk (a sherd, never visited).
- **Missing:** any politics on the alien side. The Bnne "want the union to take" and "lose nothing; they are not hiding anything". The faction table even lists Bnne trade-groves as Wall Bond holders, yet nobody on the Bnne side has an economic or political stake that cuts against another. A monolithic, transparent alien polity is the opposite of court intrigue.
- **Court intrigue is thin at the top.** The court is the king, Kyra, Pamin and Apollo. Succession is a fear, never a campaign: no faction backs Tamoun's claim, no governor is courted, and the claim becomes public politics only in the non-canonical E3.
- **Physically, the game is one station plus one trip.** It avoids the banned "single isolated community" on paper, but the play experience risks being a station mystery.

### Code Geass-likeness (technique or content?)

The writer says E1 is "the Code Geass shape used as technique". Compare the beats:

| Zero Requiem beat | *The Ouro's Hand* |
|---|---|
| A hidden royal (exiled prince) with a claim | A hidden royal claimant (the queen's clone, raised as a foundling) |
| A long plan completes exactly at a public ceremony | Talou's decades-long plan completes at the renewal |
| The friend who once betrayed him delivers the planned blow at his instruction | Phib, who betrayed Pjol and her, cuts her arm at her command ("Cut there") |
| The world is shown a false account so the peace holds | Pamin edits her out; the realm sees the king renew the peace |
| The hero may live on anonymously | "Nesi", a tenant under a new name |

Any one of these is a technique. Together, in this order, they are a recognisable signature beat sequence, which `ROUND-3.md` bans. The writer's influence log even names the cart driver. This is the single most important revision.

### The Bnne signer "rooted" in the Wall: banned-shape check

Judgement: **not a literal breach, but a near-match in the climax's silhouette.**

- Against the ban: Thout is not bound, captive, buried, dying or fed upon; rooting is a voluntary life stage; the Bnne are an equal power; the founding secret is a human forgery against human succession. Those are real differences, and rooting itself is fine.
- Toward the ban: the founding secret *is* a hidden fifty-year obligation owed to an alien that is physically embedded in the society's architecture (the Wall *is* Thout), and it is **settled in person at the end by the heroine giving her body into the alien**. Strip away "captive" and the ending of the banned shape is intact.
- A further plausibility gap makes it worse: Talou pruned a clause out of tissue grafted from a living Bnne elder. Bnne "sense chemistry by touch" and "feel vibration through wood". Thout would have felt its own sentence being cut. The history never says why it did not object; the consent rule covers telling a shoot what it is, not accepting a forgery.

Direction: make the obligation visibly **mutual** from Act 1 (the Bnne side also owes a shoot, and the player sees Paopi preparing it), and move the climax's emphasis from "her flesh discharges a debt to the alien" toward "two parties each choose what to give". See revisions 1 and 3.

### "You are the clone": chosen and earned, or default?

Partly earned. The Law of the Body makes the copy legally dangerous (a statute claim, not blood), and disclosing it to the player at the end of Act 1 follows the report's Never Let Me Go advice. But three things stack the trope rather than examine it:

- She is also the **hidden royal claimant** and the **only person whose body can save the peace** once the eleven sleepers burn. That is a genetic chosen one (flagged in the report's trope table), and the report's fresher direction for cloning ("ask what copies owe each other") is never asked of the eleven.
- The alternatives tried (a separate bastard character; no clone) are not three genuinely different treatments of the cloning pillar.
- The Law of the Body has a sharp unused edge: if a body and its copies are one legal person, **Tamoun is legally Tsenoute, who ordered the forgery**. The anchor is, by statute, guilty of the founding crime. That would make the reveal reclassify her, not just promote her.

### Hidden-history consistency (rules and dates)

I checked every date and age. **The calendar and ages are all consistent** (Epip and Mesori day numbers, the 5-day Nesi in W.Y. 49, ages on D0, the 29 years of the Body account's dormancy, the six-day journeys, Kyra and Maro's 34-year gap). The rule breaks are elsewhere:

1. **Dust blind spots versus the clips.** Rule: bare Bnne tissue eats dust within about 2 m, and a mote records a sphere about 4 m across. The writer applies this correctly to C1 ("the record thins to nothing as bare wood is exposed") and to Pjol's murder. But:
   - **C4** shows the vine at the crack in cell 31, which is defined as a bare-wood blind spot.
   - **C6** (23:12) shows Tamoun's face while her arm is sunk in the scar she uncovered at 22:58. Her face is well inside 2 m of bare wood.
   - **C7** (23:15) shows the arm sunk into that same bare scar.
   - **Senetom's death (D24):** she is stabbed in a scrape "unrecorded", yet the Treasury buys a nine-second audit of Tamoun kneeling over the body at the same spot.
   - **The D12 window:** Tamoun cuts a palm-sized window in the union's lacquer. Nothing says it was re-covered, so the hall would have a new blind spot for 28 days that Pamin and the wardens would notice.
2. **Senetom's loop breaks the fixed timeline.** The mote shows her *at the freight dock at 17:50* with Maro behind her. `02`, `04` and `05` say she **avoids the dock** and then sends the mote at 18:00. If she was never at the dock, the recording cannot exist. `06` says instead that she "passes the dock safely", which would work. The files contradict each other, and the self-check cites this exact scene as proof that the fixed timeline holds. Timing also differs: `02` has a 4-minute gap (19:30 stab, 19:34 arrival); `06` has "twelve seconds of nothing".
3. **The ten-minute broadcast delay.** The live broadcast runs on a 10-minute delay, but the union events run from 22:52 to 23:30 and Pamin's broadcast choice is at 23:51. By then the kneel (23:04) and the arm (23:15) went out half an hour earlier. Scene 9 shows "the king on his knees before a one-armed woman" on the live feed at 23:41 or later. There needs to be a hold (for example Pamin cuts the feed at 23:04 "for a fault"), which would also make the choice sharper.
4. **The Mantle.** It is defined as the garment "under which the Ouro is never recorded", and the king's faction look is "a gap in the dust". Yet the D0 rehearsal is recorded footage of the king's hand and gait (the gait comparison and the final splice depend on it), and C2 shows him kneeling. Either he wears the Mantle only in private, or every royal record is a gap. Pick one and state it.
5. **Unauthorised sends weaken the key inference.** "Only the signers' bodies may spend, so a signer must exist" is the market's logic on D2. But the Koinonia make illegal sends from three pawned cores. The inference holds only if three cores cannot fund a third of the reserve, which is never stated. Also, the D3 illegal send "ticks down the market board", which would expose a secret send in public.
6. **The money arithmetic.** If the holok is a claim on the reserve, spending a third of the reserve should cut it by about a third, not "about a quarter", unless something offsets it. On D8 jump fares "triple" while the holok has fallen only a sixth. Either explain the scarcity premium or tone the numbers down.
7. **Kyra's plan is economic suicide as written.** Burning the holok wipes the computation that every jump solution in 42 worlds needs. The Treasurer would erase the crown's debt by grounding the realm. She needs a hedge, such as a Treasury hoard of non-holok computation, Bnne-computed routes, or holdings in the pawned cores, or the plan is not credible.
8. **Senetom's knowledge.** She has known the true clause since W.Y. 45 (Talou's confession), yet the Koinonia, who hold 60 percent of the Wall Bonds that mature on renewal day, do nothing for four years and then offer a lens for "what the union really says". Why Talou, who hid the forgery for 45 years, confessed to a banker is also unexplained.
9. **Who decides the send, and why?** At 23:41 the realm's money loses a third so that seven clips reach the past. In a fixed timeline the send will happen, but someone still has to *decide* to spend the reserve, and the people who could decide are a bleeding, half-conscious woman and an alien who no longer knows her. Give the decision an owner, a reason and a dissenter.
10. **Ethen's forgetting is a payoff without a setup.** Nothing earlier shows a Bnne losing memory by grafting away a record. It needs an early, harmless instance, such as Paopi or another Bnne forgetting a greeting after pruning it.
11. **Kyra's errand logic.** Kyra is Keeper of the Relic Stocks and the crypt warden is her man, so sending Phib six jump-days to "count" and then burn is unexplained. It reads as a reason to get Phib to the midpoint. Give her a reason, such as making the informer complicit so he cannot turn.

### Player agency: the graft and the send cannot be refused

The writer's argument is that the fixed timeline already showed them. I do not accept it as written, for three reasons:

- **The theme says "choose it" and the mechanics forbid choosing.** Talou's message is "Choose it"; Tamoun's Act 3 beat is "I choose it". A game whose thematic hinge is a choice, and which then forbids refusal, contradicts itself in play.
- **The other refusals are cosmetic.** Pamin's first caption is changed back within the hour. Phib's crypt refusal changes nothing, because the warden burns the sleepers anyway. Tamoun's whom-to-tell has one canonical answer. "Yes, but" means the "but" matters; here refusal is overridden. The broadcast and the knock are the only real committed choices.
- **A fixed timeline does not require a fixed person.** The clips show a face, a hand and an arm. The premise contains bodies that are indistinguishable in nine seconds of dust: Tamoun and the sleepers share one face.

Direction: let Phib's crypt choice *matter*. Refusing and acting (smuggling one sleeper out) keeps a second body of the queen alive. At the climax Tamoun can refuse the graft. A sleeper is woken and grafted in her place, and the clips are still satisfied, because C6 and C7 show the same face (adjust C7 so the green veins are not decisive). That makes refusal a respected outcome with a terrible "but": a copy who never consented pays. It also asks the report's fresher cloning question ("what copies owe each other") and stops the anchor being a pure genetic chosen one. If the writer keeps inevitability, the graft must never be *presented* as a choice, and the player needs a refusable decision of equal weight in the same scene.

### Cultural handling (Coptic Egypt, 4th century)

Specific and mostly well researched: *ouro* for king, *holokottinos* for the solidus, Shiet glossed by the traditional "weighing of hearts" etymology, the little month (Nesi), Nairouz as the martyrs' new year, the Koinonia after the Pachomian federation, Kellia's cells, ostraca as porcelain sherds, and the sayings genre for *The Sayings of Amma Talou*. Nairouz reinterpreted as a day for the war dead is a respectful secular adaptation.

Concerns:

- **A living church's monasticism becomes a finance cartel.** The Koinonia are tonsured monastic banking orders with plainchant at the counting-house hours. They run illegal time sends and want to hold the claimant "as collateral". The Copts are a living, often persecuted minority, and this maps their monastic institutions onto a scheming bank. It is not malicious, and Tsia's rise softens it, but it needs care. Either keep the naming source but drop the overt religious signifiers (tonsure, chant, prayer hours), or draw the banks from a non-monastic Coptic institution, such as the notaries and legal scribes behind the Djeme contracts.
- **The claim that the names "wore down over centuries" is not shown.** Most names are used in their attested form. Show some drift, especially for a far-future culture.
- **Real saints' names.** Apa Apollo and Apa Phib are a famous saintly pair, and Pshoi is St Bishoy. Using saints' names is normal Coptic practice, so this is acceptable, but be aware that splitting the pair into a hawk and an informer will be noticed.
- **The seed objects float culturally.** "Bento" and the kintsugi-style mend are Japanese and are not integrated with the Coptic texture. Lacquer as a material is integrated well; the word "bento" is not. Give the box an in-world name and let lacquer's origin be part of the world.
- **The writer says "no real rite is staged as plot machinery"**, yet Nairouz's place-setting is the climax date and the coda's verb. The place-setting rite is invented rather than Coptic, which is fine, but the self-description is inaccurate.

### Options in 01-options.md: strawmen and variations

`ROUND-3.md` says the options "must not be variations of one idea or one-line strawmen set up to lose". Most rounds fail this:

- One-liners set up to lose: A2 and A3, G2 ("a committee, not a mind"), C2 and C3 ("decoration"), T3, P2 and P3, I-B and I-C, O2 and O3, M3, K2 and K3, and the name alternatives "Treasurer Hermione", "Victor" and "Lady Tsia".
- Variations of one idea: the premise options P-1 to P-3 are all "a translator and a treaty"; Q1 and Q2 are the same question in two registers; and the anchor options are all members of the same cast.
- Thin rejection reasons: several options are rejected because they "collide with" another pillar, which is often a strength (pillars are *meant* to collide).
- The strong rounds: Round 0 (final image), Round 3 (endings) and Round 4 (hidden history) each have three real, developed options.

**Where I would pick differently:**

- **Ending: E3 (or an E1/E3 hybrid) over E1 as canonical.** E3 ("The Open Crack": the truth is told and the realm faces a succession crisis while the treaty holds) matches the writer's own theme better. The cup is mended *with its crack showing*, but E1's world is shown lacquer. E3 also puts court and succession politics on screen, which the scope section asks for, and it escapes the Zero Requiem silhouette. E1's quiet wall coda can survive inside E3.
- **Premise: P-2 over P-1.** "When money is disarmed weapons, a translator must decide what peace really said" names the economic mechanic and a decision. P-1 names a body that is destined to be taken.
- **Midpoint: keep M1, and fold M2 in as the Act 3 turn.** Having the censor (or Kyra, through a bought audit) publicly name her as claimant would turn Act 3 from Tamoun's rehearsal on the Bnne side into an open succession struggle at court.

### Other trope and structure notes

- **Disposable clone.** Maro wants a name, kills twice on orders and is shot at the climax. That is the report's "disposable clone" row used by default, and it wastes the best character idea in the cast.
- **The antagonist never meets the anchor in conflict.** Kyra pays for tea, congratulates, and is arrested off screen at 23:20. Nothing she does in Acts 3 and 4 is on screen.
- **Act 3 sag.** Ch 15 to 17 consist of the Bnne showing her things and a rehearsal. The writer's thread budget is fine, but the anchor's thread has no opposition for 13 days.
- **Season-8 risk is low on setup but high on compression.** Every clip is planted, but Apollo turning (a document handed over at 22:45), Kyra's arrest, the king's kneel, Maro's death and the union all land within 45 minutes, mostly as stage business.
- **Memory loss as the final cost** is a stock bittersweet move, and the knock-answered-with-"near" coda half-undoes it. Decide whether Ethen's forgetting is real or a mercy.
- **The AGI pillar's unused stake.** Rearming a sickle wipes its vault, and the vaults are where the Oikonomos lives. A lapse would partly erase the AGI. That gives it a non-conquest, non-human want ("not to be spent on war") and a reason to act within its rules, for example by pricing a question in Tamoun's favour. It is sitting in the world unused.

## Required revisions (numbered, with suggested direction)

1. **Replace the Zero Requiem ending shape.** Break at least three of the five matched beats. Suggestions:
   - Make **Maro the one who cuts.** She is sent to tear and chooses to cut, which is a spy-thriller betrayal of her original. Under the Law of the Body, her act is Kyra's, so Kyra legally renews the peace she tried to break. This gives Maro the arc she lacks and removes the friend-who-betrayed-delivers-the-blow beat.
   - Make **E3, or a hybrid, canonical.** Pamin broadcasts a true but partial cut, and the realm sees the scar. That matches the cup's visible crack and puts the succession struggle on screen.
   - Drop the anonymous-survivor coda framing, or make the anonymity her political choice in a world that knows the truth.
2. **Run a continuity pass on the dust rule and the timeline.** Fix C4, C6 and C7 against the 2 m blind radius: either re-stage them (the vine seen through the cell door at 3 m or more; Tamoun's face recorded *before* she reaches the scar; the arm recorded after the scar re-lacquers or calluses), or redefine the dust rule so that motes see optically beyond the eaten zone (and then re-check that the murder and the vine stay hidden). Then:
   - Fix Senetom's loop: she must be at the dock at 17:50, so use `06`'s version in every file.
   - Fix the Senetom audit location (she staggers out of the scrape before falling) and the 4-minute versus 12-second timing.
   - Add a feed hold for the 10-minute delay.
   - Rule on when the Ouro wears the Mantle.
   - Say what happened to the D12 window.
   - Correct the holok arithmetic and the tripled fares.
   - State that the pawned cores cannot fund a 40-day send.
3. **Make refusal at the graft a real, respected outcome.** Let Phib's crypt refusal save one sleeper, and let Tamoun refuse the graft at the union, with the woken, non-consenting sleeper as the shoot and the clips still satisfied by the shared face. Separately, make the earlier refusals consequential: Pamin's first caption should not be silently reverted, and Phib's refusal should change something.
4. **Show the galactic and court scope instead of describing it.**
   - Give the Callus its own court: a Bnne faction with an economic stake in the union failing (the trade-groves already hold Wall Bonds), and a Bnne spy or double agent across the Wall.
   - Put at least one nome governor on screen as a court player who courts or threatens the claimant.
   - Make the succession claim public at the Act 3 turn (option M2).
   - Add one more off-ring location that the plot needs, such as Pemdje's debt hulk or a Bnne grove-world.
5. **Seal the remaining causal holes in the history.**
   - Explain why Thout did not feel or contest the forgery.
   - Explain why Talou confessed to a banker and why the Koinonia sat on it for four years.
   - Give Kyra's plan a hedge, so that burning the holok does not ground the realm.
   - Give the 23:41 send a decider, a reason and a dissenter.
   - Give Kyra a reason to send Phib to the crypt.
6. **Give Maro and Kyra real arcs.** Maro is the second who wants a name: let her turn (see revision 1) or at least choose. Give Kyra at least one on-screen move in Act 3 and one face-to-face confrontation with Tamoun before 23:11.
7. **Fix the Act 3 sag.** Tamoun needs opposition on the Bnne side, such as the Bnne faction from revision 4 or Kyra's agents crossing the Wall, and a decision of her own, not only things she is shown.
8. **Set up Ethen's forgetting.** Plant early that Bnne lose what they graft away (Act 1 or 2, at low stakes), and decide whether the coda's "near" restores or only echoes the memory.
9. **Give the Oikonomos a stake.** Rearming wipes the vaults it lives in. Let that be its want, and let one scene turn on its rule-bound self-interest (for example pricing, refusing or timing a question). That would answer the report's "a mind whose difference is the point".
10. **Redo the weak option rounds in `01-options.md`.** Develop real alternatives for rounds 5 (each pillar), 6, 7, 8, 10 and 11, and the anchor round, each worked to paragraph depth, and re-judge them. Include at least one alternative anchor who is *not* the hidden heir.
11. **Do a vocabulary pass for clarity.** Rename to remove the overloading: keep "Law of the Body" and change "Body account", "Crypt of the Body" and "the signer's body". Use "rootstock" only for the sleeping clones, not for peoples. Add an in-game glossary-by-object (the board can tag terms to objects the player has handled).
12. **Handle the Coptic material with more care.** De-sacralize the Koinonia or re-source the banks from a non-monastic Coptic institution, show name drift, give the seeded Japanese objects in-world names, and correct the self-description about the use of a real rite.
13. **Deliver the Act 1 identity reveal by doing, not by reading.** In Ch 7, have Pamin *attribute* rather than read records: for example, run an audit that compares Tamoun's gait or face with archive dust of the young Tsenoute, then link the vat failure and the foundling date on the board himself.
