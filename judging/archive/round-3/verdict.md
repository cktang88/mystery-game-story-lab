# Judge's verdict: round 3

Judge: independent. I read `BRIEF.md`, the principles, checklist and trope table in `research/REPORT.md`, `outlines/ROUND-3.md`, the three graders' reviews, and every revised file in `outlines/writer-a/`, `writer-b/` and `writer-c/`. I did not open either archive. The reviews predate the revisions, so I checked each fix in the files and did not rely on the revision logs.

## Verdict in brief

**Winner: Writer A, "The Unsigned"**, taken as the base, with five named grafts of technique from B and C (listed below).

- **A** has the one premise of the three that passes the brief's Pacific Rim test outright: the future arrives as a faceless x-ray, so a foreseen death can be *cast* with someone else's body, and the only open question is who signs for it. Its anchor, its object payoffs and its committed choices are the best in the round. Its weakness is still scope on screen.
- **B, "The Light Weight"**, now shows the most galaxy, the most courts and the most Code Geass-mode intrigue, and it did the most honest revision. Its core idea is the least new, and its cast and term load is the heaviest.
- **C, "The Ouro's Hand"**, turns its four pillars into the best verbs and has the most inventive world mechanics. It still plays mostly on one ring, it stacks the clone, hidden heir and chosen body tropes on its anchor, and it keeps the nearest echo of the banned round-2 shape.

## Were the graders' problems actually fixed?

### Writer A

**Fixed in the files.**
- **Authorship law.** Leasing is now separate from authoring. The purpose-of-closure field, the standing rule and the vidai statute are in `02` rules 4 to 6 and are applied consistently in `03`, `05` and `06`.
- **X-ray rule.** Lead sheaths, the inspection cadence, the prologue as the 04:00 safety-film sweep, and Selvan's single-inspection lease all hold.
- **Dates.** The leaf is developed in AS 188, the proxy is grown after it, the loop is twenty-four years deep everywhere, and Ponni docks on Day −7. I rechecked the ages table and the vault arithmetic (30 vault minutes is 25 outside hours; two vault hours is about four days) and they hold.
- **Refusal.** Casting and signing are now two choices. Unsigned is a full ending, the ordeal has four real terms, and the stricter term now buys something (the sole-author rule frees Kayal and Kathir).
- **Act 5** is a real act with its own investigation. The verb list is capped at eight. Proxy property, Nedumaran's knowledge, the garlands, the thali rite and the Ch 5 silhouette are all repaired.

**Only partly fixed.**
- **Scope.** Every playable scene but one is still in a single star system. The galaxy arrives through one flagship chapter, envoy pollen and handled objects.
- **Dune distance.** Feuding houses joined by marriage plus a foresight economy remain. The writer marks this Partial, which is accurate.

**New problems the revision introduced.**
1. A vidai registers "foreseen loss" on a leaf that, by rule 1, cannot tell living from dead. The statute never says what the leaf must show.
2. On the unsigned branch "no one is charged" with taking the body. The Doctrine covers loop closures, not theft in defiance of a court order.
3. Selvan was to file a purpose of closure *after* his loop closed. If retroactive filing is possible, Ponni's 22:55 deadline loses force; if it is not, his promised signature was never a landmark.
4. Thamarai's plan still lacks a causal link: how does letting a proxy die foreseeably produce a signature? The tracker also has her learning of the long leaf on Day −3 with no stated channel.
5. The Balance's "shallowest loop only" rule fixes the Ch 3 leak but reads as a patch, and it must be taught on screen.

## Why A, and not B on scope alone

Scope was the reason for this round, and B now delivers the most of it. But the brief ranks "something new" beside it, and names the Avatar sequels as the anti-model: more worlds and higher scale are not a new idea. B's added courts are well made, and they sit on the least distinctive premise of the three. A's scope gap can be closed with material its own rules already permit. B's premise gap cannot be closed by revision.

C has the highest ceiling as a play system. I did not pick it because its anchor's stacked destiny and its ending's silhouette are structural, and because its 42 worlds have nowhere to appear without a second location thread.

## Recommended combination: A as base, five grafts

Each graft is a technique, not content, and each uses a rule A already has.

1. **From B: a second court played over several chapters.** Expand the Mawsim council from one chapter into a recurring thread with its own succession or faction fight among the nakhodas, as B does with the Ordu captains' council. *Why it fits:* the flagship is already in orbit, so no one breaks the sublight rule, and astrolabe tokens can use the existing trade-beads verb.
2. **From B: a thread that sits on another world and never travels.** B makes courier lag political. A can put a viewpoint on Thinnai (the Irai treasury that cannot post its coupon) who is cut off the moment the bloom closes. *Why it fits:* A forbids fast travel, not a character who is already there. It turns "Thinnai closed in sympathy" from a pollen reading into a played scene.
3. **From B: an arithmetic table for the economy.** B's pool shares and percentage falls make its crash legible. A should state berth-note supply, the bond coupon, the route-share cut and the back-duty in numbers that sum.
4. **From C: rival factions inside the alien polity.** C's union groves and trade-groves give its aliens intrigue. A's Crossing "would lose nothing by the truth". Let blooms disagree across the three envoy rounds, with at least one that gains route share from Ilavenil's sanction. *Why it fits:* the Crossing already deliberates by rounds, so this explains why it takes three.
5. **From C: a living copy with her own arc, and a glossary built from objects.** C's Maro and Nesi ask what copies owe each other through living people. A's proxies' bench is sixty faceless votes. Give one living proxy a face, a fear of gifts and a stake in the decree. Separately, take C's board that tags each term to an object the player has handled. *Why it fits:* one proxy per depositor is unchanged, and A's ledger board already exists.

**Do not import:** B's mutable history or C's priced backward motes. A's single self-consistent timeline is what makes "cast, don't change" work, and a second time model would break it. Do not import B's hostage ending or C's Hearing: A's signature and ordeal already carry the same weight.

## The five biggest open problems in the winning direction

1. **The galaxy is still mostly described.** No playable scene stands on a second world, the alien polity has no internal politics and no individual the cast can deal with, and the foreign court gets one chapter. Grafts 1, 2 and 4 are the minimum. The test is three polities making plot-moving decisions on screen and a meaningful share of play away from Ilavenil.
2. **Rule, verb and branch load.** Eight verbs, about twelve legal and physical rules, some fourteen power players, and a branch matrix of two committed choices, two votes, the urn, four ordeal terms and the last letter. It needs a published branch budget, a decision on which "canon" outcomes are really the player's, and a cold playtest of Acts 1 and 2 before anything else is added.
3. **Five logic gaps in the sealed history.** What a vidai leaf must show when a leaf cannot show death; why no one is charged with the body theft on the unsigned branch; whether a purpose of closure can be filed after the fact; how Thamarai's "let it run" could foreseeably yield a signature, and how she learns of the long leaf; and an on-screen teaching beat for the Balance's shallowest-loop rule.
4. **The shape of the ending.** The emotional climax is Day 0 and the story runs twelve more days. Ponni spends four of them alone in the vault, the killer dies without ever facing her, and the canonical outcome puts the enabler in power and the anchor away for forty years. Act 5 needs a confrontation with Thamarai that is played, not exchanged in two quiet lines, and proof that its second frame-break lands as hard as the first.
5. **The alien pillar is the thinnest in play, and originality needs one more pass.** "Read strata" is lens work on a species that never addresses anyone. The Dune constellation (feuding houses, a marriage, a foresight economy) is still marked Partial, and licensed deaths echo Minority Report. Kathir's thali on Minmini must be reviewed by Tamil readers before it is locked.
