# Review: Writer B, *The Last Tide*

Grader: independent. I read all seven files in `outlines/writer-b/` against `BRIEF.md` and the principles, checklist and trope table in `research/REPORT.md`. I did not open the other writers' outlines.

**Overall: 7 / 10.** This is a serious, well-built outline. Its central device (a hollow in the reef that already waits in your exact shape, and "Nobody crosses. Somebody stays.") generates scenes the way the brief asks. The four pillars depend on one another instead of sitting side by side, and the human anchor is strong. It is held back by four things. First, the chronology breaks in a game whose core verb is reading the tide. Second, the midpoint deduction does not work as written. Third, the structure sits too close to *Dark*, including a character name that collides with one of its characters. Fourth, the investigator-culprit dread lasts about one chapter. Every one of these can be fixed without changing the core.

## Top strengths

1. **A premise mechanic that creates scenes.** "You can only go back where a hollow already waits in your shape" turns the past into a set of constraints the player must make true (Ch5, Ch7, Ch17). Hollow-reading, the hollow hunt, casting faces and the "no hollow on the 17th" proof all follow from one rule. This is the Pacific Rim standard met.
2. **The four pillars collide.** KERF cannot hear Mem, Mem's children are the copies, the copies are the time travel, and the ledger is KERF. Each pillar has its own verbs, its own price and something only it can save, which is close to what Principle 4 asks.
3. **A durable human anchor and an ending that is one act.** Jildou walking her father's killer by the hand is the win and the cost at once. A small decision is left to the player (the forecast), and there is no thesis speech.
4. **Planted objects and lines pay off.** "No enemy out here. Only the tide." moves from Jildou's habit (Ch1) to the gully recording (Ch8) to the murder (Ch12) to Ubbo (Ch18). The shave, the blue clay, the missing lamp and the stuck boot are all photographed in Ch1 and *played* in Ch12. The minidisc reclassifies a childhood grief.
5. **Disciplined documentation.** The sealed solution with canon tiers, the clue matrix (R1 to R11 with at least three clues each), the reveal ledger, the knowledge ledger per character, trap doors per thread, and a self-check that logs fixes honestly.
6. **Names and register.** Frisian naming logic, the copy-class vocabulary split by faction, and *skuld* meaning both debt and guilt carry the theme in the culture's own language.

## Required revisions

1. **Fix the 3 March tide chronology, and lock one tide table for the whole game.** The outline puts low water at 01:50 (02: "2 to 3 Mar, 01:50 low water") and Gerlof drowning on the flood at about 04:30, yet has Jildou's party walking the *bared* Rib at 05:30 to 09:00 (02: "07:10, the Spring Reckoning"; 05 Ch1). On a semi-diurnal coast that is close to high water: the Rib and the Deep Gully would be well under water, and the "four minutes before the gully fills" photography beat cannot happen. The other tide times also disagree: 6 Mar 05:12 and 20 Mar 06:58 are about 14 days apart, which should give nearly the same clock time, not 1 h 46 m later. *Direction:* move Gerlof's dig to the evening low on 2 March (it is dark after about 18:30) and the murder to the evening flood, so that the dawn Spring Reckoning falls on the *next* low. Then generate every tide in the game from one predicted table for an invented gauge, and audit 02, 05 and 06 against it. In a game where the player computes tide curves, the writer's own tides must hold up to a player checking them.

2. **Repair the midpoint deduction (R5): the navel rule cannot rule Femke out.** Rule 5 says *all* copies lack a navel. So a cast with no navel proves only that Kerf Nul is a copy, which is true of every hollow. Femke's having a navel is irrelevant, because any copy of Femke would also lack one (05 Ch9 step 4; 04 decoy list; clue matrix R5; 06 scene 7). *Direction:* let the player rule Femke out with rules already taught. **Age plus the window**: the face is 60 to 65, Femke is 38, and no crossing can be sent after 20 March 2026, so a 63-year-old Femke-face can only be Marrit, her copy. Add the 2003 keyhole scars that only Marrit carries. This is a stronger and fairer deduction, and it teaches the player to reason with the window rule before the finale depends on it.

3. **Take the structure away from *Dark*, and log every remaining match by name.** Specific changes:
   - **Rename Inês Valadares / Dona Inês.** *Dark* has Ines Kahnwald, also shown at different ages. Keep the Portuguese logic and choose another given name. Avoid "Graça", which collides with "the Grace".
   - **Cut the uncaused design loop.** "I learned it to break it. He learned it from me." (the Great Sluice) is the same shape as *Dark*'s self-originating time machine and book. *Direction:* give the knowledge a real first cause. Master Douwes builds the sluice from his ordinary trade and adds one bypass *because he remembers the 2026 silting*. Tjalling opens that bypass. Information then flows from an event, not from nowhere.
   - **Reduce how many people exist at two ages, or make it visibly systematic.** Show every copy on screen by the legal convention the writer already invented (given name + hollow number, e.g. "Marrit 1994-212"), and add a lineage strip on the Attribution Board. Consider leaving Tjalling's copy as a monument and a set of drawings, never staged as a "double".
   - **Rewrite the influence-log entry for *Dark*** so it lists, beat by beat, the elements that match (two-age casts under different names, an older self opposing a younger self, a body from another time belonging to a relative, bootstrap artefacts, a northern coastal town) and what was changed for each. Add *Outlander* for the time-travelling woman tried as a witch, and say what makes the Mud-Woman's chapter different (she is drowned *as the record requires*, and the player knows it).

4. **Stop the founding crime from being taught to the founders by Marrit's copy.** In 02 the witch tells the survivors that "anyone who lies in her makes a frightened child flee into the past in their shape". That is the instruction manual. Kanter's test then closes a loop with no first cause (his copy appears because he lies down because his copy appeared). So the nation's "ancient crime" originates in 2026, which weakens the iceberg's premise (Principle 13) and adds another *Dark*-style ontological loop. *Direction:* let the founders discover the reflex from the fourteen animal copies already in the history (seals "walking into houses", 22 to 24 Jan), and make Kanter's experiment a deliberate choice made after that. Then the witch's words cause only the *misreading* that leads to the throat-cutting. Make that her tragedy: Marrit has heard the 1371 recording (Ch14), knows her warning will be misheard, and the player in Ch17 can try other words while the founders still hear "she calls the sea". That gives the no-fail-state chapter a real choice inside it.

5. **Close Marrit's and Gerlof's motive holes.**
   - Marrit has believed since 2019 that the throat is a mortal danger to 11,000 people and has been Chief Reckoner with legal power over the Witch's Grave. Why did she leave it in the grave for seven years?
   - Gerlof has known since 2024 to 2025 where the throat is. Why does he dig on the one night he knows he dies?
   - The slab then lies open on the nation's most forbidden site for nine days after a seismic alert the Chief Reckoner herself answered, and nobody notices before the Ch9 casting.

   *Direction:* one rule fixes all three. Kerf Nul (by the church stump, the lowest point) bares only at the rarest springs. Make the 3 March full-moon spring the first such tide since 2019, plant it in Ch1 ("the first time the tower stump has shown in seven years"), and have Marrit plan to remove the throat at the *next* one. Have Marrit reseal the slab with fresh clay after the murder, so the Ch9 team finds new clay over old: a clue in its own right.

6. **Make the investigator-culprit play as real dread, as the report's fresher direction asks.** At present the player learns Marrit is the killer at the end of Ch12 and Jildou proves it at the start of Ch13. There is not a single scene of Marrit and Jildou together under dread. *Direction:* move Gerlof's Last Night to directly after the midpoint (before The Gate). Then let Marrit console Jildou after Okke's death, arrange the funeral, and offer to protect Femke, all played while the player knows. Keep Ch11's archival chapter after it. That also makes the guessable culprit an asset instead of a weak surprise.

7. **Give Jildou her own route to the seventh trap.** Ch16 says Jildou finds the throat only if the player remembers Ch12 ("only Ch12 tells them to"), and her clues for R9 are thin ("seventh trap underwater", "traps untouched"). The climax then rests on what the player carries over, not on the anchor's competence. *Direction:* keep the player's knowledge as dramatic irony, but give Jildou three in-world clues so she can reach the same conclusion on the board:
   - in Ch8, the hum shows footsteps leaving Mem's body *toward the trap line* and returning empty-handed;
   - in Ch2, trap six holds something Gerlof left that night (a fresh knot, his razor);
   - in Ch3, a childhood line: "the seventh only bares at the year's lowest tide".

   Jildou's growth from tide-reader to listener then pays off in her own deduction.

8. **Let the player make the frame-breaking deductions; cut the announcements.** In Ch14, KERF says "The reef is a mother. The Slikborn are her children ... The call is a choice." That breaks "the avatar never voices a deduction the player has not entered". Marrit's Ch13 confession is the summation trope. *Direction:* make R8 an Attribution Board set the player assembles (navel, "Mem", the Sob as a scream, the inverted structure of the Promise, the witch's "free to go home"). KERF confirms only the set. Cut Marrit's confession to two or three lines against the evidence the player has laid out (the lamp on the plinth already does most of the work).

9. **Pay off disaster three and give Ubbo a real endgame.** The one-paragraph outline makes "the state now knows a stolen throat exists and hunts it" disaster three, yet in Act IV Jildou carries the throat through Ubbo's guarded queue unchallenged, and he ends lying on a dome. The main antagonist never meets the anchor in a scene of opposition. It is also not explained why the state lets a pilot with a criminal record lead the last crossing. *Direction:*
   - Plant that Jildou is the only licensed pilot who can read the silted Rib after the blast (cost: the guild's other pilots refused, or were the Skuldfrij's drivers).
   - Stage one face-to-face scene between Ubbo and Jildou in Ch15.
   - In Ch16, make getting the throat past the search a stealth-pilot beat. She hides it in the eel trap she carries, or hands it to Marrit, who carries it under her gloves to Kerf Nul, which is a better hand-off.
   - Give Ubbo one active choice at the Mouth before the dome.

10. **Lock the lore around the call and fix the epilogue's setup.**
    - (a) State in 02 whether children of copies hear the call (655 years of fertile copies imply a large descended population, and players will ask), and what "walking into the sea" is: death, transformation or departure. At present it is ambiguous at the emotional peak.
    - (b) KERF's last question ("compute a forecast of your life") is a new power in the final act. Nothing shows KERF forecasting lives. Either plant it (KERF writes the Skuldigen's schooling curricula from their copies' testimony, which is a forecast) or change the last question to one KERF can actually offer.
    - (c) Give the world-scale consequence one playable beat before the epilogue (one Grauwarf family deciding whether to follow their Slikborn mother), so it is not only footage on dike screens.

11. **Remove the omnitemporal-perception cheat from everything that solves the mystery.** 03 says the cheat "is never used to solve a problem". But the hum (every sound ever made, from Mem's all-at-once perception) solves R7, and the Promise (a future echo) is evidence for R8 and drives Ch14. *Direction:* make the hum physical. Each tide lays down a mineral layer that records sound, much as a lathe cuts a record, which also explains why pitch follows tide height. Keep the admitted cheat only for the crossing itself, which is the premise. Rework the Promise as the brood's inherited call-pattern that Mem has hummed incompletely since the Sob. This also moves Mem away from the *Arrival* signature.

12. **Make the current question unambiguous at each act.** Act II and Act III both list "who drowned Gerlof?", while the midpoint says it flipped the question to "why will Marrit go to the founding?". *Direction:* one question per act on the Tide Board. Act I: who is he? Act II: who was in the gully, and what was under the grave? Act III (after the midpoint): what did the witch do, and what is the state hiding? Act IV: what will the Last Tide do? Keep "who killed Gerlof" as Jildou's personal want line, not the act question.

13. **Fix the premise sentence.** "A tide-pilot walks her father into the past to learn who drowned his older self" contradicts the world's own key line: nobody goes into the past, the father stays. It also gets the causality wrong, because walking him does not help her learn anything. *Direction:* "A tide-pilot must walk her father to the hollow that made his drowned double." (14 words, names the mechanic and the loss.)

14. **Small continuity fixes.**
    - 02 has the 2001 police report record "a bearded tramp", but Gerlof arrives clean-shaven, as a copy of 47-year-old Okke, and 05/06 have the report say "a man in a cassock".
    - Jildou identifies her own godmother's voice "from her own licensing exam" (Ch13). She would know it from a lifetime.
    - The **Mouth** first appears in Ch18. Plant it in the Ch14 echo-walk, where the founders dig.
    - The Hiske–Dona Inês bond that pays off in Ch18 is never seeded.
    - Femke's "I couldn't have thrown the light away" is set up and paid off in the same chapter. Plant Marrit as a keeper of objects earlier (Ch6 in the Gallery).
    - The 2003 entry is out of order in the chronology.

15. **Redo three option rounds with a separate grader and genuinely different options.**
    - Round 5: H1 and H2 are the same idea (a modern person is the witch) with the character swapped. Add at least one hidden history in which the witch is *not* from 2026, for example a 1371 Ammerwarf girl copied by Mem in the first hours who speaks for her mother.
    - Round 6: E2 and E3 are strawmen.
    - Round 7: the "twelve-year-old" anchor and the "journalist" outsider are strawmen.
    - Add the missing rounds: three options for the single identity reveal (the Slikborn are Mem's children) and for the Ch17 frame-breaker.

## Where I would pick differently in `01-options.md`

- **Round 8 (premise):** P1 as picked is factually wrong about the world (see revision 13). I would keep its personal scale and use the corrected sentence. P2 stays rejected for spoiling the reveal.
- **Round 3, Aliens:** L1 is right, but I would not have it "absorb L3's sound" through all-of-time perception. I would absorb it as a **physical recording** (see revision 11). The *Arrival*-style perception is the least original part of the alien, and it is the one thing the writer admits to as a cheat.
- **Round 5 (hidden history):** I would keep **H1**. It gives the best midpoint, the best culprit motive and a single causal chain from murder to founding crime to ending. But I would graft on the fix in revision 4 (the founders learn the reflex from the animal copies, not from the witch), so H1 stops being a self-founding loop.
- **Round 1 (setting):** A is the right pick and Frisian naming is a real asset. The *Dark* risk should be handled the way the writer promised ("by the rules, not by changing the coast"), and revision 3 is that work, not yet done. A different coast is not required.
- **All other picks** (Round 2 A, T1, C1+C3, A1, Q1, E1, the culprit Marrit, the three anchor scenes): I agree with them, for the reasons the writer gives.

## On the writer's flagged risks

- **Copies at different ages (clarity).** The risk is real, and it is already showing in the writer's own logic (the navel error in revision 2 comes from this confusion). Okke/Gerlof and the two Inêses (once renamed) are clear, because each pair has one strong visual difference and is introduced plainly. The Femke → Marrit → Mud-Woman chain, where Marrit is both a copy and an original, is the hard case. It needs the on-screen lineage convention and the age-and-window deduction from revision 3 and revision 2, plus the cold-reader test the writer proposes for the Ch2 and Ch9 boards.
- **Investigator-as-culprit.** It earns its place because Marrit's motive comes from the founding lie and her arc is the largest in the story. In its current placement it gives neither surprise nor dread. Revision 6 fixes that.
- **The modern person becoming the founding witch through a loop.** It earns its place as the midpoint and as the pageant's reclassification; it is the most memorable idea in the outline. In its current form it also founds the crime itself, which it should not. Revision 4 fixes that.
- **The not-human reveal (the Slikborn are Mem's children).** It earns its place. It is clued from minute one (the navel, "Mem"), it reframes the copies as a question of origin rather than "they were monsters", and it is the only identity reveal on the alien side. It needs its own options round (revision 15) and its lineage rules locked (revision 10a). Without those, 655 years of descendants make it leak.
