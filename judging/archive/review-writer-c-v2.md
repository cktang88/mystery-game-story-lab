# Review: Writer C v2, *The Walking Market*

Grader: independent. I read only `outlines/writer-c/` (all seven files), `BRIEF.md` and `research/REPORT.md`. I did not open the other writers' work or the archives.

**Verdict in one line.** This is the most *generative* premise I would expect from this round: split bone receipts that show their future marks, a city that may never stop, and the dead walking in puppeted clone bodies give the game four investigative verbs that no other story has. The seeds are fully digested and the naming system is excellent. But the foresight rule is not applied consistently to the receipts the plot itself uses. Two "respected" refusal branches are really the plot overriding the player. The cloning pillar dies with Uke on D1. And the nearest works are not *2312* but *Snowpiercer* and Christopher Priest's *Inverted World*, which the self-check misses.

## 2. Top strengths

1. **A premise that generates its own verbs.** Each pillar has a verb that only it can produce: reading and staking out shadow marks (time), version-diffing two centuries of stagings (AGI), body attribution by feet and grip (cloning), and gift-grammar with ledger recall (alien). The four collide properly: clones are the only unforeseeable people, and on D60 everyone becomes one.
2. **The seeds are digested, not decorated.** Puppeteer, pigeon courier, receipt, abacus, astrolabe, colour blindness, bone, buffalo, market day and debtor-creditor are all on the critical path. The astrolabe alone does triple duty: the colour-blindness clue, the midnight prop and the navigation home, which turns a lore clue into a final gameplay correction. The silk-road oasis, reinterpreted as a moving spring band, is the boldest reinterpretation.
3. **A naming system the player can learn in one scene.** Japanese-founded houses give Mande given names, Malian houses give Japanese ones, and founders keep unmixed names. It dates people, it comes from the treaty of mutual debt, and it avoids both the generic pool and an ethnicity-equals-class reading.
4. **The twist is a reading error, repeated at three scales.** The record is true; the reading is wrong (Ofusa's hobble read as a tether, Dōjima's reading of "was left", the column's reading of the blank receipts). This is the research's "reclassify, don't add" principle at its best, and the D1 buffalo scene plants it inside a scene that is about something else.
5. **Memorable, planted moments.** Jūzaburō asking Sayo to work his feet one last time (D19); the receipts lying whole in the ashes (D44, paying off the D3 cookfire); the Mother hobbled mid-stage and the abacus going silent (D55); "settled" cut into a dead friend's receipt, a forgiveness she had seen for five years. The D19 walk is designed as the inverse of D60, which is exactly right.
6. **Seydou's thread.** The bomb-under-the-table design (the player knows he held Uke's arms from D1, while Sayo does not) honours "hide by viewpoint, not by withholding". The foretold D31 confession makes the time pillar a character beat.

## 4. Banned list and seeds

- **Sea, coast, island and northern-European naming; drowning; site- or interval-bound time; alien voices; village setting; reunion across time:** all **pass**. The time rule rides on portable receipt pairs and the creditor's life. The dead's voice belongs to the AGI and a human chanter, not to the alien.
- **The bound-alien ban (the writer's "Partial"). My judgement: no literal breach, a partial breach in spirit, acceptable only with revision 5.** It is not buried, bound, beneath anyone or captive, and 31,000 people is not a small community. But the deep skeleton of the banned template survives intact: a society's founding secret is an unpaid debt to a non-human being whose body supplies the society's key resource (here, rule through receipts); the anchor uncovers the debt and settles it in person at the end, and the alien's want is met by her choice. The inversion (the "sacrifice" was really company) is real and good, but it inverts the *price*, not the *shape*. The writer's own defence ("the caravan does not depend on it to survive") is weakened by the fact that the front's entire legal order, credit and marriages run on its bone.
- **Seeds:** all ten types visibly shape the story. Strongest: puppeteer, receipt, colour blindness, water buffalo (hobble against tether is the whole price) and market day (a structural clock). Weakest: *erosion* rests mostly on the replastering custom (Timbuktu) and lands as a word more than as a mechanic, except in the version-diff; and the *silk road* half of "silk-road oasis" (cross-cultural long-distance trade) barely appears beyond the off-screen Kitasugu caravan. Neither is dropped, but both could do more: Kitasugu trade at the equator crossing could be the iceberg glimpse that carries the silk road.

## 5. Required revisions (numbered, concrete)

### A. Rules and coherence (must fix)

1. **Write a cutting protocol and audit every receipt in the plot against it.** State which events are cut on which receipt (life, court, debt, post, pay). Suggested direction: court receipts record only *corrections to the court record*; verdicts and sentences are cut on the accused's *life* receipt, whose house half only Dōjima reads and whose body half is taboo. This removes the D7 spoiler (problem a) and creates a great beat: Dōjima knew since YM 153 that Jūzaburō "was left" on D19 and ordered him to kill anyway, because fate must be helped. Then re-run consistency check 1 in `02` against every receipt in the outline, including the day-count, the newborns' receipts and the plate supply (problem g).
2. **Fix ledger recall for refused gifts.** Either refused objects are left at its feet and taken into its plates, which makes recall a rule (and "unchanged" means "refused, and kept as evidence of the offer"), or Uke left the tether there after the refusal. Pick one and teach it during the D25 experiments.
3. **Rebuild Branch B (refusing to stay) so that refusal is respected *and* the receipts stay true.** At present refusal is punished by forcing Sayo into the same physical place, and she must cut a "settlement" she did not make. Suggested direction: the hobble-mark does not name Sayo, so in Branch B **Gonbei** stays. His silent hour at the creature's side on D60 is already canon, and his D45 mask gift is already planted, so the man who came to kill it becomes its companion and cuts the hobble-mark. The hard-line houses still leave Sayo tethered (her "was left" is passive and literally true), and the Jurutigi's last gift turns her tether into a hobble. Refusal then changes *who* pays the debt, at a visible cost to someone the player knows, and the receipt records a real settlement. Update the locked "same cutter in all branches" line in `02`.
4. **Fix the D19 and D26 to D30 Seydou choices (writer-flagged; I agree, with a different fix).** The rules do not require the arrest to happen: Tōkichi's routing only fixes his arrival at the House of Forms at D20 dawn. So let the refusal stand. If Seydou refuses, there is no arrest, Tōkichi still arrives (the routing is fixed) and finds Kadiatou packing to flee; she confesses the same way. Seydou's D43 motive for the D20 cut changes from "undo my arrest order" to "put the boy where she would run". Remember that Seydou has held the office half since D2: on D19 he and the player *already see* a courier routed to the House of Forms at dawn, so let that dramatic irony inform the choice. For "wait" at D31, either write how the midpoint reversal (R8) reaches the player when the correction clears Tenmaya instead, or remove the choice and move the agency to *what Seydou says* when he cuts. Do not label overrides as respected.

### B. Originality and banned-list spirit

5. **Break the Snowpiercer and Inverted World geometry, and push the alien further from the banned skeleton.** The nearest works are not *2312*. They are *Snowpiercer* (a closed society that must never stop, a rich front and a poor rear, rear insurgents attacking the front's sacred "engine", here literally called "the front's oracle engine" in the Juruntan's belief) and Priest's *Inverted World* (a city that must keep moving to stay in its habitable zone, guild secrets, and an apprentice who learns the truth of the world). Directions: (a) the spring band is hundreds of kilometres wide, so make the column a **broad crescent abreast** with class by *distance from the wells or the creature*, or rotate positions by debt on every market day, rather than front versus rear; (b) re-aim the Juruntan's D44 target away from the "sacred engine" pattern, for instance at the dead's vessels rather than the vault. For the alien: give the Jurutigi one want that is **not about the caravan**. The old split receipt with its missing half is already planted: let its last act point outward (for example, the missing half is among the bone arches on the far plain, or with the Kitasugu caravan), so that the ending opens the world instead of closing a community's private debt to its creature. Add *Snowpiercer*, *Inverted World* and *Arrival* to the influence log, each with its difference stated.

### C. Pillars, stakes and convergence

6. **Keep cloning alive after Act 1.** Uke dies on D1, her personhood is proved on D13, and afterwards cloning is only an off-screen line ("Kadiatou wakes two of nine"). The collision the writer advertises, *a clone is the one person the machine cannot foresee*, is never used by a living clone; the Juruntan duplicate that power and make it moot. Direction: a woken vessel joins in Act 3 or 4. The strongest candidate is **Vessel 14, the murder weapon itself**: Kadiatou and Sayo wake her, so a living woman whose body killed Uke must decide what she owes. She is the person who can act unforeseen on D57 to D60 where a receiptless Juruntan cannot (she can pass as the Mother). This also gives Kadiatou an on-screen arc.
7. **Turn the Vessel 13 recognition gap into a choice Sayo made (problem h).** Rather than explain it away, make it her secret from herself: she half-knew whose feet those were in YM 208 and chose not to ask. Reveal it on D5 when her hands recognise the gait. This deepens "the record is the truth" into "I chose not to read", and makes her the third person after Ofusa and Dōjima to misread on purpose.
8. **Plant an active "stayed" mark, and teach the stay/leave glyph.** The bevel lesson uses *paid* / *was paid* (the same verb), but the ending needs *stayed* / *was left* to be one glyph read two ways, and no active "stayed" mark ever appears on screen. Add one: an old herder receipt from YM 1 to 3, before the stayer custom was abolished, in the Sidibé chest or at a founders' wellhead, showing the active form. Teach the glyph pair in the D1 Mochi scene. Otherwise the D53 deduction leans on a rule the player was never shown.
9. **Make the D60 world stake depend on choices, and give Act 5 a face.** At present foresight ends on D60 whatever anyone does; the "three hard-line houses" are unnamed; and in canon nobody actually opposes Sayo on D60. Directions: (a) name and plant the leader of the hard-line houses in Act 1 (a Nakama member with a personal stake, such as a house whose dead foretold their own account); (b) tie the column's survival to the choice, for example the hard-liners plan to **halt the whole column** on D60 to perform the tethering, so that never-stop and the summer front become the world-scale stake and Sayo's staying alone is what lets the city keep walking. Also move the Gonbei reveal and Tōkichi's denunciation choice earlier than D56 (or give them a second scene), so that Act 5 does not open with a new revelation plus a committed choice four days before the climax. That is a season-8 compression risk.
10. **Cut the bloodline (writer-flagged chosen-one risk; I agree).** Sayo's "was left" mark exists because of her own choice on D60, so the Bakary lineage adds nothing causally and reads as fated descent. Make her of an unrelated rear herding house. The hobbled staff reaches her through Ume as a herders' guild relic, not a Sidibé heirloom, and **Dōjima** is the one who believes the account names Bakary's line, as one more inherited misreading.

### D. Options process

11. **Replace the strawman options.** Several option sets were built to lose: C2 ("the Avatar-sequel problem"), C3 ("the disposable-clone trope"), L3 ("the body-snatcher trope"), A2, F2 and T3, plus setting options S2 and S4, which are rejected as "the most generic". Write at least one serious competitor for cloning and one for the alien. For example, cloning: *vessels are the caravan's legal stayers, grown to be left with the dying so that no living person has to*; alien: *the creditor is one of many, and the caravan has been walking through a herd's graveyard*. Grade them honestly. Also write out the three options for the minor names the writer admits are missing.

## 6. Where I would pick a different option in `01-options.md`

- **Premise (Round 7):** I would merge P1 with P2's world clause: *"In a city that must never stop, a puppeteer's receipt says she'll be left behind."* (15 words.) P1 omits the most striking element of the setting, and never-stop is what the ending breaks.
- **Woken clone (Round 6):** keep (b), Uke as the victim, but add a living woken vessel later (revision 6). The writer rejected (a) as though the two were exclusive; the story needs both.
- **Anchor's ancestry (Round 6):** reject the "of Bakary's line" detail attached to option (c) (revision 10).
- **Everything else:** I agree with S3, N3, Q1, H2, T1, A1, C1, L1, F1, E2 followed by E1, and the midpoint and climax choices. N3 and H2 in particular are the right picks by a wide margin.
