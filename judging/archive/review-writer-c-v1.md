# Review: Writer C, LANDUNTER

Grader: independent; did not read writers A or B. Sources checked: all seven files in `outlines/writer-c/` against `BRIEF.md` and `research/REPORT.md` (principles, checklist, trope table). I checked the crossing ledger and both key nights (21 Sep 2028, 21 Sep 2047) minute by minute, plus every age, date and object that crosses between files.

**Overall: 6.6 / 10.** This is a strong and genuinely specific premise. The weight-for-weight exchange and the archive that "can't lie, only look away" both create new scenes, and the setting and names are the best defence against averaging I would expect to see. But the sealed history is not sealed. It has rule ambiguities that matter on exactly the two nights the mystery depends on, several continuity contradictions between files, and a state antagonist that only stays ignorant because nobody ever asks its own machine an obvious question. The self-check marks several items "Pass" that fail. The *Dark* risk is larger than the writer logged, and on one central point the self-check claims the opposite of what the hidden history says. The ending's fixed crossing also does not yet respect refusal as the report requires.

## Top strengths (keep these through every revision)

1. **The Waag's exchange rule generates scenes.** "Weight for a weight. Nineteen years. Once." To send someone back you must take a stranger forward. Agents starve themselves to a weight, a girl is fed to 56 kg, and the floor load sensors show people no camera saw. Hum pitch gives weight, so an old woman's hobby tapes become hard evidence. This is the brief's "core idea generates new kinds of scenes" done properly.
2. **The Schriever is the best AGI concept in the brief's terms.** It is append-only, it cannot lie and it volunteers nothing. Archived versions can be cross-examined, it has a real want of its own (the 41 minutes), and its arc is bookended by a line planted in the first minute ("Close your eyes. Just for a bit."). It fits the report's "unreliable instrument" exactly and avoids every AGI cliché in the trope table.
3. **The setting and names are specific and derived, not averaged.** The tide works as a clock, flushed birds as an alarm, fog as a stealth layer and the Warften as islands in a flood. Names carry in-world logic (the "name comes back" custom, patronymics, Hark / Schriever / ZG-Archiv as three layers). This is the brief's "Fighting the average" at its best.
4. **The founding crime is a working causal chain.** The Wedderkinner trade leads to the pastor's changeling lie, to Keike's rejection of her "daughter", to the debt doctrine, to the Nommensen director and to the modern Kept. *Schuld* as both debt and guilt is a gift. Graves that glow under their own boasts is a memorable image.
5. **The ending image and the "known-timeline bar".** The goodbye and the reunion happen in the same minute. The interface shows where everyone's knowledge of the future runs out, and the climax is fought past it. This is new structural territory for a fixed-timeline story.
6. **Clue craft.** There is a three-clue matrix, guilt over the bird band is refuted by a date the player must find, the band text the player typed returns in the camera blur and in the kitchen, and the Priel lesson pays off as Knutt's death.

## Required revisions

### A. Make the sealed history actually sealed

**1. The genome and gestation loop contradicts the self-check, and it is the *Dark* / *Predestination* signature.**
02 §5 says the mother is "implanted with a clone made from her own cell". Her own body *is* Ebba's, so Ebba's genome is copied from Ebba, and the genome has no origin. It equals Inken's only by stipulation. Ebba also gestates and gives birth to herself. 07 §4 says the opposite ("no person who is their own ancestor (Ebba's genome comes from Inken)"), and that claim is false.
*Direction:* Make the genome come from a stored sample of Inken. For example, Inken's milk teeth that Keike kept in a tin on the dresser: the mother steals one, and the missing tooth becomes one of Keike's reasons for calling her a changeling, which also gives Keike a clue role. Then either have a surrogate carry the child (for instance at the Odense clinic Kerrin's family used, which pulls Kerrin into the plot), or keep the self-gestation deliberately. If you keep it, list it as the story's one admitted causal loop, log *Predestination* ("All You Zombies") and *Dark* (Charlotte and Elisabeth) in the influence log, and correct 07.

**2. Make the Waag's rule exact, then write a ledger row for every living body inside the ring on both key nights.** As written, the rule cannot answer questions the key nights raise:
- **Direction and trigger.** The rule is phrased one way ("nineteen years *earlier*"), yet Tade stands in the 2028 ring from 23:30, where he could also match a 68-kg body from 2009. Say which side triggers a crossing, whether only the later year's entry triggers it, and how long the crossing window stays open on a Weegnacht. The 2028 Bote crosses at 23:20 and the others at 23:40, so the window is not a single instant.
- **Tolerance.** A fixed ±2 kg pairs a 50 g crab with a 550 g oystercatcher, and every mudflat organism with every other. Every lugworm and snail on the flats would also cross and hum, which ruins "Marret's tapes catch every hum". Use a proportional tolerance (about ±1 %, which still fits 56.0 / 56.3 and 68.0 / 68.2) and a minimum mass, or limit crossings to air-breathing bodies standing above the water line.
- **What counts as weight.** Clothes and held objects travel, so do they count? Levin crosses in a station drysuit (3–4 kg). If clothing counts, 68.0 kg plus the suit is outside the tolerance.
- **Held people.** Inken's fist is in Tade's jacket, which Tade is wearing. This works only because both crossings happen in the same instant, so state that rule, because it is load-bearing for the convergence image.
- **Moiken at 23:40.** In 2047 Moiken "walks Ebba into the ring". She is Inken's genetic daughter and nobody gives her weight. If she is within tolerance of Inken (56.3), she competes with Ebba for the match. Either keep Moiken outside the posts (hand-holding up to the line) or state her weight.
- **Soldiers, guards and cutters in the ring after 23:40.** The 2028 guards are stumbling blind around the ring, and the 2047 soldiers and cutters move in at 23:42. Under the rule as written they could swap. If fruiting ends all crossings at 23:41, say so, and say what that means for the 2028 side.
- **Inken in the ring on 20 March 2028.** She is in the ring on Weegnacht when she finds the bird. Why does she not cross?

**3. Fix the continuity contradictions.**
- **The Schuldbuch.** 02 §3 says Jens Nommensen found it in 1984 and "takes it to Bonn". 02 §6, 05 Ch 15 and 06 §8 have Marret take it down from the tower in 2047. Pick one: for example, Jens took a copy, or Marret's family hid the original before 1984 and Bonn holds a forgery.
- **The ewe.** A "young ewe with a 2028 ear tag" appears in Ch 4, which is in March and before the 20 March 2047 Weegnacht. A body from 2028 can only arrive in 2047 on 20 March or 21 September 2047. Move the ewe after Ch 5, or give it a 2027 tag and an arrival in September 2046. Also say whether it is the sheep that glows in Keike's fold in Ch 15, since Knutt took the first one away.
- **The oilskin.** 02 §4 says Ebba crosses in a *replica*. 05 Ch 10 says the guest-room oilskin, size S, is "for her to wear into the ring". 04 and 06 §10 say Keike gives her "her real daughter's yellow oilskin". But Inken wore her real oilskin into the ring in 2028 and arrives in it. Decide which oilskin crosses. If it is a real object of Inken's, track the duplicate it creates: it would exist twice between 2028 and 2047.
- **The grave name.** The gravestone reads "Ebba Brarens, 2029", yet the child later "names herself Ebba" after the ebb tide. This is a name with no origin. Either the mother names the grave (and the child) knowingly, which you should say, or the grave carries another name.
- **Smaller fixes.** Henning still wears "2009 clothes" after 19 years in Haus Seegatt. "My son is older than my little brother" fails if both are 18: make the son 19. "The state has always known its horizon would stop at March 2047" contradicts the state learning it in 2029. And how does Henning see a wrist tattoo on a girl running past in fog, at night, in oilskin sleeves?

**4. Close the plot holes that rest on state incompetence.** The secret survives 19 years only because the state never asks its own perfect witness anything:
- Crossers glow under 370 nm UV, and the state has run crossings for 38 years. After a lost Bote and Inken's admitted interference, it would UV-screen "Inken" in 2028.
- The floor load sensors would record the pregnancy, a live baby on the night of a "stillbirth", the mother's weekly night walks to Süderwarf, and Knutt's "68 plus a child's 20".
- The state has hunted a 68-kg lost Bote since 2045, yet never queries the frames after the 41-minute gap, never audits a logged fork, and leaves a schoolchild's nickname working as a master key that blinds its surveillance after the 2028 incident.

*Direction:* Put the blindness in law, not luck. Give Arfst Ipsen's 2016 charter a privacy clause: residents' homes and the nature reserve are sealed from state query without a court order, only residents may query their own household, and any resident may invoke "unwitnessed minutes" (the Hark request becomes a legal right rather than a password). This explains why Moiken finds what the director cannot. It also sharpens Nommensen as a fake ally: he gives her the key because she is his legal proxy. Separately, make the glow detectable only as the Waag ripens, from about 2045, which the Shining already implies.

**5. Replace the faked stillbirth and the 18 hidden years with a plausible cover.** The writer flagged this, and it does not hold. A German stillbirth over 500 g needs a medical certificate and a doctor's examination before burial. The reserve is closed only from April to July. The island has 41 people and a state station 1.5 km away.
*Direction:* Ebba is born at the Odense clinic as a legal Danish copy under a Danish name, and raised by Knutt on a Danish Wadden island (Mandø or Rømø) or in Jutland, where nobody knows Inken's face. She is hidden at Süderwarf only for the 2047 breeding-season closure. Keep the "light coffin" by repurposing it: the mother staged a public miscarriage story, the coffin holds the clinic's letter, and Kerrin recognises that letterhead from her own birth papers. That also gives Kerrin a real plot function. Keep the telescope photographs by having Knutt bring Ebba over in winter, or as the mother's photographs.

**6. Remove the remaining information loops that have no origin.** The writer fixed one (07 §3.4), but others remain. Ebba says "I'm your sister" "because the mother told her that is what she said". Ebba points Levin south "as she was told". "Knutt" is a nickname given independently at two ages, which is a coincidence supplying an identity clue, against the report's "no coincidence supplies an answer". Give each one an origin inside its own time. Ebba says "sister" because that is what she has felt watching through the telescope. She sends Levin south because Süderwarf is the only unsensored place and, by then, her own childhood home. Islanders call the warden Knutt because he introduces himself that way, keeping his own name under the false papers.

**7. Fix Nommensen's plan, which contradicts his own evidence.** He believes in the fixed timeline and has known since 2029 that nobody ever arrives from after September 2047. So cutting out the core "to keep the program operational" cannot produce a single future crossing, and he knows it. Make his aim explicitly legal and political: prevent the Schriever from certifying the last crossing so the record stays sealed and the Kept stay hidden. Then decide whether the excavator is needed at all. As written, the core crumbles on its own, so his main action and the player's counter-action (Inken leading the excavator onto soft ground) change nothing.

### B. Agency, the climax and convergence

**8. The last crossing must respect refusal (report checklist: "refusal and inaction are respected outcomes").** "The game waits indefinitely" is not refusal, it is the Spec Ops problem the report cites. The fixed history only requires that Ebba crosses at 23:40. It does not require that Moiken walks her in.
*Direction:* Make the mother's memory less specific ("a hand in the fog; I never saw whose"). Then the player may walk Ebba in, let Levin take her, or let go and stand at the posts. Each choice changes meaning, not events: Ebba's last line, what the mother says in the kitchen, and whether Moiken calls her "Ebba". Do the same for the Ch 15 rail-cart attempt. At the moment it is a forced failure the mother already knows about. Make it a choice the player can decline, with a "yes, but" either way.

**9. Give the free hour a real choice, and examine the data release.** As written, the "world stake" is the option the writer rejected in Round 7 (W3, "exposure only ... familiar and passive"), delivered without cost. The release also publishes 19 years of every islander's home, including their weights in every room, which is a privacy disaster for 41 people who never consented. In a story whose question is "who does a life belong to", that cannot be a free victory.
*Direction:* The player chooses whether to carry the hydrophone. Certifying frees the Kept and exposes everyone. Not certifying keeps the island private, and the Kept are then freed more slowly through Swantje's testimony. This puts the theme into an action, gives the climax a cost the protagonist chooses, and makes the kitchen choice a smaller echo of the larger one rather than the only real decision in the game.

**10. Earn Act IV instead of compressing it.** Keike's turn after 19 years ("Wear hers") has no scene that earns it: give her one in Act III, for example the Shining showing her the commanders' graves her sermon protected, or a meeting with Ebba. Levin's turn happens offscreen between "he does not answer" and opening the door: show one decisive beat. The standoff "resolves because everyone wants the same thing", which is a convenience, so give it a cost. Nommensen's arrest is only in the fates table: put it on screen. Swantje's suspension and return on Kerrin's ferry in a storm surge needs a cause. Otherwise Act IV reads as the season-8 compression the brief warns against.

### C. Originality, structure and clarity

**11. Reduce the *Dark* overlap. The writer's log understates it.** Specific matches: Swantje is a police officer whose younger brother vanished one cycle earlier and returns unaged (Ulrich and Mads). Henning is a body found on the edge of town in period clothes after a cycle (Mads). Knutt is a traveller from the future living under an alias as a mentor (the Stranger). The cycle is explained by "the moon sits where it sat" (Dark's sun-moon alignment). A woman is her own mother (Charlotte and Elisabeth). The title is a single German word.
*Direction:* Change at least three of these. Make Swantje Henning's daughter (her father vanished in 2009 and came back younger than her) rather than Tade's sister, and make Tade's relative someone else. Have Henning found alive and talking, not as a body in period clothes. Make the Metonic explanation the islanders' folk belief, which the tapes later complicate, rather than the authoritative reason. Fix the loop as in revision 1. Then rerun the "would a reader of both see two different stories" test and record the result.

**12. Stay within the identity-reveal budget.** The report advises at most one hidden identity reveal. The story has three "same person at two ages" structures (the mother and Ebba, Knutt and Levin, Tamme in 1966 and 1985), and on top of them "Mama is a copy". The trope table flags "two characters are one person at different ages".
*Direction:* Tell the player that Knutt is Levin by the end of Act II, for example with a Levin interlude in which the watch engraving is made, so the 00:14 frame and the raid play as Hitchcock suspense (the player dreads that Moiken's triumph will kill him). The midpoint stays the one true reveal.

**13. Add shocks, and introduce the world stake earlier.** The brief asks for many deaths, betrayals and reversals; this has one significant death (Knutt) plus the Nursery. Add at least one fair betrayal or reversal, planted in a scene that plays as a triumph. Candidates: Marret sold tapes to the station years ago, or Kerrin reports Moiken because of her family's copy-law politics. Introduce the Silence in Act I (the horizon chart glimpsed in the station lobby in Ch 6 or earlier, and the staff's gallows humour), not in Ch 11 at about 60 % through. Thin Act III out by moving the Schuldbuch to the start of Act IV or splitting the Shining from the cart.

**14. Make sure depositions cannot turn into text-reading spam.** Questioning an AI by free question is L.A. Noire's failure and a reading wall waiting to happen. Specify depositions as constrained queries: choose a version, a time, a place and a sensor. Answers come back as reconstructions, sounds and weights, with at most one short sentence. Have the player play Inken's 2028 thread through reconstruction verbs (pairing, scrubbing, weighing), not watch it.

**15. Clarify names for the player.** "Mama" is legally Inken Brarens, the original is also Inken, the young copy is Ebba, and the grave says Ebba. Fix an on-screen convention early (the board calls the mother "Mama" until she is named "Ebba" in the kitchen) and run the cold-playtester relationship test the self-check left open.

**16. Rerun the options rounds that were not real choices.** Round 0 is genuine. From Round 3 onward almost every round is constrained by the rule that "all keep the ending image possible", so the alternatives are strawmen or variants of one idea:
- H3 is a flip of H1, and H2 is H1 with a different motive.
- E1 and E2 are set up to lose.
- K2 and K3 are "spectacle" and "no event".
- The three Round 10 premises describe the same story.
- In Round 2 all three settings are small coastal communities in 2047.

Run at least Rounds 3, 7 and 9 again with options that do not share the chosen skeleton, and grade them honestly, even if the pick does not change.

## Where I would pick differently in 01-options.md

- **Round 3 (hidden history): H1, but revised.** Use a stored Inken sample, a surrogate or a declared loop, and a Danish upbringing (revisions 1 and 5). H2 and H3 are not real competitors, so H1 wins by default rather than on merit.
- **Round 4 (ending events) and Round 9 (final decision): a hybrid, not a pure E3 + D1.** Keep E3's "fixed facts, free meaning, free hour", but move the player's main decision into the free hour (whether to certify and release, revision 9) and allow refusal at the walk (revision 8). Keep D1 as the small closing echo. On its own, D1 leaves the only real choice in the game to a question of whether to keep watching a private scene.
- **Round 7 (world stake): W1, without the W2 post-credits hum as written.** As delivered, the stake resolves as W3 (exposure), which the writer rightly called passive. Either make the release a costly choice (above) or make W2 a promised vista inside the game: Marret's tapes contain a second, faint ring tone from a harbour hydrophone that the Schriever logged. Otherwise the post-credits hum is a sequel hook, which the checklist forbids.
- **Round 11 (opening): O1, but let the player not press [Close]** (for example by walking away from the feed). The game starts on a forced input, which is the same agency problem in miniature. O2 and O3 are well chosen.
- All other picks (S1, Q1, T1, C1, A1, X1, F1, P1, M1, K1, the names) I would keep. They are right for the brief.
