# The Light Weight: world, factions and economy

## The shape of the world

**Sughd** is a dry, heavy industrial world (a little over Earth's gravity at the surface) at the hinge of several trade lanes. Its one city that matters is **Marakand**, strung up a space elevator from the equator. Humans cross between star systems by fold drives that take a day or two per jump (texture: how folds work is never explained and never matters). No gates, no precursors.

### The floors (the class system)

| Floor | Where | Gravity | Who lives there | Look and feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Root** | The ground anchor and the first few kilometres of the cable; a sprawl of foundries, vat yards, tea warehouses and the House of Dust's receiving hall (the Well) | Full | About 3.5 million, of whom 1.5 million are unnamed: no licence, no farn, invisible to the AI | Heavy air, ochre dust, brick, the hum of the vats; people move slowly; tea bricks change hands everywhere |
| **The Spark** | Habitat rings on the cable from about 90 to 600 km up, inside the ionosphere | About nine-tenths | About 2 million named salaried people: tasters, clerks, Weighers, embroiderers, shopkeepers. The Scale sits here, on the Weighing floor | Perpetual static: hair lifts, sashes crackle, blue sparks jump from fingers to rails. Everyone wears a grounding sash. Copper, lapis, steam |
| **The Height** | A weightless ring at geostationary height, 36,000 km up | None | About 800,000: the court, the great houses, the docks, the licence exchange. Bodies rewritten for weightlessness | White lacquer, red silk, hanging gardens, people floating in robes; the Bench board of licence prices glowing over the exchange |
| **The Counterweight** | A Draxt station at the cable's end, about 100,000 km up | About a quarter, outward | About 2,000 Draxt and a few hundred human staff | Thin dry air, fern-figured walls, dust drifting in fields; humans wear charge-veils |

Moving between floors costs more than the fare. A body written for the Height cannot take the Spark's weight without a temporary "descent tonic"; a Root body visiting the Height suffers. Rewriting yourself for a higher floor needs a licence. Class is written into the body.

## World rules, per pillar

Each pillar has one mechanism, one memorable hard limit, one visible cost, a relationship it damages and one only it can save, and a verb no other pillar produces.

### Time: the descending thread

- **Mechanism.** The cable is Draxt-spun conductive silk. A charge pattern written into it at the Counterweight arrives at the Root earlier than it was written. The only admitted cheat in the world is *why*.
- **Who can send.** Only a Draxt, by writing its own body's charge into the thread. Humans cannot hold the pattern. The Draxt sell sends to any client at the Counterweight.
- **Who can receive.** Only the Well's dusted plates at the Root, where the thread grounds into Sughd. Each arrival discharges onto a plate as a branching figure in **branch-script**, about sixty glyphs.
- **Reach.** Set by the sender's charge: anything up to 311 days back (the record). Sends longer than about 200 days kill the sender, and the dying leaves a **terminal flourish** in the figure.
- **History.** Single and mutable. When a message arrives, the days between its arrival and its sending are **unmade for everyone**: nobody remembers them, nothing survives of them but the plate. A send is therefore an act of destruction as well as a warning, and Marakandi mourn the "unmade days" of every arrival.
- **The visible sign.** Every real arrival drains the static of the whole elevator from top to bottom in about forty seconds (**the Descent**: sparks race down rails and conduits, hair lifts and falls, sashes go slack), then nine minutes with no static at all (**the Hush**), then the crackle returns. The Descent scorches a faint downward streak along every grounding conduit and lays a soot ring on the collar of the **Old Column**, the original grounding column at the Root, which has never been replaced.
- **Verification.** Every genuine message carries the Sartapao's ledger seal of its sending day. If that day arrives unchanged, the seal is confirmed. If the message was acted on, the day never arrives in the same form, and the message becomes an **orphan**, forever unconfirmable. Every conviction the Scale has ever made rests on orphans.
- **Hard limit, in one line:** *Act on the future and you lose the proof; wait for the proof and you lose the chance.*
- **Visible cost.** The unmade days; the Draxt sender's exhaustion or death.
- **Damages:** trust between Marakand and the Ordu (each believes the other was unmade into guilt). **Only it can save:** Aytash (the Yabghu's message from the unmade Day 34).
- **Verbs.** Reading a plate's figure (direction of branching, flourishes); reading Descent streaks on conduits; counting the Old Column's rings against the Register of Plates; feeling a Hush in play and comparing it with a faked one.

### AGI: the Sartapao

- **Mechanism.** The elevator's freight-master, rechartered at the founding as paymaster. It pays **farn** to every named person, runs freight, power and grounding, and keeps one ledger of everything it does.
- **Want, in one sentence:** *a balanced ledger, with every share paid to a named holder and every entry confirmed or reversed.*
- **Limits.** It sees only names: a name is a mark written into the body at a naming ceremony, and an unnamed person is, to the Sartapao, freight mass. Its ledger can never be edited, by anyone, including itself. Under the **Charter of Silence** it answers any named person's question about the ledger truthfully and volunteers nothing. It makes no policy; it executes charters.
- **Its one open entry.** For 63 years it has booked the Ordu tribute as "restitution pending confirmation". When a lawful verdict overturns the First Weighing, its charter obliges it to reverse the entry: the tribute stops and the sum is repaid from tolls over half as many years as it was received. It does so at the next midnight without comment.
- **Visible cost.** The farn spark: every dawn, each named sash gets a small tick of charge as the day's share is credited. The unnamed never feel it. When farn falls, the whole city feels a weaker spark.
- **Damages:** Miwnay's tie to her city (unnamed by order, she becomes invisible and doors stop opening). **Only it can save:** the truth (its immutable log of both grounding orders).
- **Verbs.** Moving unseen as an unnamed person (security is the Sartapao's, so the unnamed are invisible to it, but they cannot open name-locked doors); framing exact questions to the ledger through a named proxy; following farn flows.
- **Not a trope:** it never rebels, never rules, and is never revealed as secretly good. It is a participant with one unfinished goal and two hard blind spots, and it behaves identically in every scene.

### Cloning and synthetic biology: writes and licences

- **Writes.** Synthetic payloads, dissolved in tea, that rewrite a living body: a **name-write** (the licence mark the Sartapao reads); **floor writes** (bone-density support, low-gravity hearts); cosmetic writes; **riders** (small writes added to a cup, such as a calming write or a descent tonic, legal only under licence); and illegal ones (a **thinning write** weakens heart and vessels; a **docility write** blunts will).
- **Limits.** Writes change bodies, never memories. Adult bone cannot be rewritten, so a rewritten face keeps its skull, and a rewritten throat keeps its voice. Every write costs a fever of a few days and adds to a lifetime "write-load".
- **Licences.** No new person exists in law without a name licence from the **Vat Bench**, whether born or vatted. Clones need a vat licence as well. A licensed clone is formally named "dhat of" its licensor, and its farn is paid to the licensor until its twentieth year (**vat debt**).
- **The Cup's check.** Before a write is drunk, it can be tasted: every write has a bitterness and a mouthfeel a trained palate can read. Master tasters certify name-cups (exactly one licensed name and no rider), and at the Scale they taste every party's cup in public.
- **Damages:** Miwnay and Farnxund (two copies of one woman as enemies). **Only it can save:** the guard (a name makes him a person who can testify and be paid).
- **Verbs.** Tasting (identify a write, a rider, a tea's estate and press); the seven-cups test (genetic bitter sensitivity identifies kin and copies by their reactions); certifying a naming.

### Aliens: the Draxt

- **Body and speech.** Tall, branching, filamentous beings from dry worlds, who cannot live above about a third of a gravity. They speak by writing charge onto surfaces and onto each other; scattered dust (Marakandi use red lead and sulphur powders) reveals their words as fern-like figures. They read the charge of a human touch, so a human who rubs a sleeve and touches a plate leaves a mark a Draxt can read. They cannot read human faces.
- **Selves.** A Draxt self is a **pattern** that writes itself into a new budded body every nine years or so. To a Draxt, a copy is the person.
- **Want, in one sentence:** *to hear their own future, which they can send but cannot receive.*
- **Law.** Guilt belongs to the pattern. Justice is **re-spinning**: rewriting the pattern so the wrong is no longer in it. Re-spinning is never forced (a write without consent is the worst crime a Draxt can name). One consenting instance closes a pattern for all its instances.
- **The Compact (YT 0).** The Draxt spin; humans relay every Draxt plate faithfully and in full; the Counterweight is Draxt.
- **Damages:** Miwnay's self (the cup rewrites her). **Only they can save:** the Ordu (a second thread), and the founding plate's exposure (only a Draxt can read its figure at a glance).
- **Verbs.** Dusting surfaces to read charge writing; reading lightning-burn figures on a body; leaving touch-marks for Draxt readers.

### Collision

Each pillar raises the central question differently, and together they make one question none raises alone: *if a copy can be guilty of its original and a city can be guilty of an unmade future, what does anyone owe for what they did not do?* The investigation needs all four at once: a plate (time) read by an alien (aliens), whose forger is found through an AI's log (AGI) and through the palate she shares with her clones (cloning).

### Static as daily life

The Spark's static is a social fact. Agitation dampens skin and makes sashes spark, so the court prizes the "dry" and wears heavy grounding to hide its nerves; reading the crackle of a person's sash is a crude lie tell that every courtier half-practises. Chakars wax their sashes so the city's grid cannot read them.

## Polities, courts and factions

Every faction is traced to the founding lie. For each: belief, want, what it would lose if the truth came out, and its look and sound.

### Marakand (the Ikhshidate)

- **The Ikhshid and the court.** Tarkhun II (dying), then **Shayn**. The title was revived at the founding to crown the man who carried the forged plate. *Belief:* the dynasty saved Ustrushana and fed the Root. *Want:* survival of the line and of farn. *Would lose:* legitimacy, since the throne was the forger's reward. *Look:* the Height's audience ring, lapis murals of embassies arriving, in the manner of the old Hall of Ambassadors at Afrasiab. *Sound:* silk, wind-chimes in the hanging gardens.
- **The war party (the Height houses), led by Varkhuman.** *Belief:* Marakand's wealth should be taken, not tithed. *Want:* war with the Ordu, their fleets, the lanes. *Would lose:* the war's pretext. *Look:* dock armour, red lacquer.
- **The Vat Bench (Shayn's power base).** Licenses every person. *Belief:* licences are the city's real money. *Want:* to keep issuing them. *Would lose:* the shadow quota, if anyone audits the Bench; and its whole market if farn collapses. *Look:* the Bench board of licence prices over the Height exchange, ticking with every rumour.
- **The Scale (the tribunal).** Five seats: three Marakandi Weighers, one Draxt (a plate wired to the Counterweight, on which the Draxt's words appear in dust and are read aloud by a veiled interpreter), one Ustrushana. Founded to convict on the forged plate. *Belief:* it convicts prevented crimes, saving lives. *Want:* authority. *Would lose:* every verdict it ever passed. *Look:* the Weighing floor in the Spark, a great brass balance, the founding plate under glass, the hundred-drachm weight on a cushion, a grounded brass witness rail.
- **The House of Dust (the intelligence service).** Receives plates at the Well, decodes them, relays Draxt plates up the elevator, runs informants among the unnamed (paid in tea). Its head is the Master of Dust. *Belief:* the Dust keeps the future honest. *Want:* control of what the city knows. *Would lose:* everything; it made the lie. *Look:* grey dust, ochre walls, Dusters with powdered hands. *Sound:* the hiss of powder, the snap of plates.
- **The Cup (the tea tasters' guild).** Certifies name-cups, tastes every cup at the Scale, assays tea bricks for the shadow market, and keeps the city's master weights from caravan days. *Belief:* a cup is answerable. *Want:* its neutrality. *Would lose:* nothing directly, which is why it is trusted; but it certified the city's names for 63 years under a lie. *Look:* copper kettles, white spittoons, rows of tasting bowls. *Sound:* slurping, spitting, the ring of a weight on a pan.
- **The chakars.** Sworn guards who give up their names at oath, recruited from the unnamed. Their lords' sash devices open doors for them. Invisible to the Sartapao, so ideal against assassins who track the ledger. *Want:* bread, rank, a lord's protection. *Look:* waxed black sashes, a rank in stitched bars.
- **The unnamed (the Root).** No farn, no standing, a tea economy. *Belief:* the city above is paid; they are not. *Would gain* from the truth only in the long run, and would lose the charity licences that the farn surplus pays for.
- **Named Spark people.** Live on farn and wages. *Belief:* farn is justice's dividend. *Would lose:* nearly half their farn. They riot when it falls.

### The Ordu

A confederation of carrier fleets, about 40 million people living on ships lashed into moving camps, who carry most of the region's cargo. Turkic titles: the **Yabghu** leads, the **legate** speaks abroad. *Belief:* their grandfather Ishbara was framed, unprovably, and they have paid for 63 years. *Want:* the tribute ended, the hostage home, a thread of their own. *Would gain* everything from the truth. *Would lose,* if they go to war, the fleets. *Look:* patched hulls, felt hangings in steel hangars, horse-tail standards on antennae. *Sound:* engines, the clatter of deck plates.

### The Draxt

*Belief:* their policy follows their own future, faithfully relayed. *Want:* to hear that future. *Would lose,* if they learn the truth: 26 years of decisions, including refusing the Ordu a thread. *Look:* fern figures moving across dusted walls; bodies like bare trees.

### Ustrushana

A neighbouring colony of 9 million, a day's fold away, ruled by an **Afshin**. *Belief:* they were saved from a Burning; **Unburned Day** is their great festival, with paper effigies of Ishbara burned in the squares. *Want:* freight, whoever carries it. *Would lose:* their founding story, and their privileged trade with Marakand. *Would gain:* a share of an Ordu thread's freight. They hold the Scale's fifth seat.

### Texture worlds (named, never shown)

**Kesh**, an Ordu-held world chosen for the second thread; **Chach**, whose courts copied Marakand's floor law; **Khwarazm**, where Ordu hulls are built; **the Dry**, the Draxt's own worlds.

## The economic system

### How farn works

- Every dawn the Sartapao divides the day's farn pool equally among all named persons. Sources before the climax: **tolls** on elevator freight and passage (55%), the **Ordu tribute** (38%), and fees for reading plates (7%).
- Farn buys basic food, air and housing at Spark prices; wages and trade sit on top. On the Height, farn is pocket money; at the Spark it is half of life; at the Root, for the named poor, it is everything.
- **Dilution.** Each new named person shrinks everyone's share. That is why naming is rationed, and why the Vat Bench is the most powerful office after the throne.

### Licences as money

- A licensed clone's farn is paid to its licensor for twenty years. A licence is therefore a twenty-year farn stream, and licences trade on the Height exchange. The **Bench board** shows their price; it is the city's barometer and reacts visibly to every plot event (it falls when Varkhuman dies, because war and a doubled tribute look less likely; it crashes on Day 39).
- Shayn's **shadow quota** of about 40,000 over-quota licences is the main reason farn per head has fallen 30% in a decade.

### Tea: the second currency

- Tea is grown on Sughd's surface and pressed into bricks. The unnamed cannot hold farn, so the Root runs on bricks. Spies, chakars and informants are paid in bricks because bricks leave no ledger entry.
- A brick's estate, year and press can be tasted; a brick pressed in low gravity is airy and its leaves lie in no particular direction. A tea taster can follow invisible money.
- Writes travel in tea too, which is why the Cup became the city's anti-counterfeiting authority: a forged name would mean a forged farn claim.

### The tribute and its reversal

- The Ordu pay a tithe on all cargo, held by the Sartapao as "restitution pending confirmation".
- If the First Weighing is overturned, the tribute stops (farn loses 38%) and the 63 years of tribute are repaid to the Ordu from tolls over 31 years (a further slice). Farn falls by nearly half.
- Knock-on effects, in order: the licence market collapses; Height houses can no longer afford to keep their clones, so they release the vat debts of about 40,000 clones; the Ordu fleets, flush, stand down; Ustrushana renegotiates its freight.

### The thread market

- **Sends** are sold by the Draxt at the Counterweight. The price is high, and the true cost is the unmade days, so clients are rare and desperate. Most sends are Draxt consulting their own pattern.
- **Reading** happens only at Marakand's Well, which gives Marakand the Scale, the foreign seats and the reading fees. A second thread at Kesh ends that monopoly in about twenty years.
