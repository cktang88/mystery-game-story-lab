# The Light Weight: world, factions and economy (revision 2)

## The region in play

Humans cross between star systems by fold drives, about a day per jump. No signal is faster than a ship, so news, orders and petitions between worlds travel by courier, and that lag is a plot instrument. There are no gates and no precursors.

| Place | What it is | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| **Marqand**, on the planet **Sogh** | The elevator city whose alien-spun cable is the only receiver of messages from the future; seat of the Ikhshid and the Scale; 6.5 million people | Acts 1 to 4 |
| **The Grove** | The Draxt court, on a low-gravity moon of Sogh's outer gas giant, six hours by ship from the Counterweight | Acts 3 (Takhsich) and 4 (Miwnay) |
| **The Ordu fleet** | A moving camp of thousands of lashed-together carriers at the edge of the Sogh system: the court of the Yabghu and the captains' council | Acts 3 and 4 (Aytash) |
| **Usrushan** | A colony world of 9 million a day's fold away, ruled by an Afshin; it celebrates having been "saved" | Act 3 (Aytash) |
| **Chash** | The region's second elevator city (a plain human-built elevator, with no time property). Marqand's war ally in the Toll War; it copied the farn system and pays it partly from a 20% share of the Ordu tribute; ruled by a Tudun | Act 4 (Aytash); its riots are seen on a feed |
| **Pench** | A banking world whose houses hold most of the Ordu fleets' debt and want them solvent | Its factor is on screen in the fleet (Acts 3 and 4) |
| **Kish** | An Ordu-held world, chosen as the site of a second thread | Act 4 (Aytash), and the coda |
| **The Dry** | The Draxt home worlds | Never shown |

**Play share off the elevator** (planned): Aytash's thread from Day 26 (fleet court, Usrushan, Chash, Kish), Takhsich's at the Grove, and Miwnay's Grove sequence come to about 35 to 40% of play time.

### Marqand's floors (the class system)

| Floor | Where | Gravity | Who | Look |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Root** | Ground anchor; foundries, vat-crèches, tea warehouses, the House of Dust's receiving hall (the Well), the Old Column | Full | About 4 million: 1.5 million unnamed, 1.5 million crèche clones, 1 million named poor | Ochre dust, brick, the hum of the vats, tea bricks changing hands |
| **The Spark** | Habitat rings 90 to 600 km up, inside the ionosphere | About nine-tenths | About 1.8 million named salaried people; the Scale; the Cup | Perpetual static, blue sparks, grounding sashes, copper and lapis |
| **The Height** | Weightless ring at geostationary height | None | About 0.7 million: court, great houses, docks, the licence exchange and its **Bench board** | White lacquer, red silk, people floating in robes |

Bodies are written for floors. Height bodies need a descent tonic to go down; Root bodies suffer at the Height. Class is written into the body.

## World rules, per pillar

Each pillar has one mechanism, one hard limit, one visible cost, a relationship it damages and one only it can save, and verbs no other pillar produces.

### Time: the thread

- **Mechanism.** Charge written into the Draxt-spun cable at the Counterweight arrives at the Root earlier than it was written. *Why* is the world's one admitted cheat.
- **Senders and receivers.** Only a Draxt can send, and only the Well can receive. The Draxt sell sends to anyone, including **standing sends**: "if this happens, send this text on that day to that moment". A standing send is a question put to the future. If a Hush comes at the named moment, the condition came true in a future that the send has now unmade. If no Hush comes, either it did not come true or nobody sent.
- **Reach.** Up to 311 days. Sends of more than about 200 days kill the Draxt sender and leave a **death mark** at the end of the plate's figure.
- **Unmaking.** Every day between arrival and sending is unmade for everyone, and only the plate survives. Humans mourn unmade days; the Draxt hold they were never lived.
- **The Hush.** A real arrival sends a visible wave of sparks down the whole elevator (seen from orbit as a ripple of light down the cable), then nine silent minutes. It leaves downward streaks on conduits and a soot ring on the Old Column's collar. A grounding, ordered or faulted, gives the silence without the wave, the streaks or the ring.
- **Seals.** A genuine plate carries the Sartapao's ledger seal of its sending day. That seal can be confirmed only if that day arrives unchanged, and a plate that was acted on never can be. Forgers need only the seal's format.
- **Hard limit, in one line:** *act on the future and you lose the proof; wait for the proof and you lose the chance.*
- **Visible cost.** The unmade days, and the sender's exhaustion or death.
- **Damages:** trust between Marqand and the Ordu, each of which believes the other was proved guilty by a future that did not happen. **Only it can save:** Aytash, by her father's plate from an unmade Day 34, and the Review, by her own standing send.
- **Verbs.**
  - **Composing a send:** choose a condition, a target moment and sixty glyphs, knowing an enemy may read them first (Aytash, Day 31).
  - **Act or wait:** decide whether to act on a plate (and lose the proof) or hold it (Miwnay on Day 13; Aytash on Day 32).
  - **Reading a Hush:** compare a real one with faked ones.
  - **Reading the record a Hush leaves:** streaks and rings against the Register.

### AGI: the Sartapao

- **Mechanism.** The elevator's freight-master, rechartered at the founding as paymaster. It pays **farn**, runs freight, power and grounding, and keeps one ledger that cannot be edited.
- **Want, in one sentence:** *a balanced ledger, every share paid to a named holder and every entry confirmed or reversed.*
- **Limits (the Charter of Silence).**
  - It sees only names: a name is a mark written into the body at a naming, and the unnamed are freight mass to it.
  - It answers a named person's question only about ledger lines in which that person is named. Lines naming no living person belong to the floor's keeper. The dead's lines pass to their heirs. Lines under the Ikhshid's seal are read only under that seal.
  - Every question is itself a ledger line, and the Master of Dust may audit all questions.
  - It volunteers nothing and makes no policy.
- **Why the founding hid in plain sight.** The YT 41 grounding line names only "Council seal of Tishdhat": the old logs recorded neither the executor nor any wave. Only an Ikhshid can read it, and to an Ikhshid it looks like the founder's safety order. The night −13 line names only the officer of the watch, Pukar, so only he, then his heir, could ask; and asking showed the Dust who was asking.
- **Visible cost.** The **farn spark**, a tick of charge through each named sash at dawn as the day's share is credited. The unnamed never feel it, and when farn falls the whole city feels a weaker one.
- **Damages:** Miwnay's tie to her city (unnamed by order, she is invisible to it). **Only it can save:** Pukar's account, carried in his ledger lines to his mother.
- **Verbs.** Moving unnamed (invisible to the city's security, but unable to open name-locked doors); finding the one person entitled to ask a question, and framing it; inheriting a dead person's lines.
- **Not a trope.** It never rebels, rules or reveals a heart, and it behaves identically in every scene.

### Cloning and synthetic biology: writes and licences

- **Writes** are synthetic payloads dissolved in tea that rewrite a living body: name marks, floor adaptations, faces, legal additions to a cup (a descent tonic) and illegal ones (thinning, docility). Every write has a taste a master palate can read before it is drunk.
- **Limits.** Writes never change memories. Adult bone cannot be rewritten, so a skull and a voice survive a new face. Each write costs a fever.
- **Licences.** No new person exists in law without a name licence from the Vat Bench, born or vatted. A licensed clone is formally named "dhat of" its licensor. Its farn goes to the licensor until 16, to pay for its raising. From 16 to 36 it pays a **labour tithe** of 30% of its wages into the farn pool. Licences trade as bonds on that future tithe.
- **Damages:** Miwnay and Farnxund, two copies of one woman as enemies. **Only it can save:** the guard, whose name lets him testify.
- **Verbs.** Tasting cups, flasks and residues; the seven-cups test (reactions to the bitter seventh cup reveal shared genes); certifying a naming.

### Aliens: the Draxt

- **Body and speech.** Tall, branching, filamentous beings from dry worlds, who cannot live above about a third of a gravity. They write charge onto surfaces and each other, and dust reveals their words as fern-like figures. They read the charge of a human touch, but they cannot read human faces.
- **Selves.** A Draxt self is a **pattern** that rewrites itself into a new budded body every nine years or so. To a Draxt, a copy is the person.
- **Want, in one sentence:** *to hear their own future, which they can send but cannot receive.*
- **Law.** A wrong belongs to the pattern. Justice is **re-spinning**, rewriting the pattern so the wrong is no longer in it, and it is never forced: a write without consent is the gravest Draxt crime. One consenting instance closes a wrong for its whole pattern. The player sees a re-spinning, with its consent, on Day 6 through Takhsich.
- **The court at the Grove and its factions.**
  - **The Keepers** voted the YT 79 refusal of the Ordu thread on the strength of their relayed "future". If that future was false, every Keeper who voted carries a wrong. They prefer the Marqand relay kept, audited and quietly corrected.
  - **The Spinners** have suspected the Dust since YT 79, because the relayed copy lacked a death mark. They run the pilot Kanak and, through him, the mole Takhsich. They want the relay moved to a thread spun for the Ordu, and some want a long send to correct the past.
  - **Wakhsh**, the envoy to Marqand, is a Keeper whose own pattern sent the suppressed YT 78 plate.
- **Damages:** the Marqand–Draxt partnership. **Only they can save:** the founding plate's truth (only a Draxt can read its gradient) and the Ordu's future (a second thread).
- **Verbs.** Dusting to read charge; reading burn figures on a body; leaving touch-marks; reading a court whose speech is visible on its walls.

### Collision

The investigation needs all four pillars at once: a plate (time) read by an alien (aliens), whose forger is traced through an AI ledger that only certain people may ask (AGI), and through the palate she shares with her clones (cloning). The question none of them raises alone: *if a copy can be guilty of its original, and a people guilty of an unmade future, what does anyone owe for what they did not do, and who has the standing to say so?*

### Static in daily life

Agitation dampens skin and makes sashes spark, so the court prizes the "dry" and grounds itself heavily. Reading a person's crackle is a crude lie tell, and Aytash is expert at it. Witnesses at the Scale must wear the Scale's standard grounding sash, which stops them hiding behind their own. Chakars wax their sashes so the grid cannot read them.

## Polities, courts and factions

Every faction is traced to the founding lie. For each: what it believes, what it wants, what it would lose if the truth came out, and its look.

### Marqand

- **The Ikhshid and court** (Tishdhat II, then **Shayn**).
  - *Belief:* the dynasty saved Usrushan and fed the Root.
  - *Want:* survival of the line and of farn.
  - *Would lose:* legitimacy, since the founder's seal is on the YT 41 grounding.
  - *Look:* Height audience rings, lapis murals of embassies in the Afrasiab manner.
- **The war party** (Ashtvan, Height houses, dock captains).
  - *Belief:* Marqand should take the lanes.
  - *Want:* the Ordu fleets, as work for the coming-of-age clones.
  - *Would lose:* the war's pretext.
- **The Vat Bench** (Shayn's base).
  - *Belief:* licences are the city's real money.
  - *Want:* the shadow quota hidden and the bubble rescued.
  - *Would lose:* everything, if farn falls.
  - *Look:* the Bench board, ticking with every rumour.
- **The Scale**, the war-crimes tribunal: three Marqandi Weighers, the Draxt seat, Usrushan and Chash. It was founded to convict on the forged plate.
  - *Belief:* it convicts prevented crimes and saves lives.
  - *Would lose:* every verdict it has passed.
  - *Look:* a great brass balance, the founding plate under glass, the 100-drachm weight on its cushion, the grounded witness rail, and the Draxt seat (a plate whose dust rises into figures, read aloud by a veiled interpreter).
- **The House of Dust**, the intelligence service. It receives and decodes plates, relays Draxt plates, audits ledger questions, and pays informants in tea.
  - *Belief:* the Dust keeps the future honest.
  - *Would lose:* everything; it made the lie.
  - *Look:* grey powder and ochre walls.
- **The Cup**, the tea tasters' guild. It certifies name-cups, tastes the cups at the Scale, assays tea-brick money, keeps the city's master weights and grants a few charity licences a year.
  - *Belief:* a cup is answerable.
  - *Would lose:* its neutrality, for having certified a city's names under a lie.
  - *Look:* copper kettles, white spittoons.
- **The chakars.** Sworn guards who give up their names at oath; their lord's sash device opens doors for them.
- **The unnamed.** No farn and no standing; they live on tea money.
- **The named of the Spark.** They live on farn and wages and riot when it falls.

### The Ordu

A confederation of carrier fleets, about 40 million people living on ships lashed into moving camps.

- *Belief:* their ancestor Sarbars was framed, and they have paid for 63 years.
- *Want:* the tribute ended, the hostage home, a thread of their own.
- **Their court** is the Yabghu (Tolun) and the **captains' council**, which decides war by vote of ship-captains. **Prince Temirkan**, Tolun's cousin, leads the war party and covets the succession. The Pench factor sits in as creditor.
- *Look:* felt hangings in steel hangars, horse-tail standards on antennae, deck plates ringing underfoot.

### The Draxt

See the pillar above. *Would lose,* if the truth came out: 26 years of policy, and the Keepers' standing.

### Usrushan

- *Belief:* they were saved from a Burning; on **Unburned Day** paper effigies of a horned Sarbars burn in the squares.
- *Want:* freight.
- *Would lose:* their founding story and their privileged trade with Marqand.
- *Would gain:* a share of the Ordu thread's freight.
- Holds a Scale seat.

### Chash

- The second elevator city, with a farn system copied from Marqand's and paid 30% from its 20% share of the Ordu tribute.
- *Belief:* its war debts were justly repaid.
- *Want:* its farn.
- *Would lose:* 30% of farn if the verdict falls.
- Holds a Scale seat. Its Tudun can be bought with the freight hub of the Kish thread.

### Pench

- Banking houses that hold the Ordu fleets' debt.
- *Want:* a solvent Ordu. A war would ruin their loans; an end to the tribute would make them whole.
- They lend Aytash the price of her standing send and call in Temirkan's ship debts.

## The economic system: the numbers

### Marqand's farn pool, YT 104

| Source | Share |
|---|---|
| Elevator tolls | 52% |
| Ordu tribute (Marqand's 80%) | 35% |
| Labour tithes (legal-quota clones aged 16 to 36) | 10% |
| Draxt relay retainer (paid for relaying their plates) | 3% |

- **Heads.** 5.0 million named persons: 3.5 million original plus 1.5 million shadow-quota clones aged 0 to 15, whose farn is paid to licensors. With the pool flat, farn per head fell to 3.5/5.0 = **70%** of its YT 88 level, a fall of 30%.
- **Why there is a war party.** The first shadow cohort (about 94,000 a year) turns 16 in YT 104 and owes tithes only if it has wages. Marqand has no work for it. The Ordu fleets would provide it.
- **The second forgery's purpose.** Doubling the tribute would lift the pool by 35%, enough to restore farn per head almost to its YT 88 level.

### The reversal (canonical ending)

| Step | Effect on the pool, against today |
|---|---|
| The tribute ends | −35% |
| Restitution to the Ordu, as negotiated at the Review: 10% of the pool a year for 40 years (about half of what was taken; the rest is settled in Kish thread rights) | −10% |
| Pool available for farn | 55% |

Farn per head falls by **45%**. In the "do not weigh" branch it holds for now, but the Ordu stop paying and the board drifts down.

### The crèche crisis (Day 39) and the Wards Decree

- Licensors draw a clone child's farn to pay a crèche for its raising. When farn falls 45%, that draw no longer covers the crèche fee, and the farn houses try to surrender their licences.
- Abandoning a child is illegal, so the Vat Bench must take 1.5 million crèche children as wards.
- Shayn's Wards Decree voids the shadow licences: those children will owe no labour tithe, and their own farn goes to their crèches. The houses lose their bonds, and the Bench board crashes.
- So the children are not abandoned: they are freed from a debt on their future labour.

### Chash

Chash's pool loses its tribute share (30%). Chash riots, and its Tudun trades his Review vote (an abstention) for the Kish freight hub, which over time replaces much of that income.

### Tea: the second currency

The unnamed cannot hold farn, so the Root runs on pressed tea bricks, and spies and informants are paid in them because they leave no ledger line. A brick's estate, year and press can be tasted; a brick pressed in low gravity is airy, its leaves lying in no direction. A tea taster can follow invisible money.

### The thread market

- **Sends** are sold by the Draxt. They are rare: 14 genuine arrivals in 104 years, about one a decade. Their real price is the unmade days.
- **The Draxt relay retainer** is what the Draxt pay Marqand to receive and relay their plates.
- A second thread at Kish ends Marqand's monopoly within about twenty years.

### Dune check

Marqand is the one place that hears the future, which rhymes with a single source of prescience. The design differs on purpose: there is no substance and no addiction; no navigation depends on it; arrivals come about once a decade, because each one unmakes days; its main users are the aliens themselves; and Marqand's wealth is tolls, tithes and reparations.
