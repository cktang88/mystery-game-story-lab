# Writer B: world, rules and factions

## 1. The place: Amole, the carved city

**Amole** (አሞሌ, after the salt bars once used as money on this road) is a highland megacity of about **19 million** people cut *downward* into a basalt plateau above the salt flats. The founders took the old highland technique of carving churches out of living rock and applied it to everything, because shade and stone mass were the only defence against the Burning Years. Streets are **trenches**; buildings are **monoliths** left standing when the rock round them was removed; each quarter is a **pit** open to a square of sky. Windows are cut, never built. Stairs are cut, so they wear; knees wear with them.

- **The Narrow** is the deepest trench: the old salt road from the depression, which runs straight through the city and is still walked by camel caravans carrying salt bars. Everything else was carved off it.
- **The Guest's Spur** is a dead-end side cut off the Narrow, behind a single brass-barred gate, where the Engida's house stands.
- **Bete Tsehafi** ("house of the scribe") is the tallest monolith, at the head of the Narrow: the record tower, with iron-leaf stacks cut in spirals down into the rock, and beneath them the hall where the fig sits.
- **The Lemlem house** is a monolith in the Gora pit: the lineage's home, with cells, a laundry with a kiln, a glass room, the founder's room, and the east cistern stair (a stair to an empty rain cistern, dry for decades).
- **Pits and quarters** the player visits: Gora (lineages), the Tunneler galleries (old machine halls cut sideways into the pit walls), the jar-houses (kiln quarter), the Council Pit with its great warka tree (where the Shimagile sit), the Arho caravanserai at the Narrow's mouth.
- **Shola figs** (sycamore figs) grow in every pit. Children split the fruit and find the dead wasp inside. Everyone knows the rhyme: *"the mother stays inside."*

**Its "1980s Seoul" moment.** Seventy years of development rule by the elders' council (the Shimagile) and a military line (the Fig Guard) have built the city fast and hard. Now: a student-and-copies movement in the trenches, tear gas pooling in the pits because it is heavier than air, neon cut into basalt, televised housing lotteries for newly carved flats, cassette tapes, stenotype machines, film cameras and radiograph plates, and the first direct election in the city's history. The analogy is in the structure (late developmental authoritarianism, forced democratisation, lotteries, the student generation), never in borrowed events or names.

**Naming logic.** Amharic and Ge'ez words in their ordinary meanings; a given name plus the father's given name; no family surnames. Copies take their template's name as their "father's name", so every Lemlem copy is legally **Lemlem Lemlem**, as if she were her own parent; in daily life she uses a personal use-name (Tigist, Almaz). The Tunnelers keep a Korean given name and take an Ethiopian patronymic (Jae-won Tesfaye). The Arho use Afar-style names (Dahir, Hamadu). The election ends that naming rule (see the coda), which the player sees as a wave of new names.

## 2. The four rules, with limits, costs, verbs and the relationship each damages and saves

### Time: the melikt (message) and the ra'iy (vision)

**Rule.**
1. A clay-grown body, or a template whose genome has been grown in clay, can send the last stretch of its own lived experience (sight, sound, touch, its own voice) back in time.
2. **Hard cap:** at most one turn of a sixteen-minute glass of black sand. Every glass in the city is made to this measure.
3. Only into one body of the **same genome**, alive at the target moment, which has **never received** before (it may be the sender's own earlier body, if she has never received).
4. Not before the genome's first jar was sealed.
5. Addressing needs the Tsehafi: the sender lays her hand on an iron plate (every glass room has one), names the body and the moment, and turns the glass.
6. The receiver has **one night of fever** from the target moment and lives the sixteen minutes in fragments; she wakes with a **rust ring** in one iris, for life. A body receives once.
7. **Cost:** the sender loses all lived memory between the target moment and the send. Skills and language stay: "the hands keep what the hands learned."
8. **History is fixed.** Every vision received was sent. A send aimed at a body that never received fails at no cost ("no ring, no road"). The Tsehafi publishes a register of **open receipts**: fevers whose sending has not yet happened. Paying them is a civic duty, and a lineage can be shamed for an unpaid one.

**Visible cost.** Rust rings; glass-widows (senders who have lost decades) who still carve, weave or set bones with hands that remember what they don't.
**Damages:** Almaz and Aster (a vision sends one copy to kill another). **Only it can save:** the Guest's patience and so its offspring (the founder's vision gave it seventy-three years of patience).
**Verbs no other pillar has:** **fever-reading** (walk a degraded reconstruction of a vision, date it, place it, identify the sender by body evidence) and **staging** (live sixteen minutes knowing they will be sent, and choose who pays).

### Cloning: the clay-born

**Rule.**
1. A copy is grown from a living or dead person's cells in a **sealed terracotta jar** for **one hundred days** and born a baby with no memories: a delayed identical twin.
2. Copies share a genome and differ in palm lines, fingerprints, scars, wear and bone history.
3. The hundredth day leaves a **clay line** in the long bones. The first jars (2089-91) left a doubled line.
4. **Law (And Sew):** all bodies of one genome are one legal person: one name, one vote, one housing-lottery ticket, one estate, one guilt. A lineage's steward (Azaj) runs it as a household. A body may leave service but not the person.
5. Killing a body is not murder while another body of the genome lives: it is a lineage matter ("self-harm").
6. Unregistered bodies are **Pagume** (after the thirteenth month that belongs to no season): non-persons, seized and "returned" to their lineage or to clay.
7. Dead copies are **returned to clay**: ground into the clay of new jars.

**Limit:** copies remember nothing; growth takes one hundred days; jars need licensed clay. **Visible cost:** shared vote, shared guilt; a lineage can spend its younger bodies' single receipts.
**Damages:** Tigist and Almaz (the law makes Almaz's killing no crime). **Only it can save:** Tigist and Hiwot (the law is why the lineage could not take Hiwot from "herself" when Tigist left with her).
**Verbs:** **bone-reading** (attribute identity among identical bodies by touch and radiograph: wear, breaks, clay lines, disease) and **passing** (as Sitota, live among 310 identical sisters without being noticed).

### AGI: the Tsehafi

**Rule.**
1. It seals each day at midnight into an **iron leaf** holding that day's version of itself.
2. It hears through listening shafts in public halls, ration halls, glass rooms and lineage houses (lineage houses must have one by law, since lineages send); it counts every gate by the genome in the sweat on its brass bar; it addresses every send.
3. A sealed leaf can be **woken only by a party** to what it records. Under And Sew any body of a genome is a party to every record of that genome.
4. A woken leaf **testifies as that day's Tsehafi**: it replays what it heard and answers within that day's knowledge. It never lies and never volunteers.
5. A woken leaf **rusts** and cannot be resealed. The rust is visible in the stacks and on the public wake-register, which says "woken by Lemlem Lemlem", never which body.
6. It cannot tell bodies of one genome apart, and is bound to treat a genome as one account.

**Want:** that no record be kept from the one it concerns. Every leaf since December 2090 closes "**Outstanding: one.**"
**Damages:** privacy between copies (Tigist can hear Almaz's fever night). **Only it can save:** the hostage, whose birth it alone recorded.
**Verb:** **leaf-waking** (scrub a day's sound, question the day's version of the mind, and accept that every question is a public move the antagonists will see).

### Aliens: the Engida (the Guest)

**Rule.**
1. It perceives beings only by genome; to it a lineage is one person.
2. It is silent and speaks only by **exchange**: an object given and an object returned form one sentence. It cannot take without giving. It can refuse only by returning the very object it was given.
3. Its kind live as fig-wasp foundresses: they carry "pollen" (the jar method and the sending property) to a people, ask for a chamber built to their pattern, shed their pleated wings at its mouth, enter, seed and die; one hundred days later a successor emerges.
4. It cannot open its chamber itself; the people who promised must.

**Want:** to enter the chamber it was promised, seed, and end there.
**Damages:** every Lemlem body (to it, each is the one who promised). **Only it can save:** Hiwot's future (exchanging with it makes her the city's first fluent speaker of exchange).
**Verb:** **exchange** (ask and answer by trading objects; read paired objects as sentences).

### The collision

Visions travel only within a genome, so **time needs cloning**. Senders need the Tsehafi to address a receiver, so **time needs the AGI**. The Tsehafi treats a genome as one account because the founders' law says so, so **the AGI needs cloning law**. Copies and visions exist only because of the alien's pollen, so **cloning and time need the alien**. The alien cannot tell copies apart, so it could be given a copy as the founder herself, so **the alien needed cloning to be deceived**. And the alien's genome-blindness is the evidence the founders cited for And Sew, so **cloning law needs the alien**. Together they raise the question none raises alone: *if the law, the ledger, the alien and the future all say 311 women are one person who has already kept her promise, who in that hall has to keep it?*

**The one admitted cheat**, spent on the premise: why clay-grown bodies can send experience backward is never explained.

**Key resource that creates problems.** The **receipt**: each body can receive once in its life. A lineage with many bodies holds many receipts, so copies are grown partly *for* their receipts (the foresight trade), and every receipt spent on someone's behalf is a piece of someone else's life. The same rule that makes visions precious makes copies valuable as containers, which is what the doctrine was quietly protecting.

## 4. Iceberg lore, tagged

**Promised** questions are answered on the main path, consistently, and each answer opens a further question. **Texture** may stay open forever, and no scene treats it as a promise.

| Question | Tag | Answer and the vista it opens |
|---|---|---|
| Who is the dead woman? | promised | Aster. Opens: then who is "Aster"? |
| Why was a Lemlem body living veiled with the Guest? | promised | She is the pledge of 2090. Opens: what was pledged for? |
| What is in the fig? | promised | The first book. Opens: what did the founders know? |
| What does the Guest want? | promised | To seed and die. Opens: what will its successor be to the city? |
| Who killed her? | promised | Almaz, meaning to kill someone else. Opens: who sent Almaz the vision? |

| Where did the Guest's kind come from? | texture | Never answered. Arho songs disagree. |
| Is there another Guest on another road, and did that city build? | texture | Rumoured by a ferenj trader; never shown. |

| What happened to the student Yonatan? | texture | He died in Guard custody; Dawit owns it in one line in Act IV; the details stay off-screen. |

**Delivery order, per lore fact:** place first (the carved city, the sealed jar, the empty Spur), then object (paired exchange objects, the founder's fan, the black-sand glass, radiographs), then sound (leaf testimony, fever dictation, cassettes, overheard faction lines), and only then short in-world text with a named author and a bias (a jar-house plaque, a Fig Guard creed card, an Arho song transcribed by a Tunneler).

**In-world authors who disagree (Tolkien's "many hands"):** the Lemlem's official founder life ("The Woman Who Saw"); the Fig Guard creed; Arho caravan songs about Amole-Abat; jar-house plaques ("the first jar failed"); Hand pamphlets; Tunneler stenographers' marginal notes; the Tsehafi's own leaves (honest but confined to their day).

**White space** (named, never shown): the city that did not build; clause 9; the Burning Years' lowland cities; the ferenj houses' interiors; Yonatan's cell; the founder's own natural-born family; the first winter in the camp.

**Optional depth, gated by effort:** the Engida's dispersed house (hundreds of exchange pairs scattered across the city on day one: recovering pairs reconstructs Sitota's seventy-three years as sentences); rusted leaves in the stacks for the curious; the glass-widows' hands.

**Lore bible rules.** One keeper (Writer B). Shown-in-game is canon; this file is locked for anything the solution uses and working for texture. Every in-world source is written as fallible, so later corrections can be in-world events.
