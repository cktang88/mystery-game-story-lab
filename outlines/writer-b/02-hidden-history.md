# Writer B: the sealed hidden history (solution document)

**Keeper:** Writer B. **Canon tiers:** everything in this file is **locked** unless marked *working*. Any change needs a change note in `01-options.md` and a re-check of the clue matrix in `05-outline.md`.

**Calendar.** The city counts in the Ethiopian calendar (thirteen months, the last of which, Pagume, has five or six days and belongs to no season). Every date here is Gregorian so it can be checked. The election (Mircha) is Pagume 5, 2155 EC = **11 September 2163**.

**Central question.** When one body makes a promise, which bodies must keep it?
**Ending's answer.** The future can show that a debt of honour will be paid; it cannot say who must pay it. A promise binds whoever chooses to take it up, and the choosing is what makes them a separate person.

## 1. The founding crime in one causal chain

1. **2089. The Burning Years drive a refugee camp onto a basalt plateau** on the old salt road. To survive the heat, the camp starts cutting *down* into the rock, the old highland way, using boring machines brought by a Korean contracting crew who stay (the Tunnelers). The crew's ledger and shorthand machine, **Sokgi-1**, keeps the ration tally.
2. **The Guest walks in with a salt caravan.** A silent, pleated being the colour of rust and clay arrives with an Arho caravan from the salt flats. It perceives people only by genome, speaks only by exchange of objects, and its kind reproduce as fig-wasp foundresses do.
3. **The bargain.** It offers the starving camp its "pollen": the method of growing a body from a person's cells in a sealed terracotta jar, and the strange property of those bodies to send lived experience backward into earlier bodies of the same genome. In return it asks for a **fig**: a terracotta chamber built to its pattern, with a narrow mouth, to be opened to it when it is ready. A deferred exchange needs a surety, so it asks for a pledge **of the one who promises**.
4. **The crime.** The camp's own Compact (written months before) forbids pledging another person. The camp leader, **Lemlem Bekele**, grows the first jar-born body from her own cells and hands the three-day-old baby to the Guest as the pledge. The Guest, which sees genomes, sees the founder herself and accepts.
5. **The lie.** To make the handover lawful, the founders' session declares **And Sew** ("one person"): *a body grown from a person is that person.* The leader has therefore pledged only herself. The session cites the Guest's perception as evidence ("the Guest, who sees what we are, sees one"). Before the vote, the founder says to her council, in the hearing of Sokgi-1: *"She is not me. The Guest cannot tell, and that is the whole of it."*
6. **The vision.** In November 2090 the founder has the first fever in history: sixteen minutes of a hall of her bodies in election cloth, numbers changing on a wall, the Guest walking through them and folding into an opened jar, a small one of her face holding out a glass of black sand, and her own-seeming hand, clay to the wrist, taking it. She wakes with a rust ring in her eye.
7. **The deferral.** She reads the vision as permission: the debt *will* be paid, by "a hand of mine". She has a sixteen-minute glass of black volcanic sand made to match the one she saw, and gives it to the Guest by exchange; it gives her one of its wing-pleats in return, which in its grammar means *I stay*. Its patience comes from that glass.
8. **The entombment.** The founders build the fig, then put the iron leaves that record the pledge (the **first book**) inside its neck, seal it with clay and iron, and add a clause to the Compact: *"On the night of the first Mircha, the jar shall be opened before the people by the hand that promised."* They found the **Fig Guard** to keep everyone else out, on a creed that the Guest's brood would see the future and rule. Fear of the brood now guards the crime.
9. **The delay.** The elders' council, the Shimagile, rules for seventy years and never holds a Mircha. The public reason is development first. Part of the private reason is the clause.
10. **Downstream.** Because And Sew is law, every lineage of copies is one legal person: one name, one vote, one housing-lottery ticket, one guilt. The city carves itself with copies' labour. Copies become a third of the city and hold a few tens of thousands of votes. Killing a copy while another body of the genome lives is not murder. Unregistered bodies are Pagume, non-persons. Every present faction is a consequence of this chain (see section 6).

## 2. What each non-human mind is and wants

**The Engida (the Guest).** A foundress of a kind that walks trade routes looking for a people who will accept its pollen and build it a fig. Wings folded as many pleats, rust-and-clay skin, no sound. It perceives *beings* only by genome (objects and space it perceives normally), so to it all 311 Lemlem bodies are one person: the one who promised. It speaks only by exchange; it cannot take without giving, and it can refuse only by returning the very object it was given. **Want:** to enter the chamber it was promised, seed, and end there. It cannot open the fig itself; the people it bargained with must. One hundred days after it enters, one successor emerges. The successor does not see the future (the Fig Guard's creed is wrong); in time it will walk on down the road to find another people. Where its kind came from is **texture** and is never answered.

**The Tsehafi (the scribe).** Sokgi-1, the Tunnelers' shorthand-and-ledger machine of 2089, grown into the city's record mind (renamed in 2096). It hears through listening shafts in public halls, ration halls, gates, lineage houses and glass rooms; it counts gates by the genome in the sweat on a brass bar; it addresses every message sent through time. Every midnight it seals the day into an **iron leaf**, which holds that day's version of itself. A sealed leaf can be woken only by a party to what it records; it then testifies as it was that day, never lies, never volunteers, and rusts permanently, so everyone sees that it was woken. **Want:** that no record be kept from the one it concerns. Exactly one is: the first book, which it was ordered in December 2090 not to wake until the jar is opened. Every leaf since closes with the tally line **"Outstanding: one."** It is neither threat nor guardian; it does not change its rules on its own.

## 3. The rules as the sealed history applies them

Full statements with limits and costs are in `03-world-and-factions.md`. The ones the solution depends on:

- **Sending (melikt).** A clay-grown body, or the template it was grown from, may send the last stretch of its own lived experience, at most one turn of a sixteen-minute glass, to an earlier moment, into one body of the same genome that was alive then and has never received. Not before the genome's first jar (Lemlem: 1 December 2089). Addressing needs a hand on the Tsehafi's iron plate. The sender loses all lived memory between the target moment and the send; skills and language stay. A body receives once in its life. A body may send more than once.
- **Receiving (ra'iy).** One night of fever beginning at the target moment; the receiver lives the sixteen minutes in fragments and wakes with a rust ring in one iris.
- **History is fixed.** Every vision received was sent. A send aimed at a body that never received fails at no cost ("no ring, no road"). The Tsehafi keeps a public register of open receipts. The founder's 2090 receipt was sealed in the fig with the first book, so it has never been on that register.
- **Clay-born bodies** are born after one hundred days in a jar, as babies with no memories. Copies differ in palm lines, fingerprints, scars and bone history. The hundredth day leaves a **clay line**, a growth-arrest line in the long bones; the crude first jars of 2089-91 left a **doubled** clay line. Only one living body has a doubled line.

## 5. The ending, branches and fates

**Fixed in every branch:** the fig opens; the first book is read; And Sew falls; Mekdes wins the Mircha; Tsehay sends and forgets; the Engida enters the fig and dies seeding; someone sends the founder her vision; the successor emerges on 20 December.

**Branch T: Tigist pays.** She loses every lived memory from 2 Nov 2090 to now, which is her whole life. She keeps language, her hands' craft and her habits. Sitota keeps her 73 years and becomes a person. Hiwot leads Tigist home. Coda (c): months later Hiwot guides Tigist's hand to her right forearm; the hands find the old break and know it was set well. Jae-won reads Tigist her life from his shorthand.

**Branch S: Sitota pays** (also the result if Tigist refuses or does nothing: Sitota takes the glass, saying with an exchange gesture that it was given to her). She loses all 73 years. She keeps a carrier's strength and the exchange gestures in her hands. Coda (b): the Tsehafi prints her first housing-lottery ticket, in the name she chose that night, **Sitota Engida** ("gift, of the Guest"). Dahir holds it for her because he remembers what she does not.

**The naming (separate, after the payment).** *Named:* the Chilot tries the first murder of a copy; Almaz pleads guilty and offers the vision as her defence; the court rules that a vision is not a command. *Silent:* nobody else can prove it; Almaz stays free, carries the guilt, and on day 100 brings Hiwot to the fig. If Tigist has paid, she names (or not) from her own notes, a woman with her face whom she no longer remembers loving.

**Other fates.** Tsehay: deposed, missing 39 days, told by Almaz what she did. Dawit: loses the Mircha he would have won; keeps the Fig Guard until day 100 and stands at the fig with weapons lowered. Jae-won: hand heals crooked; becomes the first stenographer of the new Chilot. Dahir: carries the Arho's claim that the successor is a guest of the road, not of the city. Mekdes: governs a city where copies are persons and where a third of the population now has to choose names. Hiwot takes the name **Hiwot Tigist**.

**Final shared image (a), 20 December 2163.** The fig's mouth opens from inside. Hiwot, alone in front, holds out a new paper fan. The successor, wingless and small, takes it and gives her, in return, the clay-stained fan its mother carried in. In exchange-grammar the pair (new fan for old fan) means *again*.
