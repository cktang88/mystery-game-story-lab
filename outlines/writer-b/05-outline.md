# Writer B: outline — *The Last Tide*

Working title: **The Last Tide**. (Alternatives on file: *Skuld*; *What the Ebb Takes*.)

## One sentence

A tide-pilot walks her father into the past to learn who drowned his older self.

## One paragraph (setup, three disasters, ending)

On the morning of the Spring Reckoning, when the islands of the Sielacht send their owed citizens back in time by laying them in person-shaped hollows of a buried reef, the pilot Jildou Okkes finds a drowned old man in the mud with her father's face and no navel: an unregistered copy. He was the eel-man who taught her the flats; her father knew, and in three days she must walk him to the hollow that made him. **But** the Chief Reckoner closes the case, **therefore** Jildou digs on with an engineer, an acoustician and an archive of the reckoning machine's deleted conclusions, and lifts the face out of the oldest hollow of all: it is the Chief Reckoner, owed to the founding of the nation, where history says she was drowned as a witch (**disaster one**). **Therefore** the head of state, now certain the founding is about to repeat, orders every remaining debt paid by force at the Last Tide, which drives Jildou's friend Tjalling to sabotage the sluice her father keeps, and her father dies in it (**disaster two**, caused by her own search). **But** in the reckoning machine's oldest version she finds the proof that history could never unravel and that the coercion was always a lie, and in the mud's own hum she proves the Reckoner drowned the eel-man; **therefore** the Reckoner confesses and is seized by the state to be sent to the founding by force, and the state now knows a stolen "throat" exists and hunts it (**disaster three**). **Therefore**, rebuilding the founding night from the reef's six centuries of sound, Jildou learns the reef is a dying alien mother, the copies are her children in borrowed shapes, and the founders cut out her voice so she could never call them home. At the lowest tide of the year, the last tide on which any crossing is possible, Jildou retrieves the voice from the eel trap where her father's copy hid it the night he died, walks the woman who killed him to the founding and holds her hand while she goes, and returns the voice to the mother, who sings once and dies. Every copy on Earth hears, and chooses.

## One paragraph per major thread

- **Jildou (the anchor, 2026).** From finding a body to walking the world across the mud: she learns that the copy she resented was the better half of her father, loses the father she had, and ends by forgiving his other self's killer with her hands rather than her words.
- **Gerlof (2001 to 2026, the past).** A man who already knows the date and shape of his own corpse spends 25 years working out why he will die with blue clay under his nails: he tries to change the past and cannot, chooses instead what to be inside it, raises a daughter who is not legally his, and spends his last night saving the voice of the world and shaving so she will know his face.
- **Marrit (2026, and 1371).** The keeper of the nation's ledger has known for seven years that she will be the founding witch, and has fought her own future self to the point of murder; when she hears her copy's actual words in the reef's memory, she stops fighting, asks to be walked to the founding, and then refuses her own freedom.
- **Inês (2026).** An outsider owed to 1974 comes to pay a debt she has always resented, meets the 86-year-old woman she will become, and discovers that what she pays for is the recording that lets everyone hear the truth.
- **Femke and KERF (2026).** The spare original of the Reckoner and the auditing machine both of them kept blind restore the conclusions that were deleted, and the machine, following the oldest rule in its office, makes the whole ledger public the moment it closes.
- **Tjalling (2026).** The man who refuses his debt teaches himself, in order to refuse it, exactly what his copy needed to know to build the nation's great sluice; his refusal kills his friend; his debt becomes his atonement.

## Structure at a glance

Four acts, eighteen chapters, about 18 to 22 hours. Three plot lines run through all four acts:

- **P1 The Drowned Double** (who was he, who drowned him): Acts I to III.
- **P2 The Last Tide** (debts, doctrine, the state and the world): rising through Acts II to IV.
- **P3 Mem** (what the reef is): planted from Ch1, answered in Act IV.

The clock is public from minute one: the Last Tide is 20 March 2026, seventeen days after the opening. A **Tide Board** opens every chapter: the date, the hours to the next low water, a portrait of each active character with their want in one line, and the current question. A persistent **Reef Map** records every place, sound and hollow the player has found, and an **Attribution Board** holds identity claims the player has made (confirmed only in sets of three, never one by one).

Each act opens on a small, personal hook that turns out to carry lore.

## ACT I — THE DROWNED DOUBLE (3 to 6 March 2026)

**Act question:** Who is the drowned man with my father's face?
**Viewpoint:** Jildou only. One new mechanic per chapter.

### Ch1. The Spring Reckoning (3 March, 05:30 to 09:00)

*Hook:* an ordinary job. *New mechanic:* piloting (tide-stick, gully sounds, tide clock).

1. Pre-dawn on the Grauwarf pilot station. Jildou takes a party out: Auke, a 19-year-old owed boy whose hollow is dated 1952; a church deacon; Marrit, the Chief Reckoner, in white gloves with a KERF tablet. The player reads the flats by sound (water draining in gullies is louder where it is deep) and the tide-stick, leading the party on the safest line. Teaches: the tide is the clock and the threat.
2. On the dike behind them the island stages the **Long Ebb pageant**: a straw Mud-Witch is drowned at the Witch's Gate sluice at sunrise. Okke, Jildou's father, works the gate for it. The Diekgreve, Ubbo Kanter, presides. A foreign delegation in grey coats watches from a van; one of them, Inês, has hydrophone cases and waves to the pilots. Tjalling and a few Skuldfrij heckle with cut ropes tied to sticks. Everyone the story needs is in one place, and the Tide Board names them plainly: "Okke Hylkes, your father. Marrit, your godmother. Tjalling Douwes, your father's friend."
3. At the reef the bared **Rib** shows a field of domes and hollows. Jildou finds Auke's hollow by its number. He lies down; it **fits him exactly**. The controller hums; nothing visible happens. He gets up shaking. Marrit stamps PAID. Jildou says the pilot's patter: **"Nobody crosses. Somebody stays."** Auke: "Do you know your tide?" Jildou: "I haven't got one." (Anomaly and harmless experiment for the crossing rule.)
4. Walking back, curlews break from the **Deep Gully**. A man face down in the mud. Jildou turns him: her father's face, 25 years older, freshly shaved.
5. **Photograph before the tide** (new verb, timed by the flood): the player has about four minutes of real time before the gully fills and Marrit's people arrive. Things to frame: the left boot buried to the shin in soft gully mud; no lamp and no pole (no pilot or fisher walks the flats at night without both); razor nicks on the jaw; **blue clay under the fingernails**; a cut rope on a stake thirty metres away (Skuldfrij sign; honest red herring). When Marrit's assistant lifts his jumper to check for a navel, the player can catch it: **none**.
6. Okke comes down off the dike in his sluice-keeper's cap as the body is lifted. He looks at his own older face for a long moment and does not look surprised; the camera lets the player catch it. Marrit takes the case: an unregistered death on the Rib is Rekkenkeamer business. She touches Jildou's face: "Go home. I'll find out who he was."

*Ends on:* the question on the Tide Board: **Who is the drowned man with my father's face?**

### Ch2. The Eel-Man (3 March, afternoon)

*New mechanic:* identity attribution.

1. Among the photos, an oilskin with a mended collar Jildou knows: **Old Gerlof**, the bearded eel-man on Lütjewarf who taught her the flats from age eight. She has not seen him in three years. Without his beard she never saw his face.
2. She sails to his hut. It has been **searched** by someone who knew they were looking for one object: floorboards up, the stove taken apart, but money and his radio untouched.
3. His **eel-trap line**, the one he taught her, runs from a post with a red rag. Habit: she checks them in order. Six are reachable at this tide. **The seventh stands in water.** (She says nothing; the player sees it. Recurring object planted.)
4. In the hut: beard clippings in a basin, fresh; a framed newspaper photograph from **October 2004**, the search for her mother, with an unidentified man in an eel boat circled in pencil; a tin of minidiscs with one empty case labelled "14.10.04".
5. **Attribution puzzle.** The player lays three items side by side: Gerlof's eel tally book, Okke's sluice log (Jildou has one at home) and a postcard her father sent her. Same hand: the same backward-leaning 7, the same crossed zero. Second: the morgue photograph of the left thumb shows a fishhook scar Okke got at twelve. Third: no navel. The board accepts the set: **Gerlof is a copy of Okke.**
6. KERF, queried through the pilot station terminal, answers in its usual way: "No hollow in the ledger matches this face. No living person whose face matches is registered as owed." An **unregistered copy**: illegal, hidden for a lifetime.

*Ends on:* If Gerlof was my father's copy, my father is owed. Did he know?

### Ch3. Two Hands (4 to 5 March)

*New mechanic:* reading a hollow (waterline and silt).

1. At the Witch's Gate, Okke shows Tjalling how the gate's counterweights work, as a friend teaching a friend; he says he checks the gate himself at every spring tide, "because the gate doesn't care who's on shift". Plays as warmth. (Plants Ch10 twice.)
2. Jildou watches her father's wrist: his watch has an alarm set for **6 March, 05:12**. She asks for the photo from the 2010 eel fair she remembers. Both men are in it, ten metres apart, not looking at each other.
3. **Confrontation, in his kitchen.** Okke confesses: in October 2004, the night her mother died, a man with his face came to the door wet to the waist and told him everything. He has known for 22 years that he is owed and that his tide is Friday at 05:12. He let Gerlof teach her because "it was the only way I could be there twice". Jildou walks out.
4. Okke does not know where the hollow is. Gerlof hid it the day he arrived and told Okke only one thing: "Jildou will find it."
5. **Hollow hunt** (new verb, against the tide). Jildou walks Gerlof's old anchorage. Candidate depressions under silt. For each, she clears it and reads the fine mineral **waterline** inside: the water height at the moment of the sending. She computes Friday's 05:12 water level at that spot from the tide table and her stick. One matches: under a rotten **fish-box lid**, a man-shaped hollow, filled with silt for 25 years.
6. Marrit visits. She has closed the case: "accidental drowning of an unregistered Slikborn". She offers to register Okke so his crossing is legal; Okke refuses; Marrit agrees to look away. "Some debts are best paid quietly." (Plays as kindness. It is also how she ends an investigation.)
7. Okke, on the dike at night, tells Jildou the rule in his own words: "It fits you like a coat. Nobody else could lie in it. And I won't go anywhere. He will."

*Ends on:* She will walk him.

### Ch4. The Crossing (6 March, 03:30 to 05:20)

*New mechanic:* none; a down-beat scene built from known verbs.

1. Night piloting: lamp, sound, stick, father. Short exchanges, voiced. He tells her the one thing Gerlof told him about the future: "He said you'd be the one who found him. He said, tell her I'm sorry about the beard."
2. At the hollow. He lies down. It fits. He asks her to hold the lamp up so he can see her face. The hum rises through everything. He gets up, wet and cold, and laughs once.
3. Jildou: "Nobody crosses." Okke: "Somebody stays." They walk back.
4. Cut to black. Breath. Mud. A naked man's hands pulling at silt in the dark.

*Act I answer:* the dead man is Okke's copy, and Okke knew. *Opens:* Who killed him? Why was he hiding? Why the blue clay?

## ACT II — THE WITCH'S GRAVE (7 to 12 March 2026; past 2001 to 2004)

**Act question:** Who drowned Gerlof, and what was he digging for?
**Viewpoints:** Jildou; Gerlof (past); Femke (at KERF); Inês.

### Ch5. Mud (Gerlof, 11 March 2001)

*Hook:* you wake as the man whose body you found. *New mechanic:* **fulfil the record**.

1. The player is Gerlof, 47, naked in a hollow before dawn, with 40 minutes to the flood. He carries Okke's memory up to the moment of lying down, which means **he carries the player's own Ch1 evidence**: he knows he will be found in the Deep Gully on 3 March 2026, shaved, boot stuck, no lamp, blue clay under his nails. The Tide Board gives him his want: *Find out who will kill me. Leave her what she needs.*
2. **The record as constraint.** The game shows three documents Gerlof remembers from Okke's life: KERF's 2001 reef survey (no new hollow recorded), a 2001 police report of a theft from the Grauwarf church vestry ("cassock, boots, oilskin; a man in a cassock seen walking to the ferry"), and Okke's own memory that he never saw this man before October 2004. The player must make all three true: hide the hollow (fish-box lid, silt), steal those clothes and be seen in the cassock, and cross Grauwarf without young Okke, 22, seeing him at the sluice. Stealth with history as the guard.
3. He rows to Lütjewarf and takes his grandfather's name. First shave he will not take for 25 years.

*Ends on:* a man in a cassock alone in an eel hut, writing a date on the wall: 03.03.2026.

### Ch6. The Blip (7 to 8 March, present)

*New mechanics:* KERF reconstruction; the hum is introduced as a sound only.

1. Jildou goes to the **Rekkenkeamer** in the old sluice house. The **Gallery of the Owed**: a hall of 38,000 white plaster faces lifted from hollows, each with a brass number. She meets **Femke**, the engineer: "Marrit's original. Yes, she's older than me. Everyone asks."
2. Femke shows KERF's **reconstruction** of the night of 2 to 3 March as a walkable diorama built from tide gauges, reef seismometers, the dike camera and phone pings. A grey figure (KERF draws unknown people as grey blanks) ties an eel boat at the reef's south end at 01:30 and walks to the **Witch's Grave**, the slab over the oldest hollow by the drowned church tower. **Seismic event 01:50 at Kerf Nul.** The figure leaves the reef at 02:10 toward Lütjewarf and is lost. At **03:10 a Rekkenkeamer car** parks at the dike: "on-call response to seismic alert; report: no anomaly found". (Planted in plain sight.)
3. Asked to analyse Kerf Nul, KERF answers: "Statute 1371/1. The Witch's Grave is not to be analysed." Blue clay, Jildou knows, lies only in the drowned church floor. Gerlof dug at the Witch's Grave.
4. Femke, idly, on whether the ledger is secret: "Only while it's open. A closed ledger is public record; that's the oldest rule in the building. It's never been closed." (Plant for Ch18.)
5. **Inês** (new viewpoint), in the Accord compound with her minder. She knows she owes a 1974 hollow and pays on the Equinox. She asks the archive for the 1974 to 1990 reef tapes her copy is supposed to have made, and learns from the archivist that her copy is **alive**, in the Slikborn quarter.

*Ends on:* What was under the Witch's Grave?

### Ch7. Fog (Gerlof, 14 October 2004; present 9 March)

*Hook:* Okke's memory says Sjoukje drowns tonight. *Bill for the crossing rule.*

1. Gerlof knows the night, the gully and the hour from Okke's memory. **Personal use of the rule**: he sets out to change it. Boat navigation in fog against the tide clock. Whatever line the player chooses, he grounds on a sandbar he has forgotten (the same bar that delayed the real rescue boats, per the rescue report he remembers). The rule enforces itself through an ordinary cause.
2. He reaches her on foot in the Deep Gully as the tide rises. She is alive and cannot be moved in time. The player can hold her hand and choose what to say; there is no wrong answer. She asks who he is. If he tells her, she laughs: "You've got his hands." He records the last minutes on the minidisc recorder he uses for eel-sounding. She is not afraid.
3. **Fulfil the record**: the newspaper photograph shows an eel boat in a searchlight. The player must be in the boat, in the light, at the right moment.
4. That night he goes to young Okke's door. (Seen from outside, wordless.)
5. Two years later (a short coda), he sets the red-rag post for the eel line he will teach Jildou and buries a tin under it.
6. **Present (9 March).** The player, as Jildou, can dig anywhere. If they dig under the red-rag post (which only playing Gerlof suggests), they find the tin: the minidisc "14.10.04" and a photo of eight-year-old Jildou holding her first eel. She plays the disc: her mother's voice, and a man's voice that is her father's, older. Her mother was not alone. (Reclassifies her childhood grief; the first gift across time.)

*Ends on:* Jildou in the cottage with headphones on, and the first time she says Gerlof's name without anger.

### Ch8. The Promise (9 to 11 March)

*New mechanic:* **tide-tuned listening** (the alien verb).

1. **Inês meets Dona Inês**, 86, nearly blind, in a kitchen full of tape. Same voice, half a century apart. Dona Inês gives her the tapes and the method: the reef's hum holds every sound ever made on it; each moment's sound sits at a pitch set by the tide height of its moment. "I remember deciding to come. If I tell you why, it becomes my reason, not yours." On every tape, under everything, two features: a thin high tone the Hearers call **the Promise**, and a low break they call **the Sob**.
2. **Jildou and Inês on the flats.** The collaborative verb: Jildou computes the tide curve for the night of 2 to 3 March at each spot (her skill); Inês tunes the hydrophones to it (hers). Sounds rise out of the hum and the player places each on the Reef Map: digging at Kerf Nul at 01:50; footsteps leaving the reef north-west (then silence: off Mem's body, out of range); footsteps returning onto the reef near the Deep Gully; **two voices**, a man's and a muffled woman's, the gully walls smearing her words. Then, clear: **"Out here there's no enemy. There's only the tide."** Gerlof's line, taught to Jildou as a child. She takes the headphones off.
3. **Tjalling**, confronted about his cut rope by the body, lies, then admits he was surveying the Witch's Gate for "a demonstration". The hum puts his outboard at the gate at 02:00, a kilometre from the gully: cleared of the murder. But now Jildou knows Skuldfrij are studying her father's gate. She tells Okke; Okke laughs: "Tjalling's all talk." (Seeds Ch10.)

*Ends on:* a woman was in the gully with him. Who walks the flats at 03:40?

### Ch9. MIDPOINT — Lifting the Face (12 March)

*New mechanic:* **version archaeology**.

1. Femke, using the read access she has kept to KERF's archive for years, opens **KERF v7 (2019)**, rendered as a pale holographic face, and asks the question the current KERF refuses. v7 answers: "Kerf Nul. Human female, sixty to sixty-five. No umbilicus. Healed BCG vaccination scar, left upper arm. Three abdominal scars consistent with keyhole surgery, post-1990. Dense foreign object, forty centimetres, held against the chest." The current KERF, asked why it no longer knows this: "Version 7 was retired by order of the Chief Reckoner."
2. **The first person from now went to the founding.** Who?
3. **Night casting**, illegal: Jildou, Femke and Inês on the reef at low water. The slab is already off (Gerlof's work). The hollow is open and empty: whatever the "object" was is gone. Casting gameplay: mix plaster against the flood, pour, wait while the water creeps, lift.
4. The face is **Femke's**, aged. Femke goes white: "That's me." **The navel rule decides**: the cast has none. Femke has one. It is Marrit. Femke checks Marrit's file: keyhole appendectomy, 2003.
5. A lamp on the reef. **Marrit**, alone. She looks at her own face in the plaster. "I know. I've known since 2019." She does not arrest them. She asks Jildou to stop, "for your own sake", and walks away.
6. The witch of the Ch1 pageant, drowned in straw every year at the gate Okke keeps, is the Chief Reckoner.
7. **Consequence (cut to dawn).** KERF logs the illegal casting; the Diekgreve reads it. Ubbo now believes the founding is about to repeat itself and the nation's history depends on every debt being paid. On every screen: all remaining owed will be crossed by force at the Last Tide; Skuldfrij members will be arrested. Tjalling, owed to 1966, watches.

*Midpoint flips the question* from "who killed Gerlof?" to **"Why will Marrit go to the founding, what did the witch really do, and what was in her hollow?"**

## ACT III — THE LAST TIDE (13 to 17 March 2026; past 2011 to 2026)

**Act question:** Why is everyone so afraid of the Last Tide, and who drowned Gerlof?
**Viewpoints:** Jildou; Gerlof (past); Femke; Inês. Marrit becomes a viewpoint only after the player has seen what she did.

### Ch10. The Gate (13 to 14 March)

*Hook:* supper. *Genre shift:* detective story to political thriller.

1. Okke and Jildou cook together for the first time in years. He agrees to skip tonight's gate check. They laugh about Gerlof's terrible bread.
2. 23:40 she wakes to a blast. He went anyway.
3. Run the dike at flood through police lines (tide and stealth). The gate chamber. He is dying under a counterweight; he asks whether the gate held. It did not fully. He dies. (No device will undo this.)
4. **KERF reconstruction** of the sabotage: four grey masked figures. Attribution by gait and hands: one moves exactly as Okke taught Tjalling to move round the counterweights (Ch3). Tjalling.
5. The blast silts the basin channel: at the Last Tide the Rib may not bare at all. Ubbo declares emergency. The world is watching now: Accord delegations land with their owed in custody; the Last Tide is militarised. Tjalling is gone.

*Cost of the jump to national scale:* Okke. *Caused by:* Jildou's midpoint casting.

### Ch11. The Versions (Gerlof 2011 and 2019; present 15 March)

1. **Gerlof, 2011.** He meets **Hiske**, a Hearer, pressing a copper cup to the mud. She teaches him to listen. He hears the Promise and the Sob. Hiske: "Mem is crying." He asks who Mem is. She looks at him as if he were stupid.
2. **Gerlof, 2019.** From the dike he watches Femke's crossing with Marrit presiding: the original lying down so the copy can exist. Afterwards Marrit stands a long time at the Witch's Grave slab. (Shared landmark with Ch9; the player now understands why.)
3. **Gerlof, 2025.** In the Grauwarf church library, two accounts of the founding night side by side (short, authored, biased texts): the **Ebbe-Brief** ("the throat of the Grace was cast into the deep") and priest **Enno's chronicle** ("she went back into the grave she came out of and took the throat into the dark with her"). The Brief is a state proclamation; Enno was a frightened man writing at night. He chooses to believe the frightened man.
4. **Present.** Femke and Jildou open **KERF v4 (1998)** in green terminal text. Asked whether an unpaid hollow can unravel history: "No hollow has ever gone unpaid in 627 years. Of 212 owed who fled, all paid: one was shipwrecked onto the reef; one came back to bury a parent; one was arrested elsewhere and extradited. Probability of an unpaid hollow: zero. Enforcement is not required. Recommendation: end compulsory crossing." Retired by order of the Diekgreve, 1998.
5. **The Unravelling is a lie the state has kept for 28 years**, and the manhunts of the Reckoning Decade were never necessary. Jildou wants it published. Femke: "Then they'll arrest me." At dawn they do.

*Ends on:* the throat. Gerlof was looking for the throat. Where is it?

### Ch12. The Last Night (Gerlof, 2 to 3 March 2026)

*The player sees the murder before the detective does.*

1. Evening, Lütjewarf. **The player shaves him** in a cracked mirror. "There you are."
2. 01:30. He lifts the slab of the Witch's Grave and digs out **blue clay** with his hands (fulfilling his own nails). Inside the hollow, held where a body's chest would be, a stone the size of a ewe's head, warm, faintly humming: the **throat**.
3. He carries it off the reef to Jildou's eel line and **puts it in the seventh trap**, which at this tide stands in knee-deep water. "At the equinox you'll be bare."
4. Back across the reef toward his boat. In the Deep Gully, a lamp. **Marrit.** Short, quiet exchange: she asks for the stone; he says it is where it belongs; she sees his face: "You're Okke Hylkes." "Not tonight."
5. She takes his lamp and his pole. His left boot is held by the gully mud. The player can pull and twist; the boot will not come (the record). He says: **"Out here there's no enemy. There's only the tide."** She leaves.
6. The water comes up in the dark. The hum is everywhere. The player can do one thing: listen. Under the Sob, the Promise. Fade.

*The player now knows two things Jildou does not:* Marrit killed him, and the throat is in the seventh trap. From here every scene with Marrit is played under dread (the investigator-culprit, disclosed to the player first).

### Ch13. The Gallery of Faces (16 to 17 March)

*New mechanic:* **ask the original**.

1. Jildou visits Femke in custody. Femke, about Marrit: "Ask me what I'd do. I'd have wanted it to be the sea. I'd have taken his lamp so I didn't have to see. And I couldn't have thrown the light away." So the lamp still exists.
2. **Evidence set**, built on the Attribution Board, confirmed only as a set:
   - Inês reruns the gully recording tuned to the exact tide phase inside the gully walls; the woman's voice clears. Jildou knows it from her own licensing exam.
   - KERF: the on-call Reckoner on 3 March was Marrit; her report said "no anomaly"; KERF's own seismic log contradicts it.
   - **Gerlof's lamp**, with a G scratched in its base, in a locked drawer of Marrit's office in the Gallery (a short stealth scene among 38,000 faces).
   - Tjalling's alibi (Ch8) and Ubbo's recorded call in Sielhaven at 03:40 clear the decoys.
3. **Confrontation** in the Gallery. Marrit confesses everything, without excuses: the Annex ("If it sings, the Echoes go home"), which she reads as the death of every Slikborn; the seven years of knowing she would be the witch; her vow that her copy would fail; the night in the gully; the hut. "I killed one of us to keep eleven thousand alive. I didn't touch him. I let the flats do it. I haven't slept."
4. She does two things before the guard arrives: she signs Femke's release, and she lifts KERF's ban on hearing the reef. "If I'm going there, I want to hear what I did." Then Ubbo's guard takes her: the witch must go to the founding on the Equinox, by force.
5. **17 March, dawn.** Jildou and Femke watch the three closed domes. Ubbo has said he will lie on one at the Equinox and send a copy of himself back to today, knowing the outcome. If that will happen, a fresh hollow appears now. KERF: "No arrivals." It never will. (Fair-in-hindsight clue to Ubbo's end.)

*Ends on:* Marrit becomes a viewpoint for the first time, in a cell on the dike, with the hum coming up through the floor.

## ACT IV — MEM (18 to 20 March 2026; 1371)

**Act question:** What is the reef, what did the founders do, and what will the Last Tide do to everyone?
**Viewpoints:** Jildou; Marrit; Inês; then the Mud-Woman in 1371.

### Ch14. The Sob (18 March)

*Hook:* a sound in a cell. *New mechanic:* **echo-walk** (AGI and alien together).

1. Marrit, in her cell, feels the hum as every Slikborn does: bodily, without dates.
2. KERF, allowed to hear the reef for the first time in fifteen years, joins Inês's hydrophones. Jildou supplies tide curves for 22 to 26 January 1371 (from the medieval chronicle's tide notes and the drowned church's flood marks). KERF builds **Ammerwarf in white lines from the echoes**: walls, nave, the mound, the new sluice. The player walks the founding night as sound and outline. (This is also the map the player will need in Ch17.)
3. The Long Ebb: people walking out on the bared seabed. The return wave. Then, days later, **a woman's voice** among the survivors: Marrit's voice, unmistakable, older, speaking an accent the founders mock. She tells them the reef is a mother, that her children flee into the past in our shapes, that one day she will sing, and that they will be **"free to go home. Free. Not taken."** The founders' voices: "She calls the sea." Torches. Digging. **The Sob**, now heard as what it is: a scream.
4. KERF's analysis, now permitted: the Promise has the structure of the hum inverted, a call; it is in every recording ever made because it is the loudest thing she will ever do, echoing back through her life. The Hearers have called her Mem for five centuries. The Slikborn have no navel because they were never born of a woman. **The reef is a mother. The Slikborn are her children in our shapes. The founders cut out her voice so she could never call them home. The call is a choice.**
5. **Marrit** hears her own future voice through the cell's speaker. The belief she killed for breaks. She asks the guard for one thing: to speak to the pilot.

*Ends on:* The call will come; the Promise proves it. But the throat is missing and the Rib is silted.

### Ch15. The Sluice (19 March)

1. Tjalling surrenders to Jildou at Okke's grave. There is no forgiveness scene; she does not give one.
2. The silted channel means the Rib will not bare at the Equinox. Tjalling knows how to open the **Great Sluice**, the monument his own copy designed in 1970, because he studied it to sabotage it. "I learned it to break it. He learned it from me."
3. **Sluice puzzle under patrol**: night, guards, a sequence of gates the player has seen operated twice (Okke in Ch3, the drawings in Ch11). Tjalling works; Jildou keeps watch on the tide.
4. Ubbo's position, through his own broadcast: the throat, if it exists, will be destroyed; every owed will pay at dawn; and he will lie on the Grace and be taken. He does not believe the reef's absence of a hollow means anything; doctrine says the Grace provides.
5. Down beat: Jildou, Inês and Femke on the pilot station roof. Inês decides to pay. She does not say why.

### Ch16. The Seventh Trap (20 March, 05:30 to 06:58)

*The player knows where the throat is; Jildou does not.*

1. 05:40, Tjalling opens the Great Sluice. The basin drains. For the first time since the night Gerlof died, the tide falls far enough to bare **the seventh trap**.
2. The player can walk Jildou anywhere. If they go to the trap line (only Ch12 tells them to), she checks them in order, as he taught her: one to six empty; the seventh holds a warm stone that hums. No line of dialogue explains it. She holds it and laughs, once, like her father.
3. **The queue.** Jildou pilots the last crossing party in history across the bared flats in the dark: foreign owed with minders, Inês, Tjalling under guard, Marrit under guard, Ubbo and his officers. Pilot gameplay at its hardest: dozens of people, deep gullies, the guards suspicious of what she carries.
4. Marrit asks the guard to let the pilot walk her. **The walk**: Jildou leads her father's killer across the Rib by the hand to Kerf Nul, the hollow Gerlof dug open. (If the player stops, the record is still kept, but by a guard dragging her; the epilogue remembers which.)
5. Marrit lies down. It fits. She asks Jildou to say it. Jildou: "Nobody crosses." Marrit: "Somebody stays."
6. 06:58. Along the Rib, Inês lies in her 1974 hollow, Tjalling in his 1966 hollow, a dozen foreigners in theirs. The hum rises.

### Ch17. The Long Ebb (24 to 26 January 1371)

*The player is the Mud-Woman.* *Verb:* fulfil the record, at its hardest.

1. She climbs out of Kerf Nul into a drowned town. The player knows its layout from the Ch14 echo-walk, now in colour, torchlight and freezing water. She has 63 years of Marrit, three weeks of the investigation, and one aim: save the voice.
2. **The record**, which the player carries as two texts (the Brief and Enno's chronicle): she warns the founders and is mocked; Kanter's copy walks out of the mud and is locked in the church as a demon; on the 25th they cut the throat; she is drowned at dawn at the gate. The player may try anything. The founders still lock her in the church while they dig; the scream still comes.
3. Escape the church (stealth among frightened survivors). The throat on a cart by the sluice. Steal it. Carry it through the flooded nave. **Press it into her own hollow and seal it with blue church-floor clay**, with her hands (the clay Gerlof will dig out 655 years later).
4. Caught at dawn. Walked to the new sluice: the gate Okke will keep. The player walks there; there is no fail state and no escape. Water.

*The pageant of Ch1 is reclassified:* every year the nation drowns, in straw, the woman who saved its mother's voice.

### Ch18. The Call (20 March, 07:00 to 08:00) and Epilogue (May)

1. 07:05. Jildou carries the throat to the **Mouth**, the scarred gap in the reef by the church stump. She presses it in.
2. **Mem sings.** No words, no subtitles: one long song through every speaker, every controller motor, every hydrophone. The three domes split.
3. KERF, on every Rekkenkeamer screen and every phone in the Sielacht: "Last hollow paid. Ledger balanced. Under Rekkenkeamer law a closed ledger is public record. Publishing." The whole ledger goes out to the world: every hollow, every face, every tide, every rollback, v4's proof.
4. On the flats, eleven thousand Slikborn across the world feel the call at once. The dike screens show cities: people walking into harbours, a cabinet minister wading off a beach in a suit. Here, **Dona Inês** walks out across the mud, guided by Hiske's hand, and passes **Inês** walking back from her hollow. They stop. They do not speak. They go on in opposite directions.
5. **Marrit** stands in Kerf Nul with the call in her bones. She could go home, free, and nobody could stop her. She stays. "Somebody stays."
6. **Ubbo** lies on a dome, waiting for the Grace. It opens under him. Three small luminous things swim past his face and out toward the sea. The tide turns. Jildou walks him back. "Out here there's no enemy. There's only the tide."
7. **Epilogue, May.** The Gallery is being emptied; families carry plaster faces home or to the sea. Jildou sets seven eel traps from a post with a red rag. Femke is KERF's last keeper. Marrit and Tjalling await trial. Jildou's phone: **KERF**: "There are no tides left in my ledger. I can compute a forecast of your life. It would be the first forecast anyone has had in 655 years. Shall I?" The player answers, or doesn't. The tide comes in over the flats; nobody knows what it brings.

## How gameplay delivers each revelation

- **Identity revelations (R1, R5, R7)** are stated by the player on the Attribution Board, confirmed only in sets of three, never per item. The answer space is every named person times every body, voice and cast (hundreds of combinations).
- **Sequence revelations (R3, R6)** come from KERF's reconstructions and version archaeology: the player scrubs a night, places events, then asks archived versions the same question and diffs their answers.
- **Sound revelations (R7, R8)** come from tide-tuned listening: the player computes a tide curve (Jildou) and tunes (Inês), then places sounds on the Reef Map. The witch's words are heard, never read.
- **Past revelations (R4, R11)** come from fulfilling the record: the player plays the past against documents it must make true, and learns by doing what the record hid.
- **Location revelations (R9)** are pure knowledge gates: the player carries a fact across eras that no present character knows, and acts on it without a line of dialogue.
- **Text** is limited to short authored documents (the Brief, Enno's chronicle, police and rescue reports, v4's terminal output), each a few sentences, each biased, each optional depth except where a record must be fulfilled.
