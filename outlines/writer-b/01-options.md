# Writer B, round 3: options and picks (revision 2)

Working title: **The Light Weight**

**Starting point: world first.** I began from a founding lie beneath an interstellar order and derived the polities, courts, intelligence services, economy and incentives from it, then the cast. No material from `outlines/archive/` or `judging/archive/` is used.

**Who picked.** The picks are the writer's. The grader's review (`judging/review-writer-b.md`) overturned or reshaped several of them, and every such change is marked **[revised]** with the reason. Rounds the grader found weak (4, 5, 6, 7 and 10) have been rebuilt with real rivals.

## Round 1. Setting and scale (three options)

**S1. Marqand, the receiving elevator.** An elevator city whose alien-spun cable receives messages from the future: the only such place known. Floors are classes.

**S2. The inverted ladder.** An elevator down into an alien gas giant, where the aliens hold the deep floors and humans are a tolerated client people at an alien court.

**S3. The Ladder Standard.** Several elevator cities under one paymaster standard, so that "Floor 3" means the same class on every world.

**First pick:** S1. **[revised] Final pick: S1 with S3 and S2 grafted on**, as the grader proposed:

- **From S3**, the city is no longer alone. **Chash** is a second, plain elevator city that copied Marqand's farn system and pays it partly from the same Ordu reparations, so the verdict's fall hits two cities, and Chash's ruler is a voting party at the climax.
- **From S2**, the aliens get a real court. The Counterweight is now an embassy, and the Draxt **court at the Grove**, on a low-gravity moon, is a place the player visits, divided into two factions.

Together with the Ordu fleet's own court, Usrushan, Pench and Kish, about 35 to 40% of play is now off the elevator.

## Round 2. The founding crime (three options)

- **F1. The Unburned verdict.** A message "from the future" convicted the Ordu's leader of a war crime he had not committed. The reparations fund the AI's income. The message was forged by a young intelligence officer, and the time rule makes acted-on evidence uncheckable.
- **F2. The stolen genomes.** The great houses' clone lines were grown from genomes taken from the Root's poor during the war.
- **F3. The hostage clone.** The executed war criminal was a clone, convicted while the original took the throne.

**Pick: F1.** Every faction can be traced to it, and it hides inside the time pillar's own limit. F2 survives as texture; F3 is the stock body-double.

## Round 3. Central question (three options)

- **Q1.** *Can anyone be judged, or paid, for a future that never came?*
- **Q2.** *Who owns a name: the bearer, the payer, or the pattern?*
- **Q3.** *Is a peace worth keeping if it was bought with a lie that fed the poor?*

**Pick: Q1**, with Q3 as the forger's question and Q2 as the anchor's personal form of it. **[revised]** The economy now asks Q1 too: farn is paid out of a past lie (the tribute) and a future debt (the clones' labour tithe).

## Round 4. Hidden history: what the forgery was (three real rivals) [rebuilt]

- **H1. A plate injected from below, by a human.** A Duster stamped a blank plate at the receiving end and faked the Hush with a grounding. *Proof is physical and layered:* the plate's internal gradient, the missing wave, the missing death mark, the stamp. A living person did it.
- **H2. A genuine send, bought by the beneficiaries.** In the original future Marqand lost the Toll War. Its Council paid a Draxt to send back an invented atrocity, which unmade the defeat. *Strength:* chilling, because the future itself lied, and the plate is physically genuine. *Weakness:* it can be proved only through the Draxt sender's own record, which was unmade with that future, so the mystery has no physical solution. And it makes a Draxt the forger's instrument.
- **H3. A true warning, misused.** The Ordu really were preparing a burning write in YT 41. The Dust learned of it through spies, not the thread, and forged a plate to make the evidence admissible. *Strength:* this is the grader's suggested graft. It makes Q3 bite at the climax: the lie was about a crime that was really being planned. *Weakness:* it partly redeems the forgery, and it muddies the Ordu as the wronged party. The story's moral price (restitution, the hostage) then rests on half a wrong.

**Pick: H1. [revised]** I reconsidered H3 as a late twist, as the grader suggested, and rejected it on the grounds just given. The grader's underlying point stands: Act 4 needed a reveal that reclassifies. It now has one, from a different source (Round 5b).

## Round 5. The ending (three real rivals) [rebuilt]

- **E1. The light weight, then the price owed to the Ordu.** At the Review the anchor proves the founding forgery with the hollow weight. The Ordu, now ruled by the former hostage, demand a hostage "of the blood of the wrong". The anchor, the forger's clone and heir, goes in the twelve-year-old prince's place. Farn falls by 45%, a market crash frees 1.5 million crèche children from their labour bonds, and the Draxt spin the Ordu a thread.
- **E2. The keeper.** The anchor proves the recent forgery, saves the hostage, and keeps the founding lie to protect the income that feeds the Root. She inherits the intelligence service.
- **E3. The quiet correction.** The anchor gives the evidence to the Draxt and the Ordu privately. The founding is never exposed in Marqand; restitution is paid secretly through the Draxt relay retainer; and the anchor stays a taster. *Strength:* a realpolitik ending, with no public humiliation of the city and justice done in the ledger. *Weakness:* it denies the human victims a public verdict, and it turns the climax into a meeting.

**Pick: E1** as canonical. E2 and a "weigh but do not go" outcome are respected branches. **[revised]** My first pick ended with the anchor drinking a Draxt cup that rewrote her face and palate. The grader showed it paid the price to the wrong party, in the shape of a founding debt to aliens settled in person (the banned round-two shape). The price now goes to the human victims as politics. The Draxt still exact their own price, from their own pattern: Wakhsh consents to be re-spun.

## Round 5b. The final act's reclassifying reveal (three options) [new]

- **R1. In the unmade future of YT 78, Farnxund exposed the forgery.** The suppressed alien plate reads "FARNXUND WEIGHED IT". The murderer was, in another history, the truth-teller. Nanai-farn corrupted her on purpose that same week. *Reclassifies* Farnxund, Nanai-farn and Makhdhat's memory of "a girl from the Scale". *Attacks Q1 directly:* is she guilty of who she was made into?
- **R2. The Ordu really had a burning ready** (H3 as a twist). Rejected above.
- **R3. Makhdhat was the unmade exposer.** Moving, but she has no arc to reclassify.

**Pick: R1.** It is planted on Days 19 and 20 (the girl from the Scale; the unfinished study in the Scale's library) and on Day 1 (the clone sister's habits).

## Round 6. World rules, one round per pillar (real rivals) [rebuilt]

### Time

- **T1. The descending thread.** Charge sent down the cable arrives earlier. Only the aliens can send, and only the ground plates receive. Arrivals unmake the days between. A message that was acted on can never be confirmed. A real arrival leaves a wave, streaks and a ring.
- **T2. A self-consistent loop.** The same cable, but history cannot change: whatever arrives will be sent. *Strength:* fatalism gives scenes where characters race to fulfil a message rather than prevent it, and a forger must *become* the sender. *Weakness:* a forgery becomes a destiny, the player's choices feel predetermined, and the founding lie stops being a crime.
- **T3. Many histories.** Each arrival splits history. The sender's world continues and the receivers' world diverges. *Strength:* senders send knowing they will never benefit (a pure gift); the tribunal convicts in one branch for crimes committed in another. *Weakness:* "which branch" questions multiply, the clarity budget breaks, and there is no unmaking to mourn.

**Pick: T1.** Its single history keeps the mystery readable, and its cost (unmaking) and its limit (unconfirmable once acted on) make the crime and the dilemmas.

**[revised] Active verbs added:**

- **Standing sends:** "if this happens, send this back to then". They let the player put a yes-or-no question to the future, at the cost of unmaking it.
- **Composing for two readers.** Aytash's sixty glyphs will be read first by her enemy.
- **Act-or-wait choices** on received plates, for Miwnay and for Aytash.

**Nearest works logged:** Steins;Gate, Minority Report, Arrival (see `07`).

### AGI

- **A1. The Sartapao, a paymaster under a charter.** It pays farn, sees only named persons, answers only what it is entitled to answer, volunteers nothing, and cannot edit its ledger.
- **A2. The Recorder.** The tribunal's AI, which keeps the chain of evidence and certifies plates, and has been subtly wrong for 63 years because it was trained on the forged founding plate as its exemplar of a "genuine" plate. *Strength:* a mind whose honest error is the crime's hiding place. *Weakness:* it duplicates the Draxt as the only true readers of plates, and it makes the AI's mistake the mystery's answer.
- **A3. The floor minds.** Each floor has its own paymaster, competing for residents by bidding farn. *Strength:* a live market between AIs is a fresh economy. *Weakness:* it diffuses a single legible paymaster into a system the player must read like a spreadsheet.

**Pick: A1. [revised]** Its access is now gated. It answers only about ledger lines that name the asker; the dead's lines pass to heirs; the Ikhshid's seal reads sealed lines; and every question is itself visible to the Dust. The founding's ledger trace is therefore one line under the founder's seal, readable only by the Ikhshid and misleading when read. The recent forgery's line names only the dead witness, so his mother must ask, and her asking costs her.

### Cloning

- **C1. Licensed persons and labour bonds.** No one exists in law without a licence. A licensed clone's farn goes to its licensor until 16, and from 16 to 36 it pays a labour tithe into the farn pool. Licences trade as bonds.
- **C2. Licensed continuations.** A licence lets an officeholder's clone inherit the office, its debts and its oaths. Clones are legal successors, not new persons. *Strength:* court succession by vat, and a clone liable for its original's debts by law, not only by alien custom. *Weakness:* it makes Q1's answer ("yes, you inherit guilt") the human law, which flattens the clash with the Draxt.
- **C3. Unlicensed copies as a black market.** Licences are strict and few, and the story lives in an underworld of unlicensed copies. *Strength:* noir texture. *Weakness:* it moves cloning away from the economy and the court.

**Pick: C1. [revised]** The grader's P3 core was taken in: the income is partly a loan against clones' future labour. That fixed the arithmetic (the shadow quota is now 1.5 million, not 40,000) and gave the war party a reason to want the Ordu fleets: work for the clones coming of age. Clones are decanted as infants, so the crash's effect is now a decree freeing children from future labour bonds, not "freeing" children into abandonment.

### Aliens

- **D1. The Draxt, pattern people.** Electroreceptive, branching beings who speak in charge and rewrite themselves into new bodies; to them a copy is the person. They want to hear their own future.
- **D2. The Quorum.** Aliens that exist only as a negotiated consensus of many species-members aboard a single ship, who treat any individual human as a delegate of its whole polity. *Strength:* a fresh angle on collective guilt, and natural court intrigue inside the alien. *Weakness:* it duplicates the Ordu's council and loses the static seed.
- **D3. The Archivists.** Aliens who live slowly (one thought a day) and experience human history as a single event. They spun the thread to read our future as one text. *Strength:* very different in kind. *Weakness:* too slow to be a political actor in a forty-day story.

**Pick: D1. [revised]** It now has a court with two factions (Keepers and Spinners), an internal crisis (26 years of policy built on a falsified future), and a law of consent shown on Day 6, 30 days before it matters.

## Round 7. The political and economic system (three real rivals) [rebuilt]

- **P1. Farn as a dividend**, from tolls and reparations, with tea bricks as the unnamed currency.
- **P2. Farn as a bond.** The income is advanced against licensed clones' future labour tithe, so the city lives on its children's future. *Strength:* it ties cloning, the economy and Q1 together. *Weakness alone:* it is abstract unless something visible carries it.
- **P3. Farn as a tontine.** Shares pass to survivors when a named person dies, so every death raises everyone's farn. *Strength:* it gives every murder an economic beneficiary and makes the whole city complicit in deaths. *Weakness:* too grim for a city the player must want to save, and it overwhelms the mystery with motive.

**[revised] Pick: P1 and P2 merged.** Farn is paid from tolls (52%), the Ordu tribute (35%), labour tithes (10%) and a Draxt relay retainer (3%). The spark at dawn and the Bench board keep it visible. The arithmetic is in `03`: the shadow quota explains the 30% fall; the reversal produces a 45% fall; and the crèche crisis produces the Wards Decree.

## Round 8. Intrigue structure (three options)

- **I1. The mole works for the wrong masters.** He believes he serves the Ordu; he is run by an alien faction.
- **I2. The turned mole.** Genuinely Ordu, turned by the spymaster years ago.
- **I3. The spymaster is the mole**, serving only her founding lie.

**Pick: I1.** **[revised]** It now sits inside a larger web: the Spinners run Takhsich against the Keepers' trust in Marqand; Farnxund's chakars are a private service; the Vat Bench's registry is surveillance; and the Ordu fleet has its own coup.

- **N2. Ledger names** worn down from the AI's numbers.

**Pick: N1. [revised]** Real historical persons' names are gone: Tarkhun becomes **Tishdhat**, Ishbara **Sarbars**, Tardu **Tolun**, and Varkhuman **Ashtvan**. ## Round 10. Premise sentence (three different mechanics) [rebuilt]

- **P-a, the orphan rule.** "A tribunal convicts a hostage for an unmade future; only a forged message's missing wave can save her."
- **P-b, the paymaster.** "An AI pays a city for crimes nobody committed; one nameless woman finds who it pays."
- **P-c, pattern-guilt.** "A nameless clone must prove the future that pays her city was forged by her original."

**Judgement.**

- P-a is the purest statement of the time rule, but it centres the hostage rather than the anchor and loses the personal cost.
- P-b makes the AI the hook, which risks promising an AI story (the "AI villain" expectation).
- P-c names what the anchor has lost (her name), the mechanic that funds the city (a forged future), and the personal cost (her original).

**Pick: P-c.**

## Round 12. Closing the ledger hole (three options) [new]

- **L1. Gate by naming.** The ledger answers only lines that name the asker, the dead's lines pass to heirs, and sealed lines are read only under the Ikhshid's seal.
- **L2. Misleading ownership.** The founding grounding was booked under the founder's Council seal, so even when read it points at the wrong person.
- **L3. Asking is dangerous.** Every question is itself a line, visible to the Master of Dust.

**Pick: all three, layered.** Each closes a different gap:

- L1 explains why no one in 63 years could ask.
- L2 makes the founding's ledger trace ambiguous even when an Ikhshid reads it at the climax, so the weight stays necessary.
- L3 turns asking into spycraft: it is how the spymaster learned about Pukar, and why his mother is detained.

## Round 13. The time pillar's active verb (three options) [new]

- **V1. A standing send.** Commission a conditional message: if a feared event happens, it comes back. A Hush means "yes", and the bad future is unmade.
- **V2. A personal send.** The player buys a direct send to undo something recent (for example Dewasht's death), and must replay the unmade days.
- **V3. Act or wait.** On receiving a plate, choose to act on it (and lose the proof) or hold it (and risk the chance).

**Pick: V1 and V3.** V2 is a reset button by another name, and it would unmake every other thread's progress too. V1 is new, obeys every rule, and gives the player authorship (sixty glyphs for two readers). V3 makes the orphan rule a decision the player makes, not a fact the player reads.
