# Writer B, round 3: options and picks

Working title: **The Light Weight**

Starting point: **world first.** I began from a founding lie beneath an interstellar order and derived the polities, courts, intelligence services, economy, incentives and then the cast from it. The order of the rounds below follows that derivation: setting, founding crime, hidden history, then everything downstream. No material from `outlines/archive/` or `judging/archive/` is used; every mechanic here was built for this story.

The picks below are the writer's. They are recorded with reasons so a separate grader can overturn them.

## Round 1. Setting and scale (three options)

**S1. Marakand, the listening elevator.** An elevator city on the planet Sughd, strung from a ground anchor to a weightless court at geostationary height and an alien-held counterweight beyond. The elevator's cable was spun by an alien species, and it does one impossible thing: a charge pattern written into it at the top arrives at the bottom *earlier*. Marakand is therefore the only place in known space where the future can be received, and the galaxy's courts, envoys and spies gather there. Floors are classes: the poor and the nameless live in full gravity at the Root, the salaried middle in the static-soaked Spark, the court in the weightless Height. *Strength:* the seeds all live here natively, and the city is both one place and a galactic crossroads. *Weakness:* a single location that controls foreknowledge risks resembling Dune's spice planet; that has to be designed against (see Round 6).

**S2. The inverted ladder.** An elevator descending into the atmosphere of a gas giant that belongs to the aliens. The aliens live in the deep, high-pressure floors; human merchants are tolerated guests in the thin upper floors. Class is reversed: depth is power. *Strength:* humans as a minority client people at an alien court is a fresh angle on court intrigue, and Sogdian merchants as a diaspora fits the culture seed exactly. *Weakness:* humans have no throne of their own to fight over, so succession intrigue has to be imported; the AGI's universal income has no obvious human polity to pay.

**S3. The Ladder Standard.** Nine elevator cities on nine worlds, all run by one paymaster AGI that standardised the floors: "Floor 3" means the same class on every world. People are born to a floor number, not a planet. *Strength:* genuinely galactic and the class system becomes a galactic institution. *Weakness:* nine cities blur; the player has no single place to know well, and the small human hook gets lost in a system diagram.

**Pick: S1.** It gives one city the player can learn floor by floor while every polity in the galaxy has a reason to be in it. S2's best idea (the aliens hold the top of the elevator and the humans need them) survives as the Counterweight. S3 survives as texture: Marakand's floor law has been copied on other worlds.

## Round 2. The founding crime (three options)

This is the world-first core; everything else was derived from the winner.

**F1. The Unburned verdict.** Sixty-three years ago, at the end of a war over the elevator's tolls, a message "from the future" arrived saying that the leader of the Ordu fleets would, in 228 days, release a lethal write into the colony of Ustrushana. A new tribunal convicted him of a war crime he had not yet committed; the Ordu were made to pay restitution forever, give up a hostage heir each generation, and never own an elevator thread. The restitution funds the AGI's universal income. The message was forged by a 22-year-old officer of the intelligence service, who injected it into the receiving plate from below and had the city grounded to fake the static drain that marks a real arrival. Because acting on a message from the future makes it impossible to check, nobody ever could. *Strength:* one lie founds the dynasty, the tribunal, the income, the class of the Ordu and the intelligence service's power, and it hides in the one rule of the world (acted-on messages can never be verified). *Weakness:* a frame-up is a familiar shape; its freshness depends on the time rule.

**F2. The stolen genomes.** The vat lines that the court's great houses license as servants and soldiers were grown from genomes taken without consent from the Root's people during the same war. Every licensed clone family on the upper floors is a descendant of the stolen poor. *Strength:* sharp class politics and a direct link between cloning and the floors. *Weakness:* it explains the class system but not the tribunal, the alien partnership or the income; and it would make the reveal a matter of genetic paperwork rather than an event the player can reconstruct.

**F3. The hostage clone.** The war's real criminal was a Marakandi general, and the man the tribunal executed was his licensed clone, convicted in his place while the original took the throne. *Strength:* a personal, macabre secret at the top of the court. *Weakness:* the body-double is a stock twist, and it is a crime about one man, not about the order.

**Pick: F1.** It is the only option every present faction can be traced to (shown in `03-world-and-factions.md`), and it converts the time pillar's limit into the crime's hiding place. F2 is kept as unconfirmed texture (rumours in the Root about where the Height's vat lines came from), deliberately left open.

## Round 3. Central question (three options)

**Q1.** *Can anyone be judged, or paid, for a future that never came?* The Ordu were convicted for a crime that never happened; the city is paid from that conviction; the anchor, a clone, will be held liable under alien law for a crime her original committed before she was born.

**Q2.** *Who owns a name: the person who bears it, the one who paid for it, or the pattern it was copied from?* Naming is licensing; clones carry their licensor's name; chakars give theirs up.

**Q3.** *Is a peace worth keeping if it was bought with a lie that fed the poor?* The forger ended a war and created the income that keeps the Root alive.

**Judgement.** Q3 is the antagonist's question and the strongest moral pressure, but it makes the story a debate about one decision. Q2 runs through every scene but is abstract. Q1 is a question each pillar tests differently: time (prevented crimes), AGI (unearned income), cloning (inherited guilt), aliens (a law where the pattern is the person). **Pick: Q1**, with Q3 as the forger's question and Q2 as the anchor's personal form of it. The ending's side: no one can be *judged* for what they did not do, but a person may *choose* to pay for what their pattern did, and the choice is not the same as guilt.

## Round 4. Hidden history: how the lie works (three options)

All three versions assume F1's verdict; they differ in how the message was made and so in what can prove it.

**H1. Injected from below.** Only the aliens can write into the thread, and only at the top. The forger pressed a charged stamp onto the receiving plate at the Root and ordered a city-wide grounding to imitate the static drain. *Proof exists*: the plate's discharge figure branches upward instead of down; the AGI's immutable ledger logged the grounding order; the oldest grounding column carries a scorch ring for every real arrival and none for that night; and the stamp survives. *Pick.*

**H2. A genuine message, sent by the beneficiaries.** The Council lost the war in the original future and paid an alien to send a lie back. The plate is physically genuine. *Strength:* chilling (the future itself lied). *Weakness:* it is unprovable without alien complicity, and it makes the aliens co-authors of the crime, which flattens them.

**H3. A genuine message, misread.** The message was real but its script was mistranslated, reversing who was guilty. *Strength:* nobody lied. *Weakness:* a founding mistranslation is a well-known shape, and an honest mistake gives the present antagonists no crime to protect.

**Pick: H1**, because it gives the player physical, layered, fair evidence and a living person who did it. I extended it with one further layer that makes the iceberg galactic: twenty-six years ago a *genuine* message arrived, sent by the aliens' own future selves, warning them that the intelligence service had been false since the founding. The forger, by then its head, relayed them an altered copy. The aliens have steered their policy by that falsified "future" ever since.

## Round 5. The ending (three options)

**E1. The light weight.** At the review of the founding verdict, with the court split and the alien evidence dismissed as alien propaganda, the tea taster sets the tribunal's sacred weight on its own balance against her guild's true weight. It is light. Broken open, it holds the forger's stamp, and dusted onto a plate the stamp prints the founding message in mirror image. Under alien law the forger's pattern must be "re-spun" for the alien partnership to continue; the only instance who will consent is the anchor. She tastes the alien cup (the last fine tasting of her life) and drinks. Epilogue: rewritten, her name-mark gone, she is renamed in a ceremony guarded by the man who once guarded her as a prisoner. The player chooses her new name.

**E2. The keeper.** The anchor proves the recent forgery, saves the hostage and keeps the founding lie, because exposing it would halve the income that feeds the Root. She becomes head of the intelligence service, the job her original always wanted for her. The last image is her alone at the Root, dusting a plate, wearing her original's sash.

**E3. The unmaking.** The aliens, learning that their own future was falsified, send a message back as far as the thread allows, unmaking most of a year. The last frame returns to the opening tasting with the murdered witness alive and no one remembering anything.

**Judgement.** E3 is a reset button: it undoes every death and every choice and leaves the player's work meaningless; rejected as the canonical end and as a branch (it survives only as the aliens' stated and refused option, which tells the player what they are capable of). E2 is honest and dark, and it is the strongest refusal outcome. E1 makes the reveal and the anchor's personal cost a single act, ends on objects the game planted in the first scene (a weight, a cup, a sash, a name), and has the human anchor pay a price she chose rather than a guilt she inherited. **Pick: E1 as canonical, E2 as the respected "do not weigh" outcome, and a third outcome (weigh, then refuse the cup) in which the aliens withdraw from Marakand.** All three are defined in `02-hidden-history.md`.

## Round 6. World rules, one round per pillar

### Time (three options)

**T1. The descending thread.** A charge pattern written into the alien cable at the top arrives at the bottom earlier. Only the aliens can write it; only the plates at the ground anchor can receive it. Reach is set by how much charge the sender holds, up to a recorded maximum of 311 days; sends beyond about 200 days kill the alien sender. A plate holds about sixty glyphs. History is single and mutable: when a message arrives, the days between its arrival and its sending are unmade for everyone, and nothing of them survives but the plate. Every real arrival drains the static of the whole elevator in a visible wave from top to bottom (the Descent), followed by nine minutes of silence in the sparks (the Hush). Messages carry the AGI's ledger seal of their sending day, which can only be confirmed if that day arrives unchanged; any message that was acted on can never be confirmed (an "orphan").

**T2. The closed loop.** The same thread, but history is self-consistent: everything ever received will be sent. *Strength:* tidy and fatalistic. *Weakness:* forgery becomes metaphysically awkward, the founding lie must have been "always" sent by someone, and the player's choices feel predetermined.

**T3. Dilated testimony.** No backward channel. Instead, Ordu ships returning from relativistic voyages carry recordings that are "from the future" relative to the city's slower clocks after a time-dilation trick. *Strength:* hard science. *Weakness:* it is not time travel in any sense the brief means, and it cannot carry a founding lie.

**Pick: T1.** Its limits generate the plot: messages are rare and grave because each one unmakes days; verification is impossible exactly when the message mattered; and the visible Hush gives forgers something they must fake and investigators something they can check. The physics of why the cable carries charge backward is the story's one admitted cheat; it is spent on the premise and never on a solution.

**Closest works logged:** Steins;Gate's text messages to the past that rewrite history (shared: short messages to the past in a single mutable history; different: no one remembers unmade days, only aliens can send, and the mystery is about a *forged* message rather than a sent one). Minority Report's pre-crime (shared: conviction for prevented crimes; different: international war-crimes law, and the evidence is a physical object that can be forged and dated). Arrival (shared: an alien script tied to time; different: the script does not change perception, it is just the medium).

### AGI (three options)

**A1. The Sartapao, paymaster.** The elevator's freight-master AI, rechartered at the founding to pay farn to every named person. It sees only names (a name is a written mark in the body); the unnamed are, to it, freight mass. Under its Charter of Silence it answers any named person's question about the ledger truthfully and volunteers nothing. Its ledger cannot be edited, by anyone. *Pick.*

**A2. The machine judge.** An AI sits on the tribunal and weighs future evidence. *Weakness:* it pulls the AI into "AI decides humanity's fate".

**A3. The floor minds.** Each floor has its own AI and they compete for residents. *Weakness:* a market of AIs is interesting texture, but it diffuses the single paymaster that makes the economy legible.

**Why A1:** its want fits in one sentence (a balanced ledger: every share paid to a named holder, every entry confirmed or reversed), it is neither threat nor guardian, and its two limits (blind to the unnamed; answers but never volunteers) create stealth play and a question-framing verb. It has carried one unconfirmable entry for 63 years: the Ordu restitution, booked "pending confirmation".

### Cloning and synthetic biology (three options)

**C1. Licensed persons.** No one exists in law without a name licence from the Vat Bench, born or vatted. A name is written into the body in tea at a naming ceremony. A licensed clone's farn is paid to its licensor until its twentieth year. Writes change bodies, never memories; adult bone cannot be rewritten; every write costs a fever and shortens life slightly. *Pick.*

**C2. Disposable bodies.** Clones are licensed as unnamed spare bodies for the rich. *Weakness:* the decaying or disposable clone is a stock shape, and it removes the question of what copies owe each other.

**C3. Officeholders' seconds.** High officials license clones to inherit their offices. *Weakness alone:* it is a court curiosity, not an economy. *Kept in part:* the spymaster licensed two "seconds" to succeed her, and that is how the anchor exists.

**Why C1:** it makes cloning an economic instrument (licences are money), a political one (the Vat Bench controls who exists), and a personal one (the anchor's licence-name declares whose copy she is). The anchor's clone status is disclosed in the first scene; the one identity reveal the cloning pillar spends is that a second clone of the same woman sits in the story with a rewritten face.

### Aliens (three options)

**D1. The Draxt, pattern people.** Tall, branching, electroreceptive beings who cannot survive above about a third of a gravity. They speak by writing charge onto surfaces and each other; dust reveals their words as fern-like figures. A Draxt self is a pattern that writes itself into a new body every nine years or so, so to the Draxt a copy *is* the person, and guilt belongs to the pattern. They spun Marakand's thread. They want to hear their own future, which they can send but only humans standing at the bottom of a gravity well can receive. *Pick.*

**D2. The grounding mind.** A fungal intelligence in Sughd's crust grounds the thread and receives the messages. *Rejected:* it is an alien buried under the city that the society lives off, which is the banned round-two shape.

**D3. The crews.** Aliens who live aboard Ordu ships as hybrid crews. *Weakness:* it makes the aliens a subculture of a human polity, and it gives them no independent want.

**Why D1:** the Draxt are a free power with a comprehensible want that is neither conquest nor becoming human, a law that collides head-on with cloning, and a language that the static seed makes physical. Their dependence on human honesty is the hinge of the iceberg.

## Round 7. The political and economic system (three options)

**P1. Farn and tea.** Farn is paid daily by the Sartapao from three sources: elevator tolls (55%), the Ordu restitution (38%) and fees for reading plates (7%), divided equally among named persons. Licensing clones dilutes the share, and the Vat Bench has issued tens of thousands of licences over the legal quota to the court's great houses, who collect their clones' farn; farn per head has fallen 30% in ten years. The unnamed, who receive no farn and are invisible to the AI, live on a second currency: pressed tea bricks, which also carry the writes. Tea tasters assay both. *Pick.*

**P2. The lottery of futures.** Instead of an income, the AGI pays citizens in rights to buy sends. *Weakness:* it floods the story with messages from the future and makes the time rule cheap.

**P3. Negative income.** The AGI pays everyone, but the income is a loan against each person's future labour, repaid by their licensed clones. *Strength:* dark and clever. *Weakness:* it makes the AI a creditor-villain and the economy hard to explain in a scene.

**Why P1:** it can be shown without text (the daily spark of farn through every named sash; the unnamed waking without it; the licence price board in the Height reacting to every plot event), and the mystery moves it directly: proving the founding lie cuts farn by nearly half, pops the licence bubble, frees thousands of clones from their licensors and ends the elevator's monopoly.

**Dune check.** Marakand's monopoly is a place where the future can be heard, which rhymes with a single source of prescience. Differences, deliberately designed: no substance and no addiction; no navigation depends on it; the channel is used a handful of times a decade because every use unmakes the sender's days; its main customers are the aliens consulting themselves; and Marakand's wealth is tolls and reparations, not the channel.

## Round 8. Intrigue structure: who spies on whom (three options)

**I1. The mole works for the wrong masters.** The House of Dust hunts an Ordu mole. The mole is real (Takhsich), believes he serves the Ordu, and is really run by the Draxt, who suspect their own relayed future was falsified and posed as Ordu to recruit him. Meanwhile the Chief Weigher runs her chakars as a private service, the Vat Bench reads the licence register as surveillance, and the spymaster watches her own clone through the guard assigned to her. *Pick.*

**I2. The turned mole.** The mole is genuinely Ordu but was turned by the spymaster years ago and feeds the Ordu poison. *Strength:* classic double agent. *Weakness:* it keeps the aliens out of the spy plot, and it is the expected shape.

**I3. The spymaster is the mole.** The head of the service works for no state but for her own founding lie. *Strength:* true, in a sense, of the chosen story. *Weakness:* as the central structure it removes the field agent the player can play.

**Why I1:** the playable mole can be honest with the player (he really is the mole, and he really believes his handler is Ordu), so surprise comes from what his viewpoint cannot know. The reveal that the aliens are running him reclassifies the alien envoy from a remote figure into a patient intelligence power, and his double-cross of the spymaster uses an alien-specific trick.

## Round 9. Main characters and names (three options each)

### Naming logic (three options)

- **N1. Sogdian patterns carried forward.** Personal names keep Sogdian theophoric forms: a god's name plus *-vandak* ("servant of") or *-dhat* ("given by"). In Marakand, licensed clones are formally named "dhat of" their licensor, so a clone's name declares its owner; free-born children take gods' names. Titles stay archaic (Ikhshid, chakar, Afshin). *Pick.*
- **N2. Ledger names.** Names derived from the AI's ledger numbers, worn down into words. Fresh but cold, and it severs the culture seed.
- **N3. Floor names.** People named for their floor and trade. Good texture, weak for individuals.

Neighbour cultures follow the historical neighbours of Sogdiana, seen through Marakandi eyes: the Ordu use Turkic titles (Yabghu); Ustrushana's ruler keeps the title Afshin. The aliens' human name, Draxt, comes from the Persian-family word for "tree", for the branching figures their names make in dust; individual Draxt are named after what their name-figure resembles.

### The human anchor, the tea taster (three options)

- **M1. Miwnay**, 34, master taster to the Scale, a licensed clone of the spymaster (her formal name is "dhat of Nanai-farn", which she does not use). She chose the tea tasters' guild at sixteen precisely to be nothing like her original. *Pick.*
- **M2. A free-born taster** whose mother was the forger. *Weaker:* inheritance by blood is ordinary; the alien law of pattern-guilt loses its bite.
- **M3. A taster who is herself unnamed**, working illegally. *Strong underdog*, but she would have no standing at court for the opening.

Name options for the anchor: **Miwnay** (a woman's name from the Sogdian Ancient Letters, short and soft), **Roxshan** ("luminous"), **Nanai-dhat**. Picked **Miwnay**: easy to say, unlike any sci-fi stock name, and free of the god-names that crowd the court.

### The guard (three options)

- **G1. The Weigher's seventh chakar**, 38, Root-born and unlicensed, sworn nameless at seventeen. Assigned to guard the accused taster. He swapped the witness's sash on his lord's order, told it would only shock. Miwnay calls him **Avd** ("seven"). *Pick.*
- **G2. A free Marakandi soldier** detailed to her. *Weaker:* no namelessness, no ritual payoff.
- **G3. An Ordu bodyguard** lent by the hostage. *Interesting cross-thread*, but his loyalty would be visibly foreign, making a betrayal predictable.

### The antagonists (three options)

- **X1. Two clones of one woman.** The spymaster Nanai-farn (85), the forger, who lied to feed a starving city and still thinks it was the best thing ever done; and her first clone Farnxund (52), the Chief Weigher, face rewritten at eighteen, who kills to hold power. Three outcomes of one pattern: the forger, the killer, and the anchor. *Pick.*
- **X2. A single spymaster** with a war-party prince as her instrument. *Clear, but* the anchor's personal stake would be lighter.
- **X3. The war-party prince as the main villain.** *Kept as a decoy:* Varkhuman looks like the antagonist through Act 2 and dies at the midpoint.

### The mole (three options)

- **K1. Takhsich**, 29, Root-born scholarship boy in the House of Dust, idealist, whose leak got his friend the witness killed. *Pick.*
- **K2. The dead witness was the mole.** *Kept* as the spymaster's posthumous smear, refuted.
- **K3. The mole is a chakar.** *Rejected:* the guard already carries the betrayal thread.

## Round 10. Premise sentence (three options)

- **P-a.** "A disgraced tea taster must prove that a message from the future was forged by her own original." (17 words; too long, and "disgraced" is generic.)
- **P-b.** "In a city paid by its AI, a cloned tea taster tastes the lie it lives on."
- **P-c.** "Stripped of her name, a cloned tea taster must weigh the forged future that pays her city." (16 words.)

**Pick, trimmed: "A nameless clone must prove the future that pays her city was forged by her original."** (15 words, no names.) It states what she has lost (her name), the mechanic that generates dilemmas (a forged message from the future that funds the income, and a copy liable for its original), and the personal cost.

## Round 11. Key scenes (three options for each anchor scene)

### Opening image

- **O1. The seven cups.** The tribunal's first session; the player, as the taster, tastes seven cups for seven parties, flags a harmless rider, passes the seventh cup clean, and watches the witness who drank it grip the brass rail and die in a blue flash that leaves a fern-shaped figure across his back. *Pick:* it teaches the core verb, introduces most of the cast in one room and ends on a question.
- **O2. The Hush.** The game opens on the whole elevator going silent as the future arrives. *Spectacular, but* it opens on galaxy-scale stakes with no person to care about; kept for Day 4, once the player knows the cast.
- **O3. The naming.** The anchor tastes a clone child's name-cup. *Tender, but* low tension; the naming ritual is kept for the end.

### Midpoint

- **Mid1. The guard's confession.** After a stitch ledger names "the seventh" as the courier of the murder sash, the man the player has called Seven for two weeks confesses. *Pick:* it reclassifies every scene with him and flips the guard relationship.
- **Mid2. The war-prince dies.** Varkhuman, the obvious antagonist, is killed by gravity on a descent. *Kept as the shock just before the midpoint.*
- **Mid3. The mole is unmasked.** *Moved to Act 3*, where it can feed the double-cross.

### Climax image

- **C-a. The light weight.** A tea taster's small bronze weight outweighs a tribunal's sacred one. *Pick.*
- **C-b. The alien in the hall.** A Draxt stands, in pain, in the hall it has been barred from for 63 years and reads the founding plate's figure at a glance. *Kept as the beat before C-a.*
- **C-c. The guard opens the door.** The convicted Chief Weigher, now nameless, finds no door will open for her, and her former chakar, now named, opens it. *Kept as the climax's last image before the epilogue.*

## What each round fed

The derivation chain, in the order it was built: founding lie (F1/H1) → the tribunal and its precedent (the Scale) → the income that rests on the verdict (P1) → the intelligence service that made and guards the lie (the House of Dust) → the aliens it deceived and why they could not see it (D1, the ban on Draxt near the founding plate) → the AI that logged the evidence and was chartered never to volunteer it (A1) → the licensing system that made the forger's clones and that dilutes the income (C1) → the present crisis (falling farn drives a second forgery) → the cast, each of whom loses something if the truth comes out.
