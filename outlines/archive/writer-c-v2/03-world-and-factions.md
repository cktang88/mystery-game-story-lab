# World, rules and factions (revision 2)

## The world in one paragraph

**Dugutsuchi** ("land-land") is a dry, high-tilt planet whose seven-year year sends a band of spring, several hundred kilometres wide, crawling from pole to pole and back. Behind the band the summer burns the plain to glassy gravel, and the **scour**, a wall of dust thrown off the summer edge, erodes everything left on it. Ahead of the band the winter frost is still breaking. **Ichisugu** ("market-market") is a city of 31,000 people on about 4,000 buffalo-drawn wagons. It walks with the spring for ever as a forty-kilometre **crescent abreast**, sleeping in shifts and trading as it goes. Its founders were two Earth peoples bound by a treaty of mutual debt: Osaka merchants, who brought shop-houses, split tallies, puppet theatre and the abacus, and a Timbuktu charter of scholars and caravaneers, who brought caravan-craft, manuscript libraries, the annual replastering of walls and the herders' customs.

The crescent is a **moving oasis**: every night the hub drills wells into the thawing ground. The founders' old stone wellheads, eroding on the route and carved with a glyph that is not human, are the only fixed places in the world. Once in each half Great Walk, at the equator crossing, Ichisugu meets **Kitasugu**, a second caravan walking the other hemisphere, for a three-day market of the **silk road**: cloth, seed, buffalo bloodlines and news, traded across a line neither caravan otherwise crosses.

## The crescent (place as class)

| Part | Who | Look and sound |
|---|---|---|
| **The hub** (*Ido-kɔlɔn*, "well-well") | The twelve founding houses, their dead, the Exchange wagon, the Jurutigi, the night wells | Fresh water, cool air, indigo noren with white house marks, the clatter of Dōjima's abacus, the chanter's voice |
| **The shoulders** | Craft houses: the Kanté puppeteers, the House of Forms, the pigeon post, the library wagons | Lacquer, dust veils, pigeons circling the loft wagons |
| **The horns** | Herders (Sangaré, Ballo), carters, indebted families, the Juruntan | Water hauled by buffalo from the hub, grazed-out ground, the scour at their backs |

Position is set by **debt**, not by origin, and it moves. On every market day the Exchange **reshuffles** wagons by account: debtors move out toward the horns, creditors in toward the wells. Both founding peoples have hub houses (Tenmaya, Kagaya, Keïta-ya) and horn houses (Sangaré, Diarra). The horns' poverty is a structure of hereditary apprenticeship debts recorded on receipts, and the reshuffle makes it visible every sixth day.

*Why not front and rear:* a front-and-rear class column with insurgents attacking the front's sacred engine is the *Snowpiercer* geometry. The crescent abreast puts class on an axis of distance from water, makes the reshuffle a ritual of debt, and makes the scour a threat to the edges rather than to the back.

## Laws and customs derived from the rules

- **"The Market stops for no one, living or dying."** Broken wagons are stripped on the move. The dying are carried. The worst punishment is **to be left**: set down at the trailing edge with three days' water, in front of the scour.
- **Never halt in a scour.** A halted crescent in dust loses its horns.
- **Market day**, every sixth day: wagons are lashed into lanes and the crescent becomes a walking bazaar. Debts are settled before witnesses, court sits, the dead walk in their vessels and speak through the chanter, the Jurutigi trades, and the Exchange reshuffles. Once a year, the **replastering** market day renews the dead and stages the founding play.
- **Great debts are settled at market before witnesses.** This is the custom the hard-liners use to justify halting the crescent on D60 for an extraordinary market.
- **Audit** (*kanjō-aratame*): any member of a house may demand an audit of its books. Every record must be produced, and the auditor has sanctuary until the audit closes.
- **Life receipts**: split at birth. The house half goes to the Exchange vault, and the body half is worn wrapped at the throat. *One does not read one's own receipt.* At death the body half is burned with the body. A funeral waits until the half shows no shadows (the "waiting dead").
- **The treaty custom of names**: Japanese-founded houses give Mande given names, and Malian-founded houses give Japanese ones. Founders born on Earth keep unmixed names.
- **Shadow routing**: the pigeon post and many trades plan by foreseen marks.
- **Refused gifts are left at the Owner's feet**, never carried home.

## The cutting protocol (summary; the full table is in `02`)

Life receipts carry births, house debts, marriages, children, verdicts and sentences (never deaths). Court receipts carry only corrections to the court record. Debt receipts carry payments and settlement. Post receipts carry routings. The old stayer's receipt carried the "stayed" mark, cut by the stayer on the dying one's half. **A receipt shows only what someone will cut on it**, so Dōjima can read lives but not raids, storms or murders.

## Teaching order (anomaly, test, named rule, personal use, bill)

- **Receipts.** *Anomaly* (D1): Awa Kagaya's newborn receipt comes out short. *Test* (D3): Sayo's knife skids on a shadow, and the troupe's pay receipt will not burn in the cookfire until the mark is cut. *Named rule* (D4): Ume's "a receipt remembers forward only as far back as its split." *Personal use* (D7): the court receipt's D31 correction. *Bill* (D14): "was left."
- **Stay and leave glyph pair, and the bevel.** Taught on D1 at the guild chest (Sékou Ballo's active "stayed" mark: "cut it on your own and it says you were left"). Reinforced on D3 (pay receipt held toward herself: "now it says we were paid, not that we paid"). Seen as truth on D19 (Jūzaburō's passive mark). Deduced on D53. Paid off on D60.
- **Vessels.** *Anomaly* (D1): "this year the Mother rides." *Test* (D2): Sayo checks Vessel 12's feet. *Named rule* (D2): a copy gets the body, not the life. *Personal use* (D5): Uke's feet, and Sayo's own recognition. *Bill* (D13): a person was killed as property. *Second cycle:* D44 (sleepers smashed), D50 (Kaeshi wakes), D60 (Kaeshi passes as the Mother).
- **Dōjima.** *Anomaly* (D1): the Mother praises a merchant for a deal he has not yet made. *Test* (D10): two years' recordings of the Mother answer the same question differently. *Named rule* (D12): "The dead are replastered." *Personal use* (D16 onward): diffing. *Bill* (D50 to D55): the audit.
- **The Jurutigi.** *Anomaly* (D1): children's toys returned wrapped. *Test* (D25): cup unchanged, spindle wrapped, coin notched, rope hobbled; a merchant leaves his refused gift and the plates take it in. *Named rule* (D25): it answers in what it gives back, and it keeps what is left. *Personal use* (D25): ledger recall. *Bill* (D31): the broken count.

**White space on purpose:** the eleventh founder's vessel line, ended in YM 90 for reasons the Nakama will not record; the 300 cryo-buffalo that never thawed; Bakary's widow's second marriage.

**Erosion, as theme and mechanic:** the scour erodes the plain and the creature; replastering erodes the dead (the version-diff); the founding play erodes the truth year by year; the wellheads erode, yet keep the glyph; and the hub's foresight erodes with the plates (short receipts).

**Delivery order for lore:** place first (wellheads, bone arches, the Gully, which the crescent passes within a day's ride in the D50s); then object (receipts, the staff, the astrolabe, the stayer's receipt, Kitasugu cloth); then sound (abacus, chanter, Ume's herding songs, the diary recited by Dōjima); and only then a little in-world text with a named author (the Juruntan pamphlet; a clerk's note).
