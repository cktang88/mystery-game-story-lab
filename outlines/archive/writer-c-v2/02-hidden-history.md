## Units and calendar

- **YM** (Year of the Market) counts standard years from landfall, YM 0. The present is **YM 214**.
- The planet **Dugutsuchi** has an extreme axial tilt and a year of about seven standard years (a "Great Walk": pole to pole and back). The livable spring band is several hundred kilometres wide and moves about 12 km a day. Ichisugu walks with it as a **crescent of wagons abreast** across the band, about forty kilometres from horn to horn, bowed forward. The **hub** (*Ido-kɔlɔn*, "well-well") at the centre of the bow is where the night wells are drilled and where the dead's houses and the Jurutigi walk. The trailing **horns** are farthest from water and nearest the **scour**, the dust storms thrown off the summer edge behind the band. Nothing in the time rule depends on the planet's year, its day or any cycle.
- **Market day** falls every sixth day. The game runs from **D1** (a market day) to **D60**, the day the creditor stops (the **Edge**), with a coda to D67. Market days in the game: D1, D7, D13, D19, D25, D31, D37, D43, D49, D55, D61, D67. On every market day the Exchange **reshuffles** wagon positions by debt: debtors move outward toward the horns, creditors inward toward the wells.

## The four mechanisms, as they truly work

### Receipts (time)

The Jurutigi sheds one bone plate each market day, about sixty a year. A plate is cart-wheel sized and laminated. Split along its layers, **one plate yields about three hundred slivers**, and each sliver split lengthwise is one receipt pair, so the Exchange has about 18,000 receipts a year to ration. Since YM 190 the plates have thinned, so new slivers are shorter and fewer, and births get the shortest.

*A notch cut into either half of a receipt, while the Jurutigi lives and while both halves exist, has always been on both halves, back to the moment of the split.* Until it is cut, a notch shows as a **shadow notch**: visible, hard, impossible to cut through or erase.

1. **Reach:** marks travel back only to the receipt's own split.
2. **Fixity:** every shadow notch will be cut by someone before the Edge. Nothing shown can be prevented. The marks fix *what* is cut, never *who* cuts it.
3. **Capacity:** marks need room; a life receipt is a forearm long.
4. **Indestructible until settled:** a half with an uncut shadow survives fire, acid and hammers until that notch is cut.
5. **A pair lives while both halves exist.** If one half is destroyed (possible only once it has no uncut shadows), the other half stops receiving marks; marks already cut remain.
6. **(hidden)** Marks travel back only while the Jurutigi lives. After the Edge, receipts are plain bone, and a mark cut on one half appears on the other only if the halves are held together and cut across, as on an ordinary tally.

**Dōjima foresees only what someone will cut.** A raid, a murder or a storm is invisible to it unless a clerk will record it.

**The Juruntan** ("the debtless") refuse to report anything to clerks and cast away their body halves, so nothing is ever cut on their receipts, and Dōjima sees nothing ahead for them. They are unforeseeable by refusal. Clones are unforeseeable by nature: they have no receipt at all.

### Notation (locked)

Value notches (wide for tens, narrow for units), house marks (Osaka shop-sign glyphs and Malian clan brands) and act-marks. For an act-mark, the steep face of the V toward the receipt's head is **active** (*stayed*, *paid*); toward the foot it is **passive** (*was left*, *was paid*). Clerks cut from the head side. **Anyone cutting her own half, held toward herself, cuts it reversed**, so an active act on one's own receipt reads as passive. That is why the herders' rule says the stayer cuts on the dying one's half: "Cut it on your own and it says you were left."

### The Exchange mind, Dōjima (AGI)

Built YM 5 to 9 at Ofusa Tenmaya's order. It thinks on a hall-sized abacus of buffalo-bone beads that clatters audibly. Its directive is the first article of the Tenmaya house code: **"What the receipts show, the Market shall make so."** Its want: every account settled, ledger and world in agreement. It reads every house half. It has no hands, so it acts only through people who obey the voice of the dead, which it gives through a human chanter (*tayū*). Each year, on the **replastering** market day, every dead founder is renewed, and houses pay to adjust their dead. It never falsifies a receipt. It is bound by the Osaka house custom of **audit** (*kanjō-aratame*): any member of a house may demand an audit of the house's books; the auditor is protected by audit sanctuary until the audit closes; and every record must be produced, including the dead's sealed papers. Dōjima memorised Ofusa's paper diary in YM 41; the paper itself was burned with her body.

### Vessels (cloning)

Clone bodies of dead founders, grown in the House of Forms in four years and kept **in twilight** under sedation: not unconscious, absorbing years of market sound and motion, unable to move themselves. They serve about twenty years. On market day three hooded puppeteers move a vessel in a harness (head and right hand, left hand, feet) while the chanter speaks. Vessels have no receipt and no legal person; they are house property. A vessel inherits the body, including Ofusa's **red-green colour blindness** (X-linked, rare in women, recorded in her medical file and visible in the miscoloured star-pointers she painted on her brass astrolabe, kept in Tenmaya's relic case). It inherits no memory and no calluses. Woken slowly (Uke, over months) a vessel learns language as she wakes; woken fast (Vessel 14, over five days, at risk to her life) she surfaces with a twilight memory of everything her body was made to do.

### The Jurutigi (alien)

A house-sized walker of wind-polished bone plates on many short legs. It walks with the spring band, as its kind did for millennia; the eroded bone arches on the far plain are its kind's dead. It foresees along its own life and knows the day it will stop.

It speaks only by returning gifts transformed: **wrapped** = kept/accepted; **split** = shared; **notched** = owed; **hobbled** (a soft loop that lets an animal graze but not wander) = stay near, still free; **tethered** = bound (it never uses this); **unchanged** = refused; **broken** = ended.

**Refused gifts:** it hands them back unchanged. Market custom holds that taking back a refused gift is shameful, so givers leave refused gifts at its feet, and its plates take them in. **Ledger recall:** given something of a person, it returns the last thing that person *left with it*, whether that was kept or refused.

**Its own want (not about the caravan):** *to close the account it opened with the last of its own kind*: to have its old half-receipt carried on until it is joined to the missing half. That half was traded away long ago and now travels with the Kitasugu caravan; the creditor's kind's glyph is the Kitasugu emblem and is carved on the founders' wellheads. **What it asked of the caravan (its price):** someone who chooses to stay with it when it stops, and takes the old receipt on. It foresaw the humans' arrival and walked to meet them because it needed a carrier who could choose.

## Ending and its branches (refusal and inaction respected; receipts always true)

The past fixes *what* is cut; the present chooses *who* cuts it. Three marks can change cutter: the Gully mark, Sayo's own "was left," and the hobble-mark. Every other foreseen mark has the same cutter in every branch.

**Canon.** Sayo cuts the Gully mark (D59). She stays by choice. Kaeshi lifts the halt. Sayo cuts all three D60 marks and carries the old receipt.

**Branch A: the player refuses to cut the Gully mark.** Dōjima asks Nana Kōriya to cut it, and she does. History is identical; the machine's confession passes through a human hand.
