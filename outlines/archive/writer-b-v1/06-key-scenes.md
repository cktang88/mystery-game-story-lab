# Writer B: key scenes

Each sketch says what the player **does**, what they **see and hear**, what small detail it pays off, and what it plants. On-screen text is listed when there is any; the target is none beyond names, dates and the Tide Board.

---

## 1. Opening: The Spring Reckoning (Ch1)

**Does.** Walks a party of three out across the flats before dawn: plants the tide-stick ahead, listens (gullies gurgle louder when deep, the controller ticks with the tide clock), picks a line, waves the party on with a whistle. At the reef, finds the boy's hollow by its brass number and watches him lie down in it. Later, kneels in the gully and photographs a body against the rising water.

**Sees and hears.** Black sky going grey, the island's lights behind, a straw figure on the dike being lowered into the sluice to cheering while the dike bell rings. The bared Rib: a field of smooth domes and human-shaped holes like a sculpture garden turned inside out. The boy fits his hollow exactly; a hum comes up through the controller and the speakers like a bowed wire under the floor; nothing visible happens; he sits up crying and laughing. Curlews lift from the gully. A man face down. Jildou turns him: her father's face, older, shaved, with nicks on the jaw. The water is already at his ears.

**Text on screen.** Tide Board names (Okke Hylkes, your father; Marrit, your godmother; Tjalling Douwes, your father's friend; Inês Valadares, foreign owed). "PAID", stamped.

**Plants.** The witch effigy and the Witch's Gate (Ch9, Ch17). "Nobody crosses. Somebody stays." (Ch4, Ch16, Ch18). "Do you know your tide?" "I haven't got one." (epilogue). Blue clay, missing lamp, stuck boot (Ch12). The navel check (Ch9). The cut rope (red herring, Ch8).

---

## 2. The Crossing (Ch4)

**Does.** Walks her father across the flats in full dark with one lamp. The player can shine the lamp on the path or on his face; he talks more when it is on his face. At the hollow, holds the lamp up when he asks.

**Sees and hears.** Okke lying down in a hole that is exactly him. His face lit from below. The hum. He gets up, shivering, and laughs once.

**Dialogue (voiced, the whole scene).** "He said you'd find him. He said tell her I'm sorry about the beard." / "Nobody crosses." / "Somebody stays."

**Pays off.** Ch1's patter, now about her own father. **Plants.** The beard (Ch12: the player shaves him). Cut to black, then mud and breathing: the player is about to wake as the man they found dead.

---

## 3. Waking in the Mud (Ch5)

**Does.** Claws out of a hollow, naked, in sleet. A tide clock counts 40 minutes. Drags a fish-box lid over the hollow and kicks silt in. Steals a cassock from the church vestry and makes sure a ferryman sees him in it. Ducks behind a wall when a young man with Okke's walk passes the sluice whistling.

**Sees.** Grauwarf in 2001: orange sodium lamps, older cars, the gate with fresh paint. His own younger self, 22, laughing with a girl on the dike: Sjoukje.

**Text.** Three short documents the player carries as constraints: "2001 reef survey: no new hollows." "Police report: theft from vestry; man in cassock seen walking to ferry." "Okke Hylkes never saw this man before October 2004."

**The new verb in one line.** History is the guard: get caught by the past and the scene rewinds to the last checkpoint with the line, "That isn't how it happened."

---

## 4. Fog (Ch7)

**Does.** Steers an eel boat through fog by sound and compass toward a gully he knows from memory, against a tide clock. Grounds on a bar whatever line he takes. Wades. Finds her. Kneels. Holds her hand (the player holds a trigger; letting go is possible and nothing punishes it). Chooses what to say from three spoken options, each kind. Climbs back into the boat and stands in the searchlight when it comes.

**Sees and hears.** White fog, a red pilot's vest, the sound of water rising in a gully. Sjoukje: "Who are you?" If he tells her: "You've got his hands." The minidisc recorder's red light.

**Present half.** Jildou, at the red-rag post (only the player knows to dig there), finds a tin; plays a minidisc in her cottage with headphones on. Her mother's voice; a man's voice she knows. The player watches her face; no subtitles.

**Pays off.** The 2004 photograph from Ch2. **Plants.** The red-rag post and the trap line (Ch16).

---

## 5. The Voice in the Gully (Ch8)

**Does.** Two-handed puzzle across two characters: as Jildou, draws the tide curve for one night across the reef map using the tide table, her stick readings and gauge history; as Inês, turns a tuning dial and filters until sound rises out of the hum. Drags each sound to the place it happened.

**Sees and hears.** A drone of six centuries, then, as the curve locks: a spade in clay; footsteps; silence; footsteps; two voices, one muffled; then clearly, an old man's voice: "Out here there's no enemy. There's only the tide." Jildou pulls off the headphones. Inês does not ask.

**Pays off.** A line the player has heard Jildou mutter to herself on the flats in Ch1 and Ch3 without knowing where she got it. **Plants.** The muffled woman's voice (Ch13). The Promise and the Sob, which the player can now hear under everything.

---

## 6. Meeting Dona Inês (Ch8)

**Does.** As Inês, walks through a kitchen of tape reels toward an old woman by the window. Can sit, or not. Can take her hand, or not.

**Sees and hears.** The same voice, fifty years apart. Dona Inês touches her face to see it: "We had better teeth than I remembered." Then: "I remember deciding. If I tell you why, it becomes my reason, not yours."

**Pays off at the end** when the two pass on the flats without a word.

---

## 7. Midpoint: Lifting the Face (Ch9)

**Does.** As Femke, puts the same question to three versions of KERF: the current one refuses; v7 answers. As Jildou, mixes plaster on the reef in the dark with water creeping in, pours it into the open hollow, waits as the tide clock runs, and lifts. Then turns the cast over on the Attribution Board and drags names onto it: Femke? Marrit?

**Sees.** A face in white plaster: Femke, twenty-five years older. Femke stepping back. The player checks the belly of the cast: smooth. The board accepts "Marrit". A lamp approaching across the reef. Marrit looking at her own face in the plaster.

**Dialogue.** Femke: "That's me." Marrit: "I know. I've known since 2019."

**Pays off.** The navel rule (Ch1-2); the witch effigy (Ch1). **Plants.** The KERF log of the casting, read by the Diekgreve at dawn (Ch10's cause).

---

## 8. The Gate (Ch10)

**Does.** Runs along the dike at night in a gale, slipping between police; climbs down into the gate chamber; lifts a beam that will not lift.

**Sees and hears.** Water pouring through a cracked gate. Okke pinned under a counterweight. "Did it hold?" It didn't. He dies with the dike bell ringing because nobody has thought to stop it.

**Pays off.** Okke's habit, "the gate doesn't care who's on shift" (Ch3), and the supper he promised to stay for. **Plants.** The silted channel (Ch15).

---

## 9. The Last Night (Ch12)

**Does.** Shaves an old man's face in a cracked mirror (the player draws the razor). Lifts a stone slab with a pole. Digs blue clay out of a human-shaped hole with bare hands. Carries a warm stone through knee-deep water to the seventh trap. Walks back. Meets a lamp. Tries to pull a boot out of the mud and cannot.

**Sees.** "There you are," to the mirror. A stone that hums and glows faintly through the clay. Marrit's face behind a lamp. Her taking his lamp and pole and walking away until her light is a point. Dark water rising past his chest; the hum everywhere; under it a thin high tone.

**Dialogue.** "You're Okke Hylkes." "Not tonight." / "Out here there's no enemy. There's only the tide."

**Pays off.** Every Ch1 photograph: the shave, the clay, the boot, the missing lamp. **Plants.** The seventh trap (Ch16) for the player only.

---

## 10. The Gallery of Faces (Ch13)

**Does.** Sneaks through a hall of 38,000 white plaster faces at night to Marrit's office; opens a drawer; finds a lamp with a G scratched in its base. Then stands in the Gallery when Marrit comes in, and places the lamp on the plinth between them.

**Sees.** Rows of faces lit by one lamp. Marrit sitting on the step of the empty Kerf Nul plinth. She tells it plainly, short sentences, no tears.

**Dialogue.** "I killed one of us to keep eleven thousand alive. I didn't touch him. I let the flats do it. I haven't slept." Then, signing two papers: "If I'm going there, I want to hear what I did."

**Pays off.** Femke's "I couldn't have thrown the light away." **Plants.** KERF's hearing restored (Ch14).

---

## 11. The Sob: walking the founding in sound (Ch14)

**Does.** Walks a town that is not there: a white line-drawing rebuilt by KERF from how sound echoed off its walls 655 years ago. Moves toward voices to sharpen them; places each sound on a map of 1371.

**Sees and hears.** Outlines of houses filling with a roar of water; silence; outlines of survivors on a mound; a woman's voice the player knows, older and frightened, explaining patiently to men who laugh. Torches as bright smudges. Digging. A scream that every player has heard under every recording for fifteen hours and never recognised: the Sob.

**Witch's words (voiced, no subtitles needed).** "She'll sing. And they'll be free to go home. Free. Not taken."

**Cut to Marrit** in her cell with the sound coming through a speaker. She puts her hand flat on the floor where the hum comes up.

**Pays off.** "Mem" (Ch8, Ch11); the navel; the Promise; the Sob. **Plants.** The town's layout, which the player will need in Ch17.

---

## 12. Climax: The Seventh Trap and the Walk (Ch16)

**Does.** In the dark before the year's lowest tide, can go anywhere. The player who remembers walks to the red-rag post and checks seven traps in order. Then pilots the last crossing in history: a line of forty people with lamps across the bared reef, guards in front and behind, gullies to read by sound. When Marrit asks, holds out a hand. Walks her to the hollow. Kneels beside it.

**Sees.** Traps one to six empty, seven heavy. A warm stone in Jildou's hands; she laughs once like her father. The line of lamps across the Rib. Marrit's gloved hand in Jildou's bare one. Kerf Nul, open. Marrit lying down in it. It fits.

**Dialogue.** "Nobody crosses." "Somebody stays."

**Why it is the hinge.** The climax rests on two small earlier things: a child's habit of checking traps in order (Ch2), and a pilot's rule that anyone who asks is walked (Ch1). The win (the voice, the founding) and the cost (walking her father's killer, by the hand) are one act.

---

## 13. The Long Ebb (Ch17)

**Does.** As the Mud-Woman: climbs out of the mud into torchlight and sleet; speaks to men who mock her; is locked in a church; escapes through a window into the flooded nave; steals a stone from a cart; carries it through waist-deep water; presses it into a hollow and smooths blue clay over it with both hands; is caught; walks to the sluice.

**Sees.** The same town the player walked in white lines, now in amber, mud and terror. A man walking out of the mud with Kanter's face and being dragged into the church as a demon. The new sluice gate, raw timber, the shape of the one Okke kept.

**No fail state at the end.** The player walks to the gate because that is what happened. The water closes. Cut to white.

**Pays off.** The pageant (Ch1): the nation has drowned its mother's saviour in straw every year. Gerlof's blue nails (Ch12): he was digging out her clay. Enno's chronicle (Ch11).

---

## 14. The Call (Ch18)

**Does.** Carries the stone to a scarred gap in the reef and presses it in. Then mostly watches, and walks: Jildou can move freely on the flats while the song plays.

**Sees and hears.** One song, no words, through every speaker and controller motor. The three domes split. On the dike, screens showing harbours around the world where people in office clothes wade into the sea. Hiske leading Dona Inês out across the mud. Inês walking back from her hollow. The two Inêses stop a metre apart, look, and go on. Marrit standing in Kerf Nul with her eyes shut, then opening them and stepping out toward shore. Ubbo lying on a broken dome as three small luminous things swim past his face toward the sea. Jildou offering him her hand; the tide turning.

**Text.** KERF, on every screen: "Last hollow paid. Ledger balanced. A closed ledger is public record. Publishing."

**Pays off.** Femke's throwaway "a closed ledger is public record" (Ch6); "Nobody crosses. Somebody stays." (Ch1); "Out here there's no enemy. There's only the tide." (Ch2, Ch8, Ch12), now said to the man who ran the doctrine; the Promise (Ch8), now heard in full.

---

## 15. Epilogue: The Forecast

**Does.** Sets seven eel traps from a post with a red rag. Picks up a phone.

**Sees.** May light. The Gallery's faces carried out in boxes. On the phone, KERF: "There are no tides left in my ledger. I can compute a forecast of your life. It would be the first anyone has had in 655 years. Shall I?" The player chooses yes, no, or puts the phone down. Each gives one silent image: a number she reads and pockets / the screen going dark / the phone face-down on the mud. Then the tide coming in over the traps.

**Pays off.** "Do you know your tide?" "I haven't got one." (Ch1.)
