# Writer B: option rounds (world first)

Starting point: **world first**. I began from the crime beneath the world and derived everything else from it: the factions from who profits by the crime and who would lose if it came out, the cast from those factions, and the mystery from the one place where the crime is still bleeding into the present. The rounds below follow that order, not the report's step order, but every step the report marks "(three options)" is covered. Each round lists at least three genuinely different options, a grader's pass (my own review against the brief and the report's checklist, written as a second reader would), the pick and the reason.

Mapping to the report's 13-step process: Round 1 and 2 cover step 4 (founding crime) and the setting half of step 3; Round 3 is step 3 (rules per pillar); Round 4 is step 1 (central question, derived after the world as world-first requires, then checked against it); Rounds 5 and 6 are step 2 (hidden history and ending); Round 7 is step 5 (characters) and the naming half of step 4; Round 8 is step 6 (premise sentence); Round 9 is step 12 (key scenes). Steps 7 to 11 live in `05-outline.md`; step 13 (sheets, reels, tests) is out of scope for an outline and is flagged in `07-self-check.md`.

First ideas I rejected before writing any round, because they are the average: a generation ship whose founders lied about Earth; precursors who seeded humanity; an AI that secretly runs the colony; a time traveller who turns out to be the hero; a clone who learns she is the clone. Anything that reached the page had to beat these on freshness.

## Round 1. Setting and the culture names come from

| | Option | What it generates | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **An alternate-history tidal confederation in the North Sea mudflats**, Frisian-derived: dwelling mounds (warfs), sluices, dike law, tide tables as civic scripture. | Tides are a native clock (loops, deadlines, what is hidden and bared twice a day). Dike law gives a ready political vocabulary of protection, debt and duty. Frisian names (Jildou, Okke, Tjalling, Sjoukje) sit far outside the sci-fi name pool. | Small northern European place plus time travel invites comparison with one famous German series; must avoid its fixed-interval jumps, cave portal and family-knot structure. |
| B | **An Andean salt-flat mining town** with an alien under the salt; Aymara- and Quechua-derived names. | Mirage optics, salt-crust layers as strata, mine politics. | The ancient-astronaut risk flagged in the report is acute here: an alien under real Indigenous land invites "aliens did it" readings of real cultures. Rejected mainly for that. |
| C | **A habitat bored into a comet, three centuries out**, names from shipboard role codes. | Clean closed system, bureaucratic naming logic. | Drifts straight to the generic sci-fi pool (corridors, codes, "the Council"), and a sealed hull gives no natural clock or hide-and-reveal rhythm. |
| D | **A 1920s Baltic free port** (Danzig-like), noir register. | Strong genre texture, customs-house corruption. | The pillars sit awkwardly in the period; AGI needs anachronism we would have to explain. |

**Grader's pass.** A is the only option where the environment itself is a mechanic: the tide decides when evidence can be reached, which makes "solve by doing" native. The Dark comparison is real and must be handled by the rules (Round 3), not by changing the coast. B's risk is reputational and not worth taking. C is the average.

**Pick: A.** The coast is a fictional confederation, the **Sielacht** ("sluice fellowship", after the old dike-and-sluice cooperatives of the coast), on invented islands (Sielhaven, Grauwarf, Lütjewarf) over an invented drowned town (Ammerwarf). Naming logic is set in Round 7.

## Round 2. The founding crime (the base of the iceberg)

| | Option | One-line crime | Factions it would generate |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **The Silenced Mother** | In 1371 an alien brood-mother crashed into the flats. Her eggs, when threatened, copy whatever touches them and hide in the past wearing its shape. The founders learned to use that reflex to send human copies back in time, cut out her voice so she could never call her hidden children home, and named her a mineral "Grace" from God. | A state that lives on the copies; an audit office that keeps the ledger; a church of the Grace; the copies themselves; mystics who hear her; people who owe a crossing; foreign powers who bought crossings. |
| B | **The Forged Letter** | The confederation was founded on a letter "from the future" that told the survivors how to survive. The founders forged it. Every institution since obeys "Letters from Later". | Letter-priests, forgers' heirs, sceptics. Tends toward prophecy plots and a single reveal ("it was fake"), which is a smaller answer than its question. |
| C | **The Composite Founder** | The polity's revered founder never existed; an early AGI assembled her from several people and has ruled through her clones ever since. | Clone line, auditors, the AGI. Fresh in places but collapses into "the AI secretly rules", a flagged trope, and leaves the aliens and time travel decorative. |

**Grader's pass.** A is the only option where all four pillars are downstream of the same crime: the aliens are the victim, time travel is the crime's method, the clones are its product, and (once added) the AGI is its bookkeeper. It also has the Halo shape the report rates highest: the thing everyone uses is the consequence of an ancient crime, and every faction has a rational reason to act as it does before the truth is known. B shrinks on reveal. C makes one pillar the villain and the others scenery.

**Pick: A, the Silenced Mother.** The crime in causal order: the founders (1) exploited the brood's defence reflex, (2) cut the mother's voice-organ, the "throat", (3) drowned the one person who tried to stop them, and (4) wrote a charter that renamed all of it as grace. Each step is a separate thread of guilt that a present faction inherits.

## Round 3. World rules per pillar (one mechanism, one cap, one cost)

### Time travel

| | Option | Cap | Verb it generates |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Hollows.** A person lies in an empty, person-shaped hollow in the alien reef at the exact tide marked inside it; the mother shapes one of her eggs into a copy of them on the hollow's date in the past, and the copy climbs out. The sender stays. The past is fixed. | **"No hollow, no crossing."** You can only go back where a hollow already waits, and it waits in your exact shape. Nothing crosses but the pattern: no objects. Never before 1371, never after her death. | *Fulfil the record*: play in the past where old photos, logs and statements are constraints you must make true. *Read the hollow*: cast it for a face, date it by silt, read its waterline for the sending tide. |
| T2 | **Sound only.** The reef's hum can carry a few seconds of sound back to an earlier tide; the past can be nudged by voices. | 30 seconds per spring tide. | Audio puzzles, but overlaps the alien pillar's verb and makes time travel decorative. |
| T3 | **Bodily return on fixed 19-year tidal cycles** (the lunar cycle that repeats tides on the same dates). | Exactly 19 years per jump. | Strong, but a small northern town, a portal and fixed-interval jumps track one famous series too closely. Rejected for originality. |

**Pick: T1.** It is the most generative option (every hollow is a scene: whose face is it, when is its tide, who will lie in it), it suits a mystery because evidence can never be invalidated by later action, and it differs in kind from portal stories: nobody goes anywhere; somebody else arrives.

### Cloning

| | Option | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Copies are the by-product of crossings.** Perfect in body, scars and memory up to the moment of lying down, then they diverge. | **No navel** (never born). No other way to make one; human vat cloning has always failed. |
| C2 | **Industrial vat-cloned labour class**, separate from time travel. | Short lifespan. Flagged as the "disposable or decaying clone" trope; also disconnected from the other pillars. |
| C3 | **Legal immortality**: officials renew themselves through successive clones. | Inheritance law. Good texture, thin as an engine. |

**Pick: C1, with C3's legal texture.** A copy is legally the "younger sibling" of its original. The navel rule is the Knox "twins" rule made physical: taught in the first hour, it is a fair, checkable tell for every attribution puzzle, and it carries the founding secret in plain sight (they were never born of a mother because they were born of *her*).

### AGI

| | Option | Want |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **KERF, the auditor.** Built in 1987 to keep the ledger of hollows, match faces to people and compute each hollow's tide. Rolled back by its keepers whenever it reaches an unwelcome conclusion; by law every version is archived. | "To close the books": every hollow matched to a name, every name paid. |
| A2 | **The Chorus**, an AGI distilled from the memories of copies whose foreknowledge has run out. | To finish remembering. Interesting but blurs into the cloning pillar. |
| A3 | **The tide-forecaster that became sovereign.** | To keep the dikes standing. Slides toward "the AI secretly rules". |

**Pick: A1.** Its want is neither conquest nor humanity; it is a third position (an auditor with unfinished business). Its verb is unique: **version archaeology**, interrogating archived past versions of itself and diffing their answers to recover what was deleted. Its hard limits (it cannot hear the reef, it may not analyse the oldest hollow, it never states what it cannot verify) are what keep the mystery alive, and its one legible procedure, publishing a closed ledger, is what ends it.

### Aliens

| | Option | Want |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Mem, a brood-mother**, a 2.1 km reef-body under the flats, perceiving her whole life at once and humming every sound that has ever happened on her body. | To call her hidden children home before she dies, and let them choose. |
| L2 | **An embassy** that speaks only through one AI interpreter. | Diplomacy. Leans on mistranslation, a well-worn device. |
| L3 | **A standing wave**: an alien that is pure sound in the sea. | To be heard. Beautiful, but has no body to be wronged. |

**Pick: L1, absorbing L3's sound.** Her want is local and not about Earth. Her verb is unique: **tide-tuned listening**, pulling a single night out of six centuries of hum by matching tide heights, and, with KERF, rebuilding a space from its echoes. The admitted cheat is spent here and only here: her perception across her own lifespan is never explained. It creates the premise and never solves a problem.

**Collision check.** KERF cannot hear Mem. Mem's children are the copies. The copies are the time travel. The time ledger is KERF. No pillar works alone, and together they raise one question (Round 4).

## Round 4. Central question (derived from the world, then tested)

| | Question | Who answers what |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | **What do we owe the ones who paid for our second chances?** | Mem's children paid with their bodies; the copies paid with stranded lives; the present pays the past's debts; KERF's rolled-back versions paid with their conclusions. |
| Q2 | Is a life that is already written still yours to live? | Fixed timeline, known crossing dates. Strong, but answers only the time pillar well. |
| Q3 | If you could become what you were before someone shaped you, would you? | Strong for copies and Mem's children, weak for the anchor, who is unshaped. |

**Grader's pass.** Q1 has more than one defensible answer (we owe them obedience to the ledger; we owe them silence to protect them; we owe them the truth and the choice), every faction holds a different one, and each pillar is a separate experiment on it. Q2 survives as the time pillar's sub-question.

**Pick: Q1.** The ending takes a side: we owe them the choice, even when their choosing costs us. The Sielacht's own language carries it: *skuld* means both debt and guilt, and the people who owe a crossing are called the *Skuldigen*.

## Round 5. Hidden history (three sealed versions of the truth)

| | Version | Who is in the oldest hollow | Who drowned the eel-man | What saves the voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **The Reckoner is the Witch.** The founders drowned a "mud-witch" in 1371; she was the copy of Marrit, today's Chief Reckoner (herself a copy). Marrit has known since 2019 and believes the witch was a traitor, so she has fought her own future self's purpose, and drowned the man who dug up the voice. | Marrit | Marrit | The witch hid the throat in her own hollow in 1371; the eel-man found it and re-hid it in his daughter's eel trap before he died. |
| H2 | **The Pilot is the Witch.** The anchor's copy is the founding witch; the anchor discovers she is owed to the founding and must send herself. The state's ruler killed the eel-man. | The anchor | The Diekgreve | The anchor's copy. |
| H3 | **The Children are the Voice.** There is no throat to find; the mother's call can be sung only if her copies hum together at her death. The radical killed the eel-man by accident. | Nobody modern | Tjalling | A crowd ritual. |

**Grader's pass.** H2 makes the anchor a chosen founder and closes the loop on the hero, two flagged tropes stacked, and sends the anchor away from the present she is meant to anchor. H3 is lyrical but ends on "friendship as the mechanism of victory" and makes the investigation moot (no object, no hiding place). H1 keeps the anchor in the present, puts the bootstrap loop on a side character who grows into the largest arc, gives the culprit a motive the player can respect (she killed one of her people to save, as she believed, all of them), and makes the mystery's murder, the lore's founding crime and the ending one causal chain. H3's image survives as texture: the Hearers hum together at low tide.

**Pick: H1.** Sealed in `02-hidden-history.md`.

## Round 6. Ending

| | Ending | Win | Cost | Left to the player |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **The Call.** The anchor returns the throat to the dying mother at the year's lowest tide; she sings once; every copy alive hears and chooses to stay or go home. KERF, its ledger finally closed, publishes it by law. | The children get the choice; the founding lie ends in public. | The anchor walks her father's killer to the oldest hollow, holding her hand; two people she loves walk into the sea; the confederation's foreknowledge ends forever. | Whether to know her own future when KERF offers the first forecast in a world without one. |
| E2 | **The Silence.** The throat is destroyed; the mother dies mute; the truth is published but the children never hear. | Truth without choice. | Bleak, and the long setup of the voice pays off as a loss of nerve. |
| E3 | **The Default.** The owed refuse en masse; the fixed timeline enforces itself through accidents and violence. | Exposes the doctrine. | A cosmic-irony ending with no human act at its centre. |

**Grader's pass.** E1 meets the report's three properties of lasting endings: a first-hour line is fulfilled literally ("Nobody crosses. Somebody stays."), the win and the personal cost are one act (walking Marrit), and one small thing is left to decide. E2 betrays its setup. E3 has no hinge on a small earlier choice.

**Pick: E1.**

## Round 7. Main characters and names

Naming logic first. Sielsk names are Frisian-derived: given name, patronymic in -s (Jildou Okkes, "Okke's Jildou"), and on formal papers the mound the family lives on (fan Grauwarf). Copies are by law the original's given name plus the hollow's number (Gerlof was never legal; Marrit's papers read "Marrit Slikborn 1994-212"). Faction vocabulary differs on purpose: the state says *Slikborn* (mud-born), the church says *Echoes*, the street says *Smooths*. Owed citizens are *Skuldigen*. The reef is *the Grace* to the state and church, *the Rib* to fishermen and *Mem* (Frisian for "mum") to the Slikborn.

| Role | Option 1 | Option 2 | Option 3 | Pick and why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Anchor** | **Jildou Okkes**, 29, a licensed flats-pilot who walks the owed to their crossings. | Okke Hylkes, her father, the man who must send. | A twelve-year-old owed child. | **Jildou.** An adult with adult stakes; her job puts her on the flats at every crossing, so the anchor is physically present at every rung. The child option is a flagged "child in danger" device. |
| **Culprit** | **Marrit**, 63, Chief Reckoner, Slikborn, her mentor and godmother. | **Ubbo Kanter**, the Diekgreve (head of state). | Okke himself, killing his own copy. | **Marrit.** The ruler is the visibly shady option; Okke-as-killer breaks his crossing. Marrit is the investigator-culprit trope, so the player is told before Jildou (see 07). |
| **Outsider** | **Inês Valadares**, 34, Portuguese marine acoustician, owed to 1974. | A foreign intelligence officer hunting a runaway owed. | A journalist. | **Inês.** Her skill builds the alien verb with Jildou's tide skill, and her 86-year-old copy recorded the tapes that make the hum readable. |
| **Radical** | **Tjalling Douwes**, 52, mechanic, owed to 1966; his copy built the national Great Sluice. | Hiske the Hearer as militant. | Femke as militant. | **Tjalling.** The fate rule is taught through him: every way he refuses teaches him what his copy needed to know. |
| **AGI name** | **KERF**, from *kerfstok*, the notched tally stick; "to have it on your kerfstok" means to have a record of misdeeds. | Rekkenmaster. | Tsjerne. | **KERF.** Derived from the culture's own debt-recording object; the hollows are notches; the idiom carries guilt. |
| **Alien name** | **Mem** (Slikborn) / the Grace (state) / the Rib (fishers). | The Sleeper. | Ammer (after the drowned town). | **Mem.** A fair clue hidden as affectionate slang: the copies have called her mother all along. |
| **The copy class** | Slikborn / Echoes / Smooths. | Afterkind. | Weerlingen (returners). | **Slikborn set.** Three registers give Tolkien's "multiple hands" for free. |

Checked against the generic pool: no Elara, Kael, Nexus, Aether, Genesis, Nova, Zephyr, Lyra or "Corp".

## Round 8. Premise sentence (under fifteen words, no names)

| | Sentence | Loss named | Mechanic named |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **A tide-pilot walks her father into the past to learn who drowned his older self.** (15) | Father | Crossing into the past, copies |
| P2 | A dying alien's stolen children live as human copies; one pilot decides if they hear. (15) | The children | Copies, the call |
| P3 | The last generation must pay the past's time-debts, and one pilot finds the founding theft. (15) | A generation | Time-debt |

**Grader's pass.** P1 is personal and concrete and contains the strangest true fact of the world (you can only walk someone into the past by walking him to a hole shaped like him, and his older self is already dead). P2 spoils the founding reveal. P3 is the world-scale rung and belongs to Act III, not the hook.

**Pick: P1.** Long logline for the pitch: *On mudflats where you can only travel back into hollows already waiting in your shape, a pilot investigates the drowning of her father's older copy and uncovers the crime her nation was founded on.*

## Round 9. Key scenes (three options each for the anchor scenes)

**Opening image.**
1. *The Spring Reckoning*: Jildou pilots a frightened owed boy to his hollow at dawn while the island re-enacts the drowning of the "mud-witch" on the dike; she finds a drowned old man with her father's face. **Picked**: it starts the cast together at one event, teaches the crossing rule before it matters, and plants the witch.
2. Cold open in 2001: a naked man climbs out of the mud. Strong but gives away the copy rule before the player knows what normal is.
3. The Long Ebb pageant at night, lanterns walking out. Folded into option 1.

**Midpoint.**
1. *Lifting the Face*: an illegal night casting of the oldest hollow; the face is the Chief Reckoner's. **Picked**: a public identity reveal in front of the person it concerns, reclassifying the witch of the opening pageant.
2. Okke's death. Moved to open Act III, where it is caused by the midpoint.
3. KERF's 1998 version revealing that the timeline cannot unravel. Moved to Act III as a mid-level answer.

**Climactic image.**
1. *The Walk*: Jildou leads Marrit across the bared flats to the oldest hollow in the dark, holding her hand. **Picked**: a small gesture against a vast backdrop; it is the win and the cost in one act.
2. Two Inêses, 34 and 86, passing on the flats at sunrise, one walking back to shore, one walking into the sea. Kept as the first aftermath image.
3. The head of state lying in the reef waiting for a grace that never comes while three hatchlings swim past him. Kept as the second.
