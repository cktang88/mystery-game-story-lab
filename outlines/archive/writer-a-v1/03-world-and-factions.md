# Writer A, file 03: world, factions and iceberg

## 1. Eiðey at a glance

Eiðey is a basalt island republic of about 38,000 people in the North Atlantic, settled in the 10th century by Norse settlers and their Gaelic thralls. The capital, **Hornvík**, is a harbour town on the south coast below the cliff that holds **Málhellir**, the Speech Cave. The year is 2043, a **Standing** year, the first in 18.6 years. Names are matronymic. The island lives on fish, geothermal greenhouses and a sovereign fund that has never had a bad year. The world knows two of its exports: the **Thul**, the incorruptible notary mind that has registered the world's treaties since 2027, and Eiðey's quiet, expensive **foresight**. The world does not know that the foresight comes from a living alien, which islanders call **the Hearer**.

**The look.** Black rock, white sky, net racks, cod drying. The Oath-Keeper's ceremonial veil of undyed wool, eyes only. Silver spiral earrings for the Mother's House. Red-painted kitchens on the north coast. The Thul's small glass lenses in the cave roof.

**The sound.** Surf in a cave. A reel recorder's hiss. **The Ebb-Song**, a wordless rising and falling chant the whole island sings at funerals and Hearings, which ends in the one spoken line, the **Long Word**: "Farðu heim, Bergi". The Thul's level voice over harbour loudspeakers announcing tides. At every slack, the island's held breath.

### Critical-path vocabulary (the player needs only these)

**Horn** (with its two mouths), **Store**, **Pool**, **Thul**, **Count**, **Law of Same**, plus the names of people and places. Every other coined term is optional depth, glossed by context where it appears, or kept to the writers' documents. The words "Bergmál", "Span", "the Received" and "Goðar" are cut from on-screen use (see 07).

## 2. The four pillars as the player learns them

Each rule is taught in the order **anomaly, harmless test, named rule, personal use, bill** before any reveal depends on it.

### The Horn (time travel)

- **Mechanism.** At the low-water slack of a Standing spring tide, sound spoken into the Horn arrives at the same mouth 6,798 days earlier, with a faint halo of whatever sounds within a few paces of it.
- **Cap.** Sound only. About nine minutes per window and about 25 windows per Standing year. One step of eighteen years per send.
- **Cost.** The Horn takes the words. Nobody present hears them and no device records them, so every send has exactly one witness, the sender. Every received voice is a voice from the future that the past has already acted on.
- **The Tidings Law (1671).** Readers at the Inner Mouth may only read the sworn Book of Tidings, a chronicle of the coming eighteen years as seen from the far side. So the archive is foresight about the years *between* Standings, never a running report of the Standing year itself. The last window carries the relay of the Long Word.
- **Damages:** trust. A sender can lie about what they sent, and nobody alive can check.
- **Saves:** goodbyes. You can speak to someone who has since died, and they heard you.
- **Unique scenes:** hearing your own future words; a mouth moving with no sound; a recordist's five-second level checks that hold the year's empty windows and a burst of foreign radio.
- **Verb: tide-matching.** Align the received archive with the present to predict who will be where. Taught at low stakes in Ch4 and used as a set piece at the Turning (Ch19-20).
- **Teaching order:**
  - anomaly: a reader's silent lips and the Silence (Ch1); the tape (Ch4);
  - harmless test: a neighbour's mark heard on a 2024 level check, then watched spoken soundlessly (Ch4);
  - named rule: the Thul's tide announcement, "Words given to the Horn are given away";
  - personal use: Jórunn finds her own voice in the archive (Ch10);
  - bill: the Silence's meaning, and the empty windows.

### The Thul (AGI)

- **Mechanism.** A notary mind that holds every sworn record on the island and cannot state a falsehood about it. Its yearly versions (sittings) can be woken to answer as of their dates.
- **What it is not.** It attests filings, not facts: every certificate ends "Attested as filed". It does not interpret.
- **Caps.**
  - Waking a sitting needs an estate officer's petition and warrant for an open estate.
  - A woken sitting answers only about that estate's own matters: the holder's identity, assets, debts, promises and death. Anything else gets "That is another estate."
  - Every waking is publicly logged.
  - A seal closes its mouth to everyone, the seal's holder included.
- **The Count.** A sealed Thul may still say how many statements in a set are true ("a count is not testimony", 1702). The set must have at least three statements, all about an open estate in the officer's warrant, each backed by evidence entered in the estate file. Every count is publicly logged with its headings. This is the game's batch validation, explained inside the fiction. The evidence rule stops it working as a yes/no oracle, and the public log means every use warns whoever is being investigated.
- **Cost.** It forgets nothing, so anything sworn near it is permanent, and every question you ask it is seen by the people you are investigating.
- **Damages:** confiding. Islanders stopped talking freely in rooms with speaker grilles.
- **Saves:** a dead woman's last requests, kept by a version of the machine that no longer runs.
- **Verbs:** waking sittings and diffing their answers; filing a Division (dragging evidence objects into statement slots) for the Count.

### Aftergrowth (cloning)

- **Mechanism.** Blood offered in the Pool grows a newborn copy. No memories pass. Copies age normally.
- **Cap.** One living copy per pattern: the Pool will not grow what is already walking.
- **Cost.** The Law of Same: a copy inherits the original's name, debts, marriage and crimes.
- **Damages:** the bond between a copy and the original's family (a widow made "wife" to a young man who is not her husband).
- **Saves:** the same bond, freely chosen (the widow who keeps him as a son).
- **Verbs:** lineage reading (portraits, teeth, acquired scars and habits, voice) and Law-of-Same casework (the Thing pleading). Veil-swap stealth belongs to the Oath-Keeper's office, not to cloning, and is tagged that way.

### The Hearer (aliens)

- **Mechanism.** A stranded being grown into the cave. Its body is the Horn, the Pool and the Store. It speaks only in collages of stored sound, mostly the island's dead.
- **Caps.**
  - It cannot invent a sound.
  - It cannot act against a promise.
  - It hears promises and releases only from its keeper, in the Pool, at Standing slacks.
  - It knows its keeper by the word kept, in an unbroken chain of dawns.
  - It can die only at a Standing's last window, and it buds as it dies. The Dead Horn is its parent's husk.
- **Cost.** Its foresight made the island dependent. Its Store means the dead never fully leave, which is both a comfort and a captivity.
- **Damages:** the island's freedom not to know.
- **Saves:** the living's bond with their dead. The Store will replay a lost voice for anyone who asks in its grammar, and the release ends that.
- **Verb: collage conversation.** Attribute each fragment it quotes to a speaker and era to understand it; answer it by citing promises.
- **Budding taught at low stakes:**
  - The Schooling's line that the Hearer "was born in the cave; the Dead Horn is its mother" (Ch3).
  - Sitting One's promise mentions "the bud" (Ch8).
  - The Greeting Wall's deepest layer is sounds older than the Hearer, inherited from its parent (Ch9).
  - The overheard plan: "it buds at the Turning, as its mother did" (Ch12).
  - The 983 collage shows that a stored plea outlives its speaker (Ch14).

### The one admitted cheat

How the Hearer's kind span 18.6 years, and what they are, is never explained. It is the premise's single impossible fact and is never used to solve anything.

| **The Register** (International Treaty Register at Hornvík; face: **Sanne Verhoeven**, head of mission and security) | The Thul's word underwrites the world's treaties, and Eiðey's foresight has saved millions, Rotterdam among them. | Keep both, **lawfully**: "We take nothing the Thing does not grant, and only for as long as it grants it." | Its treaty architecture; the knowledge that it sold warnings in price tiers. | Blue lanyards, flat English, security contractors in grey, a chartered ferry, a tank barge for the Transfer. |
| **The Hearer** | A promise cannot be broken. | To go home when she lets it. | Nothing; it is the victim. | Black glistening rock that breathes with the tide; the voices of the dead in the dark. |

Every faction acts from visible incentives (Tufekci's sociological mode), so a player can predict them and a death does not break the plot.

## 5. Iceberg lore

Delivery order for every fact: place first, then object or creature, then sound (usually a Store fragment or a received voice), and only then a short text with a named author and bias. The main path needs none of the rows marked optional.

### Texture (may stay open forever; never promised by early scenes)

- What the Hearer's kind are, where the 927 parent came from, and where the bud goes home to.
- Whether a Gaelic thrall girl heard the cave before Steinunn (folk account against saga).
- The full relay chain: each Standing's relayers, including the relayer of 1615 who refused, after which the chain held anyway because someone else did it (a relayer's diary, optional).
- **The Quiet Standing of 1652-53** (named, never shown): the island sent nothing except the relay.
- The readers who strayed from the Book of Tidings, and how the past knew.
- **The Loan-Women's stones** at Hornvík: who each was.
- **The Burned Thing of 1350** (named, never shown).
- The House's Fitting rooms: centuries of received lines and the harmless ways they were made true (the 1783 "the boats burned at Hornvík", made true by burning old hulls at Midsummer).
- The Thul's minority opinions: Sitting Eleven dissented from its own seal in a sealed annex.
- The folklore that a person who dies in the Horn at slack goes back as a voice for one tide (never confirmed; the game never uses it).
- A poet regrown six times who wrote the same first line each time (optional collage).
- Where the Twenty-first will grow up, and who she will be.

### Deliberate white space

Some events, places and people are named and never shown: the Quiet Standing, the Burned Thing, the Danish crown's 1660 attempt to seize the cave, the relayer of 1615, and Eiðey's foresight sale to "a southern power" in 1969.
