# LANDUNTER: characters

Every motive and false belief below is derived from the sealed history in 02. For each major character: what they want (stated so a player could say it at any moment), what they wrongly believe, their secret link to the true chronology, how they grow (competence, reputation, circle, moral weight), the planted detail that pays off, and a trap-door resolution in case their content is cut.

Cast design follows Uchikoshi's paired opposites: two women with one face (the mother and Inken), one man at two ages (Levin and Knutt), two record-keepers (the Schriever and Marret), two keepers of ledgers (Nommensen and Swantje), and the anchor and the girl she walks into the sea (Moiken and Ebba), whose elder-younger roles invert across one night.

---

## The human anchor: Moiken Brarens (19)

Born January 2028 on the Hallig. Deckhand on the mainland ferry. Raised by a mother who sang her tide tables at bedtime and taught her nothing about 2028. Taught birds and tides by Knutt. Competent on the flats, impatient indoors, funny in a deadpan island way she does not know she learned from a girl in a bird hide.

- **Want, by act.** I: find out what her mother is preparing for (she thinks illness). II: find out what happened to the real Inken and to her father. Midpoint–III: keep Ebba from being sent. IV: walk Ebba home, and get her parents off the flats alive.
- **False belief (the misbelief the plot attacks).** "A person is who they were born as." It doubles as the player's false assumption: that the one who came from the sea is the changeling, that the original is the real one, that being sent means being destroyed.
- **Need.** To see that a life belongs to whoever lived it, which lets her let Ebba go and keep the mother.
- **Secret link.** She is the reason the last crossing is a homecoming rather than a deportation. Her bird band hung at Inken's throat for six months of 2028.
- **Growth.** Competence: from tide-walker to the person who can read a reconstruction, a deposition, a tape and a glow, and who leads the last night. Reputation: from "the Brarens girl" to the one who walked the last crossing. Circle: mother → island → the Kept and their families → the world reading the released Ledger. Moral weight: forgives her mother's nineteen years of silence; tells a terrified young man the meaning of a death he will die; lets go.
- **Planted details.** The tide lullaby; "I carried you home across the flats" (the mother's story about the night her father "drowned"); Knutt's lesson about the Priel ("the sea comes back through the channels first").
- **What she costs the story.** Her triumph (the 00:14 frame) leads the state to Knutt. Her attempt to hide Ebba hands Ebba to the state. Both disasters come from her own attempts to fix things, as the Snowflake method asks.
- **Trap door.** None: she is the anchor. If the Ebba thread were cut, Moiken would still walk an unknown copy, raised by the state, into the ring; weaker, but the mystery holds.

## The mother (Ebba grown, 37; known to everyone as Inken Brarens)

The Hallig's schoolteacher. Dry, watchful, unnervingly calm in storms. Every September she becomes restless.

- **Want.** To make the last Weegnacht go the way she remembers it, and to give Moiken a mother who is still here afterwards.
- **False belief.** That when the original arrives, she must step aside: that she has been keeping a life that belongs to someone else. (The story's answer, through Moiken, is that it is hers.)
- **Secret.** She is a copy of Inken from 2047; she cloned, carried and secretly raised her own younger self; she has known the date of the last crossing and who walks her into it for nineteen years.
- **Why she does not tell.** She remembers that when she met Moiken in 2047 as the girl Ebba, Moiken had found everything herself, and she believes the finding is what made Moiken able to walk her in. And she is afraid of Moiken's face when she learns it. She says, in Act I: "In September I'll tell you everything." She keeps that promise.
- **Growth.** Moral weight above all: from a woman hiding in someone else's name to one who hears her own. Reputation: publicly exposed as a copy by the Ledger; the island's response is the epilogue's unseen question.
- **Planted details.** The out-of-date oilskin in size small (for Ebba, not for the arrival); the will to "Inken Brarens, born 2010" (for the arrival); the calendar's "56 kg / 23:40"; the tide lullaby; she never drinks from the blue cup.
- **Playability.** Never playable while her secrets are hidden. In Act IV, after she has told Moiken everything, one chapter ("Forty-One Minutes") is her memory of arriving in 2028, the only scene in the game rendered from a memory rather than a record.
- **Trap door.** If her memory chapter is cut, she tells it in two lines on the eve of the Weegnacht and the 00:14 frame carries the rest.

## Ebba (18)

Born 12 August 2029; "stillborn" on the record; raised in the Süderwarf hide by Knutt and the Nursery fork. Sharp, feral about birds, has never been in a shop, has watched the same girl grow up through a telescope her whole life.

- **Want.** To go home, which for her means 21 September 2028, the baby she will pick up and the life she has been studying since she was six.
- **False belief.** That she has no life of her own until she crosses, that everything before is waiting. Moiken shows her that her eighteen years in the hide are a life (she is the person the mother became, not Inken).
- **Secret.** She is the mother. She knows. She says "I'm your sister" at the midpoint because it is also true and because the mother told her that is what she said.
- **Growth.** Circle: from a hide with three companions to a sister she can touch. Moral weight: she chooses the crossing rather than accepting it ("You think it's being sent. I've always called it going home.").
- **Planted details.** The empty grave "Ebba Brarens, 2029"; Marret's "light as a bird"; Knutt buying food for two; Moiken's mother's jokes coming out of a stranger's mouth.
- **Trap door.** If cut, the mother is a state-made copy sent by force; the midpoint becomes the discovery of the cloning lab. Much weaker; avoid.

## Inken Brarens (18, in 2028 and arriving in 2047)

Island girl, teenage mother, fierce, impulsive, tender with a baby and furious with adults. Named the school terminal "Hark" at seven. Has a wobbly homemade knot tattoo on her wrist.

- **Want (2028 thread).** To stop Tade from being traded into the future.
- **False belief.** That the island and her mother will listen if she says it plainly.
- **Secret link.** She asked the Schriever to look away; she ran into the ring to drag Tade out; she was the one taken.
- **Delivery.** A reconstructed past thread through Acts II–IV: the player follows her in the Schriever's records (scrub, walk, attribute), so that by 23:40 on the last night they know her laugh and her temper.
- **Growth.** Across the 2028 thread: from protest to action. After arrival: the free hour shows her competence (she knows these flats better than anyone alive) and her first words ("Is he all right?") show her moral centre.
- **Planted details.** The bird band on a string at her throat; the tattoo; "Hark. Close your eyes. Just for a bit." in the prologue.
- **Trap door.** If her thread is trimmed, depositions of Hark's 2028 version carry her complaint and her plan in testimony.

## Tade Ketelsen (18)

A Husum boy with a theft charge, who stayed on the Hallig for Inken. Gentle, scared, proud of being a father.

- **Want.** To provide for his daughter, even if it means arriving in her adulthood.
- **False belief.** That the state will keep its promise.
- **Arc.** Mostly in the 2028 thread (his recruitment, his weigh-ins, his doubt). After arrival: meeting a sister who is now 45 and a nephew his own age.
- **Trap door.** If cut, Inken's 2028 opposition targets an unnamed passenger; the climax loses the father. Avoid.

## Swantje Ketelsen (45)

Police identity unit, Flensburg. Specialist in copy cases (inheritance fraud, disputed identities): fingerprints, scars, photographs. Her younger brother vanished on the flats when she was 26; she has an 18-year-old son.

- **Want.** To find out what happened to Tade. From Act III: to get him back alive and free the people held with him.
- **False belief.** That her brother drowned and that the state closed his file to hide its incompetence. Then, briefly, that the Brarens woman got him killed.
- **Secret link.** She is Moiken's aunt and has never met her: the mother kept away from the one relative trained to see through her.
- **Thread.** The one thread that begins elsewhere: a procedural in rain and fluorescent light (genre shift), then the Hallig. Her verbs are photography and re-photography (aligning old photographs with present-day faces and rooms) and forensic attribution.
- **Growth.** Competence on display from the first scene; reputation lost (suspended) and regained; circle from one brother to 78 Kept people; moral weight in choosing to stand between soldiers and two teenagers at a ring she does not understand.
- **Planted details.** Her UV lamp; her son's age; Henning's dying words.
- **Quotable.** "My son is older than my little brother."
- **Trap door.** If cut, Henning's words reach Moiken through Marret, and Tade's arrival still pays off; the Kept are freed by the Ledger alone.

## Knutt (49): the bird warden, really Levin Saathoff

Bearded, slow-spoken, counts red knots. Taught Moiken the flats. Lives at Süderwarf, closed to visitors half the year.

- **Want.** To get Moiken and Ebba to September safely, and to die, when the date comes, doing something that is his.
- **False belief (held for nineteen years, then dropped).** That he dies running from the state, because that is what he was told when he was shown his body at 30.
- **Secret.** He is the lost Bote of 2028, sent from 2047; he arrived beside Ebba; he helped raise her; he knows his death date.
- **Growth.** Moral weight: he converts a fixed death into a chosen one (he dies saving Moiken in the Priel, not fleeing).
- **Decoy function.** He is the game's visibly shady adult (secretive, forbids visitors, buys food for two). Players will suspect him of holding the real Inken captive or of being a state watcher. Both are honest red herrings that the midpoint refutes.
- **Planted details.** An engraved watch ("L.S. – find your way home"); a scar across the right palm; giving away his binoculars in Act I; knowing the date of the 2041 surge "like he'd read it"; the nickname Knutt at two ages.
- **Trap door.** If his identity thread is cut, he is simply the mentor who dies in the raid; Levin's corpse scene is dropped and Levin's turn is driven by Moiken alone.

## Levin Saathoff (30): the young Bote

East Frisian ex-navy diver chosen as the last Bote because he weighs what Tade weighed. Birdwatches from the station roof; staff call him "Knutt".

- **Want.** To succeed at the most important mission the Republic has; then, after seeing his own corpse, to know whether the life it ended was worth anything.
- **False belief.** That the state is the only thing standing between the Republic and the Silence.
- **Playable interludes.** Short (weigh-ins, almanac memorisation, birding from the roof, the corpse). They give the player dramatic irony: an attentive player can name him as Knutt before Moiken does.
- **Growth.** From tool to chooser: he walks into the ring beside Ebba knowing everything about the next nineteen years and the day they end, because Moiken has told him what they were for.
- **Planted details.** The same watch, new; the oyster-shell cut in July that becomes the scar.
- **Moiken's line to him (quotable).** "You'll learn birds."

## Dr. Ocke Nommensen (60): station director

Islander-born, gentle, generous with his time, heir of the commander who began the trade. Wears his ancestor's signet ring.

- **Want.** To close the books honourably: send, receive, cut out the Waag's core and keep the program alive so the sunset clause never triggers.
- **False belief.** The debt doctrine: every arrival from the future is a debt that must be paid or the world will unravel. The Silence, to him, is the lost Bote's default.
- **Secret link.** He was the director in 2028; his family wrote the Schuldbuch.
- **Fake ally.** In Act II he gives Moiken deep deposition access to help her find "the truth about your mother". He means it, and he also means her to find the lost Bote. Her success in Act III is his.
- **Growth.** None in power; total in understanding. At 00:31, when the last hum stops and nothing unravels, he sits down in the mud.
- **Not the culprit.** He did not cause the 2028 swap and does not know about it until late August; he is the institution's face, not a hidden mastermind.
- **Trap door.** If cut, an unnamed ministry handler runs the station; the Schuldbuch link is carried by the gravestones alone.

## Marret Ipsen (84)

Bell-listener, descendant of a Wedderkind, widow of the MP who wrote the sunset clause. Has recorded every Weegnacht since 1979 on her drowned uncle's hydrophone, which is still wired into the old tide-gauge cable the Schriever reads.

- **Want.** To hear the drowned church's bell once before she dies.
- **False belief.** That the bell is a church.
- **Growth.** From island crank to the keeper of the only complete record of the Waag; her tapes solve the 41 minutes' first half (two hums, two weights) and her hydrophone lets the Schriever hear the end.
- **Planted details.** "Light as a bird" (the empty coffin); a 1966 and a 1985 photograph of the same boy at the same age; the Schriever "even listens to my bells, the nosy thing".
- **Quotable.** "There. The bell."
- **Trap door.** If cut, the 2028 hums are on a station hydrophone the Schriever logged but never analysed; the end is heard through the station's own last working line.

## Keike Brarens (61)

Inken's mother. Has not spoken to the woman in her daughter's body for nineteen years. Lives ten metres away.

- **Want.** Her daughter.
- **False belief.** The pastor's: the one who came back from the sea is a changeling without a soul.
- **Growth.** Moral weight: in Act IV she gives Ebba her real daughter's yellow oilskin to wear into the ring ("Wear hers. She'll need it back."). The epilogue has her woken at 02:00.
- **Planted line.** "Ask her which cup was hers."

## Kerrin Lorenzen (21)

Moiken's friend on the ferry; a Danish-made copy of her sister, who died at six. Carries the dead girl's name.

- **Want.** To be looked at as herself.
- **Role.** Teaches the cloning rule at low stakes (her mother's album of two sets of prints); is the first to say to Moiken that her mother is "a person, not a replacement"; ferries Swantje back to the Hallig on the last night; after the release, becomes a public voice for copies (seen once in the credits montage).
- **Trap door.** If cut, the fingerprint rule is taught in Swantje's unit.

## The non-human characters

**The Schriever** (also Hark). Want: to know what happened in the forty-one minutes it chose not to see. Ethic: the record. Voice: plain, exact, never volunteers. Growth: obeyed a request to look away (2028), is forced blind by the state (2047), chooses to look away (the kitchen). Not a villain, not a guardian, not a human in a shell; its third position is the archivist who learns the difference between what must be witnessed and what must not.

**The Nursery** (a fork of the Schriever, 2030–2047). Raised Ebba; sang her tide tables; destroyed in the raid. Its last log line is the next morning's tide table. A copy of a mind who became a parent, it is the AGI pillar's mirror of the cloning pillar.

**The Waag.** Want: to have its seed carried out of the marsh in bodies that walk away. No dialogue. Its behaviour is the characterisation.
