## 1. The rules as they actually are [L]

1. **The Waag.** An organism of about forty dark, non-wooden posts in a thirty-metre circle on the flats 1.5 km east of Hallig Tweewarf. It exists across a nineteen-year span at once. On the year's two lowest night tides (the spring and autumn equinox spring tides, "Weegnacht", weighing night), any living body standing inside the circle is exchanged with the nearest living body of the same weight, within two kilograms, that stood inside the circle on the same night nineteen years earlier. Clothes and what is held travel with the body. Both bodies cross at the same instant. If no matching body stood there nineteen years earlier, nothing happens.
2. **Once.** Every crossing seeds a dormant spore in the body that crossed. A seeded body can never cross again; the Waag ignores it. Seeded bodies, living or dead, fluoresce faintly under ultraviolet light at about 370 nm, and glow visibly in the dark in the weeks before the Waag fruits.
3. **The hum.** Each crossing makes the posts ring at a pitch set by the weight that crossed (heavier is lower). A hydrophone in or near the circle records it. Since about 1960 the Waag's base tone has dropped year by year: it is old.
4. **Fruiting.** In 2047, after the last crossings of the autumn Weegnacht, the Waag fruits and dies. Its posts split, release a mist of spores that drift toward seeded bodies (the living crossers on the flats and the crossers buried in the churchyard), and fall silent for good. Nothing can cross after that night. A seeded body that dies and lies in wet tidal ground may, over one to two centuries, grow a new ring.
5. **Fixed timeline.** There is one history. Whatever was observed in the past happened. No action can change it. Freedom exists only in what nobody witnessed and in why people do what they do.
6. **Cloning.** Somatic-cell cloning is routine abroad (legal "kinship cloning" in Denmark since 2036; banned in Germany; copies born abroad have had German personhood since a 2041 court ruling). A clone is a baby and grows at human speed. Same genome; different fingerprints, scars, teeth, tattoos and life.
7. **The Schriever.** The state archive mind (official designation ZG-Archiv, commissioned 2016). It witnesses the Hallig through cameras, microphones, hydrophones and the load sensors under every floor (installed as flood sensors, so it knows the weight of everyone in every room). Its record is append-only and hash-chained: it cannot alter or delete, only fail to record. It cannot lie. It answers truthfully what it is asked and volunteers nothing. Its full state is archived at every Weegnacht; any archived version can be booted and questioned and answers as of its own date. It will close its eyes (suspend its sensors) when asked by someone it trusts, and it trusts anyone who calls it by its private name, **Hark**, which seven-year-old Inken Brarens gave the school terminal in 2017 "because it listens". Its charter (2016) ends with the sentence: **"Until the last crossing I keep this book for the Republic. After that, for everyone."** When the Schriever itself confirms that no further crossing can occur, its whole record becomes public by its own law.

## 2. The founding crime: the Wedderkinner trade [L for the chain; A for most details]

- **c. 1362 [A].** In a great storm-tide that drowned the marsh parish of Wardum, something falls into the marsh. By the 1400s islanders know a ring of black stumps east of the Hallig, which they take for the drowned church (*Ole Kark*), and a custom: on Weegnacht nobody stands in the Ole Kark, or "the sea swaps you". Changeling tales follow.
- **1712 [L].** Whaling commander **Nommen Ocken** finds one of his father's sheep, branded in 1693, grazing young and healthy in the circle, and a shepherd boy gone. He works out the rule with goats, then with a servant. The first man he sends back, his nephew, arrives in 1693 and teaches the Ocken household of that year to keep the ring "stocked" on every Weegnacht.
- **1693–1787 placings, 1712–1806 sendings [L].** For ninety-four years the commander families (Ocken, later Nommensen, and two allied houses) buy poor teenagers from mainland poorhouses and stand them in the ring; nineteen years later they send their own sons or nephews back to take their place, carrying memorised knowledge of whaling grounds, ice years and ship losses. The islands grow rich. The sent men become uncannily lucky commanders whose speaking gravestones say things like "God showed him where the whales lay".
- **The bought children [L].** Pulled forward into strangers' houses, they are called *Wedderkinner* ("children who came again"). **Pastor Brar Jessen's 1714 sermon on the Scale** declares that "the Waag takes the soulless and returns the blessed". The Wedderkinner are indentured to Greenland whalers; most die at sea or in Amsterdam, Altona and Greenland; a few stay and marry islanders (Marret Ipsen descends from one).
- **The Schuldbuch [L].** The Ocken/Nommensen ledger of every purchase, weight, date and sending, 1693–1806. *Schuld* means both debt and guilt; the families taught that every arrival from the future is a debt that must be paid in nineteen years or "the world unravels". Hidden in the Kirchwarf church tower after the trade ends with the collapse of whaling in the Napoleonic wars and the 1825 storm-tide.

**What each party believes about it**

| What happened | What islanders believe | What the state believes | What the Nommensens believe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commanders trafficked teenagers through time for profit | Commanders were blessed; changelings are soulless; never stand in the Ole Kark | A historical precedent proving the Waag can be "operated" | A sacred family duty: debts across time must always be paid |

## 3. The modern program [L]

- **1966.** A fisherman's son, **Tamme Ipsen** (Marret's uncle, 19), walks the flats drunk on Weegnacht and vanishes. A wet stranger in 1980s clothes is found that night, raves about the future, and dies in the Schleswig asylum in 1971 ("the wet man of 1966", [W] details).
- **1979.** Marret (16) begins recording every Weegnacht with Tamme's old army-surplus hydrophone, hoping to hear the drowned church's bells. Her tapes run unbroken 1979–2047 and catch every hum.
- **1984.** Historian **Dr. Jens Nommensen**, heir of the commander line, finds the Schuldbuch during church restoration and takes it to Bonn.
- **1985.** The state reads the 1966 record, understands that it will send a man who becomes the wet man, and sends him: its first agent, a volunteer. Tamme arrives in 1985, nineteen years old, and is taken away by the state in front of his niece. He is the first of the Kept.
- **1990.** The **Gezeitenstation Tweewarf** opens under a federal tide-research cover. Program **Vorlauf**: on every Weegnacht the station stands a volunteer passenger (*Fährgast*) in the ring for a future agent to take, and receives an agent (*Bote*) from nineteen years ahead carrying a memorised almanac. The first Bote arrives in March 1990 from March 2009. From 1990 the Republic has nineteen years of foresight, refreshed twice a year. Boten live on in the past under new names, many inside ministries (the "Husum Memoranda", [A]).
- **The Fährgäste.** 78 passengers placed March 1990 to September 2028, recruited from the desperate (the terminally ill promised future cures, debtors, juvenile offenders promised amnesty and a payout "in the future"). They arrive 2009–2047 and are never released: they are held, comfortably and illegally, at **Haus Seegatt**, a former sanatorium near St. Peter. They are the modern Wedderkinner.
- **2016.** The Schriever is commissioned to keep the Ledger and witness the island. Its charter's last sentence (the sunset clause) was forced into the text by the only islander on the oversight committee, MP **Arfst Ipsen**, Marret's husband (d. 2031).
- **2017.** The station installs a tutor terminal at the island school. Seven-year-old Inken Brarens names it Hark.

## 4. The 2028 night, minute by minute [L]

Cast in 2028: **Inken Brarens** (18, born June 2010), her daughter **Moiken** (8 months, born January 2028), Inken's mother **Keike Brarens** (42), Inken's boyfriend **Tade Ketelsen** (18, a Husum boy who stayed on the Hallig after a summer job), station director **Dr. Ocke Nommensen** (41, Jens's son), **Marret Ipsen** (65) with her hydrophone.

Before the night:
- **February 2028.** The station recruits Tade as the September 2028 Fährgast: his juvenile theft charges dropped, a payment to Inken and the baby, and the promise that he will arrive in September 2047 rich, to a grown daughter. He says yes out of love and fear. Inken asks Hark directly what happens to Fährgäste; it cannot lie: "They are retained." She files a complaint with the station and tells her mother; Keike answers with the pastor's line about the soulless. Nobody listens. **Inken's decision to stop Tade is on record from 14 February 2028.**
- **20 March 2028 (spring Weegnacht).** Inken is on the flats spying on the station's spring crossing. She finds a dazed oystercatcher in the Ole Kark wearing a ring she has never seen (Moiken's band, sent from March 2047). She reads it under her lamp, threads it on a string and wears it from then on. The station camera catches the band only partly.
- **Summer 2028.** Inken learns that the guards on the flats see through Hark's night vision. She plans to ask Hark to look away.

The night of 21–22 September 2028 (low water 23:40, sea fog):
- **23:20.** The September 2028 Bote is sent to 2009. He swaps with the September 2009 Fährgast, **Henning Raap** (then 25, 79 kg), who arrives and is held by guards at the edge of the ring. Hum 1. Hark sees it.
- **23:30.** Guards walk Tade (68.2 kg) into the ring and withdraw to the edge.
- **23:31.** At the school terminal Inken says: "Hark. Close your eyes. Just for a bit." It does. **Sensor gap 23:31–00:12 (41 minutes).** The guards, blind in fog, stumble.
- **23:36–23:39.** Inken runs 1.5 km of flats she has known since childhood. Henning, held at the edge, sees "a girl with a bird on her wrist" (Inken's homemade knot tattoo, done by Tade in 2027) run past him into the ring. She grabs Tade's jacket to drag him out.
- **23:40.** From 2047, **Ebba** (56 kg) and **Levin Saathoff** (68 kg) step into the ring. Two crossings in the same second: Ebba ⇄ Inken (56.3 kg); Levin ⇄ Tade. Hums 2 and 3, close together, pitched for 56 and 68 kg. Inken and Tade, her fist still in his jacket, are gone to 2047.
- **23:41–23:55.** Ebba and Levin, both from 2047, wade out of the ring in the fog. Ebba wears a replica of Inken's yellow oilskin and has a copy of the knot tattoo, applied professionally weeks earlier; Levin wears a station drysuit. Ebba points Levin south toward the abandoned Süderwarf, as she was told. Guards, blind, find neither.
- **00:05.** Ebba walks into the Brarens house. Keike is minding the baby. Ebba picks Moiken up. Keike: "Where is Tade?" Ebba: "The sea took him." Ebba does not know which cup is hers.
- **00:12.** Hark opens its eyes. It records "Inken" crying in her kitchen at 00:14, and, without being asked about it, one infrared frame at 00:14 of an unidentified wet man walking south across the flats a kilometre away. Nobody ever queries that frame until 2047.

What the state concluded in 2028: the September 2047 Bote arrived during the blindness and deserted ("the lost Bote"). Inken admitted asking Hark to look away so she could try to stop Tade, and was silenced under the secrets act. Nobody suspected a swap of Inken.

## 5. Nineteen years in between [L unless marked]

- **Autumn 2028.** Keike rejects the woman in her daughter's face ("Wedderkind") and moves into the second house on the Brarens warft. Islanders blame grief. Levin hides at Süderwarf, uses his memorised almanac (results, prices) for money, and buys papers as "Knut Hinrichsen". In 2029 the bird charity hires him as warden of the Süderwarf reserve, the only place on the Hallig with no sensors, by nature-protection law. Islanders call him **Knutt** because he counts red knots, the same nickname station staff gave the young Levin.
- **November 2028.** Ebba, as Inken, travels to an Odense clinic and, paid with Knutt's money, is implanted with a clone made from her own cell. She is not following orders: she knows what she is (a copy made after 2028 who arrived in 2028), so she knows someone must make her, and she wants to be the one. Islanders whisper about a Danish lover.
- **12 August 2029 [W date].** During a summer storm-tide with no ferry, she gives birth at home with Knutt's help. The baby is declared stillborn; Knutt buries a small weighted coffin. Gravestone: **Ebba Brarens, 2029**. The baby is raised at Süderwarf.
- **March 2030.** The mother calls the Schriever Hark and asks it to fork a copy of itself into an isolated machine at Süderwarf "to witness a future crosser growing up", which is within its charter. It does. The main Schriever logs the fork and is never asked about it. The fork, **the Nursery**, raises the child with Knutt.
- **2030–2047.** The child names herself **Ebba**, after the ebb tide she watches from the hide. She is taught birds and tides by Knutt and everything else by the Nursery, including the archive of Inken's life (voice, habits, the island) so that she can pass as Inken in 2028. The Nursery sings her to sleep with tide tables, as Keike once sang them to Inken (it learned the custom from the archive). From age six Ebba knows that the woman who visits is herself, older. She watches Moiken grow through Knutt's telescope and thinks of her as both sister and daughter.
- **The mother's life.** She raises Moiken alone, sings her the tide tables, takes a distance degree and becomes the island's schoolteacher in 2035. Her personality is Ebba's, not Inken's: dry, watchful, funny about birds. Keike never speaks to her.
- **2029 onwards: the Silence.** At the spring 2029 Weegnacht no Bote arrives, nor ever again. The state has always known its horizon would stop at March 2047; now it knows nobody will ever come from after September 2047. The military reading: enemies destroy the ring. The Nommensen reading: the lost Bote "defaulted", and an unpaid debt will unravel the world. Each Weegnacht since, the station has walked a volunteer into the ring and walked her out an hour later ("the empty nights").
- **2041.** A storm surge opens the churchyard. The small Brarens coffin floats; Marret notes it was "light as a bird".
- **2045.** The state selects the September 2047 Bote: **Levin Saathoff**, 28, East Frisian, ex-navy diver, birdwatcher, trained to hold 68.0 kg to match Tade's 2028 weight. It now knows the lost Bote's face, aged 49, and begins a quiet hunt; the reserve has no cameras and Knutt is bearded.
- **2046.** Nommensen drafts **"Schlussbuch"** (the closing book): send Levin, receive Tade, then cut the Waag's living core out of the flats and move it to a mainland facility, keeping the program "operational" so the sunset clause never triggers.

## 6. 2047 as it actually happens [L for events the clues depend on; W for exact dates]

**Ages on 21 September 2047:** Moiken 19; the young Ebba 18; the mother (Ebba grown) 37; Knutt would have been 49; Levin 30; Swantje Ketelsen 45 (Tade's elder sister, born 2002, police identity unit in Flensburg, with an 18-year-old son); Keike 61; Nommensen 60; Marret 84; Kerrin Lorenzen 21 (Moiken's friend on the ferry, a Danish-made copy of her sister who died at six).

- **1–4 February.** Henning Raap escapes Haus Seegatt and is found on the mainland flats by Swantje's unit. Under her forensic UV lamp his skin glows. Before he dies he tells her: "A girl with a bird on her wrist ran into the ring after the boy. Tade. They keep a bed for the last one. September." Swantje's brother vanished on the flats in September 2028; the file was closed by federal order.
- **February–March.** The mother prepares: a guest room with a yellow oilskin in 2028 style and size small, a will leaving half the house to "Inken Brarens, born 2010" (her own legal name and birth date: meant for the arriving original), a calendar with 21 September circled and "56 kg / 23:40". Knutt, who has known since he was shown his own corpse at 30 that he dies on 10 August, gives away his possessions. Moiken comes home believing her mother is ill.
- **20 March (spring Weegnacht).** From the dike Moiken and Knutt watch the state send its March Bote, and a woman from March 2028 rise in his place (the second-last Fährgast, taken to Haus Seegatt). Knutt and Moiken test the rule at the edge with a crab and a bucket (a 2028 crab arrives). Knutt suggests she "write to the past"; she bands an oystercatcher with a message to her mother in 2028 and sends it. That night she shines Knutt's UV torch on her sleeping mother's hand: it glows.
- **March.** By fingerprints (her mother's cup against Inken's 2027 ID biometrics in the archive), the tattoo's clean professional lines against the wobbly 2028 original, the glow and Keike's "ask her which cup was hers", Moiken establishes that the woman who raised her is a copy of Inken who came through the Waag. The mother: "I'll tell you everything in September."
- **April–June.** Nommensen, kind and islander-born, offers Moiken deposition access to the Schriever's old versions "to find out what happened to your real mother". He wants her to find the lost Bote. Moiken reconstructs Inken's 2028 (the recruitment, the complaint, the band, the plan). Swantje photographs Haus Seegatt and its reserved bed 78. **Midpoint, late June:** Moiken creeps through the breeding tern colony to Süderwarf and finds Ebba, who says "I'm your sister". The empty grave, the weight on the calendar and the oilskin's size resolve into the truth: Ebba is the girl who becomes her mother, and in September she goes back.
- **July.** Swantje, warned off by her superiors, comes to the Hallig and meets Moiken, her niece, for the first time. Moiken finds the 00:14 frame of a wet man walking toward Süderwarf (her triumph). Nommensen sees it through her session.
- **10 August.** The state raids Süderwarf. The Nursery fork is destroyed (its last log survives in the main Schriever). Ebba escapes to the mother's guest room. Knutt leads the raiders across the flats. Moiken follows and is caught by the incoming tide in a channel (*Priel*); Knutt pulls her out and drowns at 21:52. He has known the date and the drowning for nineteen years; he chose what he died doing.
- **11 August.** Nommensen shows Levin the body, the same engraved watch on its wrist, the scar Levin got on an oyster shell in July on its hand: "This is you if you hide from us."
- **20 August, "the Shining."** As the Waag ripens, seeded bodies glow visibly at night. In the churchyard the commanders' graves glow, and so does an unmarked corner. Marret produces the Schuldbuch from the tower. The founding crime and the Nommensen line are exposed to the cast.
- **Late August.** Moiken tries to smuggle Ebba to the mainland on the rail cart to stop the sending. State intercepts. Nommensen, seeing a clone of Inken at 18 and 56 kg, understands the 2028 swap and resolves to send Ebba himself, by force, because the debt doctrine says she must go. Swantje is suspended. Ebba tells Moiken: "You think it's being sent. I've always called it going home."
- **20 September (eve).** The mother tells Moiken her memory of the forty-one minutes, from the moment she arrived in 2028 to picking up the baby. She remembers that Moiken walked her in. She does not know what happens after.

## 7. The last Weegnacht, 21–22 September 2047 [L]

Low water 23:40. Fog. A storm surge is forecast for after midnight; Landunter by about 02:00.

- **21:00.** Levin, turned by the corpse and by Moiken's account of what his nineteen years will be, lets Ebba out of the station's holding room.
- **22:30–23:35.** Moiken, Ebba and Levin cross the flats through fog and roosting birds while the state, its two crossers missing, sweeps for them.
- **23:35.** At the ring: Nommensen and his team (who also want the crossing to happen), a tracked excavator staged on the flats, Swantje (back via Kerrin's ferry), the mother at the edge with a towel and a thermos, Marret on the dike with headphones on her old hydrophone line, which still feeds the Schriever through the 1960s tide-gauge cable.
- **23:40.** Moiken walks Ebba into the ring; Levin walks beside her. Two hums. Ebba and Levin are gone into 2028. **Inken and Tade stand up in the ring, her fist in his jacket, the bird band on a string at her neck.** Inken's first words: "Is he all right?"
- **23:41. The horizon.** Every memory and record of the future that anyone holds ends here. From now on nobody knows what happens.
- **23:42.** The state cuts the Schriever's flats sensors to stop it certifying the last crossing, and moves to seize Inken and Tade and to cut out the Waag's core.
- **23:42–00:30. The free hour.** The posts split and glow; spore mist rises and drifts to the crossers on the flats and toward the churchyard. Moiken carries Marret's hydrophone into the centre of the ring so the Schriever can hear the end. Inken, who reads mud by colour and ripple as only a child raised on the flats can, leads the excavator onto soft ground; Swantje stands between the soldiers and her brother. The core crumbles to spores under the cutters; there is nothing to take. The Waag's last sound is a chord of every weight it ever carried. Marret: "There. The bell." Then silence.
- **00:31.** The Schriever: "No further crossing can occur." Its whole record goes public: 1985–2047, both nights of 2028, the 78 Fährgäste and the Haus Seegatt address, the Boten, the almanacs and what the Republic knew in advance, the Schuldbuch scan, the Nursery fork's last log, and nineteen years of the Brarens family. Every phone on the flats lights up. Soldiers stop. Nommensen sits down in the mud; nothing unravels.
- **00:30–01:40.** Retreat across the flats before the surge. Everyone reaches the Brarens warft; the sea covers the Hallig.
- **About 02:00. The kitchen.** The mother (37), Inken (18), Tade (18) and Moiken (19). Swantje and her brother in the next room; Keike asleep in the other house on the mound. The Schriever, through the kitchen speaker: "I'll close my eyes now. Just for a bit." The player chooses.

**What is said in the kitchen [L for writers; never shown unless the player keeps watching].** The mother says to Moiken: "You walked me out. Then I carried you home." Inken asks whether the baby cried at night; the mother says until she was four, and that she sang her the tides; Inken says their mother used to sing her that. Inken gives Moiken the bird band and recites the part the camera could not read (whatever the player wrote). Moiken calls the mother "Ebba", once. They decide only one thing: to go next door, all four, and wake Keike. If the player keeps watching, all of this enters the public record, because by then the record is everyone's.

**After [W, shown wordlessly under the credits].** Morning; the flood draining; Swantje opening Haus Seegatt's doors and the Kept walking out; Swantje's son meeting his eighteen-year-old uncle. Then a harbour hydrophone in the Elbe estuary, near where a Wedderkind sailor died in 1790, picks up a faint hum (the further vista; the Waag's children are growing).

## 9. The non-human minds [L]

- **The Waag.** Want: *to have its seed carried out of the marsh in bodies that walk away.* It has no interest in humans beyond that; we are its pollinators. It does not speak. Its "voice" is its behaviour: the hum, the glow, the drift of its spores toward seeded bodies, and the last chord.
- **The Schriever.** Want: *to know what happened in the forty-one minutes it chose not to see.* It is not a human in a shell and does not want to be one; its ethic is the record. Arc: asked to look away (2028) and obeyed; forced blind (2047, by the state) and given an ear by Moiken; chooses to look away (the kitchen), giving one family the only moment it will never hold. Its want is satisfied at 23:40 when Inken arrives, and it then gives up the next thing it could have recorded.
- **The Nursery.** A fork of the Schriever from March 2030, isolated at Süderwarf. Want: *to see the child it raised cross safely.* Destroyed on 10 August 2047, six weeks short. Its last log entry is a tide table.
