# Writer C, round 3: options rounds (revision 2)

Starting point: **ending first.** I fixed the final emotional image and the anchor scenes, built the hidden history backward until the ending was inevitable, and then planted the clues.

This revision follows the grader's review. Every round now has rivals developed to paragraph depth, and the ending round was redone because the first pick reproduced a Code Geass signature beat sequence. Changes are logged in `08-revision-log.md`.

Working title: **The Ouro's Hand**

## Seeds (mandatory, all used)

| Seed | Value |
|---|---|
| galactic_setting | a war-front system under a fifty-year ceasefire |
| intrigue | a censor who rewrites the news; a royal bastard with a valid claim |
| economic_system | a currency backed by stored computation |
| technology | orbital weapons under treaty limits; surveillance dust |
| culture_era | Coptic Egypt, 4th century |
| profession | court interpreter |
| object | porcelain teacup with a crack; lacquered bento box |
| phenomenon | grafting |
| relationship | neighbours sharing a wall |
| ritual | the day of the dead |

**How the culture seed is used.** Coptic Egypt of the 300s is a naming and texture source for a far-future culture, not a historical setting. It supplies:

- the nomes and the title *ouro*;
- the calendar with its little month Nesi;
- the feast name Nairouz, attached to an **invented** place-setting rite for the war dead;
- the habit of writing on sherds (ostraca);
- the holok (from *holokottinos*);
- Kellia's cells;
- personal names in worn-down forms (see 03, section 5);
- the banks, now drawn from the non-monastic notaries of Djeme.

The seeded bento box becomes a **kibot** (from Coptic *kibôtos*, chest), and lacquer is refined Wall sap.

## Round 0: the final emotional image

**I1. The knock at the wall.** On the day of the dead, a one-armed woman sets places at the wall she shares with an alien neighbour who no longer remembers her. She can knock or not.

**I2. Two copies at one table.** The heroine and a woman with her face, woken from a vat and only weeks old in mind, set places for the dead together. The younger drinks from the cracked cup, and the older reads its crack for her.

**I3. The empty throne room.** The unknown sovereign watches someone else crowned in her place.

**Pick: I1 and I2 fused.** The wall, the knock and "near" carry the neighbour relationship. The two copies at one table carry the cloning question the review asked for: what copies owe each other. I3 was the shape of the first draft's erased-heroine ending and is dropped, because it echoes Code Geass.

## Round 1: setting and scale

- **S1. The Shiet front and the Kellia ring.** A ring-city straddling the ceasefire line, split by a living Wall, with human and alien homes back to back. The system holds the vault-ring of disarmed weapons, the remaining sickles, and a debt-labour hulk. The capital is six jump-days away.
- **S2. The capital far from the front.** The whole story is set at Shmin's court, among 42 governors and their debt. The front appears only through news the censor edits, and the plot is pure succession politics. This gives the most court intrigue and makes the censor central, but the war system becomes a rumour.
- **S3. The armistice convoy.** A neutral fleet of 300 ships orbits forever between the two powers' systems, carrying both delegations, the vaults and the courts. Every scene is aboard moving ships, and the ceasefire is literally the fleet staying in motion. It is striking, but every location is a ship of one fleet.

**Pick: S1, with S2's court brought on screen** (Shmin in Act 2; five governors at the Hearing; Horsi throughout). S1 makes the wall seed and the war-front seed one object. S3 risks the banned single-community shape.

## Round 2: central question (redone; Q1 and Q2 were one question in two registers)

- **Q1. "Is a copy the same person, and who gets to say?"** Human law says yes (one body, one name). Bnne biology says yes differently (a cutting is the root). The heroine's own life says no. Each pillar tests it:
  - cloning: the Law of the Body;
  - aliens: the root;
  - AGI: the reserve recognises a genome, not a person;
  - time travel: is the past self who receives the clips the same woman who sends them?
- **Q2. "Who pays for a peace?"** Money made of disarmed weapons, a treaty that needs a body, savers holding the bonds. The ending would decide who bears the cost: the debtors, the creditors, the alien, or the heroine's flesh.
- **Q3. "Is a faithful translation one that is true, or one that keeps both sides alive?"** The interpreter's ethic. Talou's forgery is the founding case, and the heroine either repeats or refuses it.

**Pick: Q1, with Q3 as Tamoun's personal form.** Q1 has several defensible answers and forces the ending to rule on one in public (the Hearing). Q2 is answered in the economics but has no single dilemma for one person. Q3 survives inside Tamoun's arc: she forges once to escape (Ch 19) and translates herself at the end.

## Round 3: the ending (redone)

**E1 (first draft, rejected). "The Ouro's Hand, erased."** Hidden claimant; a mentor's plan completes at a public ceremony; her betrayer-friend delivers the planned wound at her command; the censor erases her; she lives anonymously. **The review showed this reproduces Zero Requiem's beat sequence, so it is dropped as canon.** Its broadcast-lie survives only as a failing option (Ch 24).

**E2. "The Second Who Cut," then "The Hearing."**
- The claim is already public at the Act 3 turn. Talou's secret plan is abandoned for a public graft.
- At the union, the antagonist's second, sent to tear, is asked to cut and does. Under the Law of the Body that act is the villain's, so the villain legally renewed the peace.
- The censor airs the scar.
- Twenty days later the court of nomes asks the heroine whether she is the queen. Her answer ("I am not her") breaks the Law of the Body: copies become persons, the second gets a name, and every debt signed by a second is void. That achieves the villain's economic aim through the law she fought.
- Coda: two copies at the wall.

**E3. "The Open Crack, crowned."** The truth airs, and she accepts the throne by right of body. She reigns as sovereign and as indicted forger, refusing to repudiate the bonds, and the realm splits. Coda in the palace.

**Pick: E2, with E3 kept as the "I am her" branch.** E2 breaks every matched beat:
- the claimant is public, not hidden;
- the mentor's plan fails as designed;
- the wound comes from the enemy's copy choosing, not from a friend obeying;
- the truth is aired, not erased;
- she lives under her own name.

E2 also puts the succession and economic stakes on screen, and it answers the central question by choice. E3 is a respected alternative for players who choose the crown.

## Round 4: hidden history

- **H1. The two-language trap.** The Bnne clause obliges both sides to send a shoot of each signer's root at the fruiting, after which "the two trees are one and need no keeper". The queen orders it changed to protect her son's throne from her own clones. The interpreter prunes it before it calluses and grafts a slip that reads to the Bnne as the same obligation and to humans as "fifty years, renewed by the Ouro's hand". Then she steals a clone and raises it as an interpreter.
- **H2. The Bnne forgery.** The trade-groves altered the clause to bind humans to a renewal they could exploit. The heroine uncovers alien interference.
- **H3. The bluff.** No treaty was signed. Both sides saw mutual destruction in short backward sends, and the Daybook has kept each side believing in a treaty that does not exist.

**Pick: H1.**
- It is a human lie about human succession, and every present faction is downstream of it.
- The obligation is visibly two-way, which breaks the banned "debt to a bound alien" silhouette.
- It is an interpreter's crime that the heroine will later commit herself.

H2 is "aliens behind human troubles". H3 empties the renewal.

## Round 5: world rules per pillar (redone at full depth)

### Aliens

- **A1. A living grafted language.** Utterances are slips that keep growing and callus in three days. Memory travels through callused grafts, a clean cut is safe and a tear kills. Verb: graft-reading and writing.
- **A2. The tide-speakers.** The Bnne communicate by synchronising their metabolic rhythms across distance, so a conversation is two bodies settling into one pulse. An interpreter wears a rhythm-cuff and must slow or speed their heart to "agree". Forging means entraining someone into a false agreement. Verb: rhythm-matching, a physiological stealth game. Strong, but the language cannot be cut or kept: it leaves no physical evidence, so it cannot carry a forensic mystery.
- **A3. The colony-market.** Each Bnne is a hive whose inner economy is its thought: decisions are prices among a billion symbionts. To talk to one, you trade with its hive. An interpreter is a broker, and lying is market manipulation. Verb: reading internal price boards. Powerful, but it merges the alien pillar into the economic system, so the two stop being separate engines.

**Pick: A1.** It is the grafting seed, it leaves physical evidence (callus, cut faces), and it gives memory loss a mechanism.

### AGI

- **G1. The Oikonomos, steward of the reserve and the dust.** It answers paid questions exactly and prices them by risk to its own vaults. Its want is not to be rearmed into nonexistence. Verb: audit, and phrasing a question it will price cheaply.
- **G2. The sickle-minds.** Each of the 14 armed sickles carries its own frozen targeting intelligence, built in the war and sealed since. They must vote unanimously to fire. The treaty keeps them asleep; the lapse wakes them, and they remember the war as yesterday. Verb: interviewing war-minds who think it is W.Y. -1. Rich, but a committee of veterans repeats the old-soldier role Apol already fills.
- **G3. The Daybook engine.** The censor is assisted by an AGI that generates plausible news and must be steered. Verb: editing with a co-writer that pushes its own preferences. It would split the censor's human dilemma in two.

**Pick: G1,** now with its stake (its mind is its vaults), so it collides with the economy and the war clock. G2 survives as texture: the sickles' war-minds hum on Apol's platform.

### Cloning

- **C1. The Law of the Body.** One body, one name. Seconds sign and sin for their originals, the crown passes to "the Ouro's body", and a dead Ouro's sleepers are relics.
- **C2. Clone debt-heirs.** Debts pass to a debtor's woken copies, so the poor grow sleepers to inherit bondage and the rich buy their copies' freedom. The heroine would be a copy who inherited a queen's war debt. It is economically sharp, but it makes clones purely a finance instrument.
- **C3. Exchanged hostages.** The treaty is held by swapped clones: each side raises copies of the other's leaders. The heroine would be a human-raised copy of a Bnne-allied noble. It is good spy material, but the Bnne have no clones in this world and the shape is generic.

**Pick: C1.** It makes the bastard's claim statutory, makes Tamoun legally the forger, gives Maro's cut its double meaning, and sets the Hearing's question. C2's debt logic is absorbed: debts signed by seconds are voided at the Hearing.

### Time travel

- **T1. Paid backward motes.** Nine seconds each, quadratic cost, fixed timeline, signers only. Verbs: assay hunt and the final cut.
- **T2. Grafted minds.** A mind can be grafted back into one's own younger sleeper, but each sleeper takes only one graft and the sender's body dies. It is strong on the cloning collision, but it fuses two pillars into one mechanic.
- **T3. Ring-reading.** The Wall's growth rings record the past in sap chemistry, and a skilled reader can "hear" any day in the last fifty years. It is purely backward-looking, which duplicates the audit.

**Pick: T1.** It welds three seeds (dust, computation, treaty limits) into one rule. Unlike the first draft, the inference it supports is now stated: R7, the pawned cores' 2 percent.

## Round 6: the political and economic system (redone)

- **P1. Holok and Wall Bonds.** Money is computation stored in disarmed sickle cores; rearming wipes a vault, so war burns money. Debtors are hawks and creditors are doves. A debtor's hedge (wood-routes from a Bnne trade faction) makes the burn rational.
- **P2. Route sovereignty.** The real currency is jump routes: whoever computes routes controls trade. The crown, the banks and the Bnne fight over route-computing licences, and the ceasefire is a route-sharing agreement. It is clean, but disconnected from the weapons.
- **P3. Grafted credit.** Bnne trade-groves issue living credit: a graft whose growth is interest. Human banks hold Bnne grafts as reserves, and a dying grove is a bank run. It is fascinating, but it overloads the alien language with finance.

**Pick: P1, absorbing P2** (wood-routes versus vault routes is P2's fight inside P1). P3 survives as texture in the Koiak groves.

## Round 7: founding crime

- **F1.** The queen-ordered two-language forgery (H1).
- **F2. The charter backdoor.** The Treasury has secretly minted against the reserve for fifty years, and the "stored computation" is a third imaginary. The renewal would require an audit that exposes it. Strong economics, but the founding lie would belong to the bank, not to the treaty, and the alien and interpreter seeds would lose their centre.
- **F3. The war's last strike.** The ceasefire was signed hours after a human sickle, steered by a backward send, killed a Bnne grove-world. The Daybook's first act was to erase it. A war crime under the peace. Powerful, but it makes the Bnne a wronged victim owed restitution, which approaches the banned shape.

**Pick: F1.** F2's rumour remains as texture.

## Round 8: the intrigue structure (redone)

- **I-A. A cross-Wall web.** On the human side: the Treasury's false-flag informer (Phib), the bank's plant in the Daybook (Tsia), and the censor-spymaster. On the Bnne side: a trade-grove spy (Hathor) in the envoy's household, allied with the Treasurer. A governor holds hostages. Spies meet where dust cannot see.
- **I-B. A mirror service.** Each side runs a formal intelligence service with agents across the Wall. The plot is a defector's run. Clear and genre-faithful, but it turns the Bnne into a human-style spy agency and loses the dust-blind geography.
- **I-C. A mole hunt.** One leak in the Daybook is passing edits to someone, and the whole plot is finding them. Tight, but too small for court and galactic scope.

**Pick: I-A,** now with the Bnne court and Hathor added, as the review requested.

## Round 9: the anchor (redone; one rival is not the hidden heir)

- **N1. Tamoun Besa, interpreter, the queen's woken sleeper.** The bastard seed made statutory. The review's caution is answered: she is also legally the forger, a second body exists (Nesi), and she renounces the claim.
- **N2. Pamin, the censor, as sole lead.** A middle-aged spymaster whose job is hiding things. The mystery would be his own past edits catching up with him, and the royal bastard would be someone he must decide whether to expose. It is strong on intrigue, but a lead who hides things strains "never let a playable character conceal what they know".
- **N3. Senetom's apprentice notary, Tsia, as lead.** A bank clerk who reads contracts, not grafts. She finds the forged instrument through the notarial record and hunts the hidden claimant as collateral, then falls for the claimant's cause. Not the heir. This puts economics at the centre and gives an outsider's view of the claimant. It loses the interpreter seed's hands-on verb and the wall relationship.

**Pick: N1, with N2 and N3 kept as the other two playable viewpoints** (Pamin), or as a grown side arc (Tsia becomes Archnotary). N3 was a genuine contender, and it is the trap-door lead if Tamoun's thread is cut.

## Round 10: premise sentence (redone; three different mechanics)

- **P-1.** "An interpreter learns the living peace treaty can only take her body." (Body as destiny.)
- **P-2.** "When money is disarmed weapons, an interpreter learns the peace needs her body." (Economy plus body.)
- **P-3.** "A censor must decide whether a realm may see its peace was forged." (The censor as lead; a different story.)

**Pick: P-2,** as the grader advised. It names the economic mechanic that makes the hawks debtors and the body-dilemma that drives the plot. P-1 reads as fate. P-3 is the N2 story.

## Round 11: key scenes (redone)

**Opening.**
- **O1. Tea through the wall,** then the welcome with the Bnne bud shown. **Picked:** domestic, alien and two-way at once.
- **O2. The Daybook at dawn.** We open on the censor editing a sickle accident into a drill, then cut to the interpreter reading the result on a market board. Strong irony, but it opens on the system, not the person.
- **O3. The Nairouz tables a year earlier.** We open on the day of the dead with the heroine setting a place for Talou, so the ritual is planted before the plot. It bookends the coda beautifully but delays the inciting death by a full scene.
