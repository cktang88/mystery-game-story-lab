# Writer B: world, rules and factions (revision 2)

## 1. The place: Amole, the carved city

**Amole** (አሞሌ, after the salt bars once used as money on this road) is a highland megacity of about **19 million** people cut *downward* into a basalt plateau above the salt flats. The founders took the old highland technique of carving churches out of living rock and applied it to everything, because shade and stone mass were the only defence against the Burning Years. Streets are **trenches**; buildings are **monoliths** left standing when the rock round them was removed; each quarter is a **pit** open to a square of sky. Windows are cut, never built. Stairs are cut, so they wear, and knees wear with them.

- **The Narrow:** the deepest trench, the old salt road from the depression, still walked by camel caravans.
- **The Guest's Spur:** a dead-end cut off the Narrow, behind one brass-barred gate sixty metres from the Guest's door.
- **Bete Tsehafi (the record tower):** the tallest monolith, at the head of the Narrow. Its iron-leaf stacks spiral down into the rock; the telex room where the foresight trade wires its news abroad is on its upper floors; the hall and the fig are beneath.
- **The Gora house:** the Lemlem lineage's monolith in the Gora pit, with cells, a laundry and kiln, the glass room, the founder's room, and the east cistern stair (the cistern has been dry for decades).
- **Other places:** the Tunneler galleries (old machine halls cut sideways into pit walls); the kiln quarter (jar-houses); the Council Pit with its great warka tree, open to the sky; the Arho caravanserai at the Narrow's mouth.
- **Faith is part of the city, not set dressing.** The Gora pit has its own carved church: dawn chant rises up the pit walls, priests in white cross the trench at feast days, and the church has always baptised each copy separately, giving each body her own baptismal name even though the law gives them one name between them. The Arho, who are Afar and Muslim, stop to pray at every halt on the Narrow, and Dahir prays before he walks the Guest in. Neither faith is put to work in the plot, and no rite is staged as a puzzle; both are there as a voice in the factions' chorus, and the church's quiet disagreement with the law is one of the iceberg's layers.
- **Shola figs** grow in every pit. Children split the fruit and find the dead wasp inside: *"the mother stays inside."*

**Its late-developmental moment** (seed: 1980s Seoul). Seventy years of rule by the elders and a military line have built the city fast and hard. Now: a students' and copies' movement, tear gas pooling in the pits (it is heavier than air), neon cut into basalt, televised housing lotteries, cassettes, stenotype, film radiographs, and the first direct election in the city's history. The resemblance to Korea's 1987 is acknowledged in the influence log and **bent on purpose**: the death that forced the election was a copy's, which the court ruled was "nobody's"; the regime's general is the honourable man in the story; and the side that hides a death is the opposition.

**Naming logic.** Amharic and Ge'ez words in their ordinary meanings; a given name plus the father's given name. Copies take their template's name as their "father's name" (every Lemlem copy is legally **Lemlem Lemlem**) and use personal use-names (Tigist, Almaz) alongside their separate baptismal names. Tunnelers keep a Korean given name and take an Ethiopian patronymic (Jae-won Tesfaye). The Arho use Afar names (Dahir, Hamadu). The doctrine is spelled **Ande Sew** ("one person"); the player-facing name is **the One Person law**.

**Player-facing vocabulary (one name per concept).** The Guest; the scribe; a fever (receiving); sending; the Shadow (until the midpoint), then Sitota; the One Person law; the court and its judge; the elders; the steward; the election; the record tower; the Fig Guard; the Hand. The Amharic words (Engida, Tsehafi, ra'iy, melikt, Chilot, Afe Chilot, Shimagile, Azaj, Mircha, Pagume, Arho, Fitawrari) are heard only in faction voices and seen in world text, never as interface labels.

## 2. The four rules

### Time: sending and fevers

1. A clay-grown body, or a template whose genome has been grown in clay, can send the last stretch of her own lived experience (sight, sound, touch, her own voice) back in time.
2. **Hard cap:** one turn of a sixteen-minute glass of black sand.
3. Only into one body of the **same genome**, alive at the target moment, that has **never received** (possibly her own earlier body).
4. Not before the genome's first jar.
5. Addressing needs a hand on the scribe's iron plate (one in every glass room and on the hall's dais).
6. **A fever:** one night from the target moment, the sixteen minutes lived in fragments, and a **rust ring** in one iris for life. By law every fever is sat in a glass room under a shaft so its receipt can be entered on the **register of open receipts** (listed by genome, date and glass room, never by body). A body receives once.
7. **Cost:** the sender loses all lived memory between the target moment and the send; "the hands keep what the hands learned."
8. **History is fixed.** Every fever received was sent; a send aimed at a body that never received fails at no cost ("no ring, no road").

**Damages:** Almaz and Aster. **Only it can save:** the Guest's patience and so its offspring. **Verbs:** fever-reading (walk a rebuilt vision; date, place and identify its sender from body evidence) and staging (live sixteen minutes you know will be sent; choose who pays).

### Cloning: the clay-born

1. A copy is grown from a person's cells in a **sealed terracotta jar** for **one hundred days** and born a baby with no memories.
2. Copies share a genome and differ in palm lines, fingerprints, scars, wear and bone history.
3. The hundredth day leaves a **clay line** in the long bones; the first jars (2089-91) left a doubled line.
4. **The One Person law:** all bodies of one genome are one legal person (one name, vote, ticket, estate and guilt). The steward runs the lineage. A child's single receipt belongs to her person, so the steward may sell it.
5. Killing a body is not murder while another body lives.
6. Unregistered bodies are **Pagume**: non-persons, seized and "returned" to their lineage or to clay.
7. Dead copies are **returned to clay**, ground into new jars.

**Damages:** Tigist and Almaz. **Only it can save:** Tigist and Hiwot (the lineage could not take Hiwot from "herself"). **Verbs:** bone-reading and passing.

### AGI: the scribe

1. It seals each day at midnight into an **iron leaf** holding that day's version of itself.
2. **Where it hears.** Shafts are cut only under carved roofs, in public halls, ration halls, gates, glass rooms and lineage common halls. There are **none** in open air (the Council Pit), inside the Guest's house (a 2090 condition of the bargain), in Tunneler galleries (the Tunnelers' 2090 price) or in private cells. A gate's shaft hears the gate, not the street beyond it.
3. It counts gates by the genome in the sweat on a brass bar, so its tallies name a person, never a body.
4. **Waking.** Only a party may wake a leaf; any body of a genome is a party to every record of that genome. **One wake per body per day.** A woken leaf testifies as that day's scribe (it replays what it heard and answers within that day's knowledge), never lies, never volunteers, then **rusts** for good. Every wake goes on the public wake-register **by genome only**. A party may seal her own leaf **two-handed**, so that two bodies of her genome must wake it together.
5. It addresses every send.

**Want:** that no record be kept from the one it concerns. Every leaf since December 2090 closes "**Outstanding: one**": the founder's receipt, which it heard dictated and was ordered to keep off the register, and which concerns a sender who has not yet come.
**Damages:** privacy between copies. **Only it can save:** the hostage, whose birth it alone recorded. **Verb:** leaf-waking, a rationed, public move.

### Aliens: the Guest

1. It perceives beings only by genome.
2. It is silent and speaks only by **exchange**: an object given and an object returned form one sentence. It cannot take without giving. It refuses only by returning the very object it was given.
3. Its kind live as fig-wasp foundresses: they bring "pollen" (jars and sending) to a people, ask for a chamber built to their pattern, shed their pleated wings at its mouth, enter, seed and die; one hundred days later one successor emerges.
4. **It sheds only when the body it holds as pledge in its house dies** (the Arho know this).
5. It cannot open its chamber itself. It is free to walk where it likes, and the city takes nothing from it or from the fig.

**Want:** to enter the chamber it was promised, seed, and end there.
**Damages:** every Lemlem body. **Only it can save:** Hiwot's future (she becomes the first fluent speaker of exchange among the city's counted persons; Sitota, fluent all her life, was never counted).
**Verb:** **exchange**, played as a constrained statement, never as "use item on NPC". The player chooses one object from Tigist's (or Hiwot's) inventory to give; the Guest gives one back; the pair is entered in the **Guest's lexicon**, a page of object pairs, and the player assigns each pair a meaning from a small set of glyphs (*yes, no, wait, this one, came from, carried, promised, again*). Meanings are confirmed in batches of three, never one at a time. The main path needs only four pairs (`05`, chapter 9); every other pair is optional depth.

### The collision

Fevers travel only within a genome (**time needs cloning**); senders need the scribe to address a receiver (**time needs the AGI**); the scribe treats a genome as one account by law (**the AGI needs cloning law**); copies and fevers exist only because of the Guest's pollen (**cloning and time need the alien**); the Guest could be given a copy as the founder (**the alien needed cloning to be deceived**); and its blindness is the founders' cited evidence for the law (**the law needs the alien**). Together they raise the question none raises alone: *if the law, the ledger, the alien and the future all say 311 women are one person who has already kept her promise, who in that hall has to keep it?*

**The one admitted cheat**, spent on the premise: why clay-grown bodies can send experience backward is never explained.

**The key resource that creates problems: the receipt.** Each body can receive once. A lineage holds many receipts, so copies are grown partly *for* them, and the steward can sell a child's. This is the foresight trade, and the One Person law is what makes it legal. Free the copies and the city loses its richest export.

**Delivery order per lore fact:** place, then object, then sound, then only short in-world text with a named, biased author (jar-house plaques, the Fig Guard creed card, Arho songs, Hand pamphlets, ferenj contract forms, the church's baptism rolls, stenographers' notes).

**White space** (named, never shown): the city that did not build; clause 9; the lowland cities of the Burning Years; the buyers abroad; Yonatan's cell; the founder's natural-born family.

**Voices of the dead** are rationed. The climax carries one recorded voice from the dead (the first book). Aster's last tape is now in the naming beat with Mahlet's statement and a photograph. The 2131 voice comes earlier, in Act IV.

**Lore bible.** One keeper; what is shown in the game is canon; this file is locked where the solution depends on it.
