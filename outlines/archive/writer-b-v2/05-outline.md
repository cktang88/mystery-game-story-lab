# Writer B: outline, "The Jar That Was Never Opened"

(*Working title.* Alternatives: "Lemlem Lemlem"; "Outstanding: One". The grader may pick.)

## One sentence

Where copies count as one person, a copy chooses who repays their founder's promise.

## One paragraph

In Amole, a megacity carved downward into rock, every body grown from a person in a terracotta jar is legally that person: one name, one vote, one guilt. On the night before the city's first election, a veiled woman with the face of the founder's 311 copies is found dead at the door of the silent alien Guest, and the bonesetter Tigist, herself one of those copies, is asked to say who she was. **First disaster:** Tigist's bones prove the dead woman is not the Guest's veiled companion, yet when the lineage's lottery ticket wins on television, all 311 copies stand up: someone extra is passing among them. **Second disaster, caused by Tigist's own search:** her examination of the elders unmasks the passing woman as the hostage the founders pledged to the Guest in 2090, and Tigist's lie to protect her, her waking of the steward's fever record and her trust in the speaker of the elders together expose the hostage, get her taken and break her protector's hand. **Third disaster, caused by Tigist's rescue:** to free the hostage she wakes the founder's last testimony and learns that the city's founding promise will be paid on election night by whoever in the hall sends the founder her first vision, at the price of every memory they have lived. **Ending:** on election night the jar is opened, the first copy demands that her record be read to her face, the doctrine of one person falls and the vote count flips, the Guest walks through 311 identical women into its jar to seed and die, and the child Tigist is raising holds out the glass of black sand. Tigist pays with her whole remembered life, or lets the hostage pay with hers, and then decides whether to name the woman who raised her as the killer. A hundred days later the child holds out a fan to the Guest's successor, and it answers.

## Per-thread paragraphs

**Tigist (investigation).** A bonesetter who walked out of her lineage is called to identify a body that legally belongs to her; *therefore* she reads bones and records until she proves a death the law denies; *but* proving it reveals an extra body, and finding it makes her a liar, a conspirator and finally the one who must decide who pays.

**Jae-won (oath).** A stenographer sworn to deliver a dying friend's deposition on election night does not know whether the dead woman is his friend or the person his friend was trying to save; *therefore* he searches the wrong places (the Pagume underground, the Arho camp), finds the hostage's history in the alien's scattered house, joins Tigist; *but* his gallery becomes the hiding place that is betrayed and his hand is broken, so on the night he cannot take down, and has to speak.

**Hiwot (anchor).** A child with no name of her own gives the alien her fan and gets a figurine back; *therefore* she learns its grammar before anyone else; *but* that is why, in the hall, she is the one it reaches for, and the one who holds the price.

**Sitota (passing, playable from Act III).** The first copy, never counted, walks out of the alien's house in a dying woman's robe to reach the hall; *therefore* she must live as Aster among 310 sisters; *but* to stay she has to break her own wrist and trust the one bonesetter who can see through her.

**Almaz (killer, seen from outside).** The steward receives a vision of election night and believes it can be refused; *therefore* she kills the woman she thinks is the hostage; *but* she has killed her own sister, and the second book will show her that nothing was ever refused.

**Tsehay (fake ally).** The speaker of the elders knows she is meant to send that vision and swears she never will; *therefore* she betrays Tigist to keep the hostage from the hall; *but* in the hall, by reflex, she sends it.

**Dawit (honest red herring).** The Fig Guard commander wants the jar shut and looks like the killer; *but* he keeps his law when it costs him the election.

## Full outline

The game is in four acts and a coda, about twenty chapters, set over twenty days counted down on the city's Mircha boards (20 to 0). Each act opens on a small personal hook that turns out to carry lore. The player knows at each moment what each active character wants and what the current question is; the **current question** is shown here at the head of each chapter.

### Prologue: "Twenty Days" (22 August)

*Question: what is normal here?*

1. **A shoulder in the trench.** Tigist resets a Tunneler machinist's shoulder in her setting room (bone-reading tutorial: feel, rotate, set). Lime chalk, clay casts, radiograph plates drying on a line.
2. **A jar-house check-up.** Tigist checks a newborn copy at a jar-house and shows the player the **clay line** on a radiograph: every jar-born body has one. (Planted: the doubled line exists only in "the failed first jar", on a plaque.)
3. **Hiwot and the fig.** Walking Hiwot home past the shola figs, Hiwot splits a fig and finds the dead wasp: *"the mother stays inside."* School has handed out campaign fans; Hiwot's has Mekdes's face, and she starts painting the Guest over it.
4. **The Narrow at dusk.** They pass the Guest's Spur. The veiled Shadow is pouring coffee for the Guest at its door. Hiwot waves; the Guest does not move. A Fig Guard patrol watches it.
5. **Almaz visits.** Almaz brings food and asks Tigist to come back to the lineage for the roll. The player can see the **new rust ring** in her eye ("a fever last month") and the **ochre** on her fingertips (she still sets bones the old way for the house). She wears the office ring. She mentions Aster: "she goes to the Guest tonight; she's been ill."
6. **The Tunneler cafe.** Jae-won takes down a client's fever on stenotype (sound of keys, the client's broken sentences). A student at the next table tries to send a warning to his lineage-brother and fails: the glass runs, nothing goes. "No ring, no road." The table laughs; the student does not.
7. **Night.** On a ration leaf read out at a public tally hall, the player hears the leaf's closing line: "Outstanding: one."

*Planted:* clay line, doubled line legend, fig wasp, fan, Almaz's ring and ochre, Aster's visit, failed send, "Outstanding: one". *Down beat:* the whole prologue.

### Act I: "Nobody Died" (23-29 August)

Small hook: a caravan boy's dislocated shoulder at dawn.

**Chapter 1. The giveaway.** *Question: why is the Guest emptying its house?*
- Dawn, the Narrow. Tigist is resetting an Arho boy's shoulder when the Guest comes out and begins handing one object to each passer-by. Everyone gives something back. **Exchange tutorial:** it gives Tigist a camel's knucklebone; the player chooses what to give (she gives lime chalk). The pair is shown as a pair, no caption.
- Shouts from the Spur: Dahir's caravan has found a body at the Guest's door; the Fig Guard has already dragged it into the trench. The veil comes off: **the Lemlem face**. The crowd counts Lemlem bodies with their eyes. Almaz arrives to claim it. Dahir says she is ours, the Shadow. The Chilot's clerk sends for the identifier.

**Chapter 2. The body.** *Question: who is she?* New mechanic: full bone-reading.
- Haptic examination in a cold cut room (no text; Tigist's hands, the player's cursor, the sound of bone): neck (a rotary dislocation at the top of the spine, done by a trained hand: *a setter's hold, turned the wrong way*); knees and stairs (city wear, not caravan); spine (no carrier's compression); hands (soft); one clear clay line; vertebrae with **punched-out lesions** (bone sickness: she was dying); an old healed left-wrist break, set well.
- The player fills Tigist's certificate slots (age range, origin, occupation-wear, cause, "is this the Shadow?"). Confirmation comes in a batch. Tigist certifies: *a Lemlem elder, 60-70, dying, killed by a trained hand, not the Shadow.*
- The Chilot rules: the body is Lemlem Lemlem's; a Lemlem body died while others live; **nobody died**. Almaz has it taken to the jar-house to be returned to clay. Tigist, alone for two minutes, shoots **radiographs** of the whole skeleton (photography verb). Dahir: "Then where is she?"

**Chapter 3. The gate.** *Question: if she isn't the Shadow, where did the Shadow go?* New mechanic: leaf-waking.
- Tigist, as a Lemlem (a party to any Lemlem record), wakes the Spur gate leaf of 22 August at Bete Tsehafi. The leaf speaks in the Tsehafi's voice of that day and plays its gate sounds. The tallies are by genome: *Lemlem in 22:40, Lemlem out 23:35, Lemlem in 01:50, Lemlem out 02:25.* Natural-born: Dawit and five at 05:45.
- Puzzle: the Shadow lived beyond the gate; net, one Lemlem stayed. If the dead woman is not the Shadow, the Shadow **walked out** in the night, and one of the arrivals stayed and died. The leaf closes "Outstanding: one." The player watches it **rust** on its shelf, and sees the wake-register line: *woken by Lemlem Lemlem.*
- Cut to Almaz at the jar-house, hand flat on the sealed jar where the body is being returned to clay. She does not cry where anyone can see. (*Fair clue: why would the steward grieve the Shadow?*)

**Chapter 4. The stenographer** (Jae-won thread). *Question: is it the Shadow?*
- Jae-won in his Tunneler gallery plays cassettes of Aster's deposition (excerpts only; voice, no text): *"She has my face. She is older than any of us. They gave her as a pledge."* The player now knows the Shadow is a Lemlem body the lineage gave away, and that Aster meant to bring something to the hall on Mircha night. He does not know how. The last tape is in a Tunneler time-lock box set for 21:00 on Mircha night.
- He tries the Lemlem house to see Aster; the steward's men turn him away; "Aster" sends word through a servant that she is unwell. (*The player may note: Aster never refused him before.*)
- He finds Tigist at her setting room: he needs to know if the dead woman is the Shadow. The two leads meet on day one; their relationship is stated plainly. He does not tell her about the deposition (sworn); the player knows.

**Chapter 5. The fever** (Jae-won, then Tigist). *Question: what did Mahlet see?* New mechanic: fever-reading.
- Night of 25 August. The Lemlem house hires Jae-won to take down a girl's fever. **Capture mini-game:** Mahlet's broken sentences, gasps, a count ("eight"), "chalk", "the kiln". How well the player keeps up sets how much of the vision can be rebuilt; a minimum is guaranteed.
- Tigist rebuilds the vision as a **fever-space**: a walkable, fragmentary room assembled from the dictation and Mahlet's sketches. It is the Lemlem glass room. A Mircha board shows **8** (so 3 September); a clock says 16:00; chalked hands (adult or child: the fever blurs them) hold up a scrap of dark veil and point at the laundry kiln hatch. The sender is unknown. Mahlet's new rust ring.
- Etagegn, keeper of the glass room, demonstrates the rule by doing: she cannot remember 2110 to 2140, but her hands carve a perfect fig in the time it takes Tigist to ask. "The hands keep what the hands learned." **Rule named.** Mahlet's receipt goes up on the open-receipts register: *dated 3 September, sender unknown.*

**Chapter 6. The fan** (Hiwot, short playable). *Question: what does the Guest want?*
- Hiwot slips away to the Spur, where the Guest, under Guard watch, is giving away the last of its things. Child-height navigation through legs and Guard cordons. It holds out a small clay figurine. She gives it her fan (Mekdes's face, half painted over with the Guest). It takes it. **The first pleat falls** from its back and lies on the stone like a dropped fan.
- Tigist arrives, furious and frightened. The figurine goes on a string round Hiwot's neck.

**Chapter 7. The lottery** (Act I climax). *Question: if a Lemlem elder died, which elder?*
- 29 August. The televised housing lottery. Tigist and Jae-won watch at a trench cafe with the lineage-blind eye of the camera. The Lemlem's single ticket wins. By custom the winning person's bodies stand wherever they are, and the Tsehafi counts them live on the screen: **311.** In the cafe a Lemlem waitress stands, sits, keeps serving.
- A Lemlem elder is dead, and no Lemlem is missing. **Someone extra is standing.** That is the end of Act I's question and the start of Act II's.
- Coda: Almaz gives the won flat to Tigist and Hiwot (a gift that is also a leash: closer to the lineage, farther from the trench).

*Act I knowledge state:* the dead woman is a Lemlem elder killed by a trained hand; the Shadow is a Lemlem body the lineage pledged and is alive; one body among 311 is not who she says; someone in the future wanted the dead woman's veil saved. *Cost:* Aster, unknown to the player as yet; Tigist's distance from the lineage.

### Act II: "The Extra Body" (30 August - 4 September)

Small hook: Hiwot asks why the Guest called her "gift".

**Chapter 8. Gift.** *Question: what is the figurine?*
- Dahir sees the figurine round Hiwot's neck and goes grey: it is the object the Guest has used for seventy years to mean the Shadow. The Arho word for it is *sitota*. To the Guest, Hiwot is the Shadow; all of them are. The player can test it: give the Guest an object belonging to Almaz, then one belonging to Hiwot; it answers both identically. **It cannot tell the killer from the dead** (the alien's blindness, demonstrated).
- Dahir joins the search.

**Chapter 9. The scattered house** (Jae-won and Dahir). *Question: who is the Shadow?*
- The Guest's giveaway scattered seventy-three years of paired objects across the city. **Exchange-reading:** the player recovers pairs from the people who received them (trench vendors, children, a Guard sergeant, a ferenj trader), and each pair reassembles as a sentence in the empty house. Main path needs four: a jar-house birth token stamped with the first jar's mark and a child's tooth (*this one came from the jar*); a salt bar and a carrier's head-pad (*she carried*); a terracotta model of a fig and the stub of a seal (*the jar promised, not opened*); a black-sand grain in resin and a wing-pleat (*wait*). Optional: hundreds more.
- Jae-won checks the jar-house plaque: *the first jar failed.* The pairs say it did not. **The Shadow is the first copy ever made.** Larger question: what was she pledged for?
- Red-herring thread: Jae-won also searches the Pagume underground for a hiding Shadow (wrong, and the game says so when a Pagume woman shows him her single clay line).

**Chapter 10. The elders' leaves** (Tigist). *Question: which of thirteen elders is extra?*
- After Tigist's report, the Chilot orders the thirteen Lemlem elders over sixty identified by bone before the roll, on 4 September, by the identifier. Almaz must comply.
- Tigist prepares by waking each elder's medical leaves (thirteen rusts appear on the shelves, and Almaz walks past them). **Aster's** leaves: bone pain since 2160, the physician's note "suspect bone sickness", an old left-wrist break in 2140 set by the Azaj. Matches the dead woman's radiographs. Yet "Aster" was seen at the roll rehearsal climbing the house stairs two at a time.
- **Tailing and photography:** follow elders through the Gora pit; photograph gaits and hands. "Aster" leans into stairs like someone under a load; whenever she is handed a thing she hands a thing back; she avoids the coffee ceremony; she cannot read the duty board. (Fair clues; the alert player now strongly suspects.)
- Almaz posts "Aster" to the lineage quarry estate, leaving 5 September.

**Chapter 11. The veil and the glass room.** *Question: whose hands were in Mahlet's vision?* Personal use of sending.
- Mahlet finds Tigist: "The hands in my fever asked me to save this. I think they were yours." She gives her the **veil**, pulled from the kiln basket on 26 August. On it, at the neck, a red-ochre **palm print**. Tigist photographs it: palm lines differ between copies.
- 3 September, 15:44: Tigist, Hiwot (who has been playing with chalk) and Etagegn are in the glass room when the Mircha board turns to **8**. The player has lived this room in fever. **Staging:** for sixteen minutes the player re-enacts it (hold up the veil scrap, point to the kiln hatch) knowing it will be sent. At 16:00 someone must lay a hand on the plate and turn the glass.
- **Who pays, in miniature.** Tigist (loses 25 August to 3 September: the fever-reading, the fan day, the lottery; she forgets the promise she made Hiwot on the lottery night, and Hiwot has to tell her). Hiwot (loses the same nine days, including giving the Guest her fan; she will not remember it in the hall). Etagegn (loses nine days, including teaching Hiwot to carve: she will greet Hiwot as a stranger). If the player does nothing, Etagegn turns it: "I have lost thirty years. What is nine days?" The receipt closes on the register. The consequence is visible at once and never mentioned again by the interface.

**Chapter 12. The wrist** (midpoint). *Question: who is "Aster"?*
- *Gate:* the chapter opens only after the player has stated on the case board that the dead woman is Aster (from the myeloma leaf, the 2140 break on the radiographs and Almaz's grief), so what follows confirms the player's conclusion rather than handing it over.
- 4 September. The bone count in the Lemlem house: twelve elders, one at a time, matched to their leaves (attribution confirmed in batches of three, Obra Dinn-style). Each is a short haptic puzzle. Almaz watches from the door.
- "Aster" comes last, her left wrist in a sling: she fell on the east cistern stair last night. A fresh break is exactly where Aster's old 2140 break should be, and a fresh break through an old callus feels different. Setting it, the player feels: **no old callus**; a wrist and spine that have carried loads for sixty years; and on the radiograph Tigist shoots to guide the set, a **doubled clay line**.
- She looks at Tigist and, by reflex, puts a clay figurine in her good hand: the same as Hiwot's. **The dead woman is Aster. This is the Shadow, the first copy, living as Aster.**
- Tigist certifies "Aster" (her first lie; a scripted act the player performs by signing). Almaz, in the doorway, sees her hesitate.

*Midpoint reclassification:* every Act I fact stays true; the body, the gate tallies, the 311 and Almaz's grief all change meaning. The investigation turns from a death into a living conspiracy that Tigist has just joined.

### Act III: "The Pledge" (5-7 September)

Small hook: a wrist check at night.

**Chapter 13. Two faces** (Tigist; then Sitota becomes playable). *Question: what does she want?*
- Sitota comes to Tigist's setting room at night to have the wrist checked. Hiwot wakes and sees two of her own face, one very old, and asks which is older. Sitota answers in exchange: she takes Hiwot's figurine and gives back her own. Hiwot laughs. They understand each other better than either understands Tigist.
- Sitota's want, by objects: the salt bar and the empty space next to it; a mark of the Tsehafi's tally gate; a wing-pleat; the figurine. She wants to stand in the hall and hear it read to her face. Jae-won arrives; the conspiracy is complete (Tigist, Jae-won, Sitota; Dahir in support). The plan: get Sitota on the roll as Aster, then demand.
- **Playable as Sitota (passing):** she must go back into the Lemlem house for Aster's roll papers and keys and get out again. Stealth-social among identical sisters: walk like an elder, not a carrier; take a cup and do not give one back; answer to "Aster"; avoid Almaz. In the founder's room she sees **the founder's fan**, the lineage's relic, and stops: it is one of the Guest's wing-pleats. (*The founder exchanged something with the Guest for its promise to wait.*) Out through the gate. She moves into Jae-won's gallery.

**Chapter 14. Almaz's fever** (Tigist). *Question: why did Almaz kill?*
- Tigist wakes the Lemlem house leaf of 3 August: Almaz's fever night, heard through the house shaft. **Fever-reading** from her muttering: a hall; election cloth; numbers on a wall changing; a woman in Aster's robe with her hand on the plate while an iron book is read to her; a woman's voice ("I set bones in the trench; these are the bones of a body from the first jar"): **Tigist's own voice, from the future**; the Guest reaching for a small one of them; the sender's hand turning the glass: *old, spotted, shaking, ringed.*
- The player now holds: Almaz's motive (to make the vision impossible by killing the Shadow, and to keep the Guest from the child); the fact that Tigist will testify in the hall; and a sender with an office ring and an old hand. Almaz or Tsehay? Almaz could be sending to herself; a bonesetter's eye says the hand is seventy, not fifty.
- The leaf rusts. On the wake-register: *Lemlem Lemlem.* Almaz and Tsehay both see it.

**Chapter 15. The speaker** (Tigist). *Question: who can protect Sitota?*
- Tsehay summons Tigist to the Council Pit, under the warka. She is warm, grave and tired. She says she has always suspected Almaz; she wants the truth "handled properly, in the Chilot, not in the trench"; she will put "Aster" at the head of the roll and under her own staff. She asks where Sitota sleeps so the speaker's guard can watch the door. She touches the glass on her cord as she asks.
- The scene plays as a win: the most powerful Lemlem in the city has come over. The player chooses what Tigist tells her (the gallery, or a lie). **Either way** Tsehay's people follow Jae-won home that night; the choice changes only whether Tigist feels responsible, which the next chapter shows.

**Chapter 16. The palm** (Act III climax, M3). *Question: can Tigist prove it?*
- Dawit, by the Chilot's request and Guard protocol, hands Tigist the bagged **jebena shards**. One carries dried blood: Lemlem blood, "which is to say all of theirs" (a dead end only this premise allows). Its edge is a curve.
- Almaz comes to the setting room after dark with her left hand bound: a cut that will not heal. She asks Tigist to clean it, as Tigist has since she was a girl. **Haptic scene:** clean, open, inspect: the cut's curve matches the shard's edge. Then Tigist takes Almaz's right hand to bind the wrist, and the player compares its palm lines to the photograph of the veil print. They match.
- Almaz does not deny it. She tells it plainly (voice, no cutscene lecture): the vision, the child, the founder who "refused" hers, the woman at the door she thought was the Shadow, the face under the veil, the morning she understood it was Aster. "Nobody died, Tigist. You certified it." She leaves. The law cannot touch her.
- That night: the speaker's guard raids the gallery. Sitota is taken. **Jae-won's right hand is broken** under a door. Tigist and the player understand Tsehay betrayed them only when they see who carries Sitota away: guards with the speaker's black-stone tokens.

*Act III knowledge state:* the killer and her motive; the betrayer; the sender of Almaz's vision is almost certainly Tsehay; the founder exchanged something with the Guest; the hall is three days off. *Costs:* Jae-won's hand, Tigist's trust, Almaz's mask.

### Act IV: "The Mircha" (8-11 September)

Small hook: setting a stenographer's hand.

**Chapter 17. The hand.** *Question: can Jae-won still keep his oath?*
- Tigist sets Jae-won's hand. He will not take shorthand for weeks. He gives Hiwot his cassette recorder: she will be his ears in the hall. He shows them the time-lock box: it opens at 21:00 on Mircha night, and he will play what is inside, whatever it is.

**Chapter 18. The second book** (M2). *Question: why did the founder never open the jar?*
- In Bete Tsehafi's stacks, Tigist wakes the founder's last session (14 January 2131), a Lemlem leaf no one has dared wake. Dawit is there; Bete Tsehafi is his post; he does not stop her: a party may wake.
- **Leaf-waking at scale:** the founder's old voice, the Tsehafi's 2131 voice, the elders around her. She retells her 2090 vision (the hall, the bodies, the Guest folding into the jar, a small one of her holding out a glass, "my hand, clay to the wrist, taking it") and says the line the whole game rests on: *"I saw it paid. By my own hand. Every hand that is mine is my hand."* She knew in 2090 that one of her copies would pay. She chose to wait for them.
- Consequences land at once: the founder's vision has not been sent; it will be sent tonight from the hall; whoever sends it loses every memory since 2 November 2090, which for any copy is everything. The founder did not refuse her vision. Nothing was ever refused.
- Dawit hears it too. He says nothing. (Planted for his reversal.)

**Chapter 19. The glass room** (Tigist and Almaz). *Question: where is Sitota?*
- Sitota is locked in the Lemlem glass room on Tsehay's order: the speaker's plan is to refuse her vision by keeping the Shadow out of the hall. Only the Azaj holds the key.
- Tigist goes to Almaz and plays her the founder's voice on Jae-won's recorder. Almaz listens twice. Her belief dies on screen: "Then I killed her for nothing." She asks Tigist to look at her hand once more; Tigist binds it. Almaz unlocks the glass room. Sitota walks out past her.
- Almaz asks to walk to the hall with them. Tigist says nothing, which is yes.

**Chapter 20. The Mircha** (climax). *Question: who pays?*
1. **The roll (playable as Sitota):** passing among 310 sisters in identical election cloth down the hall of Bete Tsehafi, past the counting bar, under Tsehay's eye on the dais. The wall shows *Lemlem Lemlem: 311.* Jae-won's box clicks open in his good hand at 21:00.
2. **The vote:** every body marks a ballot; the wall shows one vote per lineage and one per natural-born person. Dawit leads.
3. **The seal:** the fig clause is read; Tsehay breaks the seal; clay dust on her ringed hand. She reaches into the neck for the iron book.
4. **The demand (playable as Sitota):** step out of the roll and put a hand on the book. The player, as Sitota, makes the one constrained statement of the night: lay her hand on the iron plate and claim party. Tigist steps out to help her lift it: both have clay to the wrist. Tsehay orders the Guard to remove "this body". **Dawit orders his guards to stand aside.** ("The Compact says no officer may refuse a party. I am an officer.")
5. **The reading:** the Tsehafi wakes the first book. The 2090 session plays to the hall in the founders' voices: the baby's cry; the vote; *"She is not me. The Guest cannot tell, and that is the whole of it."* Then the founder's fever dictation. The Tsehafi addresses it to the woman with her hand on the plate, with no change of tone.
6. **The testimony (playable as Tigist):** before the Chilot and the hall, Tigist reads Sitota's bones: age, the carrier's spine, the **doubled clay line**. It is exactly the testimony Almaz heard in her fever forty days ago. The player watches Almaz hear it again.
7. **The ruling and the wall:** the Afe Chilot voids And Sew. The Tsehafi re-tallies the same marked ballots, one per body. The numbers roll; Mekdes overtakes Dawit. Dawit watches his election go and does not move.
8. **The Guest:** the Arho, Dahir at their head, walk the Engida in from the Narrow. It sheds its last pleats as it comes, one by one, on 311 identical women who are all, to it, the one who promised. It stops.
9. **Hiwot** steps out holding the fan she painted. The Engida reaches toward her. **Tsehay's hand goes to her glass**; she lays her ringed, shaking hand on the plate and turns it. She has just sent Almaz the vision that began everything. Then she stands in the hall without thirty-nine days and asks Almaz why everyone is looking at her.
10. **The exchange:** the Engida takes Hiwot's fan and gives her a glass of black sand: the founder's glass, kept seventy-three years. It turns to the fig, folds its last pleats against itself, and enters the mouth. The mouth closes from inside.
11. **The glass:** Hiwot holds it out. The founder's 2090 receipt is on the wall's register, open. The player knows what it costs, knows who qualifies (a bare adult hand, clay to the wrist: Tigist or Sitota), and knows that refusing is allowed.
   - **Take it** (Tigist): hand on the plate, turn. The last sixteen minutes go to the founder on **2 November 2090**. Tigist loses her whole life. The screen holds on her face as she looks at Hiwot without knowing her.
   - **Let Sitota take it**, or **do nothing:** Sitota takes it from Hiwot with the Guest's gesture for *it was given to me*, and turns it. She loses seventy-three years.
   - Every Tsehafi shaft in the city that night closes its leaf: **"Outstanding: none."**
12. **The naming** (quiet beat): Jae-won plays Aster's last tape to the hall: *"Tonight she walks out in my robe and I stay in her veil."* The Afe Chilot asks the identifier whose hand was at the dead woman's neck.
