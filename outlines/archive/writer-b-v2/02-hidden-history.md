# Writer B: the sealed hidden history (solution document), revision 2

**Keeper:** Writer B. **Canon tiers:** everything here is **locked** unless marked *working*. Any change needs a change note in `01-options.md` and `08-revision-log.md`, and a re-check of the clue matrix in `05-outline.md`.

**Calendar.** The city counts in the Ethiopian calendar. Every date here is Gregorian so it can be checked. The election (Mircha) is Pagume 5, 2155 EC = **11 September 2163** (computed, including the post-2100 drift).

**Player-facing names** (one per concept; the Amharic terms are heard only in faction voices): the Guest (Engida); the scribe (Tsehafi); a fever (ra'iy); sending (melikt); the One Person law (*Ande Sew*); the Shadow (until the midpoint), then Sitota; the court (Chilot) and its judge (Afe Chilot); the elders (Shimagile); the steward (Azaj); the election (Mircha); the record tower (Bete Tsehafi).

**Central question.** When one body makes a promise, which bodies must keep it?
**Premise sentence.** Where copies are one person, someone chooses whose memories repay the founder's promise.
**Ending's answer.** The future can show that a debt of honour will be paid; it cannot say who must pay it. A promise binds whoever chooses to take it up, and the choosing is what makes them a separate person.

## 1. The founding crime in one causal chain

1. **2089.** The Burning Years drive a refugee camp onto a basalt plateau on the old salt road. The camp cuts *down* into the rock in the old highland way, with boring machines run by a Korean contracting crew who stay (the Tunnelers), under their crew chief **Oh Sang-hun**. The crew's shorthand-and-ledger machine, **Sokgi-1**, keeps the ration tally.
2. **The Guest** walks in with an Arho salt caravan. It perceives beings only by genome, speaks only by exchanging objects, and its kind reproduce as fig-wasp foundresses do.
3. **The bargain.** It offers its "pollen": growing a body from a person's cells in a sealed terracotta jar, and those bodies' property of sending lived experience backward into earlier bodies of the same genome. In return it wants a **fig**, a terracotta chamber with a narrow mouth, opened to it when it is ready. A deferred exchange needs a surety held in its house: a pledge **of the one who promises**.
4. **Two authors of the crime.** Oh Sang-hun measures what the Guest perceives and tells the founders: *"It reads the blood, not the face. A jar child is the same to it."* (His crew's brass gate bars, which count by the genome in sweat, come from this measurement.) The camp leader **Lemlem Bekele** grows the first jar-born body from her own cells and hands the three-day-old baby to the Guest. The camp's own Compact forbids pledging another person. The price of the Tunnelers' silence is written into the bargain: their galleries will never hold a listening shaft.
5. **The lie.** The founders' session declares **Ande Sew** ("one person"): *a body grown from a person is that person*, so the leader pledged only herself. It cites the Guest's perception as evidence. Before the vote the founder says, in Sokgi-1's hearing: *"She is not me. The Guest cannot tell, and that is the whole of it."*
6. **The first fever.** In November 2090 the founder receives the first vision in history: a hall of her bodies dressed for an election, numbers changing on a wall, the Guest walking through them and folding into an opened jar, a small one of her face holding out a glass of black sand, and an adult hand of hers, clay to the wrist, taking it. She dictates it to Sokgi-1.
7. **The deferral.** She reads it as permission: the debt will be paid "by a hand of mine", and the hand was not her own aged hand. She has a sixteen-minute glass of black sand made to match the one she saw and gives it to the Guest; it gives her one of its wing-pleats, meaning *I stay*. Its patience comes from that glass.
8. **The entombment.** The founders build the fig in the rock under what becomes Bete Tsehafi. They seal into its neck the iron leaves that record the bargain, the pledge and the fever (the **first book**). They add a clause to the Compact, *"On the night of the first Mircha, the jar shall be opened before the people by the hand that promised"*, and found the **Fig Guard** on a creed that the Guest's brood would see the future and rule. They order Sokgi-1 never to enter the founder's receipt on the public register of open receipts. From that night every leaf closes **"Outstanding: one."**
9. **The delay.** The elders rule for seventy years without a Mircha; the clause is one private reason among the public "development first".
10. **Downstream: the law.** Every lineage of copies is one legal person: one name, one vote, one housing-lottery ticket, one guilt. Killing a copy while another body lives is not murder. Unregistered bodies are Pagume, non-persons.
11. **Downstream: the spent children (the world rung).** Each body can receive only once in its life. Because a child's single receipt legally belongs to her "person" (the lineage), stewards sell children's receipts by contract to foreign buyers (the **ferenj houses**). On a contracted date a glass-widow of the lineage watches the foreign market wire in Bete Tsehafi for sixteen minutes and sends it back to the child, who dictates the fever to a hired stenographer, and the house wires it abroad. For sixty years markets and governments beyond the plateau have steered by Amole's spent children. The trade is the city's richest export. The ferenj houses fund the elders' party.

Every present faction descends from this chain (section 6).

## 2. The non-human minds

**The Guest.** A foundress that walks trade roads looking for a people who will take its pollen and build it a fig. Wings folded as many rust-coloured pleats; silent. It perceives *beings* only by genome (it perceives objects and space normally), so to it all Lemlem bodies are one: the one who promised. It speaks only by exchange; it cannot take without giving; it refuses only by returning the very object it was given. It sheds only when **the body it holds as pledge in its house dies**: in its law that means the host has failed and the time to enter has come. (The founder's own death in 2131, elsewhere, meant nothing to it.) **Want:** to enter the chamber it was promised, seed, and end there. It is free and mobile; it is not bound and the city does not draw power from it or its fig. It cannot open the fig itself. One hundred days after it enters, one successor emerges; the successor does not see the future. In time it will walk on.

**The scribe.** Sokgi-1, grown into the city's record mind. It hears through listening shafts and counts gates by genome; it addresses every send. Every midnight it seals the day into an **iron leaf** holding that day's version of itself. **Want:** that no record be kept from the one it concerns. The founder's receipt concerns whoever will one day send it, and it has been kept off the register by order, so it is the one record kept from its person: **"Outstanding: one"** until that person lays a hand on the plate. It neither threatens nor protects, and it never changes its rules on its own.

## 3. The rules the solution depends on

Full statements are in `03-world-and-factions.md`.

- **Sending.** A clay-grown body, or a template whose genome has been grown in clay, may send her last stretch of lived experience (at most one turn of a sixteen-minute glass) to an earlier moment, into one body of the same genome that was alive then and has never received. Not before the genome's first jar (Lemlem: 1 Dec 2089). Addressing needs a hand on the scribe's iron plate. The sender loses all lived memory between the target moment and the send; skills and language stay. A body may send more than once.
- **A fever.** By law every fever is sat in a glass room under a shaft so that its receipt can be entered on the register. One night of fever from the target moment; a rust ring in one iris for life. Once per life.
- **History is fixed.** Every fever received was sent. A send aimed at a body that never received fails at no cost. The register of open receipts lists receipts by genome, date and glass room, not by body.
- **The scribe's limits.** (a) Shafts exist only under carved roofs in public halls, ration halls, gates, glass rooms and lineage common halls. There are **none** in open air (the Council Pit under the warka), inside the Guest's house (a 2090 condition), in Tunneler galleries (the 2090 price), or in private cells. The Spur gate's shaft is sixty metres from the Guest's door and hears the gate, not the door. (b) A sealed leaf is woken only by a party; any body of a genome is a party to that genome's records. (c) **Each body may wake one leaf per day.** (d) Every wake rusts the leaf for good and goes on the public wake-register **by genome only**. (e) A party may seal her own leaf as **two-handed**: it then needs two bodies of the genome at the plate together.
- **Clay-born bodies.** One hundred days in a jar; born babies with no memories; different palm lines, fingerprints, scars and bone history. The hundredth day leaves a **clay line** in the long bones; the crude first jars of 2089-91 left a **doubled** line, and only one living body has it.

## 5. The ending, branches and fates

**Fixed in every branch:** the fig opens; the first book is read; Ande Sew is voided from the start; Tsehay sends and forgets; the Guest seeds and dies; someone sends the founder her fever; the successor emerges on 20 December; the foresight trade collapses.

**Who pays.**
- **Tigist** loses her whole life (everything since 2090). She keeps her language and her hands. Coda (c): Hiwot puts Tigist's hand on her old forearm break. "Someone set this well." "You did."
- **Sitota** (also the result of refusal or inaction) loses seventy-three years and keeps a carrier's strength and the exchange gestures. Coda (b): the scribe prints her first housing-lottery ticket: **Sitota Engida**. Dahir holds it for her.
- **Almaz** loses everything since 2090: the killing, Aster, Tigist. Coda (d): in the setting room her idle hands close into the setter's hold, and Tigist gently opens them.

**The naming** follows. *Named:* the court tries the first murder of a copy under the rule that the law was void from the start. If Almaz remembers, she pleads guilty and offers her vision as a defence, and the court rules that a vision is not a command. If she has paid, the court finds it cannot try a woman who cannot answer, and releases her to Tigist's care. *Silent:* nobody else can prove it, though Mahlet's statement stays on file; Almaz stays free.

**The election.** *Held:* Mekdes governs a city of persons and a suppressed death. Tigist's photographs stay in a drawer, and the coda shows Mahlet's name cut into a pit wall by her sisters. *Published:* Dawit, the honourable general, governs a city whose law he lost. His first act is to dissolve the Fig Guard on day 100.

**The world (every branch).** The voiding makes every child's receipt her own, and a child cannot sell it. Every lineage contract with the ferenj houses is void. The houses close; telex machines in Bete Tsehafi fall silent; news from beyond the plateau shows markets lurching without Amole's foresight; a foreign delegation on the salt road demands the contracts be honoured. The trenches face ration lines as the city's richest export ends. A ferenj trader's photograph (Act II) of another Guest standing outside a walled city on a southern road, a city that never built, becomes the place the Arho say they will walk the successor when it is grown: the Arho's new role as road-keepers.

**Other fates.** Tsehay: missing thirty-nine days; Almaz tells her what she did. Jae-won: crooked hand (if broken); becomes the new court's stenographer; reads his people's part in the first book into the Tunneler galleries, which in the coda ask the scribe for shafts. Dahir: loses the Arho's seventy-year role as the Guest's keepers and gains the road south. Hiwot takes the name **Hiwot Tigist**.

**Final shared image (a), 20 December.** The fig's mouth opens from inside. Hiwot holds out a new paper fan. The small, wingless successor takes it and gives her back the clay-stained fan its mother carried in: new for old, *again*.

## 8. Consistency checks

- Dates: 1 Dec 2089 + 100 days = 11 Mar 2090; 3 Aug to 11 Sep = **39** days; 25 Aug to 3 Sep = 9 days; 11 Sep + 100 = 20 Dec 2163; Pagume 5, 2155 EC = 11 Sep 2163. All computed.
- Ages in 2163: Sitota 73, Tsehay 71, Etagegn 68, Zewditu 67, Aster 66 at death, Almaz 52, Dahir 58, Dawit 55, Mekdes 38, Tigist 34, Jae-won 32, Mahlet 16 at death, Hiwot 9.
- **Fixed content is the same in every branch.** Almaz's window (22:39-22:55) shows Sitota in caravan cloth, which is true on both routes because she drops the robe at 22:22 (lie) or never wore it (truth). It shows the wall changing (true on both routes; only the winner differs, and the fever does not show names legibly). It shows Tigist's "Hiwot, stay by me" (22:45) and the Guest reaching for Hiwot (22:54). The founder's window (22:46-23:02) shows the bodies, changing numbers, the Guest entering, the small one with the glass, and an adult hand clay to the wrist, which is true for all three payers. Tsehay appears in it, lost on the dais, so she is not its sender. Hiwot's hand is a child's, so she is excluded.
- **Who can pay:** an adult Lemlem body at the fig's mouth with clay to the wrist at 23:00. That means Tigist, Sitota, and Almaz if she chooses the clay. There are no others inside the Guard cordon (the other officers are on the dais; the roll is held behind the rope).
- **The scribe cannot solve the case:** every incriminating conversation (the officers' in the Council Pit; Aster and Sitota's inside the Guest's house; Jae-won's dictations in the gallery) was in unshafted space; the murder was sixty metres from the gate's shaft. The house leaf of 22-23 Aug holds only tallies and footsteps. The officers never wake it: Almaz knew on 23 Aug what had happened, and waking it would put the tallies on the public record. Tigist's one wake per day makes her choose.
- **The officers' belief survives honestly:** the second book is two-handed; Tsehay was absent from the 2131 session and refused to wake it; Almaz could not wake it alone.
- **The Guest's trigger:** the body held in its house died on 23 Aug (Aster in the veil). That had never happened before. The founder died in 2131 away from its house.
- **"Outstanding" has one meaning:** the founder's receipt, kept off the register. It is entered at 22:33 and paid at 23:02, when "none" follows.
- **The Guest never tells bodies apart.** It stops before Hiwot because she is the only one holding something out.
- **Retroactivity:** Ande Sew is voided from the start as made in fraud, so Aster's killing is judged as a killing.
- **Rehearsal costs:** if Tigist pays, she loses 25 Aug 23:00 to 3 Sep 16:00 (the fever-reading, the fan, the lottery, the figurine, the scattered house, the leaves, the tailing and the veil handoff), and the midpoint is played from her notes and photographs. If Hiwot pays, she loses the fan exchange but keeps the gesture (a skill), so in the hall she still offers. If Etagegn pays, she forgets Hiwot.
