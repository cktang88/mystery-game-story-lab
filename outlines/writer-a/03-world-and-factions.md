# Writer A, file 03: world, factions and iceberg

## 1. Eiðey at a glance

A basalt island republic of about 38,000 people in the North Atlantic, settled in the 10th century by Norse settlers and their Gaelic thralls. Capital: **Hornvík**, a harbour town on the south coast below the cliff that holds **Málhellir**, the Speech Cave. Year: 2043, a **Standing** year, the first in 18.6 years. Matronymic names. Fish, geothermal greenhouses, a sovereign fund that has never had a bad year, and two exports the world knows about: the **Thul**, the incorruptible notary mind that has registered the world's treaties since 2027, and Eiðey's quiet, expensive **foresight**. The world does not know that foresight comes from a living alien.

The look: black rock, white sky, net racks, cod drying, the ceremonial veils of the Oath-Keeper (undyed wool, eyes only), silver spiral earrings for the House, red-painted kitchens on the north coast. The sound: surf in a cave, a reel recorder's hiss, the Old Word chant (a rising and falling plea sung by the whole island at funerals), the Thul's level voice over harbour loudspeakers announcing tides.

## 2. The four pillars as the player learns them

Each rule is taught in the order **anomaly, harmless test, named rule, personal use, bill** before any reveal depends on it.

### The Horn (time travel)

- **Mechanism.** At slack water of a Standing spring tide, sound spoken into the Horn arrives at the same mouth 6,798 days (one lunar node cycle, "the Span") earlier.
- **Cap.** Sound only; about nine minutes per window; about 25 windows per Standing year; one Span per step.
- **Cost.** The Horn takes the words: nobody present hears them and no device records them. Every send has exactly one witness, the sender, and every received voice is a voice from the future that the past already acted on.
- **Damages:** trust. A sender can lie about what they sent and nobody alive can check.
- **Saves:** goodbyes. You can speak to someone who has since died, and they heard you.
- **Unique scenes:** hearing your own future words; a mouth moving with no sound; a government that holds the received archive, which is the script of the present year.
- **Verb:** tide-matching (align the received archive with the present to predict who will be where and what they will say).
- **Teaching order:** anomaly (the Silence, Ch1; the tape, Ch4) → test (a neighbour's banal line in a 2024 reel, then watching him say it soundlessly, Ch4) → named rule (the Thul's tide announcement: "Words given to the Horn are given away") → personal use (Jórunn finds her own voice in the archive, Ch10) → bill (Kolbeinn's death at the Outer Ear; the Silence's meaning).

### The Thul (AGI)

- **Mechanism.** A notary mind that holds every sworn record on the island and cannot state a falsehood about it; its yearly versions (sittings) can be woken to answer as of their dates.
- **Cap.** It may not speak sealed records; waking a sitting needs an estate officer's petition; every waking is logged publicly.
- **Cost.** It forgets nothing, so anything sworn near it is permanent. And every question you ask it is seen by the people you are investigating.
- **The Count.** A sealed Thul may still say how many statements in a submitted set are true ("a count is not testimony", 1702). This is the game's batch validation, explained inside the fiction.
- **Damages:** confiding. Islanders stopped talking freely in rooms with speaker grilles.
- **Saves:** a dead woman's last request, kept by a version of the machine that no longer exists.
- **Verb:** waking sittings and diffing their answers; filing a Division for the Count.

### Aftergrowth (cloning)

- **Mechanism.** Blood offered in the alien's Pool grows a newborn copy. No memories. Copies age normally.
- **Cap.** One living copy per pattern. The Pool will not grow what is already walking.
- **Cost.** The Law of Same: a copy inherits the original's name, debts, marriage and crimes.
- **Damages:** the bond between a copy and the original's family (a widow made "wife" to a young man who is not her husband).
- **Saves:** the same bond, freely chosen (the widow who keeps him as a son).
- **Verb:** lineage reading (portraits, teeth casts, voice drills, acquired accents and habits) and veil-swap stealth among identical veiled women.

### The Bergmál (aliens)

- **Mechanism.** A stranded being grown into the cave. It can only replay sounds it has stored; it speaks in collages of the island's dead.
- **Cap.** It cannot invent a sound. It cannot act against a promise. It hears promises and releases only in the Pool during Standing slacks.
- **Cost.** Its foresight made the island dependent, and its Store means the dead never fully leave, which is a comfort and a captivity.
- **Damages:** the island's freedom to not know.
- **Saves:** the living's bond with their dead (the Store will replay a lost voice for anyone who asks in its grammar).
- **Verb:** collage conversation (attribute each fragment it quotes to a speaker and era to understand it; answer it by citing promises).

### The one admitted cheat

How the Bergmál's body spans 18.6 years, and what it is, is never explained. It is the premise's single impossible fact and is never used to solve anything.

## 3. Factions

| Faction | Belief | Want | Loses if the truth comes out | Look and sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Son's House** (rulers of the Thing; heads are copies of Þorsteinn, "the Son, renewed") | Foresight is the island's life and now the world's; the Hearer serves while the swearer lives; the Hearer knows her by her voice. | Bind the new Hearer at the Turning for another thousand years. | Legitimacy; the murder of 983; the Law of Same; the substitute girl. | Grey wool, silver spiral earrings; the dry rasp of the Relay chant; the Fitting rooms (tables of transcribed received lines, each with a plan beside it). |
| **The Sendright families** | Their inherited shares in the Horn are property, as fishing quotas are. | Keep their shares and fortunes; manage the Silence. | Their wealth was built on an enslaved being's labour. | Blue-painted houses, auction bells, ledgers of "words held". |
| **The Unheard** (thrall descendants, no sendrights) | The Horn belongs to everyone; the cave listened to a thrall girl first. | A share of the Standing; dignity. | Nothing; they gain a truth, and lose a daughter they were told was dead. | North-coast red kitchens, work songs, Gaelic-derived names (Kaðlín, Mýrún, Kjaran). |
| **The afterborn** ("the Same") | A copy is not the original. Slogan: **"I am not him."** | Repeal the Law of Same. | Nothing; the truth is their weapon. | Hand-lettered placards, a hostel by the slipway, ill-fitting inherited coats. |
| **The Mouths and the Schooling** (voice-trainers, former Mouths, the Pool's midwife) | The office matters more than the woman. | Keep the voice true; survive. | Their complicity in substitutions and "retirements". | Tuning forks, mirrors for mouth-shapes, a field of unmarked stones. |
| **The Thul and its sittings** | A record is kept or released, never broken. | Close every open entry. | Its role as the world's notary (it notarised a false certificate). | A level voice; speaker grilles in every public room; a log screen in the Thing where every waking appears. |
| **The Register** (International Treaty Register at Hornvík) | The Thul's word underwrites the world's treaties; Eiðey's foresight has saved millions. | Keep both: foresight and a trustworthy notary. | Its treaty architecture; the knowledge that it bought warnings in price tiers. | Blue lanyards, flat English, security contractors in grey, a chartered ferry. |
| **The Bergmál** | A promise cannot be broken. | To go home when she lets it. | Nothing; it is the victim. | Black glistening rock that breathes with the tide; voices of the dead in the dark. |

Every faction acts from visible incentives (Tufekci's sociological mode), so a player can predict them and a death does not break the plot.

## 5. Iceberg lore

Delivery order for every fact: place first, then object or creature, then sound (usually a Store fragment or a received voice), and only then a short text with a named author and bias. The main path needs none of the rows marked optional.

### Promised (answered on the main path, each answer opening a further vista)

| Question | Answered where | What it opens |
|---|---|---|
| Why is the Horn silent? | Act II midpoint (no sea), confirmed at the Turning | What happens to a world that loses its warnings? |
| Who was the drowned net-mender? | End of Act I | Then who is the girl in the veil? |
| Is Steina a copy? | Act II (Ch9) | Who is she, and why was she taken? |
| Who killed the net-mender? | End of Act II | Who aimed him? |
| Who sent "Farðu heim, Bergi"? | Midpoint (player), Act IV (characters) | What will the release cost? |
| What are the Goðar? | Act III | Why regrow a son for a thousand years? |
| What is the Old Word? | Act III | What did the first Steinunn want? |
| How does the Bergmál know its keeper? | Act IV, by action | Were the copies ever needed? (No.) |
| What did Kolbeinn promise? | Act IV | Is Jórunn bound by it? (By choice.) |

### Texture (may stay open forever; never promised by early scenes)

- What the Bergmál is, where it came from, and where it goes home to.
- The 934 "fire in the sea".
- Whether a Gaelic thrall girl heard the cave before Steinunn (folk account against saga).
- The Mother's Cry of 964: Steinunn hearing her own death 18 years early (optional, in the saga room; the strongest rhyme with Sæunn).
- The full Relay chain: each Standing's relayers, including the relayer of 1627 who refused, after which the chain held anyway because someone else did it (a relayer's diary, optional).
- **The Quiet Standing of 1666** (named, never shown): a year when the island chose not to send at all.
- **The Mouths' stones** at Hornvík: who each was.
- **The Burned Thing of 1350** (named, never shown).
- The House's Fitting rooms: centuries of received lines with the harmless ways they were made true (the 1783 "boats are burning" fitted by burning old boats at Midsummer).
- The Thul's minority opinions: Sitting Eleven dissented from its own seal in a sealed annex.
- The folklore that a person who dies in the Horn at slack goes back as a voice for one tide (never confirmed; the game never uses it).
- A poet regrown six times who wrote the same first line each time (optional collage).
- Where the Twenty-first will grow up and who she will be.

### Deliberate white space

Some events, places and people are named and never shown: the Quiet Standing, the Burned Thing, the Danish crown's 1660 attempt to seize the cave, the relayer of 1627, Eiðey's foresight sale to "a southern power" in 1969.
