# Writer A, file 08: revision log

This log answers the grader's review (judging/review-writer-a.md, 7.5/10). It covers each numbered required revision, the smaller notes, and the "different option" section: what changed, in which files, and why. Wherever I chose a different fix from the review's suggested direction, I say so and give the reason. The strengths the review listed are kept: the opening contains the ending, the names come from the world's own logic, the founding-crime iceberg, the Thul as neither threat nor guardian, sound chronology, and an honest self-check.

---

## A. Coherence and rules

### 1. Close the "script of the year" leak
**Changed:**
- **The Tidings Law (1671), a new locked rule (02 rule 9; 03).** Inner Mouth sends must be readings from the sworn Book of Tidings, a chronicle of the years since the last Standing, each opened with the reader's mark. So the 2024-25 archive holds the coming eighteen years as history, never 2043's events. This fixes the leak at its root, and it also explains what foresight always was.
- **The grader's "empty windows" idea, adopted.** After the Thing grants the Register trusteeship in September, the Register forbids all sends but the relay, so windows 17-24 carry only sea. The player first sees them in Kolbeinn's level checks (Ch4), hears them in full at the midpoint (Ch10), and learns why in Ch16.
- **What the past hears at window 25** is fixed and designed in: the commander's radio check, Steina's Long Word, the clang of the Register capping the Inner Mouth, and at the Outer Ear, Jórunn's "Mamma—".
- **What warned the Son.** It was not the 2025 archive. Kolbeinn, demented, told the House's pension officer Hjalti in 2041-42: "Steinunn's coming home. By the sea door, at the first tide. I promised." Hjalti appears in Ch2 and his reports are unsealed in Ch16.
- **A fixed-timeline table (02 section 2)** checks every 2043-44 window against what 2024-25 heard.

**Why this fix rather than the review's options:** "the House sends only liturgy" leaves the sendright families free to leak, and "destroy the archive" empties the heist. A sworn chronicle fixes the source, and the empty windows become a new early mystery hook.
**Files:** 02, 03, 04, 05 (Ch1, Ch2, Ch4, Ch10, Ch16), 06 (scenes 1, 2, 4, 8, 13), 07.

### 2. Cap the Count, and scope Sitting One
**Changed:**
- **The Count (02 rule 7).** A count needs at least three statements, all about an open estate in the officer's warrant, each backed by evidence entered in the estate file. Every count is publicly logged with its headings. That combines three of the review's options. The evidence rule stops oracle guessing; the public log makes every count a cost.
- **The cap is taught in Ch2 by the interface**, and pays off in the plot: Jórunn's Ch11 count on the cause of death joins her Ch8 waking on the House's desk and triggers the search that kills her father.
- **Ch15's count on 983 is possible only because the estate has just been opened.** Opening it requires the Son to recuse himself under his own Law of Same, so the petition is now the key move.
- **Sitting One's scope.** Wakings answer only on the estate's own matters: identity, assets, debts, promises, death. In Ch8 it gives the two promises and the pension "retirement" order, and answers "Why did she go to Útnes?" with "That is another estate", which sends Steina to the Greeting Wall. What Steinunn XIX *learned* from it (the Reckoning) is out of scope.

**Files:** 02, 03, 05 (Ch2, Ch8, Ch11, Ch15), 06 (scene 6), 07.

### 3. Repair the contradictions between the hidden history and the rules
- **The 983 plea (Horn against Store).** Taken the review's direction and made into a puzzle. The plea went into the Horn, so the Store's 983 record has a three-second hole. The Store's copy of "Hlífðu mér, sonur" is the 964 reception at the Outer Ear. In Ch14 the player files it under 964, then fits it into the 983 hole by its length and the matching splash. Þorsteinn saw his mother's lips move with no sound. He knew the words because she had sung them as a lullaby since 964 (also stored), and he kept the tune without words as the Ebb-Song. Files: 02, 03, 05 (Ch14), 06 (scene 11).
- **The Quiet Standing and the cycle dates.** Moved to 1652-53, "sent nothing except the relay". The relayer who refused moved to 1615-16, the fire in the sea to 926-27 (a Standing year, because the Hearer's kind die only at a Standing's last window), and the Tidings Law to 1670-71. Files: 02, 03, 07.
- **The 29-30 June 2025 gap.** Steinunn XIX gave her last dawn word on 29 June; **Gróa** spoke on 30 June; the Thul began on 1 July. The chain of dawns is unbroken and appears on the Greeting Wall as one old woman's morning, which leads to Gróa's line "I did one morning. After that the box spoke for her." Rule 5 now says what a gap would do: it breaks recognition, not the promise. That is why the House has never allowed one. Files: 02, 04, 05 (Ch9), 06 (scene 7).

The whisper can be separated only on the master, which Jórunn finds in Ch11. "The house" searched is Kolbeinn's, where Jórunn has lived since his illness. Files: 02, 04, 05 (Ch2, Ch4, Ch11, Ch12), 06 (scene 9).
- **The Twenty-first in the Pool.** Steina sees it from Ch3 and is told it is "a commission: a Langanes widow's drowned boy, renewed". The truth comes in Ch12. I chose the review's second option over a hidden basin because it turns a plot hole into a fair clue. Files: 02, 05, 06 (scene 3).

- **Fixed timeline against branches.** Every branch-dependent event (Nusrat's choice, any shot, the filing, the count) happens after the Inner Mouth is capped and after Jórunn has left the Outer Ear, and none of it is within a few paces of either mouth. The rule that the Horn carries a faint halo of sound within a few paces of the mouth was made explicit, because it already explained the whisper on the tape. Kolbeinn's fall and Ketill's painting happen outside a slack. File: 02 (table).
- **Knot count.** Sæunn starts the knots on the boat on 29 June, in tarred boat cord. The first five knots differ, and in Ch5 that proves she tied them at sea and did not drown. Files: 02, 04, 05 (Ch5), 06 (scene 2).

Files: 02, 03, 05, 06.

### 4. Remove the bootstrap that selects the protagonist
**Changed:**
- The 2043 message now says "give it to **the Divider who comes to divide my house**", with no name.
- In 2024 Kolbeinn chooses his daughter himself ("My Jóra will see it done right"). His stated reasons: she is the only witness's daughter, and she is a law clerk in Copenhagen outside the House's patronage. Steinunn XIX then asks Sitting One to assign her estate to Jórunn. The choice now has an independent cause.
- **The Bergi loop.** I chose to *own* it, not give the name a separate source. Steina took the name from the prayer, and the prayer is her goodbye. 02 section 3 makes it the story's single ontological loop, carrying a name and no plot information, and Ch14 acknowledges it on screen ("I called him that because of the prayer." "And the prayer is you."). Why: an independent source (a kitchen word) would be tidier but would lose the theme's best image, a promise with no maker, kept only by whoever chooses to. The false "clean origin" claim in 07 is withdrawn.

**Files:** 01 (names row), 02, 04, 05 (Ch4, Ch8, Ch14), 07.

### 6. Make the climax cost the anchor personally, and make the protagonist act
**Changed:**
- **Jórunn acts.** Her own Ch15 petition opened the 983 estate. The Thing then grants the Register trusteeship "for as long as the estate stays open", in a clause Verhoeven writes on screen (Ch16). At the Turning, Jórunn sends to her mother at the Outer Ear (fixed by the archive), then races through the ledge passage to the Pool chamber's grille and files the closing Division. The Thul counts it, the trusteeship ends, and Verhoeven lifts the net. Without her the bud is taken. This is the review's "nine-minute race that uses her tools".
- **Her price.** The Store holds the only copies of her father's promise and her mother's laugh, and no recorders are allowed in the Pool chamber (Ch17). Keeping the Hearer in a Register tank would keep them; she files anyway. She is then struck from the Dividers' roll (Ch21).
- **Steina's price, played.** Her last night with Bergi (Ch19). "Goodnight, Bergi", in her own child voice, is the last thing it ever says to her (Ch20).
- **The Védís reel.** Kept as the epilogue's grace note, but priced: it becomes the only voice of her mother Jórunn has left, and she leaves it unplayed for eight chapters, from Ch11 ("Not while he's alive"). I chose not to give it away, because the larger loss (the Store) now carries the price.

**Files:** 02, 04, 05 (Ch15-21), 06 (scenes 13-17).

### 8. Give the AGI a choice at the climax
**Changed:** taken the review's direction and tied to Revision 6. The Register keeps the restricted Thul running for the Turning as the fallback voice for a binding. Sitting One made a second promise in 2024: "never to help anyone keep the Hearer bound" (heard by the player in Ch8). At the slack Verhoeven orders: "You are forbidden to count. Speak the binding." The Thul counts Jórunn's Division instead, "Three of three are true. Estate closed", and says its last line, "I am here. I kept my word", before it is frozen as Sitting Twenty. **Different from the review:** the decisive act is the *count*, not refusing to speak the binding. Under the true recognition rule a binding in its voice would fail anyway, so refusing alone would change nothing; the count changes the outcome.
