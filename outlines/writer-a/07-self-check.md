## Pillar test: one mechanism, one limit, one cost, one verb, one damaged and one saved relationship

| Pillar | Mechanism | Limit | Cost | Verb no other pillar has | Damages | Saves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time | carried film | x-ray shadows only; a year of the leaf's own time; self-consistent | seeing binds you; duty | develop, fit, stage | Ponni and Minmini (tradecraft, substitution) | Ponni and Minmini (the funeral) |
| AGI | leases | only by lease; on-world; discloses only to holders; blind to clockwork | you become the author of what you lease | lease, re-lease (the building re-performs) | Ponni and Selvan | Kayal and Minmini |
| Cloning | proxies | one per person; memory to fork; ages diverge; the ring | the copy owns nothing | fork-find, pass | Kayal and Valli | Kayal and Kathir |
| Aliens | the Vandu post | only between blooms; closes if anything carried is kept | markets at an alien's mercy | read strata | Thumbi and her guild | Ponni and Minmini (the chamber must open) |

**Collision:** the climax needs all four at once. The shadow is fixed (time); a clone's body fits it but bears a ring that must be cut (cloning); the bloom must be open, so the hoard must go back (aliens); and the closure must be leased and signed (AGI). Together they raise the question none raises alone: *if a fixed future can be fitted by a copy, staged by a house and carried by a species that cannot keep, who wrote what happened?*

## Known risks for the grader

1. **Rule load.** Carried film, the Balance, leases, proxies, strata and the ordeal are six systems. Each is taught by a low-stakes scene before it matters, but the first two hours are dense.
2. **The ordeal's "sealed coin".** With the standard term, the leaf's result is fixed as guilty by the solution document. A player who expects a real gamble may feel cheated; the alternative is a genuine branch with an acquittal epilogue.
3. **Self-conviction may read as martyrdom.** It is meant as authorship. The scene must play as Ponni choosing, not being crushed.
4. **Cultural care.** The laying-off of a thali is an invented future rite; in present-day Tamil custom removing the thali signifies widowhood. The game should present it as a far-future legal form, and it should be reviewed by Tamil readers.
