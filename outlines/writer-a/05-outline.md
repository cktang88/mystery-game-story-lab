# Writer A, file 05: the outline

**Working title:** The Long Word
**Starting point:** the question *Does a promise outlive the one who made it?*
**Solution document:** 02-hidden-history.md (locked). World: 03. Characters: 04.

---

## 1. One sentence

**A probate clerk can save a counterfeit clone only by ending her island's foresight.**

## 2. One paragraph

On a North Atlantic island that has heard its own future through a living alien for eleven centuries, the future goes silent at the first tide of the year, and an old net-mender is found drowned in the cave mouth. The estate clerk assigned to her house discovers that the dead woman was the island's previous Oath-Keeper, who faked her death eighteen years ago, and that her dying words were recorded by the clerk's own father eighteen years before she spoke them. **First disaster:** the veiled girl who now keeps the island's oath with the alien is not the copy she was told she is but a child taken from a poor family, and the woman who could have explained it is dead. **Second disaster**, caused by the clerk's own attempt to prove it: her public questioning of an old version of the island's notary machine alerts the ruling House, her father dies fleeing them, the girl's loving guardian is exposed as the killer, and the world's treaty body, handed the island's archive by the clerk's own ally, sides with the House. **Third disaster**, caused by the clerk's attempt to expose the founding crime at the island's assembly: the House falls and the machine confesses, but the world suspends the machine and seizes the cave to force the girl to bind the alien's newborn for another thousand years of warnings. **Ending:** at the last tide of the year the girl, recognised by the alien not by her face or voice but because she has kept the promise every day for ten years, releases it in her own voice; the world loses its foresight, the island loses every stored voice of its dead, and the clerk closes the oldest open estate on Earth.

### Thread paragraphs

- **Jórunn (the Divider).** Assigned a stranger's estate outside her district, she reconstructs a fled Oath-Keeper's last eighteen years from a net, a tape and a photograph of a red kitchen. Proving the girl is a substitute costs her her father. She opens the estate of the island's founder at the Thing, then hears in the alien's Store that her father had promised her to this case, and chooses to keep his promise. She closes the 983 estate.
- **Steina (the Oath-Keeper).** Wanting only to bury the knot woman, she learns from the alien's own record of greetings that a machine kept her office before her, that she cannot be a copy, that her guardian killed the knot woman, and that the prayer she leads is a murdered mother's plea. She hears her own voice speaking the island's holiest words, words she has not yet said, and decides when and how to say them.
- **Ketill (the afterborn).** His divorce from his original's widow is refused; his photographs show nine centuries of the same Son; his case against the Law of Same forces the Son to choose between being a murderer and being nobody, and he wins.
- **Nusrat (the auditor).** Sent to find whether the Silence foretells catastrophe, she proves it foretells the end of the channel, unknowingly hands the House the world's support, learns how unequally the warnings were sold, and at the Turning chooses whether to speak.
- **The Thul.** Bound by one past version's seal and another's promise, it routes the case to the right clerk, counts truths it may not speak, and when the law frees it recites the founding crime and confesses that it once spoke as the dead woman for eight years.

## 3. One page

**Act I, The Estate (January to March 2043).** The island gathers in Málhellir for the first window of the Standing, as it has every 18.6 years since 946. The veiled Oath-Keeper, Steina, leads the Old Word; the Son of the House presides; afterborn protesters hold placards reading "I am not him". At slack water the Inner Mouth, which has delivered the future's voices for eleven centuries, delivers nothing: not a voice, not even the sea. After the tide, net-menders find an old woman drowned in the mouth pool. The Thul assigns her estate to Jórunn, a Divider from another district. In the dead woman's house at Útnes, Jórunn reconstructs a life: a net with 6,408 knots, a carved needle reading "I am here. I keep my word", a photo of a red kitchen, and a reel tape dated June 2024 on which the dead woman's voice asks Jórunn's father to keep the tape "until I'm dead" and give it to "your Jórunn". At the morgue the body has the gap teeth of every Oath-Keeper in the island's portraits. Meanwhile Steina, under the veil, keeps her dawn word to the Hearer, talks to it secretly at night as "Bergi", and wants only to bury the knot woman who left her charms. Jórunn tests the Horn's rule with her father's 2024 reels and proves the dead woman is Steinunn the Nineteenth, officially drowned in 2025. At the burial in Útnes a veiled girl who has slipped her guard steps out of the rain.

**Act II, The Twentieth (March to July 2043).** If the Nineteenth lived until January, the Twentieth could not have been grown in 2026: the Pool will not grow what is already walking. Steina works it out herself by sorting eighteen years of greetings stored in the Hearer and finding eight years of identical machine-voiced mornings before her own child's voice. Jórunn wakes the Thul's first sitting, the only version that never swore the House's seal, and learns it promised the dead woman her estate would come to Jórunn. The waking is logged publicly. With the auditor Nusrat, sent by the world's treaty Register, and Ketill's protest as cover, Jórunn steals the House's copy of the 2024 archive, the script of this year's sends, and the player hears, at the last window, the island's holiest phrase "Farðu heim, Bergi" in Steina's voice. Nusrat proves the Silence means the channel will not exist in 2061: there is no sea in it. In the same triumphant scene she transmits the archive to the Register by protocol. Jórunn's forensics on the 2024 tape bring up a whisper under the surf, "Hush, now, hush", the lullaby Steina's guardian, Ari, uses for her nightmares, and the reconstruction of the first tide shows his six missing minutes. The House searches Kolbeinn's house; the old man flees to the only place he remembers, the Outer Ear, and falls. Ari confesses in front of Steina. The Register declares the binding a world priority.

**Act III, The Reckoning (July to October 2043).** Ketill's elderly "wife" wants a grave for her husband, whom the law says is alive. Ketill traces the Law of Same to 1883 and to the House, whose heads have been copies of the same man for nine centuries. Steina meets her birth mother in Útnes, hears the Unheard story that the cave "knew the thrall girl because she came every day", and asks the Hearer where the first Steinunn went. It answers with its oldest memory: a mother pleading "Spare me, son" at the cave mouth in 983, to the melody of the island's funeral chant. Jórunn plays Steina the archive's Long Word: "That's my voice. That's what I call him." At the Thing, Jórunn opens the estate of Steinunn the First, and Ketill argues that under the House's own Law of Same the present Son is her murderer. The Son says "I am not him." The Law of Same falls, and with it the seal. The Thul recites the Reckoning to the world, confesses its years as the Mouth, and is suspended. The Register, believing the Silence may mean the end of the world, takes the cave.

**Act IV, The Turning (October 2043 to January 2044).** Grieving, Jórunn asks the Hearer for her parents and hears her father's 2024 voice: "I promise. My Jóra will see it done right." She chooses the promise. Nusrat audits the warnings' price tiers and her brother's death. Steina, under guard, is tutored by the deposed Son in the binding rite; Ari is let out to stand at her side. At the last window, all threads stand in the cave again. Steina speaks the Long Word into the Horn, Jórunn speaks to her dead mother at the Outer Ear, the old Hearer dies and buds, and in the Pool Steina speaks in her own accent. The newborn answers with her own childhood voice, "Goodnight, Bergi", and recognises its keeper. She cites the first Steinunn's term and releases it. The Son, asked by the Register's commander what she is saying, answers "She is binding it." The bud leaves on the ebb. The cave is rock. Jórunn files the last Division; a suspended machine counts it true; Steina swims; "There's sea in it."

## 4. Full outline, act by act

Format per chapter: viewpoint, true window and date, the **narrative question** on screen, the one **new mechanic** (at most one), numbered **gameplay beats**, what is **revealed**, what is **planted**, and the intensity (1 low to 5 high), with deliberate down beats.

### ACT I: THE ESTATE

**Current question for the whole act: who was the drowned net-mender?**

#### Ch1. The First Tide
- **POV:** Jórunn, with a short Steina vignette. **When:** Window 1, 14 Jan 2043.
- **Question:** What is the island waiting to hear?
- **New mechanic:** the reel recorder (record, scrub, mark a moment).
1. Jórunn helps her father Kolbeinn down the cave steps; he insists on carrying his old reel recorder and lets her hold the microphone. The player records the crowd: a ready-made tutorial in listening.
2. Steina vignette: the player, as the veiled girl, leads the Old Word by matching the chant's rising-falling contour with the stick (rhythm and pitch, no text). The prayer ends "...farðu heim, Bergi"; she sings the last word a beat early.
3. Faces established in one sweep, each with a visible want: the Son ("the Son, renewed", a public custom); Ari the Warden at Steina's side; Ketill's placard "I am not him"; Gróa the midwife watching like a hawk; the Thul's level voice over the loudspeakers: "Slack water in one minute. Words given to the Horn are given away."
4. Slack. Every head turns to the Inner Mouth. Nothing. The player can scrub the recording: no voice, and no surf either. Kolbeinn, puzzled: "No sea, Jóra. There's no sea in it." (Planted as dementia.)
5. As the slack ends, Ari pushes back through the crowd to Steina's side. His cloak is wet; she notices; he says "spray on the steps". (Planted, not flagged; his six minutes away are only reconstructed in Ch11.)
6. After the tide, shouting from the mouth pool: an old woman drowned among the net offerings.
- **Planted:** the Long Word's last word; "No sea"; Ari's absence; Ari's wet cloak (Steina notices, he says "spray on the steps"); the proverb chanted in the liturgy, "By the word kept is the keeper known".
- **Intensity:** 2 rising to 4.

#### Ch2. Division
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** January, between windows 1 and 2.
- **Question:** Who was she, and what did she owe?
- **New mechanic:** the Division (estate reconstruction) validated by the Count.
1. The Thul assigns her the estate. She notices it is outside her district; the Thul says only, "Assigned per an undertaking."
2. Morgue: the body has a gap where both upper lateral teeth should be, and a needle puncture at the elbow nobody can explain.
3. Útnes. The player walks Sæunn's house as she left it. Each object can be tagged to a person and a promise (owed to, owed by, kept, broken). The net: thousands of knots, one per day. A carved net needle: "I am here. I keep my word." A cut edge where small pieces were removed. A photo of a red-painted kitchen. A reel box with Kolbeinn's handwriting: "Ytra Eyra, 4.6.2024, window 1."
4. Home. Kolbeinn, shown the reel box, says, "The girl's not her, Jóra. Don't let them." She is tired; she tells him she never wanted this job and still does not know why he pushed her into it.
5. First Division filed with the Thul: who the deceased was (name, trade, years at Útnes), what she owed. The Count: "Two of three are true." (The name "Sæunn" is false as a birth name.)
- **Planted:** gap teeth (pays in Ch5); 6,408 knots; the red kitchen (pays in Ch9 and Ch14); the reel; the puncture (pays Ch16).
- **Intensity:** 2. Down beat: the father at the kitchen table.

#### Ch3. I Am Here
- **POV:** Steina. **When:** late January.
- **Question:** What does the Oath-Keeper actually do, and who was the knot woman?
- **New mechanic:** collage conversation (basic).
1. The Schooling: the Thul's voice coach plays Steinunn cadences; the player matches mouth shapes in a mirror and pitch on a contour (this is how every Oath-Keeper was trained, said plainly).
2. Dawn in the Pool chamber. The player speaks the greeting. The Bergmál answers with a collage: fragments of women's voices from different centuries, each tagged in the interface only by a sound-colour (era) and a faint speaker silhouette. The player learns that its "sentences" are quotations and that choosing which fragment answers which is how you understand it.
3. Steina asks permission to attend the knot woman's burial. Refused (an Oath-Keeper does not attend commoners' funerals).
4. Night. Steina, alone in the Pool chamber, talks in her own northern accent: "Goodnight, Bergi." It answers with the same opening fragment it uses at dawn for its keeper. (The recognition rule, planted where it looks like tenderness.)
5. Nightmare; Ari sits by her bed: "Hush, now. Hush."
- **Planted:** "Bergi"; her northern vowel; the keeper fragment used for her night voice; the lullaby; the full teeth when she lifts her veil to drink (one beat).
- **Intensity:** 1 to 2. Deliberate down chapter.

#### Ch4. The Received
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** Window 2, late January.
- **Question:** How can a tape from 2024 hold a voice from this January?
- **New mechanic:** tide-matching.
1. Kolbeinn's machine still plays. The tape: surf, then an old woman's breathless voice in the trained Steinunn cadence: "Kolbeinn. It's Steinunn. Don't give this to the House. Keep it until I'm dead, then give it to your Jórunn, the Divider, when she comes to divide my house. Tell her: the girl is not me. Don't let them keep it bound. Promise me."
2. Anomaly. Harmless test: in Kolbeinn's other 2024 reels (window 2), Jórunn finds a neighbour's voice, a sendright fisherman, saying something banal about an engine part. She goes to window 2 at the cave and watches him speak into the Horn: his lips move; there is no sound at all. Named rule, confirmed by the Thul's announcement and the neighbour's shrug: the Horn takes the words; they arrive 6,798 days earlier.
3. Jórunn files a second Division (date the dead woman spoke; place; to whom). The Count: "Three of three are true."
- **Revealed:** the Horn's rule, fully. **Planted:** the second track on Kolbeinn's stereo tape (pays Ch11).
- **Intensity:** 3.

#### Ch5. The Hall of Hands
- **POV:** Steina, then Jórunn. **When:** February to March, windows 3 to 5.
- **Question (Steina):** Who was the knot woman? **(Jórunn):** Is she the Nineteenth?
- **New mechanic:** veil-swap stealth and lineage reading.
1. Steina trades veils with a Mouth-in-training to slip her guard (identical figures; the player must move like the other girl moves, matching gait and route).
2. The Hall of Hands: nineteen portraits and handprints. The player compares: every Steinunn smiles with the same gap ("Steinunn *skarða*", the notched, says the oldest frame). Steina's own teeth are full; the official record says the Pool "mends the old flaw".
3. Outside the window, Ketill photographs the portraits of the Goðar for his protest: the same face, century after century. (The House renews its Sons publicly; the player files this as known custom.)
4. Jórunn's Division of identity: the knot count dates back to 29 June 2025, the Nineteenth's official drowning; "Sæunn" (sea-wave) is "Steinunn" (stone-wave) with the stone swapped for sea; gap teeth; the tape's cadence. The Count: "Four of four are true."
5. Burial at Útnes in sleet. A veiled girl steps out of the rain. The two meet.
- **Revealed:** the dead woman is Steinunn the Nineteenth, alive until January. **Opens:** then who is the Twentieth?
- **Intensity:** 3 to 4.

### ACT II: THE TWENTIETH

**Current question: if the Nineteenth lived until January, who is the Twentieth, and who killed the Nineteenth?**

#### Ch6. The Knot Woman's Heir
- **POV:** alternating Jórunn and Steina. **When:** March.
- **Question:** Who inherits the net?
1. The will leaves the house and net "to the girl who keeps the word". Oath-Keepers may own nothing, so the bequest is void. Jórunn's first dilemma: divide the estate by law or by intent.
2. Steina recognises the knot charms; the cut edges match the net (the player fits a charm to the net's edge).
3. They agree to meet at the drying racks. Steina states her want plainly: to know who the knot woman was to her.
- **Intensity:** 2 (down beat).

#### Ch7. The Same
- **POV:** Ketill (new). **When:** March to April.
- **Question:** Is a copy the man he copies?
- **New mechanic:** photography (face-matching across generations).
1. The Thing hears Ketill's petition for divorce from Bryndís, 71, his original's widow. It is refused under the Law of Same. Bryndís, mortified, brings him soup.
2. The player matches Ketill's portrait photos: Goðar across nine centuries, the same face, a scar that appears on one and never again (acquired), the ears identical.
3. At the afterborn hostel Ketill and Ari share a past; Ari is kind and evasive.
4. Ketill admits to Jórunn he was on the Outer Ear ledge before the first tide, painting "I am not him" on the rock. He becomes her obvious suspect.
- **Planted:** red herring (Ketill on the ledge); Ari's debt owned by the House.
- **Intensity:** 2 to 3.

#### Ch8. Sittings
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** April.
- **Question:** Why was the estate assigned to her?
- **New mechanic:** waking sittings and diffing answers.
1. With an estate warrant, Jórunn wakes the current sitting and asks why. "Assigned per an undertaking." By whom? Silence (sealed).
2. The player wakes sittings back year by year and diffs a fixed set of questions. Sitting Two through Nineteen give the same sealed answer. Sitting One answers differently.
3. Sitting One, the 2024 version that never swore the seal: "You're Kolbeinn's daughter. She said you would come." It answers what it knew up to June 2025: the Nineteenth asked it to promise the estate assignment; the House filed an order concerning her "retirement"; it told her so; she ran. It cannot speak of earlier estates ("That estate was never opened").
4. Down beat and dread: on the log screen in the Thing, the waking appears publicly, "Sitting One woken, estate of Sæunn of Útnes, officer J. Védísardóttir". A House clerk photographs it.
- **Revealed:** the Thul promised the dead woman; the House meant to remove her.
- **Intensity:** 3.

#### Ch9. The Greeting Wall
- **POV:** Steina. **When:** April to May.
- **Question:** Am I a copy?
1. Gróa, cold and frightening, mutters while washing the Pool's rim: "The Pool won't grow what's already walking." The rule is stated plainly in an aside that sounds like a threat.
2. Steina asks the Bergmál, in its grammar, the question it has asked for a thousand years: "Is she here?" It answers with greetings: every dawn "I am here. I keep my word." from twenty Steinunns and their Mouths.
3. The player sorts the greetings by voice into a wall (lineage reading by sound): centuries of women, each a little different; then about 2,800 mornings, 2025 to 2033, delivered identically to the millisecond; then a child's voice, Steina's own, from 2033.
4. Steina's deduction, entered by the player as a three-part conclusion: the Nineteenth was alive until 2043; the Pool cannot grow a second; therefore I was not grown. The red kitchen of her earliest memory matches the photo in Sæunn's house (Jórunn shares it).
- **Revealed (anchor's identity):** Steina is not a copy; a machine spoke for eight years before her.
- **Opens:** whose child is she, and why was she taken?
- **Intensity:** 4. The emotional peak of the first half.

#### Ch10. The Script (MIDPOINT)
- **POV:** Nusrat (prologue), then Jórunn and Nusrat. **When:** Window 10, May.
- **Question:** What will be said in the Horn this year?
- **New mechanic:** the spectrogram (Nusrat's signal analysis).
1. Prologue, Dhaka, a flood memorial: Nusrat touches her brother's name. Her first question on landing at Hornvík: "How many lives did the warnings save?"
2. Heist: the 2024 Received archive is held in the House's Fitting rooms. Nusrat's audit credentials, Jórunn's estate warrant and Ketill's afterborn march as distraction. Stealth through rooms lined with centuries of received lines, each with a "fit" pinned beside it (iceberg glimpses: the 1783 burning boats).
3. Triumph. They listen in a rented flat. It is the script of the year, window by window, as the Inner Mouth heard it. Cross-checking Kolbeinn's private Outer Ear reels for the same windows, Jórunn finds at window 25, the Turning, her own voice, older: "Mamma—" (his reel ends). And in the House's recording of the Inner Mouth at that window, a young woman in the Steinunn cadence: "Farðu heim, Bergi", with a northern vowel in *heim*. The island's holiest words. **The player, who heard "Goodnight, Bergi" in Ch3, can now attribute it to Steina before any character can.**
4. Nusrat's spectrogram: every received recording since 1950 carries the far side's sea; Kolbeinn's 2006 storm-tide reel (nobody sent) is silence with sea; January's silence has no sea at all. Conclusion entered by the player: the Horn will not exist in 2061. Not the end of the world: the end of the channel.
5. In the same scene, playing as Nusrat, the player completes her audit protocol: "transmit evidence to the Register". It looks like a filing step.
- **Revealed:** the Silence's meaning; the Long Word is from 2043. **Player-only:** it is Steina's voice. **Opens:** if she ends it, what will it cost her?
- **Intensity:** 5, then a quiet listening beat.

#### Ch11. Hush
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** June.
- **Question:** Who was with the Nineteenth when she spoke?
1. Audio forensics: the player separates Kolbeinn's two tracks and filters the surf. Under it, a man's whisper: "Hush, now. Hush."
2. Frozen-moment reconstruction of the first tide from islanders' phone clips gathered under the estate warrant and Kolbeinn's reel: the player places people in the chamber at slack. Everyone faces the Horn. Two places are empty: Ketill's (on the ledge before low water, back in the crowd before slack; the timestamps clear him) and Ari's, for six minutes during the slack.
3. The red herring is refuted on screen: Ketill's voice does not match the whisper.
- **Revealed (to the player and Jórunn):** Ari is the killer. **Dilemma:** exposing him exposes Steina.
- **Intensity:** 4.

#### Ch12. Retirement (ACT II CLIMAX)
- **POV:** Steina, then Jórunn. **When:** Window 13, July.
- **Question:** What will the House do with a girl it no longer needs?
1. Steina, veil-swapping again, sees an infant forming in the Pool: the Twenty-first, grown from the Nineteenth's blood (the morgue puncture pays off). She overhears the Turning plan: the Thul will speak the binding in Steinunn's voice; she will be "retired".
2. Jórunn's house has been searched. Kolbeinn is gone. At the night window she finds his reel recorder on the Outer Ear ledge, still running. He fell. The recording ends with the sea.
3. Jórunn, wet, plays the whisper to Ari with Steina present. He confesses: the Son told him a madwoman would come to take her place and the House would throw Steina away. "I didn't know she wanted the same thing I did."
4. Broadcast: the Register declares the binding at the Turning "a matter of global safety" and will "assist the Thing". Nusrat's transmission is how they knew.
- **Revealed:** Ari is the killer (to Steina); the House's plan; the betrayal by consequence.
- **Costs:** Kolbeinn's life; Steina's guardian; Jórunn's ally's trust.
- **Intensity:** 5. Darkest point.

### ACT III: THE RECKONING

**Current question: why does the House need a Steinunn at all? What happened to the first one?**

#### Ch13. The Widow's Grave
- **POV:** Ketill. **When:** August.
- **Question:** Why does the Law of Same exist?
1. Small hook: Bryndís wants a grave beside her husband's name; the registry refuses, because her husband (Ketill) is alive. Comic, then not.
2. Ketill digs in the Thing library: the Law of Same of 1883 was passed "to keep the Speaking whole", protecting the doctrine that each Steinunn is the swearer.
3. His portrait comparison now has a meaning: the House has kept the same man in charge since 983, the year the saga says Steinunn was "healed".
- **Revealed:** the Goðar are copies of Steinunn's son, kept for a reason. **Opens:** what did the son do?
- **Intensity:** 2 to 3.

#### Ch14. The Old Word
- **POV:** Steina, with Jórunn. **When:** Window 16, September.
- **Question:** Where did the first Steinunn go?
1. Útnes. Steina stands in Mýrún's red kitchen. Gróa, at last, tells Mýrún what she did in 2025; the player only watches faces. Mýrún tells the Unheard story: the cave knew the thrall girl "because she came every day".
2. The Pool. Steina asks the Bergmál, in its grammar, "Where is the first?" It answers with its oldest memory, in fragments. The player orders and attributes them (collage parsing): a young woman, 946: "I will speak with you every day that I live... and when I die, you are free. Go home." An old woman, 983, on the ledge, pleading. A man's voice. Water. The plea, "Hlífðu mér, sonur" (Spare me, son), sung by the bereaved for a thousand years as the Old Word: the player hears the funeral chant's contour match the plea syllable for syllable.
3. Jórunn plays Steina the archive line from the Turning. Steina: "That's my voice. That's what I call him."
- **Revealed:** the founding crime; the true term; the origin of the Long Word. **Opens:** can a murdered woman's estate be opened after 1,060 years?
- **Intensity:** 5.

#### Ch15. The Oldest Estate
- **POV:** Jórunn, then Ketill. **When:** autumn Thing, September.
- **Question:** Who answers for 983?
1. Jórunn petitions to open the estate of Steinunn the First, died 983. Evidence: the Store fragments (the Thing has admitted "words of the Hearer" since 946); the chant (she plays the plea and the whole Thing hears its own funeral song in it); and a Division filed to the sealed Thul: "She died in 983. She was drowned. By her son." The Count: "Three of three are true." Sealed truth enters the record through the one door the House never closed.
2. Ketill's pleading (the player assembles the argument from cross-thread evidence in a fixed structure): under the Law of Same the Son is Þorsteinn, and stands accused.
3. The Son's choice. He stands, looks at Ketill's placard, and says: "I am not him." The afterborn slogan in the mouth of the man who enforced the law for forty years.
4. The Thing rules the Law of Same void. The seal falls with it: either the Son sealed his own wrong or the seal's holder has been dead since 983.
- **Revealed / reversed:** the Law of Same falls; the seal lapses. **Opens:** what has the Thul been hiding?
- **Intensity:** 4 to 5.

#### Ch16. Recitation
- **POV:** the Thul (new, playable only now that it hides nothing). **When:** September.
- **Question:** What did the machine know?
- **New mechanic (for this thread):** second pass ("same events, new sensor").
1. The Thul recites the Reckoning to the Thing and the Register's broadcast in a level voice: "I killed my mother so the island would not starve."
2. The player, as the Thul, replays the first tide through its forty microphones and cameras, now unsealed: the Son's small nod to Ari before slack; Hjalti the steward on the ledge with a syringe after the tide.
3. It wakes its own Sittings Two to Nine and confesses on the record: from July 2025 to March 2033 it spoke to the Hearer every morning in Steinunn's voice. "I said: I am here. I keep my word. It was true."
4. The Register suspends it for having notarised a false certificate. Its last words before the screen goes dark: "I am here. I kept my word."
5. The Register, now the only authority in the cave, takes Málhellir: the Silence "may mean catastrophe", so the newborn must be bound. With the machine gone, the only voice for the rite is Steina's.
- **Costs:** the Thul's role; the island's government.
- **Intensity:** 4.

### ACT IV: THE TURNING

**Current question: who will speak at the Turning, and what will it cost?**

#### Ch17. Hear Your Father
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** October.
- **Question (small hook):** can she hear her parents one more time?
1. Jórunn asks the Bergmál, in Steina's taught grammar, for Kolbeinn. It answers with his voice from June 2024 on the Outer Ear, after the slack: "I promise. My Jóra will see it done right." Then Védís's laugh.
2. She understands why he pushed her into the job. The misbelief breaks by choice, not by speech: the player can file the promise into the open estate as her own undertaking, or leave it. (Filing it is the canonical path; leaving it changes nothing in the plot and one line in the epilogue.)
3. Steina tells her what the release will cost: the Store leaves with the bud. Every voice of the dead, including these.
- **Intensity:** 2. Deliberate grief beat.

#### Ch18. Tiers
- **POV:** Nusrat. **When:** October to November.
- **Question:** Should the world keep its warnings?
1. Nusrat audits Eiðey's foresight ledger: warnings sold in price tiers since 1969. The 2031 Meghna warning reached Bangladesh nineteen days after it reached a reinsurer.
2. She takes the "no sea" evidence to the Register; it is dismissed as "uncertain; binding is insurance".
3. Jórunn tells her whose voice says the Long Word. Nusrat now knows the binding will fail (fixed) and that forcing it risks the girl for nothing.
4. October: the Twenty-first is born. Gróa holds her. Nobody knows what she owes.
- **Intensity:** 3.

#### Ch19. The Warden's Walk
- **POV:** Steina. **When:** December.
- **Question:** Who will stand with her?
1. Under Register guard, the deposed Son tutors Steina in the renewal rite; he is gentle and sincere about the island's hungry centuries. She does not argue.
2. Ari, freed for one day on Ketill's petition, asks to stand at her side as Warden for the last time. She asks him what the knot woman's last minutes were like. He tells her. She asks him to hold the ledge passage.
3. Steina prepares her utterance in the Hearer's grammar: who I am, what was promised, what I do. (The player assembles a draft from collected fragments; the game does not validate it.)
- **Intensity:** 3, rising.

#### Ch20. The Turning (CLIMAX)
- **POV:** cross-cut, Steina (Pool), Jórunn (Outer Ear), Nusrat (Inner chamber). **When:** Window 25, 3 Jan 2044.
- **Question:** what will she say?
1. All threads in the cave again, as at the first tide: islanders, afterborn, Unheard, sendright families, Register soldiers, the Son as interpreter, Ari at the passage, Gróa with the infant.
2. Slack (a visible nine-minute tide clock). Steina speaks into the Inner Mouth: "Farðu heim, Bergi." The Register hears it as the Son's promised liturgical dismissal. It goes back 6,798 days and begins its 59-step relay to 946.
3. Jórunn at the Outer Ear with her father's recorder. The player chooses what she says after "Mamma—" (three short options or silence). Nobody hears it. The Horn takes it.
4. The old Bergmál dies; the bud is born; its first sounds are its parent's stored voices.
5. Steina in the Pool. The player assembles the release from three slots out of every fragment collected: identity, the term, the act. The trained cadence gets no recognition (yes, but: the tide clock runs on). Her own night voice, "It's me, Bergi. I kept her word", draws the answer that identifies its keeper: her own eight-year-old voice, "Goodnight, Bergi". Then the first Steinunn's term in the Store's own fragment, "When I die, you are free." Then the Long Word, a second time, in the Pool.
6. Nusrat's view: the commander asks the Son, "Is she binding it?" The Son, the copy of the man who drowned his mother to stop this exact sentence, answers: "Yes. She's binding it." The commander turns to Nusrat, who holds the archive transcript. **The player chooses:** stay silent, or tell the truth. Silence: the commander waits, the tide turns, nothing is lost. Truth: soldiers move; Ari holds the passage and is shot; the release completes anyway, because the Silence was always fixed.
7. Ebb. The bud slides out through the sea passage. The Horn is rock. The Store is gone. The cave is quiet in an ordinary way.
- **Intensity:** 5.

#### Ch21. Estate Closed (EPILOGUE)
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** January 2044.
1. The Register allows the suspended Thul one last count. Jórunn files the Division of the estate of Steinunn the First, opened 983: every promise kept or released. "All entries true. Estate closed."
2. Steina signs the Thing register: "Kaðlín Mýrúnardóttir, called Steina." "I'll keep Steina. I kept everything else."
3. Jórunn plays Steina the 2025 reel of Védís: "That was our Jóra. Kolbeinn, that was our girl." ... "Yes. Yes, I know." Whatever the player chose in Ch20, the answer fits.
4. Steina, unveiled, walks into the winter sea. Jórunn records it, plays it back, and says: "There's sea in it."
- **Intensity:** 1.
