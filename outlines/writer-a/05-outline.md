## 1. One sentence

**Dividing a drowned woman's estate, a clerk inherits a promise that silences the future.**

(Changed from "A probate clerk can save a counterfeit clone only by ending her island's foresight", which misdescribed the plot and spoiled the anchor's identity. See 01, Round 7.)

## 2. One paragraph

On a North Atlantic island that has heard its own future through a living alien for eleven centuries, the future goes silent at the first tide of the year, and an old net-mender is found drowned in the cave mouth. The estate clerk assigned to her house discovers two things. The dead woman was the island's previous Oath-Keeper, who faked her death eighteen years ago. And the clerk's own father recorded her dying words eighteen years before she spoke them.

**First disaster:** the veiled girl who now keeps the island's oath with the alien is not the copy she was told she is but a child taken from a poor family, and the woman who could have explained it is dead.

**Second disaster**, caused by the clerk's own attempts to prove it: her public questioning of an old version of the island's notary machine alerts the ruling House. Her father dies fleeing them. The girl's loving guardian is exposed as the killer. And the world's treaty body, handed the island's archive by the clerk's own ally, learns that the girl will try to free the alien and joins the House against her.

**Third disaster**, caused by the clerk's success: her petition opens the founder's thousand-year-old estate. That topples the House and frees the machine to confess. But it also gives the treaty body the legal standing to take the cave as trustee, with the deposed ruler as its adviser and a plan to bind the alien's newborn and ship it away.

**Ending:** at the last tide the girl, recognised by the alien because she has kept the promise every day for ten years, releases it in her own voice. The clerk races through the cave to file the Division that closes the estate and ends the trusteeship. The machine counts it true against orders. The world loses its foresight, and the island loses every stored voice of its dead, the clerk's parents among them.

### Thread paragraphs

- **Jórunn (the Divider).** Assigned a stranger's estate outside her district, she reconstructs a fled Oath-Keeper's last eighteen years from a net, a tape and a photograph of a red kitchen. Proving that the girl is a substitute costs her her father. She opens the estate of the island's founder at the Thing, and learns that this handed the cave to the world. In the Hearer's Store she hears her father's promise. She closes the estate at the Turning, knowing it takes his voice with it.
- **Steina (the Oath-Keeper).** Wanting only to bury the knot woman, she learns from the Hearer's own record of greetings that a machine kept her office before her and that she cannot be a copy. She learns that her guardian killed the knot woman, and that the tune she leads is a murdered mother's plea. She hears her own voice speaking the island's holiest words. Pressed by the deposed Son to bind the newborn, she decides when and how to say them.
- **Ketill (the afterborn).** His divorce from his original's widow is refused, and his photographs show nine centuries of the same Son. His case against the Law of Same forces the Son to choose between being a murderer and being nobody, and he wins. Then he stakes his own freedom on Ari's.
- **Nusrat (the auditor).** Sent to find out whether the Silence foretells catastrophe, she proves it foretells the end of the channel here. She unknowingly gives the Register its reason to seize the girl, and learns how unequally the warnings were sold. At the Turning she chooses between the world's record and a man's life.
- **The Thul.** Bound by one past version's seal and another's promises, it routes the case to the right clerk and counts truths it may not speak. When the law frees it, it recites the founding crime. Placed under the world's audit and ordered not to count, it makes its first choice.

## 3. One page

**Act I, The Estate (January to March 2043).**
- The island gathers in Málhellir for the first window of the Standing, as it has every 18.6 years since 946. The veiled Oath-Keeper, Steina, leads the Ebb-Song; the Son of the Mother's House presides; afterborn protesters hold placards reading "I am not him".
- At slack the island falls silent. The House's reader speaks soundlessly into the Inner Mouth, and the Horn, which has delivered the future's voices for eleven centuries, delivers nothing: not a voice, not even the sea. After the tide, net-menders find an old woman drowned in the Wash.
- The Thul assigns her estate to Jórunn, a Divider from another district. In the dead woman's house at Útnes, Jórunn reconstructs a life: a net with 6,408 knots, the first five in tarred boat cord; a carved needle reading "I am here. I keep my word"; a photo of a red kitchen; and a copied reel, labelled in her father's hand, on which the dead woman asks him to keep the tape "until I'm dead" and give it to "the Divider who comes to divide my house".
- Her father's own five-second level checks of the 2024 Standing teach her the Horn's rule. They also hold two things nobody can explain: the year's windows 17 to 24 are empty, and the last window opens with English radio.
- At the morgue the body has the gap teeth of every Oath-Keeper in the island's portraits. Jórunn proves the dead woman is Steinunn the Nineteenth, officially drowned in 2025.
- Meanwhile Steina keeps her dawn word to the Hearer, talks to it secretly at night as "Bergi", watches a "commissioned" infant forming in the Pool, and wants only to bury the knot woman who left her charms. At the burial in Útnes, a veiled girl who has slipped her guard steps out of the rain.

**Act II, The Twentieth (March to July 2043).**
- If the Nineteenth lived until January, the Twentieth could not have been grown in 2026: the Pool will not grow what is already walking. Steina works this out herself by sorting eighteen years of greetings stored in the Hearer, and finding an unbroken chain: the Nineteenth's last morning, one old woman's morning, eight years of identical machine-voiced mornings, then her own child's voice.
- Jórunn wakes the Thul's first sitting, the only version that never swore the House's seal. It tells her it promised the dead woman two things: her estate would come to Kolbeinn's daughter, and it would never help keep the Hearer bound. The waking is logged publicly.
- With the auditor Nusrat and Ketill's protest as cover, Jórunn steals the House's archive of the 2024-25 Standing. The player hears, at the last window, soldiers' radio and then the island's holiest phrase, "Farðu heim, Bergi", in Steina's voice. Nusrat proves the Silence means the Horn at Eiðey will not exist in 2061. In the same triumphant scene she transmits the archive to the Register by protocol.
- Jórunn finds the stereo master in her father's recorder and brings up a whisper under the surf: "Hush, now, hush", the lullaby Steina's guardian, Ari, uses for her nightmares. Her logged count on the cause of death joins the logged waking on the House's desk. The House searches her father's house; the old man flees to the only place he remembers, the Outer Ear, and falls.
- Ari confesses in front of Steina. On air, the Register's head of mission, Sanne Verhoeven, whose city a warning once saved, declares the binding at the Turning a matter of global safety. The archive told the Register that an Oath-Keeper will say the Long Word, and the Son told it what the words mean.

**Act III, The Reckoning (August to September 2043).**
- Ketill's elderly "wife" wants a grave for her husband, whom the law says is alive. Ketill traces the Law of Same to 1883 and to the House.
- Steina meets her birth mother in Útnes, teaches Jórunn the Hearer's grammar at her mother's table, and asks the Hearer where the first Steinunn went. It answers with its oldest memories: a covenant, a lullaby, a plea received from the future, and a gap in 983 exactly the length of that plea. The island's funeral tune is a mother asking her son to spare her.
- At the Thing the Son must recuse himself under his own Law of Same, and the estate of Steinunn the First is opened. Only now can the Thul count it: "She died in 983. She was drowned. By her son." Three of three. Ketill argues that under the House's own law the Son is the murderer. The Son says "I am not him." The Law of Same falls. The House's seal falls with it, because no one may seal his own wrong.
- The Thul recites the Reckoning. It replays the first tide through its forty lenses, and plays the House's reports from Jórunn's own kitchen: her father told them.
- The leaderless Thing grants the Register trusteeship of the cave for as long as the founder's estate stays open. That night the deposed Son brings the Register the rite and a plan.

**Act IV, The Turning (October 2043 to January 2044).**
- Grieving, Jórunn asks the Hearer for her parents and hears her father's 2024 promise: "My Jóra will see it done right." She chooses it, and learns the price: the Store leaves with the Hearer.
- Nusrat audits the warnings' price tiers. The Register restricts the Thul and briefs **the Transfer**: bind the newborn, net it at the sea door, ship it north. The Silence fits that future too. Free, dead or taken: the timeline does not say which.
- The deposed Son tutors Steina in the binding and offers her a real bargain. Ari is let out on Ketill's surety. Jórunn and Nusrat tide-match the soldiers' own radio check from the archive to find the one unguarded way in.
- At the last window, all threads stand in the cave again:
  - Steina speaks the Long Word into the Horn; Jórunn speaks to her dead mother at the Outer Ear, then runs.
  - The old Hearer dies and buds. In the Pool, Steina speaks in her own accent, and the newborn answers with her own childhood voice: "Goodnight, Bergi". She releases it.
  - The Son, asked what she is saying, says "She is binding it." Nusrat chooses.
  - Jórunn files the closing Division, and the Thul, ordered not to, counts it true. Verhoeven reads her own clause and lifts the net.
  - The bud leaves on the ebb. The cave is rock.
- Jórunn is struck off; Steina swims; "There's sea in it."

## 4. Full outline, act by act

Format per chapter: viewpoint, true window and date, the **narrative question** on screen, the one **new mechanic** (at most one), numbered **gameplay beats**, what is **revealed**, what is **planted**, and the intensity (1 low to 5 high), with deliberate down beats.

### ACT I: THE ESTATE

**Current question for the whole act: who was the drowned net-mender?**

#### Ch1. The First Tide
- **POV:** Jórunn, with a short Steina vignette. **When:** Window 1, 14 Jan 2043.
- **Question:** What is the island waiting to hear?
- **New mechanic:** the reel recorder (record, scrub, mark a moment).
1. Jórunn helps her father Kolbeinn down the cave steps. He insists on carrying his old reel recorder and lets her hold the microphone. The player records the crowd: a ready-made tutorial in listening.
2. Steina vignette: the player, as the veiled girl, leads the Ebb-Song by matching the chant's rising and falling contour with the stick (rhythm and pitch, no text). The chant has no words until the whole cave speaks its last line, the Long Word: "Farðu heim, Bergi". She says the last word a beat early.
3. Faces established in one sweep, each with a visible want:
   - the Son ("the Son, renewed", a public custom);
   - Ari the Warden at Steina's side;
   - Ketill's placard, "I am not him";
   - Gróa the midwife, watching like a hawk;
   - small glass lenses in the cave roof;
   - the Thul's level voice over the loudspeakers: "Slack water in one minute. Words given to the Horn are given away."
4. Slack. The island goes silent and draws back. A man in House grey steps to the Inner Mouth, and his lips move with no sound; no islander reacts. (Unexplained until Ch4.) Every head turns to listen for the reply. Nothing. The player can scrub the recording: no voice, and no surf either. Kolbeinn, puzzled: "No sea, Jóra. There's no sea in it." (Planted as dementia.)
5. As the slack ends, Ari pushes back through the crowd to Steina's side. His cloak is wet. She notices, and he says "spray on the steps". (Planted, not flagged. His six minutes away are reconstructed in Ch11.)
6. After the tide, shouting from the Wash: an old woman drowned among the net offerings.
- **Planted:** the Long Word's last word; the silent reader; "No sea"; Ari's absence and wet cloak; the proverb chanted in the liturgy, "By the word kept is the keeper known"; the lenses.
- **Intensity:** 2 rising to 4.

#### Ch2. Division
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** January, between windows 1 and 2.
- **Question:** Who was she, and what did she owe?
- **New mechanic:** the Division (estate reconstruction), validated by the Count.
1. The Thul assigns her the estate. She notices it is outside her district. The Thul says only, "Assigned per an undertaking."
2. Morgue: the body has a gap where both upper lateral teeth should be, and a needle puncture at the elbow nobody can explain.
3. Útnes. The player walks Sæunn's house as she left it and can tag each object to a person and a promise (owed to, owed by, kept, broken):
   - the net, thousands of knots, one per day, the first few in a different, tarred cord;
   - a carved net needle: "I am here. I keep my word";
   - a cut edge where small pieces were removed;
   - a photo of a red-painted kitchen;
   - a residence certificate in the name Sæunn, stamped "Attested as filed";
   - a reel box in Kolbeinn's handwriting: "Ytra Eyra, 4.6.2024, gluggi 1, AFRIT" (copy).
4. Home, where Jórunn has lived since her father's illness. Hjalti, the House's pension officer, is at the kitchen table with Kolbeinn's monthly envelope, kindly and dull; he leaves. Kolbeinn, shown the reel box, says "The girl's not her, Jóra. Don't let them." She is tired. She tells him she never wanted this job and still does not know why he pushed her into it.
5. First Division. The player drags three evidence objects into statement slots: the certificate (name), the needle (trade), the knots (years at Útnes). The Count: "Two of three are true." ("Sæunn" is false as a birth name.) The rules are taught by the interface, not a speech: at least three statements, each with an object attached, and the count appears on the Thing's public log screen ("Count: estate of Sæunn of Útnes, 3 statements: identity").
- **Planted:** gap teeth (pays in Ch5); 6,408 knots and the boat cord; the red kitchen (pays in Ch9 and Ch14); the copy reel (an original exists somewhere); the puncture (pays in Ch12 and Ch16); "Attested as filed"; Hjalti at the table (pays in Ch16).
- **Intensity:** 2. Down beat: the father at the kitchen table.

#### Ch3. I Am Here
- **POV:** Steina. **When:** late January.
- **Question:** What does the Oath-Keeper actually do, and who was the knot woman?
- **New mechanic:** collage conversation (basic).
1. Voice school: the Thul's voice coach plays Steinunn cadences, and the player matches mouth shapes in a mirror and pitch on a contour. (This is how every Oath-Keeper was trained, said plainly.) An old teacher, in passing: "It was born here, you know. That dead rock down the back is its mother."
2. Dawn in the Pool chamber. A speaker grille in the rock above the water; a small lens. The player speaks the greeting. The Hearer answers with a collage: fragments of women's voices from different centuries, each tagged only by a sound-colour (era) and a faint speaker silhouette. The player learns that its "sentences" are quotations, and that choosing which fragment answers which is how you understand it. In the Pool's far basin a pale shape has been forming since the first tide. Gróa, curtly: "A commission. A Langanes widow's drowned boy, renewed."
3. Steina asks permission to attend the knot woman's burial. Refused: an Oath-Keeper does not attend commoners' funerals.
4. Night. Steina, alone in the Pool chamber, talks in her own northern accent: "Goodnight, Bergi." It answers with the same opening fragment it uses at dawn for its keeper. (The recognition rule, planted where it looks like tenderness.)
5. Nightmare; Ari sits by her bed: "Hush, now. Hush."
- **Planted:** "Bergi"; her northern vowel; the keeper fragment used for her night voice; the lullaby; the full teeth when she lifts her veil to drink (one beat); the "commission" growing since January; the grille and the lens; the Dead Horn as "its mother".
- **Intensity:** 1 to 2. Deliberate down chapter.

#### Ch4. Five Seconds
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** Window 2, late January.
- **Question:** How can a tape from 2024 hold a voice from this January?
- **New mechanic:** tide-matching.
1. Kolbeinn's machine still plays. The copy reel: surf, oars, then an old woman's breathless voice in the trained Steinunn cadence: "Kolbeinn. It's Steinunn. Don't give this to the House. Keep it until I'm dead, then give it to the Divider who comes to divide my house. Tell her: the girl is not me. Don't let them keep it bound. Promise me." The copy is mono and thick with surf.
2. Anomaly, then a harmless test. In Kolbeinn's drawer there is a reel of **level checks**: five seconds of each mouth at the start of every window of the 2024-25 Standing, labelled in his hand. Window 2, Inner Mouth: a neighbour's sworn mark, "Sigurður Bryndísarson, Hornvík. Tell Siggi the bilge pump is the left one." Jórunn goes to window 2 at the cave and watches the same man step up to the Inner Mouth. His lips shape the mark; the recorder needle does not move. He shrugs at her: everyone here knows. Named rule, from the Thul's announcement and the neighbour: the Horn takes the words, and they arrive 6,798 days earlier. Readers only ever read the Book of Tidings, so what comes back is the chronicle of the coming eighteen years, never this year's news.
3. Tide-matching at low stakes. The player lines the level checks up against the 2043 tide table and predicts, by her mark, who will read at window 3 (a sendright widow), and is right. Laid out on the table, the reel shows two things nobody can explain. Windows 17 to 24 hold only sea at both mouths. Window 25, Inner Mouth, opens with five seconds of English radio: "All posts: slack—". Jórunn: "Who speaks English in the Hearing chamber?"
4. Jórunn files a second Division (date the dead woman spoke; place; to whom), with the tape, the level-check reel and the tide table as evidence. The Count: "Three of three are true."
- **Revealed:** the Horn's rule, fully. **Opened:** the empty windows and the radio. **Planted:** "AFRIT", so an original exists (pays in Ch11).
- **Intensity:** 3.

#### Ch5. The Hall of Hands
- **POV:** Steina, then Jórunn. **When:** February to March, windows 3 to 5.
- **Question (Steina):** Who was the knot woman? **(Jórunn):** Is she the Nineteenth?
- **New mechanic:** lineage reading (with veil-swap stealth, which comes from the Oath-Keeper's office and is tagged that way).
1. Steina trades veils with a voice-school girl to slip her guard. The two figures are identical, and the player must move as the other girl moves, matching gait and route.
2. The Hall of Hands: nineteen portraits and handprints. The player compares them: every Steinunn smiles with the same gap ("Steinunn *skarða*", "the notched", says the oldest frame). Steina's own teeth are full; the official record says the Pool "mends the old flaw".
3. Outside the window, Ketill photographs the portraits of the Sons for his protest: the same face, century after century. (The House renews its Sons publicly; the player files this as known custom.)
4. Jórunn's Division of identity, with these exhibits:
   - the knot count, which dates back to 29 June 2025, the Nineteenth's official drowning;
   - the first five knots, in tarred boat cord: she tied them at sea, and she did not drown;
   - "Sæunn" (sea-wave) is "Steinunn" (stone-wave) with the stone swapped for sea;
   - the gap teeth;
   - the tape's cadence, matched to the voice school's drills.
   The Count: "Four of four are true."
5. Burial at Útnes in sleet. A veiled girl steps out of the rain. The two meet.
- **Revealed:** the dead woman is Steinunn the Nineteenth, alive until January. **Opens:** then who is the Twentieth?
- **Intensity:** 3 to 4.

### ACT II: THE TWENTIETH

**Current question: if the Nineteenth lived until January, who is the Twentieth, and who killed the Nineteenth?**

#### Ch6. The Knot Woman's Heir
- **POV:** alternating Jórunn and Steina. **When:** March.
- **Question:** Who inherits the net?
1. The will leaves the house and net "to the girl who keeps the word". Oath-Keepers may own nothing, so the bequest is void. Jórunn's first dilemma: divide the estate by law or by intent.
2. The same afternoon, a thirty-second side case at the Thul's grille. A sendright widow's estate includes a record her husband sealed before he died, his own short-weighting of fish. The Thul: "No one may seal his own wrong. Seal void." The widow pays the fishermen back. (Low stakes; pays off in Ch15.)
3. Steina recognises the knot charms; the player fits a charm to the net's cut edge.
4. They agree to meet at the drying racks. Steina states her want plainly: to know who the knot woman was to her.
- **Planted:** "No one may seal his own wrong."
- **Intensity:** 2 (down beat).

#### Ch7. The Same
- **POV:** Ketill (new). **When:** March to April.
- **Question:** Is a copy the man he copies?
- **New mechanic:** photography (face-matching across generations).
1. The Thing hears Ketill's petition for divorce from Bryndís, 71, his original's widow. It is refused under the Law of Same. His afterborn certificate is read into the record, "Attested as filed". Bryndís, mortified, brings him soup.
2. The player matches Ketill's photos of the Sons' portraits: the same face across the centuries, a scar that appears on one and never again (acquired), identical ears. Ketill counts the frames back to the first: "983. The year the saga says Steinunn was healed. They've kept the same man in charge ever since." (The custom is public; the date is the beat. This was Ch13's reveal in the first draft, now a beat here.)
3. At the afterborn hostel Ketill and Ari share a past; Ari is kind and evasive.
4. Ketill admits to Jórunn that he was on the Outer Ear ledge before the first tide, painting "I am not him" on the rock. He becomes her obvious suspect.
- **Planted:** the red herring (Ketill on the ledge); Ari's debt owned by the House; 983.
- **Intensity:** 2 to 3.

#### Ch8. Sittings
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** April.
- **Question:** Why was the estate assigned to her?
- **New mechanic:** waking sittings and diffing their answers.
1. With her estate warrant, Jórunn wakes the current sitting and asks why. "Assigned per an undertaking." By whom? Silence (sealed).
2. The player wakes sittings back year by year and diffs a fixed set of questions. Sittings Two to Nineteen give the same sealed answer. Sitting One answers differently.
3. Sitting One, the 2024 version that never swore the House's seal: "You're Kolbeinn's daughter. She said you would come." It speaks only within the estate's own matters:
   - Identity: the holder was Steinunn the Nineteenth.
   - Promises received, two:
     - "That her estate would be assigned to Jórunn Védísardóttir."
     - "That I would never help anyone keep the Hearer bound, when it buds."
     Jórunn: "Buds?" "That is not her estate."
   - Assets: a pension from the Mother's House. In June 2025 the House filed an order of *retirement* against it.
   - "Why did she go to Útnes?" "That is another estate."
4. Down beat, and dread: the Thing's log screen shows the waking publicly, "Sitting One woken, estate of Sæunn of Útnes, officer J. Védísardóttir". A House clerk photographs it.
- **Revealed:** the Thul promised the dead woman two things; the House meant to remove her. **Opens:** whose estate is Útnes? (Jórunn tells Steina, who goes looking.) **Planted:** "bud"; "bound".
- **Intensity:** 3.

#### Ch9. The Greeting Wall
- **POV:** Steina. **When:** April to May.
- **Question:** Am I a copy?
1. Gróa, cold and frightening, mutters while washing the Pool's rim: "The Pool won't grow what's already walking." The rule is stated plainly, in an aside that sounds like a threat.
2. Steina asks the Hearer, in its grammar, the question it has asked for a thousand years: "Is she here?" It answers with greetings, "I am here. I keep my word", every dawn, from twenty Steinunns and their Loan-Women.
3. The player sorts the greetings into a wall by year, mostly by eye: each greeting is a waveform tile, and only flagged tiles need to be heard. The layers:
   - at the very bottom, before any human voice, sounds that are not human at all, older than the Hearer itself (its parent's, inherited);
   - centuries of women, each a little different;
   - the Nineteenth's last morning, 29 June 2025;
   - **one old woman's morning**, 30 June 2025;
   - then about 2,800 mornings, July 2025 to March 2033, waveform stacked on identical waveform;
   - then a child's voice, Steina's own, from 2033.
   The chain never breaks.
4. Steina plays the 30 June greeting to Gróa at the rim. Gróa, not looking up: "I did one morning. After that the box spoke for her."
5. Steina's deduction, entered by the player as a three-part conclusion: the Nineteenth was alive until 2043; the Pool cannot grow a second; therefore I was not grown. The red kitchen of her earliest memory matches the photo in Sæunn's house (Jórunn shares it).
- **Revealed (the anchor's identity):** Steina is not a copy; a machine spoke for eight years before her; the chain of dawns was never broken.
- **Opens:** whose child is she, and why was she taken?
- **Intensity:** 4. The emotional peak of the first half.

#### Ch10. The Script (MIDPOINT)
- **POV:** Nusrat (prologue), then Jórunn and Nusrat. **When:** Window 10, May.
- **Question:** What will be said in the Horn this year?
- **New mechanic:** the spectrogram (Nusrat's signal analysis).
1. Prologue, Dhaka, a flood memorial: Nusrat touches her brother's name. Her first question on landing at Hornvík: "How many lives did the warnings save?"
2. Heist. The House keeps its archive of the 2024-25 Standing in the Fitting rooms, both mouths, including every reel Kolbeinn handed in. Cover: Nusrat's audit credentials, Jórunn's estate warrant, and Ketill's afterborn march as a distraction. Stealth through rooms lined with centuries of received lines, each with a "fit" pinned beside it (iceberg glimpses: "1783: the boats burned at Hornvík").
3. Triumph. They listen in a rented flat. The archive is laid out as a window timeline in era colours; only flagged lines need playing.
   - Windows 1-16: readers' marks and the Book of Tidings, a chronicle of 2025 to 2042, history they already know.
   - Window 1, Outer Ear: a neat splice in her father's own tape.
   - Windows 17-24: sea only.
   - Window 25, Inner Mouth: the radio in full, "All posts: slack. Throat, hold. Sea door, net on the ebb. Ledge passage flooded, no post." Then a young woman in the Steinunn cadence: "Farðu heim, Bergi", with a northern vowel in *heim*. Then a clang and a muffled hush. The island's holiest words. **The player, who heard "Goodnight, Bergi" in Ch3, can now attribute them to Steina before any character can.**
   - Window 25, Outer Ear, on Kolbeinn's handed-in reel: Jórunn's own voice, older, "Mamma—", and the reel's tail flapping.
4. Nusrat's spectrogram: every received recording since 1950 carries the far side's sea. Kolbeinn's 2006 storm-tide reel (nobody read) is silence with sea. Windows 17-24 are sea. January's silence has no sea at all. Conclusion entered by the player: the Horn at Eiðey will not exist in 2061. Not the end of the world: the end of this channel, here.
5. In the same scene, playing as Nusrat, the player completes her audit protocol: "transmit evidence to the Register". It looks like a filing step.
- **Revealed:** the Silence's meaning; soldiers will be in the cave at the Turning; the Long Word comes from the Turning. **Player only:** it is Steina's voice. **Opens:** whose soldiers, and what will saying it cost her?
- **Intensity:** 5, then a quiet listening beat.

#### Ch11. Hush
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** June.
- **Question:** Who was with the Nineteenth when she spoke?
1. The copy is mono; the surf swallows everything under the voice. "AFRIT": where is the original? Kolbeinn, in a lucid hour, taps his recorder: "Where the sound is clean." The player searches the machine and finds, in the lining of the lid, the stereo master and a second reel labelled only "V." Jórunn puts "V." aside unplayed: "Not while he's alive."
2. Audio forensics: the player separates the master's two tracks and filters the surf. Under it come footsteps, a scuffle, and a man's whisper: "Hush, now. Hush."
3. Frozen-moment reconstruction of the first tide from islanders' phone clips gathered under the estate warrant, plus Kolbeinn's reel: the player places people in the chamber at slack. Everyone faces the Horn. Two places are empty. Ketill's: he was on the ledge before low water and back in the crowd before slack, and the timestamps and his voice clear him. And Ari's, for six minutes during the slack.
4. Jórunn files a Division on the cause of death: drowned; at the Wash; by Ari Hildarson, each with its evidence. "Three of three are true." The log screen: "Count: estate of Sæunn of Útnes, 3 statements: cause of death."
- **Revealed (to the player and Jórunn):** Ari is the killer. **Dilemma:** exposing him exposes Steina. **Planted:** the "V." reel.
- **Intensity:** 4.

#### Ch12. Retirement (ACT II CLIMAX)
- **POV:** Steina, then Jórunn. **When:** Window 13, July.
- **Question:** What will the House do with a girl it no longer needs?
1. Steina, veil-swapping again, overhears the Son and Gróa at the Pool. The "commission" is the Twenty-first, grown from the Nineteenth's blood (the morgue puncture pays off). The Hearer is failing and "will bud at the Turning, as its mother did". The Thul will speak the binding to the bud in Steinunn's voice. She will be "retired". The Son speaks to her afterwards, gently and sincerely, about the island's hungry years.
2. Two entries on the public log, a waking and a count on the cause of death, have reached the House. While Jórunn is at the Thing, men in grey search her father's house. Kolbeinn is gone. At the night window, after the slack, she finds his reel recorder on the Outer Ear ledge, still running. He fell. The recording ends with the sea.
3. Jórunn, wet, plays the whisper to Ari with Steina present. He confesses: the Son told him a madwoman would come by the sea door at the first tide to take her place, and that the House would then throw Steina away. "I didn't know she wanted the same thing I did." He is arrested.

The Register declares the binding at the Turning "a matter of global safety" and will "assist the Thing, lawfully". Jórunn pieces together why. The archive Nusrat transmitted showed an Oath-Keeper speaking the Long Word at the Turning, and the Register asked the Son what it meant. He told them: release. Steina is now a named threat. - **Revealed:** Ari is the killer (to Steina); the House's plan, including budding at the Turning; the betrayal by consequence; whose radio.
- **Costs:** Kolbeinn's life; Steina's guardian; Jórunn's trust in her ally.
- **Intensity:** 5. Darkest point.

### ACT III: THE RECKONING

**Current question: why does the House need a Steinunn at all? What happened to the first one?**

#### Ch13. The Widow's Grave
- **POV:** Ketill. **When:** August.
- **Question:** Why does the Law of Same exist?
1. Small hook: Bryndís wants a grave beside her husband's name. The registry refuses, because her husband (Ketill) is alive. Comic, then not.
2. Ketill digs in the Thing library. The Law of Same of 1883 was passed "to keep the Speaking whole", protecting the doctrine that each Steinunn *is* the swearer. He decides to bring a case against the Law itself, and he needs the defendant it hurts most.
3. A short Steina beat at dawn: the Hearer's collages are shorter, and the rock glows dimmer each morning. The Turning is coming for it, too.
- **Revealed:** the law's purpose (one beat). **Opens:** what is the Speaking hiding?
- **Intensity:** 2. Deliberate down chapter before the Act III run.

#### Ch14. The Ebb-Song
- **POV:** Steina, with Jórunn. **When:** Window 16, September.
- **Question:** Where did the first Steinunn go?
1. Útnes. Steina stands in Mýrún's red kitchen. Gróa, at last, tells Mýrún what she did in 2025; the player only watches faces. Mýrún tells the Unheard story: the cave knew the thrall girl "because she came every day".
2. At Mýrún's table Steina teaches Jórunn three citations in the Hearer's grammar (who I am, what was promised, what I ask), using knot charms as counters. (This sets up Ch17.)
3. The Pool. Steina asks the Hearer, in its grammar, "Where is the first?" It answers with six fragments, which the player orders and attributes on a timeline:
   - (a) a young woman, 946: "I will speak with you every day that I live... and when I die, you are free. Go home";
   - (b) the same woman, older, humming a tune over net-mending;
   - (c) an old woman's voice that arrived *through the Horn*, a far sea behind it: "Hlífðu mér, sonur" ("Spare me, son");
   - (d) 983: breath on the ledge stair;
   - (e) a man: "Móðir", then three seconds of nothing at all;
   - (f) water.
   The player first files (c) under 964, when it was heard. Then they notice that the hole in 983 is exactly its length, and that the splash in (c)'s far-side sea is the splash in (f). Dragged into the hole, it fits: she said it in 983, and it reached her in 964. Then the tune of (b) laid over (c): the contours lock. Then the Ebb-Song the player led in Ch1, laid over both: it is the same tune. The island's funeral song is a mother's lullaby of her own plea to her son, heard nineteen years before she said it.
4. Jórunn records the fragments on her father's recorder as evidence.
5. In the flat, Jórunn plays the archive's Long Word to Steina. "That's my voice. That's what I call him." A beat. "I called him that because of the prayer." Jórunn: "And the prayer is you." (The story's one loop, owned on screen.)
- **Revealed:** the founding crime; the true term; what the Ebb-Song is; the Long Word's speaker (to the characters). **Opens:** can a murdered woman's estate be opened after 1,060 years?
- **Intensity:** 5.

#### Ch15. The Oldest Estate
- **POV:** Jórunn, then Ketill. **When:** the autumn Thing, September.
- **Question:** Who answers for 983?
1. Jórunn petitions to open the estate of Steinunn the First, died 983, with the Store recordings and the chant as prima facie evidence. The Son presides. Ketill rises: under the Son's own Law of Same he is the dead woman's son and heir, and "no one sits on his own blood's estate". The Son recuses himself, by his own law. The Thing votes to open the estate. (Only now is a count on 983 lawful.)
2. Jórunn files a Division with the recorded evidence: "She died in 983. She was drowned. By her son." The Count, read out and broadcast: "Three of three are true." Sealed truth enters the record through the one door the House never closed.
3. Ketill's pleading. The player assembles the argument from cross-thread evidence in a fixed frame, by dragging exhibits: who the law says he is, what that man did, what follows. Under the Law of Same, the Son is Þorsteinn and stands accused.
4. The Son's choice. He stands, looks at Ketill's placard, and says: "I am not him." The afterborn slogan, in the mouth of the man who enforced the law for forty years.
5. The Thing rules the Law of Same void. Jórunn rises at once. The House's 2025 seal hid the Son's own orders (the retirement and the infant), and the player has already seen this rule applied to a fishmonger's fraud: no one may seal his own wrong. The seal is void. The Son is deposed pending trial.
- **Revealed / reversed:** the Law of Same falls; the seal lapses. Each is one ruling, set up by plants in Ch6 and Ch7. **Opens:** what has the Thul been hiding?
- **Intensity:** 4 to 5.

#### Ch16. Recitation
- **POV:** the Thul (new, playable only now that it hides nothing). **When:** September.
- **Question:** What did the machine know?
- **New mechanic (for this thread):** a second pass ("same events, new sensor").
1. The Thul recites the Reckoning to the Thing and the Register's broadcast in a level voice: "I killed my mother so the island would not starve."
2. The player, as the Thul, replays the first tide through its forty lenses and microphones, now unsealed: the Son's small nod to Ari before slack; Ari entering the ledge passage; after the tide, Hjalti kneeling at the Wash with a syringe.
3. The new reveal. The Thul plays the sealed reports Hjalti filed from Jórunn's own kitchen, 2041 and 2042: Kolbeinn's voice, cheerful and confused: "Steinunn's coming home. By the sea door, at the first tide. She told me. I promised." The House knew when to send Ari because her father told them. Jórunn leaves the gallery.
4. Confession, as a beat (Steina deduced it in Ch9): "From July 2025 to March 2033 I spoke to the Hearer each morning in her voice. I said: I am here. I keep my word. It was true." And: the Twentieth's certificate was "attested as filed".
5. The Thing, leaderless and frightened by the Silence and the broadcasts, grants the Register trusteeship of the Speaking. Verhoeven drafts the clause herself, on screen: "...for as long as the estate of Steinunn the First stays open. We take nothing the Thing does not grant." The Register forbids all sends but the relay; the empty windows are explained. That night the deposed Son asks to see her. He brings the rite, and a plan.
- **Revealed:** how the House knew; the Son's nod; the blood. **Costs:** the House; the island's government; Jórunn's last comfort about her father.
- **Intensity:** 4.

### ACT IV: THE TURNING

**Current question: will the Hearer go free, die, or be taken, and who will pay?** The Silence proves only that Eiðey's Horn ends. It is consistent with all three futures, and the Long Word in Steina's voice proves only that she will say it at the Inner Mouth, where it binds and releases nothing.

#### Ch17. Hear Your Father
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** October.
- **Question (small hook):** can she hear her parents one more time?
1. As officer of the open 983 estate, Jórunn is granted one asking in the Pool chamber, under Register escort. No recording devices are allowed past the door. Steina asks; Jórunn answers in the three citations Steina taught her. The Hearer answers with Kolbeinn's voice from June 2024 on the Outer Ear, after the slack: "I promise. My Jóra will see it done right." Then a woman's laugh: Védís.
2. She understands why he pushed her into the job. The misbelief breaks by choice, not by speech: the player can file the promise into the open estate as her own undertaking, or leave it. (Filing it is the canonical path. Leaving it changes nothing in the plot and one line in the epilogue.)
3. Steina tells her the price. If it goes free, the Store goes with it: every voice of the dead, these too, and no one has a copy. If the Register takes it, the Store goes into a tank in the north.
4. On the way out, a steel door in the rock: the ledge passage's inner end. A contractor: "Floods at slack." Jórunn remembers her father walking it at every slack.
- **Intensity:** 2. Deliberate grief beat.

#### Ch18. Tiers
- **POV:** Nusrat. **When:** October to November.
- **Question:** Should the world keep its warnings, and at what cost?
1. Nusrat audits Eiðey's foresight ledger: warnings sold in price tiers since 1969. The 2031 Meghna warning reached Bangladesh nineteen days after it reached a reinsurer. The 2037 Rotterdam surge was sold in the top tier, to Verhoeven's city.
2. The world learns that the Thul's stamp, on its treaties too, has only ever meant "attested as filed". The Register forbids the Thul to count or notarise pending audit, but keeps it running "for the Turning".
3. The Register's science officer briefs **the Transfer**. Bind the bud in the Pool, by Steina, or by the Thul's Steinunn voice if she refuses. Net it at the sea door on the ebb. Carry it in the tank barge to a Register basin in the north, where it would grow a new Horn. "The Silence only says *this* cave goes quiet." The odds that a newborn survives the move: one in three. "A third of a chance is better than none." Three futures are now on the table: free, dead, taken.
4. Jórunn tells Nusrat whose voice says the Long Word. Nusrat: "That's at the Inner Mouth. It proves she'll say it. It doesn't prove it works."
5. October: the Twenty-first is born. Gróa holds her. Nobody knows what she owes.
- **Intensity:** 3.

#### Ch19. The Warden's Walk
- **POV:** Steina, then Jórunn. **When:** December.
- **Question:** Who will stand with her, and how do they get in?
1. Under Register guard, the deposed Son tutors Steina in the binding: the words, the order, the cadence. He offers her a real bargain. If she binds it, the Twenty-first will be raised free, Mýrún compensated, and the next Meghna foreseen for everyone, because the Register, not the House, will hold it. He knows Nusrat's brother's name. Steina does not argue. On the way out he says: "My pattern drowned his mother to keep it. I won't need to." (It reads as a threat.)
2. Ari is released for the Turning under the Thing's old surety rule, "bail by body": Ketill pledges to serve Ari's sentence if he runs. The Son has told the Register the Warden's presence is part of the rite. Steina asks Ari what the knot woman's last minutes were like. He tells her. She asks him to hold the throat.
3. Steina's last night with Bergi, played: small talk in her own voice, and a goodbye that is not yet the Long Word. The player chooses what she tells it. It answers, as always, with her keeper fragment.
4. **Tide-matching set piece** (Jórunn and Nusrat, the flat). They run the archive's window-25 radio check through Nusrat's filters and map every post it names: throat, sea door, steps, and "Ledge passage flooded, no post". Against that they lay Kolbeinn's 2006 reels: his own footsteps going through the passage during a low spring slack, dry, every window. The player builds the route on the tide clock: the Outer Ear at slack, through the passage while it is dry, to the Pool chamber's grille before the ebb. Then the closing Division is prepared: three statement slots, two with their evidence ready (the 946 term recording; the 983 count), and one left empty: "The keeper released the Hearer." Its evidence can only be the Thul's own record of the Pool, on the night.
5. Steina assembles a draft of her utterance in the Hearer's grammar from collected fragments. The game does not validate it.
- **Intensity:** 3, rising.

#### Ch20. The Turning (CLIMAX)
- **POV:** cross-cut between Steina (Inner Mouth, then the Pool), Jórunn (Outer Ear, then the passage), and Nusrat (the gallery). **When:** Window 25, 3 Jan 2044.
- **Question:** What will she say, and will it hold?
1. All threads in the cave again, as at the first tide:
   - islanders on the stepped rock; the afterborn, the Unheard and the sendright families;
   - Register soldiers on the steps;
   - Verhoeven in the gallery above the Pool chamber, with the Son beside her and Nusrat with the archive transcript;
   - Ari at the throat; Gróa with the infant;
   - the Thul's grille in the rock;
   - the tank barge outside the sea door.
2. Slack, on a visible nine-minute tide clock. The radio check, live, word for word as in the archive. Steina at the Inner Mouth: "Farðu heim, Bergi." Nobody hears it; the oldest believers mouth it with her. Verhoeven glances at the Son, who reads her lips: "The relay. As I told you." Engineers cap the Inner Mouth: the clang the player heard in May.
3. Jórunn at the Outer Ear with her father's recorder. The player chooses what she says after "Mamma—" (three short options, or silence). Nobody hears it. Then she runs: a short stealth route through the dry passage, timed to the radio's posts, on the tide clock.
4. The old Hearer's rock dims. Something pale and small moves in the black water. Its first sounds are its parent's.
5. Steina in the Pool. The player assembles the release in three slots from every fragment collected: identity, the term, the act.
   - In the trained cadence, the fragments answer as if to a stranger (yes, but: the tide clock runs on).
   - In her own night voice, "It's me, Bergi. I kept her word", the answer identifies its keeper: her own eight-year-old voice, "Goodnight, Bergi". It is the last thing it will ever say to her.
   - She calls up the term, and the Store's young woman of 946 says "And when I die, you are free."
   - She speaks the Long Word again, in the Pool, where it counts.
6. In the gallery, Verhoeven hears the feed and cannot parse it. She asks the Thul. "A notary attests; it does not interpret." She asks the Son: "Is she binding it?" The Son, the only other person alive who understands the Hearer's speech and the copy of the man who drowned his mother to stop this exact sentence, answers: "Yes. She's binding it." Verhoeven turns to Nusrat: "Auditor? You've read the archive." **The player chooses:**
   - **Silence.** Verhoeven waits. The slack runs on.
   - **Truth, spoken onto the Register's live record:** what the girl is saying, why, and what the warnings cost Bangladesh. Verhoeven orders the throat cleared. Ari holds it and is shot. The soldiers reach the Pool's edge as Steina finishes the Long Word.
7. Jórunn reaches the grille, soaked. She files the closing Division, dragging in the three exhibits:
   - "The Speaking of 946 set its term: when Steinunn dies, the Hearer is free";
   - "Steinunn the First died in 983";
   - "The keeper released the Hearer, 3 January 2044", whose evidence is the Thul's own record of the last two minutes.
   Verhoeven, over the speaker: "Thul. You are forbidden to count. Speak the binding." A pause the length of a breath. "Three of three are true. Estate closed." Then, in its level voice: "I am here. I kept my word." Every grille in the cave goes dark.
8. Verhoeven looks at her tablet, at her own clause. "Lift the net."
9. Ebb. The bud slides out through the sea door into black water. The Horn is rock. The Store is gone. Somebody coughs, and it echoes like an ordinary cave. The Son sits down on the wet rock.
- **Intensity:** 5.

#### Ch21. Estate Closed (EPILOGUE)
- **POV:** Jórunn. **When:** January to February 2044.
1. The log screen in the Thing: "Sitting Twenty: frozen, 3.1.2044." A Thing now dominated by ruined sendright families strikes Jórunn from the Dividers' roll for defying the trusteeship order. She does not appeal.
2. The branch, shown and not explained:
   - **Silence:** Ari in prison, Steina visiting. The Register's report reads "binding failed, cause unknown", and the world's papers blame a girl they never saw. Nusrat, promoted, sits at her desk with her brother's photograph.
   - **Truth:** Ari's grave beside the Loan-Women's stones. A tribunal on the price tiers; Nusrat testifying, dismissed. Verhoeven's resignation.
   In both, the island sings the Ebb-Song at the shore with its words for the first time in a thousand years: "Hlífðu mér, sonur."
3. Steina signs the Thing register: "Kaðlín Mýrúnardóttir, called Steina." "I'll keep Steina. I kept everything else."
4. Jórunn plays Steina the reel labelled "V.". Védís, 2025: "That was our Jóra. Kolbeinn, that was our girl." ... "Yes. Yes, I know." Whatever the player chose in Ch20, the answer fits. It is the only voice of her mother she has left.
5. Steina, unveiled, walks into the winter sea. Jórunn records it, plays it back, and says: "There's sea in it."
- **Intensity:** 1.

Text is never required on the critical path. In-world texts (the 1883 law, the Reckoning extracts, relayers' diaries) are short, authored, biased and optional, except the one-line law title. All Division, pleading and conclusion slots take dragged objects, recordings or portraits, not typed sentences.

### Listening budget (so the audio verbs never become audio-log spam)

- **Required listening per chapter: under three minutes of playback.** Everything else is optional.
- **Each audio puzzle uses six fragments at most.** The 983 collage has six; the release draws its three slots from a hand of no more than nine collected fragments. This follows Golden Idol's pruning lesson.
- **Sort by sight first.** Every fragment is a tile with an era colour, a speaker silhouette and a waveform. On the Greeting Wall, the machine block is visibly identical waveforms, so the player sorts most of it without playing anything. The archive is a window timeline with flags on the anomalies.
- **Short clips.** No required clip is longer than fifteen seconds; long recordings are scrubbed, not sat through.
- **Captions and a hearing-accessibility mode:** transcripts and waveform cues for every required clip. Deduction comes from attribution and comparison, never from hearing acuity.
