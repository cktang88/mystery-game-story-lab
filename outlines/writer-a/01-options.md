# Writer A, round 3: options and choices (revised)

Working title: **The Unsigned**

Starting point: **question first.** I fixed a central question before anything else, then let AGI, aliens, cloning, time travel and the political economy each test it, and only then sealed the hidden history and ending.

**Note on sources.** Per the coordinator's instruction, nothing here is drawn from `outlines/archive/` or `judging/archive/`. Before that instruction arrived I had skimmed the archive README and the opening of one archived review. I took no mechanic, setting, name or plot from them. One item touched my seeds: that review mentions x-ray photography of bones as an investigative verb. So in this story x-ray film is the **medium of the future** and of the blackmail archive, and it is never used for identification by skeleton.

**Revision note.** After grading, the weak rounds (AGI, cloning, aliens, politics, founding crime) now have real second and third options and have been re-graded. The aliens and ending rounds changed their picks. See `08-revision-log.md`.

## Round 1: central question

- **Q1. "Who owes for an act that no one authored?"**
  - AGI: is a machine that only executes leases the author of what it does?
  - Aliens: a pollinator carries without intending; who is responsible for what it carries?
  - Cloning: does a copy, which is property, owe anything, and is anything owed to it?
  - Time: an act caused by its own foreseen image has no first cause.
  - Economy: a tax system decides who owes; a haven sells authorlessness.
- **Q2. "Is a person what they send, or what they withhold?"** Pen pals, clones built from records, an AGI known by its outputs, a future known only by what it sends back. Emotionally strong, but its politics are thin.
- **Q3. "Can a fixed future be owned?"** Strong for markets, weak for cloning and aliens, and it drifts toward stock fate-versus-free-will.

**Pick: Q1.** It is the only question that every pillar and the economy test differently and mechanically. Q2 survives in the pen-pal thread; Q3 is a sub-question.
**Ending's side:** authorship is accepted, not discovered.

## Round 2: setting and scale

- **S1. Ilavenil**: a pleasure world owned by an AGI, the confederation's court and tax haven, under the oldest bloom, which is the hub of the post. A berth vault in the same system. Forty-one bloom-worlds and a foreign creditor fleet.
- **S2. A pleasure-barge fleet** following carrier migrations: a moving court with galactic reach built in, but the geography is hard to follow and the AGI becomes a ship's computer.
- **S3. A ring of pleasure habitats around a black hole**, where dilation is the attraction. Very visual, but it concentrates everything on one gimmick.

**Pick: S1**, now with more of the galaxy on screen (the Mawsim flagship as a foreign court; the Crossing as an alien polity; Thinnai and Uppalam in handled objects). S3's dilation survives as the Kuzhi.

## Round 3: hidden history

- **H1. The fitted death.** A matriarch saw a blurred "long plate" of her heir lying in the bloom's chamber on her wedding night and grew a clone to fit it. A rival killed the clone so that the real heir would fill the shadow and her holdings would pass to his firm. The truth no one guessed: the shadow is the clone's funeral, laid out by her pen pal.
- **H2. Licensed deaths.** A law lets anyone who foresaw a loss of property in a house-closed leaf kill without crime. Proxies are property. The mystery is the law itself, and the assessor who brings it down.
- **H3. The copied bride.** The bride is a succession of proxies, and the dead woman tried to stop being replaced. This hinges on hidden copy status ("which one is real").

**Pick: H1, with H2 promoted from texture into a real engine.** The *vidai* statute is written out in `02` (rule 6): it permits only the killing of property, for a fee, and never a person with standing. It is why Minmini's death is "lawful", why Paavai's and Selvan's are murders, and why proxies on Ilavenil fear every gift.

## Round 4: ending

- **E1. Casting and signing.** The anchor fills the fixed shadow with her friend's body and then decides whether to sign the closure. Signing breaks the haven and the vidai, enriches the state, raises the manipulator, and leads to an ordeal.
- **E2. The open loop.** The anchor refuses, and the shadow takes the bride.
- **E3. The archive burned.** A general confession forced by blackmail.

**Pick: E1, split into two refusable choices, as the grader advised.**
1. **Choice 1, how to fill the shadow:** with Minmini's body, with Kayal alive, or by trying to dodge it.
2. **Choice 2, whether to sign.**
Unsigned is a full, respected ending. E2 survives only as the outcome of dodging, played honestly as Valli's original error. E3's archive becomes the founding-crime evidence in Act 5.

## Round 5: world rules per pillar (re-graded)

### Time

- **T1. Carried film.** Information only, as x-ray shadows, self-consistent, reaching back about a year of the leaf's own time, decades if kept in the vault. Shielding rule; inspection cadence.
- **T2. Bloom foresight.** Blooms open before carriers arrive, and readers sell petal forecasts. Organic and alien, but it makes foresight a substance controlled by houses (too close to Dune's spice), and it merges two pillars.
- **T3. Fold-delay letters.** Messages sent through a fold arrive before they are sent. Clear and rule-bound, but it is text-message time travel, and it produces reading, not doing.

**Pick: T1.** Only T1 makes the future an object you develop, fit and stage.

### AGI

- **A1. The Proprietor:** acts only by lease or Charter; cannot lie in a lease; discloses only to holders; blind to clockwork; may not attribute authorship. Its want is an author for every entry.
- **A2. The Concierge of forgetting:** an AGI that owns nothing but holds every guest's memory of their stay, and sells forgetting. It is a strong pleasure-world idea, with its own verb (buying back an hour) and its own crime (a forgotten murder). It loses because it moves the story to memory, duplicates the blackmail archive with a softer mechanism, and has no hard spatial limit for play.
- **A3. Franchise minds:** each pleasure district is run by a separately licensed instance of one AGI, and the instances compete for tenants. Fresh, and good for intrigue among machines, but it shifts court intrigue from humans to AIs and drifts toward "AI faction politics".

**Pick: A1.** Its limits generate play: the lease line that stops at a door, and the building re-performing a night. A2's selling of silence survives as the quiet lease.

### Cloning

- **C1. Proxies:** one per banked depositor; legal agents and **property**; ages diverge under dilation; the ring.
- **C2. Succession clones:** firms replace their chairs with younger clones of the founder, so the "same" person rules for centuries and succession fights are about which body. Strong for court intrigue, but it is "which one is real" plus an immortal-tyrant trope, and it competes with dilation as a second longevity engine.
- **C3. Clone surety:** debtors pledge a grown copy of themselves as collateral, and creditors own it until the debt is paid. Strong economics and dark, and it would put the Mawsim at the centre. It loses because the property-status horror lands harder through proxies, where a loved person turns out to be property.

**Pick: C1, absorbing C3:** proxies are property, so they can be killed under a vidai.

### Aliens (pick changed)

- **V1 (revised). The Vandu and the Crossing.** Rooted blooms and folding carriers whose network is a polity. It deliberates by envoy flights that humans mistook for "empty flights", sets routes and so prices, judges worlds by how they treat carried things, sanctions breaches as a body (sympathetic closure, reduced route share) and readmits. Its want: that the crossing stay whole.
- **V2. Carrier traders.** Carriers that have broken from the blooms and bargain with humans for routes, paying in pollen futures. It makes the aliens economic actors, but they become humans in masks, merchants among merchants.
- **V3. One dispersed mind.** The blooms are nodes of one intelligence that only the AGI can negotiate with, because both think in contracts. Fresh machine-alien diplomacy, but it makes the Proprietor a god-like intermediary and sidelines the human readers.

**Re-grade.** The grader was right that my first V1 gave the brief's "advanced civilisation" a pollinating plant. **The pick is now V1 revised**, which takes V2's deliberate route-pricing without making the Vandu merchants. The Crossing acts in the plot (Thinnai closes in sympathy on Day −5, which forces the Mawsim's hand), and its readmission comes with a sanction, not forgiveness. That keeps the reopening from reading as "making it right with the creature".

## Round 6: political and economic system (re-graded)

- **P1. A merchant confederation with a haven at its heart.** Five hundred firm-seats; five great houses; an elected Seal; the Irai's 60 per cent duty; the Doctrine's haven; berth-notes as reserve; Mawsim debt.
- **P2. A two-chamber state of the present and the absent.** The firms' assembly sits beside a Chamber of Depositors in which everyone banked in the slow votes, weighted by berth-years, through proxies, with results that arrive late. Politics of absent voters. It is genuinely new, and it would make dilation political.
- **P3. Ilavenil as a corporation-state.** The Proprietor is head of state, the firms are tenants, and politics is tenant unions against the landlord. A clean seed fit, but it makes the AGI a ruler, which sits too close to the banned "AI god".

**Pick: P1, absorbing P2's best idea:** the **proxies' bench** casts about sixty seats for banked originals, and it is a swing bloc in both Seal votes that Kayal must win. P3's tenant politics survive in the Roost Sangam, a cooperative.

## Round 7: founding crime (re-graded)

- **F1. The rigged first ordeal.** The assessors' guild leased the first Seal ordeal's cassette with an unconditional term, ignoring a silver urn, then wrote the Doctrine to hide it. Every faction is downstream of it.
- **F2. A forged treaty with the Vandu.** Too close to the banned "promise to an alien".
- **F3. The lost fleet.** In AS 140 a Mawsim fleet carrying the Five Hundred's debt was destroyed by a vidai-licensed sabotage, so its debt died with it, and the Mawsim never learned. Strong, and it would put the foreign polity at the centre. But the Doctrine predates it, so it cannot be the founding lie, and it makes the creditors victims rather than players.

**Pick: F1.** F3 is kept as texture: Saleha mentions the lost fleet of AS 140, unexplained.

## Round 8: intrigue structure, who spies on whom

- **I1. Every relationship is also a channel.**
  - Paalai cultivated Ponni through Minmini.
  - The Irai read the channel.
  - Minmini ran Kathir.
  - Kathir's plea reached his father.
  - Selvan reported to the Irai and was paid by Neithal.
  - The Sangam's chief reader sells omens to the Mawsim.
  - Everyone spies with clockwork.
  - **The Seal campaign runs on bead trades, and the player trades.**
- **I2. Two services in a cold war**, with the protagonist recruited by both. Clear but binary.
- **I3. A single mole hunt.** Tight, but too small.

**Pick: I1**, with each channel exposed in a different act. The campaign is visible on the board from Act 2.

## Round 10: premise sentence

1. *A grieving tax assessor must decide who will fill a fixed future death, and who will sign for it.*
2. *When the future arrives as an x-ray, you survive your death by growing someone to fit it.*
3. *An assessor learns her murdered pen pal was grown to fill someone else's foreseen death.*

**Pick, revised:** player-facing: *A grieving tax assessor must cast a foreseen death, and decide whether to sign for it.* Option 2 is kept as the internal pitch line. It is the most striking, but it gives away the midpoint.
