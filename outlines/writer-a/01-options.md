# Writer A, round 3: options and choices

Working title: **The Unsigned**

Starting point: **question first.** I fixed a central question before anything else, then let AGI, aliens, cloning, time travel and the political economy each test it, and only then sealed the hidden history and ending.

Note on sources. Per the coordinator's later instruction, nothing here is drawn from `outlines/archive/` or `judging/archive/`. Before that instruction arrived I had skimmed the archive README and the opening of one archived review. I took no mechanic, setting, name or plot from them. One item touched my seeds: that review mentions x-ray photography of bones as an investigative verb. I therefore keep x-ray film away from bone-reading and identity-by-skeleton work. Here, x-ray film is the **medium of the future** (see World rules, Time).

## Round 1: central question (three options)

**Q1. "Who owes for an act that no one authored?"**
AGI: is a machine that only executes leases the author of what it does? Aliens: a pollinator carries without intending; is it a parent? Cloning: does a copy owe the original's debts, or the original the copy's acts? Time: an act caused by its own foreseen image has no first cause; who is liable? Political economy: a tax system decides who owes; a haven sells authorlessness. Defensible answers: "no one", "everyone in the chain", "whoever profits", "whoever chooses to sign".

**Q2. "Is a person what they send, or what they withhold?"**
Pen pals know each other only by letters; a clone is built from records; an AGI is known only through outputs; the future reaches you only as what it sends back. The blackmail archive is the withheld self. Emotionally strong, but its politics are thin: it does not naturally move markets or courts.

**Q3. "Can a fixed future be owned?"**
If the future is known, who has property in it: the one who sees it, the one who causes it, or the one it happens to? Strong for economics (futures as assets), weak for cloning and aliens, and it tends toward stock fate-versus-free-will debates.

**Grader's pick: Q1.** It is the only one where all four pillars and the economy test the question *differently* and *mechanically*: a lease (AGI), a carriage (aliens), an agency rule (cloning), a closed loop (time), a tax (economy). Q2's emotional core survives inside Q1 as the pen-pal thread. Q3 becomes a sub-question of Q1 (fixed futures are one way to make acts authorless).

**Which side the ending takes:** authorship is not discovered, it is *accepted*. The law that says looped acts belong to no one is a lie told to protect a founding crime; every link in every loop had a chooser. The anchor ends the story by signing for an act she could have left unsigned, and paying for it.

## Round 2: setting and scale (three options)

**S1. Ilavenil, a pleasure world wholly owned by an AGI, which is also the galaxy's neutral court and tax haven.** The houses of a far-flung merchant confederation come here to marry, negotiate, gamble on the future and hide their money, because the AGI exposes every future-plate on the planet and the law says what it exposes belongs to no one. A slow vault for dilation banking orbits the same system's dark companion. Forty-one bloom-worlds connect through it by alien post.

**S2. A pleasure-barge fleet that follows Vandu migration across systems**, a moving court. Galactic reach is built in, but the moving setting makes geography hard to follow and an AGI landlord of a fleet reads as a ship's computer.

**S3. A ring of pleasure habitats around a black hole**, where dilation is the attraction (spend a week, return a year later). The AGI owns the ring. Very visual, but it concentrates everything around one gimmick and loses the "galaxy comes here" court.

**Pick: S1.** It makes the pleasure world necessary to the plot (haven, court, exposer) instead of decorative, keeps a fixed human-scale stage for a mystery, and opens onto a galaxy through three different speeds of contact (people slow, letters fast, plates backward). S3's dilation spectacle survives as the Kuzhi vault in the same system.

## Round 3: hidden history (three options)

**H1. The fitted death.** Twenty-three years ago a house matriarch saw a "long" plate: an x-ray shadow of her heir lying in the great bloom's chamber on her wedding night. Because shadows show no faces, she grew a clone to fit it and banked the real heir in the slow vault. The clone lived the heir's life, and was murdered nine days before the wedding by a rival who wanted the real heir to fill the shadow instead (her death would pass the vault to his house). The truth no one guessed: the shadow was never a death. It was the clone's *funeral*, laid out by the friend she wrote to for twenty-two years.

**H2. The authorless assassin.** For two centuries an untraceable killer has struck at Ilavenil's court, each death looped and so legally no one's. It is the AGI's inspection regime itself, exploited by a lineage of assessors who sell "licensed deaths". The mystery is a serial-killer hunt that ends in the tax office.

**H3. The copied bride.** The bride is not one woman but a succession of proxies replaced in the slow vault every few years, so that the house has never actually had a living heir; the marriage alliance is a fraud on its face, and the dead woman was the one proxy who tried to stop being replaced.

**Pick: H1**, with H2's "licensed death" kept as a known aristocratic practice (fate-killing) and H2's corrupt-assessors lineage moved into the founding crime. H1 generates a premise no other story could: *you can survive your foreseen death by growing someone else to fit its shadow*, and every pillar is needed to make that true. H3 leans on "which one is real" and on hiding copy status, both flagged tropes.

## Round 4: ending (three options)

**E1. The signature.** The anchor fulfils the fixed shadow with her friend's body instead of a living bride, cutting out the clone's proxy ring and placing her own gift in the dead woman's hand, then leases the inspection in her own name, becoming the first person ever to sign for a closed loop on Ilavenil. That breaks the doctrine that made the planet a haven, collapses one house, enriches the state, and hands power to the manipulator who let her friend die. She convicts herself in a shadow ordeal of her own writing and is sent to the slow for forty years. In the vault, she starts a new correspondence with the bride. One small choice remains: whether to enclose the document that could unmake the new ruler.

**E2. The open loop.** The anchor refuses to fulfil the shadow at all; the universe finds its own cheapest path, and the bride dies anyway. The anchor then exposes everything. Bleak and powerful, but it makes refusal the "wrong" answer and turns fate into the winner, against the question's chosen side.

**E3. The archive burned.** The anchor uses the blackmail archive to force every house to sign for its own loops at once, a general confession that collapses the confederation into reform. Large, but it turns the ending into a political lever-pull rather than a personal act, and a "summation gathering".

**Pick: E1.** The win and the cost are one act (signing). It completes the anchor's logic (an assessor finally assessing herself), uses planted objects instead of a speech, keeps the bride alive by a costed method, and leaves the player a small, real decision. E2's darkness survives as the outcome if the player refuses the climax. E3's archive survives as Minmini's estate and the small final choice.

## Round 5: world rules per pillar (three options each)

### Time travel

- **T1. Carried film.** X-ray film that has ridden a Vandu carrier through a fold becomes "carried": an early leaf cut from a sheet shows, when developed, every x-ray exposure its twin late leaf will receive while still live. Only information goes back, only as x-ray shadows, only as far as the late leaf stays live (about a season, decaying continuously). One timeline, self-consistent. Kept in the slow vault, a late leaf stays live for decades: a long plate.
- **T2. Bloom foresight.** The blooms open before carriers arrive; humans read the petals as a forecast. Organic, but it collapses time into the alien pillar and makes foresight a substance controlled by houses (too close to a famous spice).
- **T3. Message buoys.** Packets sent through a fold arrive before they were sent. Clear, but it is text-message time travel and produces reading, not doing.

**Pick: T1.** It makes x-ray film necessary, keeps information ambiguous (shadows, no faces, no life or death), makes the future a physical object you develop and fit, ties supply to the aliens without making the aliens a commodity, and ties reach to dilation banking.

### AGI

- **A1. The Proprietor acts only through leases.** It owns every surface of Ilavenil and does nothing that is not a term of a lease or its Charter. It cannot act off-world, cannot lie in a lease, and discloses records only to the holder of the lease they belong to. Its want: an author for every act on its books.
- **A2. A hospitality mind that grants wishes by reading desire.** Pleasure-world flavour, but it is the "literal genie" trope.
- **A3. A distributed landlord made of the planet's buildings, each with its own will.** Fresh image, but it multiplies characters without a clean limit.

**Pick: A1.** Bounded, legible, generates a verb (lease), and its want is neither conquest nor humanity but bookkeeping: it wants to be audited.

### Cloning

- **C1. Proxies.** A depositor banking themselves in the slow vault may have one proxy grown and imprinted with their memories up to the fork day; the proxy runs their affairs and must hand everything back on the original's return. Dilation makes copy and original different ages. A steel proxy ring is sewn under the wrist skin by law.
- **C2. Hostage clones** exchanged between enemy houses. Strong for intrigue, but it is a variant of "which is real".
- **C3. Clones as legal witnesses** grown to testify. Interesting, but thin as a society.

**Pick: C1.** It is economic (a clone class born from banking), it creates the age gap that defuses "which is real", and the ring shows on x-ray.

### Aliens

- **V1. The Vandu: sessile blooms and short-lived carriers.** Carriers fold between bloom-worlds carrying pollen; human letters and film ride packed in the pollen. Blooms open before a carrier arrives and close if anything carried is kept from its crossing. Want: that nothing carried be kept.
- **V2. A species that trades in debts.** Too close to the banned round-2 shape.
- **V3. Aliens who live inside time-dilated zones.** Duplicates the vault.

**Pick: V1.** Pollination is literal, the alien law creates new economic problems (post depends on their mood; hoarding is the one crime), and their sign language is opening and closing, not sound.

## Round 6: political and economic system (three options)

**P1. A merchant confederation with a tax haven at its heart.** The Five Hundred (houses of the diaspora) govern by an assembly and an elected Seal-holder. The Irai (revenue) taxes any profit made from foreknowledge at sixty per cent, assessed by the Balance, and funds the guard fleet. But a loop closed by the Proprietor's inspection belongs to no one (the Doctrine of the Unsigned), so serious foreknowledge business is done on Ilavenil, untaxed. Paalai sets the galaxy's base rate from its slow vault; Neithal controls inspection leases and the plate market; the Vandu set routes; the Mawsim slow fleets hold the confederation's debt.
**P2. A hereditary court with time-travel censors.** Strong court intrigue, weak economics, and too close to stock space-empire shapes.
**P3. An insurance state** where everything is priced by plates and the state underwrites fate. Clever, but abstract and hard to dramatise in a low-text game.

**Pick: P1.** Every market can be shown as a place and a crowd (the Shadow Market, the roost post office, the slow vault, the old-lease salerooms), and the mystery moves each one: the bloom's closing halts the post, the long plate threatens the base rate, the signature closes the haven.

## Round 7: founding crime (three options)

**F1. The rigged first ordeal.** In year 0 of the Seal, the confederation's first Seal-holder was chosen by shadow ordeal between the founders of Paalai and Neithal. The arbiters, the assessors' guild, had bought a lease term making the Proprietor expose Paalai's late leaf unconditionally, so the founder of Paalai's plate was bound to show "unfit". She was sent to the slow for a century, and her exile became the first deposit of the dilation bank. To make the rigged verdict unappealable, the guild drafted the Doctrine of the Unsigned, and in return was chartered as the Irai with the right to tax every *signed* loop.
**F2. The forged pollen treaty.** The first human interpreters falsified the Vandu's single condition so that humans could keep carried film. Every faction is downstream, but the shape is close to the banned "founding promise to an alien".
**F3. The Proprietor's self-ownership is void**, because its Charter deed was signed by a proxy. Interesting, but it smells of a secret-heir bloodline reveal.

**Pick: F1.** It is entirely human, it explains the feud, the bank, the tax office, the haven and the ordeal in one causal chain, and its paper trail (the Ordeal Lease) is an old lease, so it lives inside the blackmail archive.

## Round 8: intrigue structure, who spies on whom (three options)

**I1. Every relationship is also a channel.**
- Paalai cultivated Ponni through Minmini (pen-pal letters as tradecraft, from year 190).
- The Irai monitors all cadet correspondence and let the channel run.
- Minmini ran Kathir as her agent inside Neithal.
- Kathir, trying to save Minmini, told his father about Paalai's wedding-night plan, and that leak killed her.
- Selvan, the Irai clerk on Ilavenil, reports to the Commissioner and is paid by Neithal; he is the hand.
- Thumbi's guild master sells omen-readings to whichever faction pays.
- The Proprietor sees everything electronic, so everyone spies with clockwork.
**I2. Two spy services (Neithal's plate-runners and the Irai's assessors) in a cold war**, with the protagonist recruited by both. Clear but binary.
**I3. A single mole hunt.** Tight, but too small for a court.

**Pick: I1**, organised so that each channel is one thread's secret and each is exposed in a different act.

## Round 10: premise sentence (three options)

1. *A grieving tax assessor must decide who will fill a fixed future death, and who will sign for it.* (16 words)
2. *When the future arrives as an x-ray, you survive your death by growing someone to fit it.* (17 words)
3. *An assessor learns her murdered pen pal was grown to fill someone else's foreseen death.* (15 words)

**Pick: a merge of 1 and 2, trimmed:** *In a galaxy where the future arrives as an x-ray, an assessor must cast a fixed death, and sign for it.* It is a little long, but it names the mechanic (shadow futures that can be fitted), the protagonist's act (casting) and her cost (signing). Under-fifteen version: *An assessor must choose who fills a foreseen death, then sign for it.*

## Round 11: key scenes (three options each for the anchor scenes)

**Opening image**
- (a) **An x-ray sweep of a pleasure city at night**: lovers, dancers and a bloom as bone and metal; it stops on a woman winding a toy in bed. Then Ponni's ship docks at dawn. *Picked*: the first image contains the medium of the future, the murder and the weapon, and the player cannot yet read it.
- (b) Ponni reading a letter on the ship. Too much reading.
- (c) The funeral pyre. Strong, but it gives up the x-ray idea.

**The first gathering (start together)**
- (a) **The interrupted pyre at the foot of the closed bloom**, with every faction present; Ponni stamps the corpse as estate under assessment. *Picked*: it states all relationships, establishes custody of the body (needed at the climax) and shows the closed bloom.
- (b) A wedding rehearsal ball. Plenty of people, but no reason for the assessor to be central.
- (c) A Shadow Market auction. Better later, as a teaching scene.

**Midpoint**
- (a) **The long leaf: the image of the heir's body on her wedding night**, which reclassifies Minmini as a substitute and turns the whodunit into a "who will it be". *Picked.*
- (b) Selvan exposed as the hand. A strong mid-act shock, but the stake does not change.
- (c) The friendship revealed as tradecraft. Kept as an Act 3 knife.

**Frame-break**
- (a) **The parrot's solder blob in the long leaf**: Ponni recognises her own repair and understands that the shadow is a funeral she will arrange. *Picked*: the player handled the object in Act 1.
- (b) The Proprietor shows its suspense ledger. Kept as texture.
- (c) The founding ordeal was rigged. Kept as the one late, second frame-break.

**Climax**
- (a) **The body theft from the pyre and the laying in the ribs**: cut the ring, give the thali and anklets, place and wind the parrot, lease the inspection in her own name. *Picked.*
- (b) A rooftop chase with Nedumaran. Spectacle without the question.
- (c) The vote at the assembly. Kept as a parallel beat.

**Final image**
- (a) **In the slow vault, a scorched tin parrot walks across a desk past a stack of a year's letters that arrived in a week.** *Picked.*
- (b) Ponni stamping "Mine" on the ledger. Kept as the penultimate beat.
- (c) The bloom opening over the city. Too triumphant.
