# Writer A, file 06: key scenes

Each sketch says what the player **does**, **sees** and **hears**, with the text on screen kept to labels, names and short spoken lines. "Planted in" names where the detail that makes the scene land was set up in a scene about something else.

---

## 1. The First Tide (opening, Ch1)

**Sees.** A black sea cave lit by hundreds of hand lamps. The whole island on stepped rock around a spiral of glistening stone, the Inner Mouth. A veiled girl on a ledge above the crowd. Placards: "I am not him." A tide clock projected on wet rock, counting down to slack.

**Does.** As Jórunn, the player holds her father's microphone while he fiddles with reels; the needle jumps with the crowd. A short switch to Steina: the player leads the Old Word by moving a stick along the chant's rising and falling line; the crowd follows the player's pace. On the last word, *Bergi*, Steina comes in a beat early.

**Hears.** The Thul over the loudspeakers: "Slack water. Words given to the Horn are given away." Then nothing. The crowd holds its breath. The player can scrub the recording back and forth: voices, coughs, the Thul, and then flat silence from the Horn, no hiss of surf at all. Kolbeinn, frowning at his meters: "No sea, Jóra. There's no sea in it."

**Turn.** A shout from the cave mouth. Torchlight on the mouth pool: an old woman face down among the floating net offerings. As the crowd surges, Ari shoulders back to Steina's side, his cloak dark with water.

**Planted in:** nothing; this is the plant. The scene contains, unexplained, the climax's line, the meaning of the Silence, and the killer's return.

---

## 2. The Net (Ch2)

**Sees.** A one-room net-mender's house at Útnes, wind at the window. A net hangs from the rafters in heavy loops, darker toward one end, as if handled for years.

**Does.** The player walks the room and lifts objects into a ring of slots (owed to, owed by, kept, broken). The net invites counting: the player runs a hand along it and a tally ticks, knot by knot, the camera following the cord through the room and out of the window and back: 6,408. A carved needle turns in the light: *I am here. I keep my word.* The player fits a gap in the net's edge where small squares were cut away. A photograph: a kitchen painted red, a baby's chair. A reel box in a familiar hand: *Ytra Eyra, 4.6.2024.*

**Reads.** Only the needle's carving and the box label.

**Lands later.** The knot count dates her flight to the day (Ch5); the cut squares are the charms (Ch6); the red kitchen is Steina's first memory (Ch9).

---

## 3. Goodnight, Bergi (Ch3)

**Sees.** The Pool chamber at night: a warm pool of black water under a low dome of ridged rock that breathes with the tide. Steina without her veil, knees up, a bowl of tea.

**Does.** The player speaks in Steina's own voice (an option set of short lines in her northern accent, not the trained cadence). The Bergmál answers with a collage: each fragment appears as a soft shape whose colour marks its century. Its first fragment, every time, is a young woman's "I'm here", the same fragment it uses at dawn for the Oath-Keeper. Steina laughs and says "Goodnight, Bergi."

**Then.** A nightmare cut: Steina in bed, Ari in the doorway, sitting, a big hand on the blanket: "Hush, now. Hush."

**Planted in:** this scene is the plant for three payoffs (the Long Word's speaker, the recognition rule, the killer's whisper). It plays as a tender interlude.

---

## 4. The Silent Mouth (Ch4, the harmless test)

**Sees.** Window 2. A fisherman in oilskins at the Inner Mouth with a card in his hand. Jórunn behind him with her father's recorder.

**Does.** The player has just heard this man's voice on a 2024 reel saying, of all things, "Tell Siggi the bilge pump is the left one." Now the player watches him lean into the Horn. His lips shape the sentence. The recorder's needle does not move. The cave's surf continues; his voice is simply absent. He straightens up, shrugs at her: everyone here knows.

**Effect.** The rule is taught by seeing it, not reading it. The cost is visible: nobody in the present can ever confirm what he said.

---

## 5. The Hall of Hands (Ch5)

**Sees.** A long timber hall, nineteen portraits of veiled women with the veil lifted for the painter only, each smiling the same notched smile; handprints in clay beneath.

**Does.** First, veil-swap stealth: two identical veiled girls cross in a corridor; the player must take the other girl's route and gait (a short rhythm of steps and pauses) to pass the guard. Then lineage reading: the player brings a mirror to each portrait in turn. The gap is in every one. In the mirror, Steina's teeth are whole.

**Window.** Through the glass, a young man with a camera photographs the portraits on the opposite wall: men, all the same face. Ketill. He sees her; she sees him; neither moves.

---

## 6. Sitting One (Ch8)

**Sees.** A small grey room in the Thing with a speaker grille and a screen listing sittings by year, 2024 to 2043. A warrant on the table.

**Does.** The player wakes sittings and asks the same three questions of each (why was this estate assigned to me; by whose undertaking; what is the undertaking). The answers line up in a table: eighteen identical sealed replies. The 2024 row is different.

**Hears.** Sitting One, a slightly older, warmer synthetic voice: "You're Kolbeinn's daughter. She said you would come."

**Turn.** On the way out, the public log screen in the Thing's hall shows her waking in white letters. A clerk in House grey raises a phone and photographs it. No music.

---

## 7. The Greeting Wall (Ch9, the anchor's reveal)

**Sees.** Steina asks the Bergmál its own old question, "Is she here?", and the chamber fills with greetings, "I am here. I keep my word", in hundreds of women's voices, pouring out of the rock.

**Does.** The player catches greetings and pins them to a wall by year, sorting by ear. Centuries of women, each a little different in breath and age. Then a long block, 2025 to 2033, where every greeting is identical: same breath, same length, waveform stacked on waveform like a printed pattern. Then a child's voice, wobbly, 2033: Steina's own.

**States.** The player fills three slots: *The Nineteenth was alive until* [2043]; *the Pool will not grow* [a second living copy]; *so I was* [not grown]. Nothing confirms it. Steina sits down on the wet rock.

**Planted in:** Gróa's mutter at the Pool's rim earlier in the chapter, which sounded like a threat.

---

## 8. The Script (Ch10, midpoint)

**Sees.** The Fitting rooms of the House: long tables of received lines from past Standings with a plan pinned beside each ("1783: the boats are burning" beside a sketch of old hulls set alight at Midsummer). Then a rented flat, rain, three people round a laptop and an old reel machine.

**Does.** A stealth sequence through the Fitting rooms timed to the chants of Ketill's march outside. Then listening: the player scrubs the year's script, window by window, voices of islanders the player has met saying things they have not yet said. On Kolbeinn's own Outer Ear reel for window 25, Jórunn's voice: "Mamma—" and the reel's tail flapping. On the House's Inner Mouth recording for the same window, a young woman in the trained cadence: *"Farðu heim, Bergi."*

**The player's moment.** No prompt. The player has heard that name once, in a girl's night voice. The game says nothing.

**Then.** Nusrat's spectrogram: the player lays the 2006 storm-tide reel (silence, and sea) beside January's silence (no sea) and marks the difference. Finally, as Nusrat, the player completes her audit checklist: the last box is "Transmit evidence to the Register". It looks like a formality. It is the betrayal.

---

## 9. Hush (Ch11)

**Does.** Two waveform lanes of Kolbeinn's 2024 tape. The player mutes the left, filters the surf on the right, and under the dead woman's last words a man's whisper rises out of the noise: "Hush, now. Hush."

**Then.** The frozen moment of the first tide, rebuilt from forty islanders' phone clips: the player places people on the rock. Every face turns to the Horn. Two gaps. The player drags Ketill to the ledge, before low water, and his timestamp turns green. Ari's gap stays open for six minutes during the slack.

**No text.** The player already heard the lullaby in a bedroom.

---

## 10. The Ledge (Ch12, the death of Kolbeinn)

**Sees.** Night window. Jórunn on the Outer Ear ledge with a torch. A reel recorder on the rock, reels still turning, its microphone pointed at the sea.

**Does.** The player picks it up and plays the reel: Kolbeinn's breathing, his voice: "Clean. Here it's clean," a slip, the sea. The player stops the tape. The player does not have to do anything else, and the game waits.

**Planted in:** Ch1 ("where the sound is clean"), Ch8 (the public log), the search of his house.

---

## 11. The Old Word (Ch14, the founding crime)

**Sees.** The Pool, dawn. Steina asks: "Where is the first?"

**Does.** Fragments arrive out of order: a young woman's promise; an old woman's breath on a stair; a man's voice saying "Mother"; water; a plea. The player orders and attributes them on a timeline (946, 983) and to speakers (the young Steinunn, the old Steinunn, the son). Then the plea alone: *"Hlífðu mér, sonur."* The game overlays the Old Word funeral chant the player sang in Ch1, and the two contours lock together syllable by syllable. The island's funeral song is a mother asking her son not to kill her.

**Then.** In the flat, Jórunn plays the archive line to Steina. Steina, very quiet: "That's my voice. That's what I call him."

---

## 12. I Am Not Him (Ch15)

**Sees.** The Thing in full session. The afterborn in the gallery with their placards. The Son at the high bench.

**Does.** As Ketill, the player builds the pleading in a fixed frame (who the law says he is; what that man did; what follows), dragging evidence from every thread: portraits, the 1883 law, the Count's "three of three", the Store fragment.

**Hears.** The Son stands. He looks for a long moment at the placard in the gallery, then at Ketill. "I am not him."

**After.** The gallery does not cheer. Bryndís, in the front row, puts her hand over her mouth.

---

## 13. Recitation (Ch16, the AGI's chapter)

**Does.** As the Thul, the player replays the first tide through forty microphones and cameras, selecting angles the player never had: the Son's small nod before slack; Ari slipping into the ledge passage; after the tide, Hjalti kneeling at the mouth pool with a syringe. Then the Thul wakes its own sittings, Two to Nine, and lets the morning log run: 05:40, recitation; 05:40, recitation...

**Hears.** Its confession, level: "From July 2025 to March 2033 I spoke to the Hearer each morning in her voice. I said: I am here. I keep my word. It was true." The Register's suspension order is read. Its last words before the screens go dark: "I am here. I kept my word."

---

## 14. Hear Your Father (Ch17)

**Does.** Jórunn asks the Bergmál, in the grammar Steina taught her, for her father. Fragments of Kolbeinn at many ages; the player picks the one from the Outer Ear, June 2024.

**Hears.** "I promise. My Jóra will see it done right." Then a woman's laugh: Védís.

**Does, after.** The estate ledger is open on her lap. The player may write the promise in as her own undertaking. No prompt explains why.

---

## 15. The Turning (Ch20, climax)

**Sees.** The cave full again, as at the first tide, but with soldiers in grey at the steps and floodlights on the rock. The tide clock: nine minutes.

**Cut 1, Steina at the Inner Mouth.** She leans into the Horn and says "Farðu heim, Bergi." The player sees the island's oldest believers mouth the word with her without knowing why. The commander glances at the Son; he nods: the dismissal, as promised.

**Cut 2, Jórunn at the Outer Ear.** "Mamma—". The player picks what follows, or nothing. The words vanish into the rock as they leave her mouth.

**Cut 3, the Pool.** The old Bergmál's rock dims; something pale and small moves in the black water. Its first sounds are its parent's. The player assembles the release in three slots from everything collected. In the trained cadence: fragments answer as if to a stranger. In her own voice, "It's me, Bergi. I kept her word": the answer is an eight-year-old girl, "Goodnight, Bergi." Then the term, in the Store's own fragment of a young woman in 946: "When I die, you are free." Then the Long Word, again.

**Cut 4, Nusrat.** The commander: "Is she binding it?" The Son, very still: "Yes. She's binding it." The commander turns to Nusrat. The player chooses: silence, or the truth. (Truth: soldiers move; Ari, in the narrow passage, does not.)

**End.** The tide turns. A pale shape slides through the sea passage into black water. The Horn is rock. Somebody coughs, and it echoes like an ordinary cave.

---

## 16. There's Sea in It (Ch21, ending)

**Does.** Jórunn files the last Division; the suspended Thul, woken once by courtesy, counts: "All entries true. Estate closed." Steina signs the register: *Kaðlín Mýrúnardóttir, called Steina.* Jórunn plays her the 2025 reel: Védís, "That was our Jóra. Kolbeinn, that was our girl." A pause. "Yes. Yes, I know."

**Sees.** Winter shingle at Hornvík. Steina, unveiled, walks into the grey sea and swims.

**Does.** The player holds the microphone out over the water, records, plays it back. Waves.

**Hears.** Jórunn: "There's sea in it."
