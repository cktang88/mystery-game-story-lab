# Writer A, file 06: key scenes

Each sketch says what the player **does**, **sees** and **hears**, with the text on screen kept to labels, names and short spoken lines. "Planted in" names where the detail that makes the scene land was set up in a scene about something else. Required listening in every scene stays inside the budget in 05 section 9.

---

## 1. The First Tide (opening, Ch1)

**Sees.** A black sea cave lit by hundreds of hand lamps. The whole island on stepped rock around a spiral of glistening stone, the Inner Mouth. A veiled girl on a ledge above the crowd. Placards: "I am not him." Small glass lenses in the roof. A tide clock projected on wet rock, counting down to slack.

**Does.** As Jórunn, the player holds her father's microphone while he fiddles with reels; the needle jumps with the crowd. A short switch to Steina: the player leads the Ebb-Song by moving a stick along the chant's rising and falling line, and the crowd follows the player's pace. It has no words until the end, when the whole cave speaks the Long Word, "Farðu heim, Bergi". On *Bergi*, Steina comes in a beat early.

**Hears.** The Thul over the loudspeakers: "Slack water. Words given to the Horn are given away." The crowd goes quiet and draws back. A man in House grey steps to the spiral and speaks into it: his lips move and nothing comes out. Nobody reacts. Then everyone listens. Nothing. The player can scrub the recording back and forth: voices, coughs, the Thul, and then flat silence from the Horn, with no hiss of surf at all. Kolbeinn, frowning at his meters: "No sea, Jóra. There's no sea in it."

**Turn.** A shout from the cave mouth. Torchlight on the Wash: an old woman face down among the floating net offerings. As the crowd surges, Ari shoulders back to Steina's side, his cloak dark with water.

**Planted in:** nothing; this is the plant. The scene contains, unexplained, the climax's line, the Horn's rule (the silent reader), the meaning of the Silence, and the killer's return.

## 2. The Net (Ch2)

**Sees.** A one-room net-mender's house at Útnes, wind at the window. A net hangs from the rafters in heavy loops, darker toward one end, as if handled for years.

**Does.**
- The player walks the room and lifts objects into a ring of slots (owed to, owed by, kept, broken).
- The net invites counting. The player runs a hand along it and a tally ticks, knot by knot, the camera following the cord through the room, out of the window and back: 6,408. At the very start the cord changes: five knots in black, tarred boat line.
- A carved needle turns in the light: *I am here. I keep my word.*
- The player fits a gap in the net's edge where small squares were cut away.
- A photograph: a kitchen painted red, a baby's chair.
- A certificate in the name Sæunn: *Attested as filed.*
- A reel box in a familiar hand: *Ytra Eyra, 4.6.2024, gluggi 1, AFRIT.*

**Reads.** Only the needle's carving, the stamp and the box label.

**Then.** At home, a kindly man in grey drinks coffee with Kolbeinn and leaves an envelope. "Your father's pension. Same time next month."

**Lands later.** The knot count and the boat cord date her flight to the day (Ch5). The cut squares are the charms (Ch6). The red kitchen is Steina's first memory (Ch9). "AFRIT" means an original exists (Ch11). The kindly man is how the House knew (Ch16).

## 3. Goodnight, Bergi (Ch3)

**Sees.** The Pool chamber at night: a warm pool of black water under a low dome of ridged rock that breathes with the tide. A speaker grille set in the rock, a pinpoint lens. In a far basin, something pale the size of a fist. Steina without her veil, knees up, a bowl of tea.

**Does.** The player speaks in Steina's own voice: an option set of short lines in her northern accent, not the trained cadence. The Hearer answers with a collage, each fragment a soft shape whose colour marks its century. Its first fragment, every time, is a young woman's "I'm here", the same fragment it uses at dawn for the Oath-Keeper. Steina laughs and says "Goodnight, Bergi."

**Then.** A nightmare cut: Steina in bed, Ari in the doorway, sitting, a big hand on the blanket: "Hush, now. Hush."

**Planted in:** this scene is the plant for four payoffs: the Long Word's speaker, the recognition rule, the killer's whisper, and the Twenty-first. It plays as a tender interlude.

## 4. Five Seconds (Ch4, the harmless test)

**Sees.** Kolbeinn's kitchen table, a reel labelled in his hand, *Prufur 2024-25* ("checks"), and the 2043 tide table. Then window 2 at the cave: a fisherman in oilskins at the Inner Mouth, Jórunn behind him with her father's recorder.

**Does.** The player plays five-second clips, one per mouth per window. Window 2, Inner Mouth: "Sigurður Bryndísarson, Hornvík. Tell Siggi the bilge pump is the left one." At the cave the player watches that man lean into the Horn. His lips shape the sentence, and the recorder's needle does not move. The cave's surf continues; his voice is simply absent. He straightens up and shrugs at her: everyone here knows. Back at the table, the player lays the clips along the tide table and predicts the window-3 reader by her mark. Then the player notices the end of the reel: windows 17 to 24, sea and nothing else; window 25, five seconds of a woman's voice in English over radio static, "All posts: slack—".

**Effect.** The rule is taught by seeing it, not by reading it, and the cost is visible: nobody in the present can ever confirm what he said. The same reel opens two questions that will not be answered for months.

## 5. The Hall of Hands (Ch5)

**Sees.** A long timber hall: nineteen portraits of veiled women, the veil lifted for the painter only, each smiling the same notched smile; handprints in clay beneath.

**Does.** First, veil-swap stealth: two identical veiled girls cross in a corridor, and the player must take the other girl's route and gait (a short rhythm of steps and pauses) to pass the guard. Then lineage reading: the player brings a mirror to each portrait in turn. The gap is in every one. In the mirror, Steina's teeth are whole.

**Window.** Through the glass, a young man with a camera photographs the portraits on the opposite wall: men, all with the same face. Ketill. He sees her; she sees him; neither moves.

## 6. Sitting One (Ch8)

**Sees.** A small grey room in the Thing with a speaker grille and a screen listing sittings by year, 2024 to 2043. A warrant on the table.

**Does.** The player wakes sittings and asks the same three questions of each: why was this estate assigned to me, by whose undertaking, and what is the undertaking. The answers line up in a table: eighteen identical sealed replies. The 2024 row is different.

**Hears.** Sitting One, a slightly older, warmer synthetic voice: "You're Kolbeinn's daughter. She said you would come." Two promises. Then the player tries the question everyone would ask, "Why did she go to Útnes?", and gets the machine's flat limit: "That is another estate."

**Turn.** On the way out, the public log screen in the Thing's hall shows her waking in white letters. A clerk in House grey raises a phone and photographs it. No music.

## 7. The Greeting Wall (Ch9, the anchor's reveal)

**Sees.** Steina asks the Hearer its own old question, "Is she here?", and the chamber fills with greetings, "I am here. I keep my word", in hundreds of women's voices, pouring out of the rock.

**Does.** The player catches greetings and pins them to a wall by year, mostly by eye: each is a waveform tile in its era's colour.
- At the very bottom, sounds that are not voices at all, older than anything, a low shimmer that no human throat made.
- Above them, centuries of women, each a little different in breath and age.
- Then a long block, 2025 to 2033, where every greeting is identical: same breath, same length, waveform stacked on waveform like a printed pattern.
- Just before it, two tiles that do not match: the Nineteenth's tired voice, 29 June 2025, and an old woman's, 30 June.
- Then a child's voice, wobbly, 2033: Steina's own.

**Then.** At the Pool's rim, Steina plays the 30 June tile to Gróa. Gróa keeps scrubbing. "I did one morning. After that the box spoke for her."

**States.** The player fills three slots: *The Nineteenth was alive until* [2043]; *the Pool will not grow* [a second living copy]; *so I was* [not grown]. Nothing confirms it. Steina sits down on the wet rock.

**Planted in:** Gróa's mutter at the Pool's rim earlier in the chapter, which sounded like a threat; the "dead rock" that is the Hearer's mother (Ch3).

## 8. The Script (Ch10, midpoint)

**Sees.** The Fitting rooms of the House: long tables of received lines from past Standings, with a plan pinned beside each ("1783: the boats burned at Hornvík" beside a sketch of old hulls set alight at Midsummer). Then a rented flat, rain, three people round a laptop and an old reel machine.

**Does.** A stealth sequence through the Fitting rooms, timed to the chants of Ketill's march outside. Then listening, on a timeline of the year's windows in era colours, with flags on the anomalies:
- The flags take the player to window 25.
- Inner Mouth: the radio in full, a woman's flat English, "All posts: slack. Throat, hold. Sea door, net on the ebb. Ledge passage flooded, no post."
- Then a young woman in the trained cadence: *"Farðu heim, Bergi."*
- Then a clang, and a muffled hush.
- On Kolbeinn's handed-in Outer Ear reel for the same window, Jórunn's own voice: "Mamma—", and the reel's tail flapping.

**The player's moment.** No prompt. The player has heard that name once, in a girl's night voice. The game says nothing.

**Then.** Nusrat's spectrogram: the player lays the 2006 storm-tide reel (silence, and sea) and the empty windows (silence, and sea) beside January's silence (no sea), and marks the difference. Finally, as Nusrat, the player completes her audit checklist. The last box is "Transmit evidence to the Register". It looks like a formality. It is the betrayal.

## 9. Hush (Ch11)

**Does.** Kolbeinn taps his machine: "Where the sound is clean." The player opens the recorder's lid and peels back the lining: two reels, one marked *Ytra Eyra 4.6.2024*, one marked only *V.* Jórunn sets *V.* aside. Then two waveform lanes of the master. The player mutes the left, filters the surf on the right, and under the dead woman's last words come footsteps, a scuffle and a man's whisper: "Hush, now. Hush."

**Then.** The frozen moment of the first tide, rebuilt from forty islanders' phone clips: the player places people on the rock. Every face turns to the Horn. Two gaps. The player drags Ketill to the ledge before low water, and his timestamp turns green. Ari's gap stays open for six minutes during the slack. The player files the cause of death, and the log screen adds a line.

**No text.** The player already heard the lullaby in a bedroom.

## 10. The Ledge (Ch12, the death of Kolbeinn)

**Sees.** Night, after the slack. Jórunn on the Outer Ear ledge with a torch. A reel recorder on the rock, reels still turning, its microphone pointed at the sea.

**Does.** The player picks it up and plays the reel: Kolbeinn's breathing, then his voice, "Clean. Here it's clean," then a slip, then the sea. The player stops the tape. The player does not have to do anything else, and the game waits.

**Planted in:** Ch1 ("where the sound is clean"), Ch8 and Ch11 (the public log), the search of his house.

---

## 11. The Hole (Ch14, the founding crime)

**Sees.** The Pool, dawn. The rock is dimmer than it was. Steina asks: "Where is the first?"

**Does.** Six fragments arrive out of order:
- a young woman's promise;
- the same woman, older, humming over net-mending;
- an old woman's voice that came *through* the Horn, with a distant sea behind it: *"Hlífðu mér, sonur"*;
- breath on a stair;
- a man: "Móðir", then three seconds of absolute nothing;
- water.

The player orders and attributes them on a timeline. The plea goes, naturally, under 964, when it arrived. Then the player sees the gap in 983, a hole exactly that long, and hears that the splash buried in the plea's far-side sea is the splash in 983. The player drags the plea into the hole, and it fits. The game then overlays the humming, and the two contours lock. Then it overlays the Ebb-Song the player led in Ch1, and it locks too. The island's funeral song is a lullaby a mother made of her own plea to her son, nineteen years before she said it.

**Then.** In the flat, Jórunn plays the archive line to Steina. Steina, very quiet: "That's my voice. That's what I call him." A beat. "I called him that because of the prayer."

**Planted in:** the tutorial chant (Ch1); the silent reader and the silent neighbour (Ch1, Ch4), which teach the player that the man in 983 saw his mother's lips move and heard nothing.

## 12. I Am Not Him (Ch15)

**Sees.** The Thing in full session. The afterborn in the gallery with their placards. The Son at the high bench, then, after Ketill's objection, stepping down from it to a side bench, by his own law.

**Does.** As Ketill, the player builds the pleading in a fixed frame (who the law says he is; what that man did; what follows), dragging evidence from every thread: portraits, the 1883 law, the Count's "three of three", the Store recording.

**Hears.** The Son stands. He looks for a long moment at the placard in the gallery, then at Ketill. "I am not him."

**After.** The gallery does not cheer. Bryndís, in the front row, puts her hand over her mouth. Jórunn stands: "No one may seal his own wrong." The player has heard that sentence once before, about fish.

---

## 13. Recitation (Ch16, the AGI's chapter)

**Does.** As the Thul, the player replays the first tide through forty lenses and microphones, selecting angles the player never had: the Son's small nod before slack; Ari slipping into the ledge passage; after the tide, Hjalti kneeling at the Wash with a syringe. Then the player opens a sealed folder the Thul could not speak of until today: *Pension visits, recordist K., 2041-42.* A kitchen the player knows. Kolbeinn, cheerful: "Steinunn's coming home. By the sea door, at the first tide. She told me. I promised."

**Hears.** Its confession, level: "From July 2025 to March 2033 I spoke to the Hearer each morning in her voice. I said: I am here. I keep my word. It was true."

**Then.** In a side room of the Thing, Verhoeven writes the trusteeship clause on a tablet and reads it aloud to the island's frightened deputies: "...for as long as the estate of Steinunn the First stays open. We take nothing the Thing does not grant." At night, the deposed Son knocks on her door.

## 14. Hear Your Father (Ch17)

**Does.** Jórunn, escorted, empty-handed (no recorders past the door), answers the Hearer in the three citations Steina taught her at Mýrún's table. Fragments of Kolbeinn at many ages; the player picks the one from the Outer Ear, June 2024.

**Hears.** "I promise. My Jóra will see it done right." Then a woman's laugh: Védís.

**Does, after.** The estate ledger is open on her lap. The player may write the promise in as her own undertaking. No prompt explains why. On the way out, a steel door: "Floods at slack."

---

## 15. The Route (Ch19, tide-matching as a set piece)

**Sees.** The flat at night. A floor plan of Málhellir drawn on wallpaper; the tide clock; Kolbeinn's 2006 reels; Nusrat's laptop.

**Does.** The player plays the window-25 radio check, filtered, and drops a pin for each post it names: throat, sea door, steps. "Ledge passage flooded, no post" gets a question mark. The player plays Kolbeinn's 2006 Outer Ear reels and finds, window after window, his own footsteps going through that passage during the slack. Dry. The player sets the route on the tide clock, Outer Ear to grille, and the clock shows the margin: about three minutes.

**Effect.** The verb taught with a bilge pump in Ch4 now plans a break-in using the soldiers' own future voices. Because the timeline is fixed, the commander will say "no post" whatever she has read. The archive is the one map she cannot change.

---

## 16. The Turning (Ch20, climax)

**Sees.** The cave full again, as at the first tide, but with soldiers in grey at the steps, floodlights on the rock, and a tank barge's lights beyond the sea door. The tide clock: nine minutes.

**Cut 1, Steina at the Inner Mouth.** The radio check, live, word for word. She leans into the Horn and says "Farðu heim, Bergi." The player sees the island's oldest believers mouth the word with her without knowing why. Verhoeven glances at the Son, who reads her lips: "The relay. As I told you." Engineers swing a steel plate over the spiral. Clang.

**Cut 2, Jórunn at the Outer Ear.** "Mamma—". The player picks what follows, or nothing. The words vanish into the rock as they leave her mouth. She runs. The passage is dry, as it was for her father.

**Cut 3, the Pool.** The old Hearer's rock dims; something pale and small moves in the black water. Its first sounds are its parent's. The player assembles the release in three slots from everything collected. In the trained cadence, fragments answer as if to a stranger. In her own voice, "It's me, Bergi. I kept her word," the answer is an eight-year-old girl: "Goodnight, Bergi." Steina knows it is the last time. Then the term, in the Store's own young woman's voice from 946: "And when I die, you are free." Then the Long Word, here, where it counts.

**Cut 4, the gallery.** Verhoeven: "Thul?" "A notary attests; it does not interpret." Verhoeven: "Is she binding it?" The Son, very still: "Yes. She's binding it." Verhoeven turns to Nusrat: "Auditor? You've read the archive." The player chooses: silence, or the truth onto the live record. (Truth: soldiers move; Ari, in the narrow throat, does not.)

**Cut 5, the grille.** Jórunn, soaked, drags three exhibits into three slots. Verhoeven's voice through the rock: "Thul. You are forbidden to count. Speak the binding." A breath. "Three of three are true. Estate closed." Then: "I am here. I kept my word." The grilles go dark one by one, up the cave. Verhoeven looks down at her tablet, at her own sentence. "Lift the net."

**End.** The tide turns. A pale shape slides through the sea door into black water, past a slack net. The Horn is rock. Somebody coughs, and it echoes like an ordinary cave. The Son sits down on the wet rock.

---

## 17. There's Sea in It (Ch21, ending)

**Does.** Jórunn hands in her Divider's seal at the Thing; the log screen behind her reads *Sitting Twenty: frozen, 3.1.2044.* At the shore the island sings the Ebb-Song with its words, for the first time in a thousand years. Steina signs the register: *Kaðlín Mýrúnardóttir, called Steina.* Jórunn plays her the reel marked *V.*: Védís, "That was our Jóra. Kolbeinn, that was our girl." A pause. "Yes. Yes, I know."

**Sees.** Winter shingle at Hornvík. Steina, unveiled, walks into the grey sea and swims.

**Does.** The player holds the microphone out over the water, records, and plays it back. Waves.

**Hears.** Jórunn: "There's sea in it."
