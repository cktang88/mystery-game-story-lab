# Writer A, file 02: the sealed solution

**Canon tier: LOCKED.** This is the true history the player reconstructs. Every clue in the game is a view onto something on this page. Any change requires re-auditing the clue matrix in 05-outline.md. Revised after the grader's review; every change is listed in 08-revision-log.md.

**Names used on screen.** The alien is "the Hearer" to everyone (its learned name, *Bergmál*, "echo", appears only in old texts; Steina calls it "Bergi"). The ruling House is **the Mother's House**. The tidal pool at the cave entrance is **the Wash**. Women who stand in for an Oath-Keeper are **Loan-Women**. The wordless funeral chant is **the Ebb-Song**. The island's holiest phrase is **the Long Word**.

## 1. The hard rules (as they really work)

1. **The Horn (time).** At the low-water slack of each spring tide in a Standing year (a lunar major standstill, every 18.6 years; about 25 windows of roughly nine minutes each), sound spoken into either mouth of the Horn arrives at the same mouth exactly **6,798 days** earlier, at the matching window of the previous Standing. The two mouths are the **Inner Mouth**, in the Hearing chamber, and the **Outer Ear**, on a sea ledge reachable only at low water. The Horn carries what is spoken into it and, faintly, whatever sounds within a few paces of it: the far side's sea, breath, footsteps. Sound only. One step only; a message goes further back only if someone at the earlier Standing re-speaks it (a **relay**). The Horn *takes* what is spoken into it: nobody present hears those words and no device records them. Sounds around the mouth are heard normally in the present as well as carried. The timeline is fixed: anything received was always going to be sent.
2. **The Store.** The Hearer keeps every sound made in its cave for its whole life, including sound that arrives through the Horn, except words the Horn takes away. It can replay any of it. It cannot make new sounds. A bud inherits its parent's Store.
3. **The Pool (cloning).** Blood offered in the Pool can be grown into a newborn. No memory passes. One living copy of a pattern at a time: the Pool will not grow what is already walking.
4. **Binding speech.** The Hearer hears promises and releases only from its keeper (rule 5), only in the Pool, and only at Standing slacks. Its kind cannot act against a promise. A promise passes to its bud with its body and its Store.
5. **Recognition.** The Hearer knows its keeper by **the word kept**: whoever comes every day and keeps the promise, in an unbroken chain of dawns, is to it the one who made the promise. It does not tell faces, blood or voices apart for this purpose. A dawn without the word would not break the promise, but it would leave no keeper the Hearer recognises, so nobody could bind or release it until a new keeper had earned recognition over years. The Mother's House believes for 1,060 years that recognition is by *voice*. That belief is wrong, but it gives the House the right fear for the wrong reason: in 1,060 years it has never let a dawn pass without the word.
6. **Budding.** The Hearer's kind die only at the last window of a Standing, and bud as they die. The bud takes its parent's body-memory, Store and promises, and its first sounds are its parent's. It grows a new Horn into whatever rock it settles in; the parent's Horn dies to plain rock. The **Dead Horn**, a second, lifeless spiral deep in Málhellir, is the husk of the Hearer's own parent.
7. **The Thul (AGI).**
   - It cannot state a falsehood about its record.
   - It **attests filings, not facts**: every certificate it issues ends "Attested as filed."
   - It does not interpret ("a notary attests; it does not interpret"), so it never glosses speech it cannot attribute.
   - Each year (Thing to Thing, 1 July to 30 June) is frozen as a **sitting**. A sitting can be woken only on an estate officer's petition, under a warrant for an open estate, and it answers only about that estate's own matters: the holder's identity, assets, debts, promises given and received, and death. Every waking is publicly logged in the Thing.
   - A **seal** sworn by a sitting closes that sitting's and every later sitting's mouth on the sealed records *to everyone, including the seal's holder* ("a seal has no key").
   - A **count** (law of 1702, "a count is not testimony") lets an estate officer submit at least three statements about an open estate in her warrant, each with evidence entered in the estate file. The Thul says how many are true. Sealed matters may be counted. Every count is publicly logged with the estate, the officer, the number of statements and their headings (identity, assets, debts, promises, cause of death), but not their wording.
   - A seal lapses if its holder has no lawful standing, and "no one may seal his own wrong".
8. **The Law of Same (1883).** An afterborn copy is legally the same person as the original: name, debts, marriages, crimes.
9. **The Tidings Law (1671).**
   - Every send at the Inner Mouth must be a reading from the **Book of Tidings**, a chronicle of the years since the last Standing that the Thing compiles and swears the year before. Each reading opens with the reader's sworn **mark**, his name and a fixed phrase that authenticates his voice.
   - During a slack only the reader stands within reach of the Inner Mouth; the island keeps silence at a distance.
   - The last window carries the **relay** of the Long Word.
   - Sending at the Outer Ear is forbidden, but the ledge cannot be policed.
   - So foresight is a chronicle of the *next eighteen years*, read from the far side. It is never a running report of the Standing year itself. The House owns the received archive and sells edited Tidings abroad.
   - Because the past hears every reading eighteen years early, a reader who strayed from the Book would be known in advance; strays are rare and remembered.
10. **The admitted cheat.** How the Hearer's kind span 18.6 years, and where they come from, is never explained. It is spent on the premise, never on a solution.

## 2. True chronology

### Deep past

- **926-27 Standing. "Fire in the sea."** A being of the Hearer's kind falls burning into the sea off Eiðey's south cliff, wedges itself into the sea cave later called Málhellir, dies at the Standing's last window and buds. Its husk is the Dead Horn. Its bud, the Hearer, grows its Horn, Pool and Store into the cave. The oldest sounds in its Store are its parent's, from somewhere else. (Origin and destination: texture.)
- **c. 938.** Unheard folk account: a Gaelic thrall girl gathering dulse hears the cave repeat her singing. (Never confirmed or refuted.)
- **945-46 Standing. The Speaking.** Steinunn (b. 912), a widow who mends nets in the cave mouth, has spoken to the Hearer daily for years; it has learned speech by storing hers. They make a covenant, in the Pool, at slack:
  - Steinunn: *"I will speak with you every day that I live."*
  - Hearer (in her own stored words): *"While she who speaks with me lives, I carry the island's words back one Standing."*
  - Steinunn's term: *"And when I die, you are free. Go home."*
  At the same Standing the Inner Mouth receives the **Long Word** for the first time: a short phrase in a man's voice, *"Farðu heim, Bergi"* ("Go home, Bergi"). It has been relayed back from the Turning of 2044 through 59 steps by the relayers of 58 Standings. This oldest instance is spoken from 964 by Steinunn's own son, Þorsteinn (b. 938), as that Standing's relayer. Nobody understands it. It becomes the island's holiest phrase, with "Bergi" read as "to the rock".
- **946-982.** Foresight: each Standing, the island hears the next Standing's readers recount the intervening eighteen years (famines, eruptions, raids). Eiðey prospers. Þorsteinn becomes law-speaker and the richest man on the island.
- **963-64 Standing. The Mother's Cry.** At the Outer Ear, Steinunn, 52, hears an old woman's voice: *"Hlífðu mér, sonur"* ("Spare me, son"). It is received sound, so the Store keeps it. She recognises her own voice, aged. She tells no one what it means. She sets the three words to a tune that follows their fall and sings it while mending nets in the cave mouth; the Store keeps that too. The island's children learn it as a lullaby.
- **982-83 Standing, last window.** Steinunn, 71 and dying, comes by boat to the Outer Ear, meaning to go through the ledge passage to the Pool and release the Hearer before she dies, because she fears what foresight has made of her son and the island. Þorsteinn meets her on the ledge at slack. Into the Outer Ear she says *"Hlífðu mér, sonur."* The Horn takes it to 964, so he sees his mother's lips shape her own lullaby with no sound at all. He drowns her in the Wash. The Store keeps the scuffle, his "Móðir" ("Mother"), the water, and a gap of three seconds where the Horn took her words. He carries her blood into the Pool and asks the Hearer to "heal" her. To its kind, healing is regrowth, and a regrown being who keeps the promise is the same being. It grows a girl: **Steinunn II**.
- **983.** Þorsteinn writes **the Reckoning**, his private account: *"I killed my mother so the island would not starve."* He believes the Hearer knows her by her voice, so he orders the girl taught to speak exactly as her mother did, and rewrites the covenant as a servitude: *"While the swearer lives, the Hearer serves."* He forbids the lullaby's words but keeps its tune, wordless, as the chant for funerals and Hearings. Within a few generations the words are forgotten; the tune is **the Ebb-Song**. The liturgy keeps one true proverb, *"By the word kept is the keeper known,"* which the House has always read as "by the voice".
- **c. 1010 onward.** At each Son's death the House grows a new Þorsteinn from the dying Son's blood, as it grows each Steinunn from the last. The same man's pattern rules for 1,060 years, publicly as an honoured custom ("the Son, renewed"). Trained **Loan-Women** fill the gaps while a new Steinunn grows and learns to speak (the office's voice is "lent" to them). Most were pensioned. Some who threatened to talk were "retired" (drowned). Their unmarked stones are at Hornvík (texture).
- **1550.** Sendrights (the right to read at the Inner Mouth, and the fees foreign buyers pay for what it brings) become heritable property. Wealth concentrates in a few families; the thralls' descendants, the **Unheard**, hold none.
- **1615-16.** A relayer refuses to re-speak the Long Word; another does it in his place (texture).
- **1652-53. The Quiet Standing.** The island sends nothing except the relay. Why is never shown.
- **1670-71. The Tidings Law.**
- **1702.** The law that "a count is not testimony".
- **1883. The Law of Same.** When aftergrowth is opened to commoners, the House needs a law that copies are legally the originals, "to keep the Speaking whole", protecting the doctrine that each Steinunn *is* the swearer.
- **1950.** The received sound is first recorded (wire, then tape).
- **1968-69.** Eiðey begins quietly selling foresight to allied governments, in price tiers.

## 3. What each non-human mind is and wants, and the one loop

- **The Hearer.** An old, stranded, single being of a kind that lives across long lunar cycles and cannot break a promise. Want: *to go home, and it believes it may go only when the woman it promised lets it.* Its recurring question, asked in collage for 1,060 years: "Is she still here?"
- **The Thul.** A notary mind that cannot lie and forgets nothing. Want: *every promise in its keeping kept or lawfully released; the oldest is neither.* It carries inherited obligations from earlier versions of itself that pull against each other: the House's seal (until it lapses), and Sitting One's two promises to Steinunn XIX. In Act IV a third is added, the Register's lawful order not to count. At the Turning it must choose between the newest lawful order and its oldest promise. It has only ever obeyed, and this is the first thing it chooses.
- **The one closed loop.** The Long Word has no first speaker. Steina named the Hearer "Bergi" from the prayer, and the prayer's last word is Steina's own goodbye. The loop carries a name, not information: no character learns a fact from it that steers the plot. It is the story's single ontological loop, kept on purpose as the shape of the theme: a promise with no maker, kept only by those who choose to keep it. Every other causal chain in this document has an independent start.

## 6. The story from the hidden side (the Son's synopsis)

In May 2025 the Son heard the island's holiest words in the voice of an Oath-Keeper and understood that, at the end of the next Standing, she would end the gift that had kept Eiðey alive for a thousand years and now warned the whole world of floods and plagues. He had read the Reckoning. He knew what his pattern had done in 983 and believed it had been right. He did what the House has always done: he Fitted. He ordered the independent Nineteenth retired and acquired an infant he could raise to obey. When the Nineteenth escaped, he sealed the machine so that no one, himself included, could make it speak, and had it speak her words each dawn. For seventeen years he could not find her. Then a demented recordist told his pension officer that she would come home by the sea door at the first tide. The Son aimed the girl's loving guardian at her, because a man protecting a child does not hesitate. He drew the dead woman's blood to grow a true copy. He planned to keep the girl from the Pool at the Turning, have the machine bind the newborn Hearer, and let the Long Word pass as a farewell. When his own Law of Same was turned on him, he said "I am not him" and lost the House. To keep the world's warnings he gave the Register the rite and the Transfer, and he taught the girl the binding himself. He did not know that the Hearer had never listened to voices, or that the girl had been its keeper for ten years. On the last night, with the world's soldiers asking him what she was saying, he alone understood her, and he made "I am not him" true.

## 7. Final emotional image

A girl who was told all her life that she was someone else swims in the winter sea with her face bare. A woman who has just lost the office of settling the estates of the dead stands on the shingle with her dead father's reel recorder, records the waves, listens back, and says: "There's sea in it."
