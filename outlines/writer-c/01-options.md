# Writer C, round 3: options rounds

Starting point: **ending first.** I fixed the final emotional image and the anchor scenes, then built the hidden history backward until the ending was inevitable, then planted the clues. Each round lists at least three genuinely different options, judges them against `BRIEF.md`, and records the pick.

Working title: **The Ouro's Hand**

## Seeds (mandatory, all used)

| Seed | Value |
|---|---|
| galactic_setting | a war-front system under a fifty-year ceasefire |
| intrigue | a censor who rewrites the news; a royal bastard with a valid claim |
| economic_system | a currency backed by stored computation |
| technology | orbital weapons under treaty limits; surveillance dust |
| culture_era | Coptic Egypt, 4th century |
| profession | court interpreter |
| object | porcelain teacup with a crack; lacquered bento box |
| phenomenon | grafting |
| relationship | neighbours sharing a wall |
| ritual | the day of the dead |

How the culture seed is used: Coptic Egypt of the 300s is a **naming and texture source** for a far-future human realm, not a historical setting. It supplies the realm's provinces (nomes), the royal title (*ouro*, Coptic for king), the calendar (Thout to Mesori plus the five "little days" of Nesi), the day of the dead (Nairouz, the Coptic new year kept for martyrs, here kept for the war dead), the banking orders (the Koinonia, after the Pachomian federation of monasteries, with *Apa* and *Amma* as titles), the habit of writing on broken pottery (ostraca, here porcelain sherds), the currency's name (*holok*, from *holokottinos*, the Coptic name for the gold solidus first struck in 309), the cell-dwelling of Kellia, and personal names (Tamoun, Pjol, Phib, Pamin, Talou, Tsenoute, Pshoi, Maro, Senetom, Apollo, Tsia). No real rite is staged as plot machinery.

## Round 0: the final emotional image (where I started)

**I1. The knock at the wall.** One year after the climax, on the day of the dead, a one-armed woman with a new name sets two places at the wall she shares with an alien neighbour: a cracked teacup mended with lacquer, and a lacquered lunch box. The neighbour no longer remembers her. She can knock or not. If she knocks, a green shoot comes through the old crack and grows one word, the first word it ever said to her.

**I2. The empty throne room.** The new sovereign, whom history will never know, sits alone in a throne room the censor has erased her from, watching the broadcast of someone else being crowned in her place.

**I3. The burning ledger.** The heroine watches a currency board across forty-two worlds go to zero as she spends the reserve, and a child in a market hands her a coin that is now worthless.

**Pick: I1.** It holds the human anchor (two neighbours, one wall), carries three objects planted from the first scene, and makes the plan's cost three-fold and quiet: her arm, her name and her neighbour's memory of her. It also leaves the player one small thing to decide (knock or not), which the report names as a property of endings that last. I2 is Code Geass-adjacent in look (a hidden sovereign behind a public one) and plays as triumph, not loss. I3 is spectacle; its emotion comes from scale, not from a relationship. Elements of I2 survive as the censor's edit, and of I3 as the economic cost of the climax.

## Round 1: setting and scale

**S1. The Shiet front and the Kellia ring.** A binary star system on the border between a human realm of 42 nome-worlds and an alien commonwealth. The fifty-year ceasefire line runs through one orbital ring-city, Kellia, built half by each side, divided down its whole length by a living wall. In the Wall Quarter, human apartments ("cells") back directly onto alien ones. The courts, banks and weapons of both powers are present for the renewal.

**S2. The capital far from the front.** The story happens at the human capital world, 6 jump-days from the front. The war system is offstage, reported only through the news the censor rewrites.

**S3. The armistice convoy.** The ceasefire is held by a neutral convoy that orbits between the two powers' systems forever; the whole story happens aboard ships that can never stop moving.

**Pick: S1.** It realises "war-front system" and "neighbours sharing a wall" at once: the treaty line is a wall, and the heroine's bedroom wall is part of it. Every scale (bedroom, quarter, ring, system, galaxy) shares one physical object. S2 gives the censor more room but makes the front a rumour, and the seed wants the front present. S3 is striking, but it isolates the cast into a single community, which the banned list warns against. The capital (Shmin) stays as a second location reached by jump, so S2 survives as one thread's destination.

## Round 2: central question

**Q1. "Can a join hold if it hides its scar?"** Grafting, translation, copies and edited records are all joins. Each pillar tests whether a union survives being honest about where it was cut.

**Q2. "Who may speak for a body: its copy, its translator, its record or its ledger?"**

**Q3. "Is peace something you buy or something you grow?"**

**Pick: Q1**, with Q2 as the political form it takes. Q1 has more than one defensible answer (the censor's answer is that the world needs lacquer; the interpreter's is that the joined parties must see the scar), and it ties directly to the phenomenon seed (a graft union is literally a hidden scar), the cracked cup and the lacquered box. The ending takes a split side: the two who are joined must see the scar, and the world may be shown lacquer. That is the plan's cost. Q2 is sharper legally but abstract to play. Q3 is a theme without a dilemma: everyone in the story already believes peace is bought.

## Round 3: the ending (three options for events and meaning)

**E1. The Ouro's Hand.** At the renewal, the interpreter learns she is a woken clone of the queen who signed the treaty, and that the alien treaty can only "take" if new growth from the signer's body is grafted into it. She grafts her arm into the living treaty. An assassin tries to tear her out (a tear kills; a clean cut does not). Her friend, who once betrayed her, cuts her arm off cleanly, and the treaty takes permanently. The censor, at her word, edits her out of the broadcast, so the realm sees its king renew the peace and the interpreter die. Her alien neighbour grafts their twelve-year conversation-vine into the treaty and so forgets her. Coda: I1.

**E2. The Interpreter's Lie.** She mistranslates the alien text at the renewal to buy fifty more years. The censor brands her the traitor who nearly broke the peace, and she is executed. Coda: her neighbour sets the cracked cup for her on the day of the dead.

**E3. The Open Crack.** She tells the whole truth in public, takes the throne by right of body, and the realm splits into a succession war. The treaty holds, but the world burns politically. Coda: she sets places for the dead at the palace, far from the wall.

**Pick: E1 as the sealed ending, with E3 kept as the player's alternative** at the censor's console (inaction lets the raw broadcast go out, giving E3's political aftermath). E1 is the Code Geass shape used as technique: a plan decades in the making (her teacher's) completes exactly, and the win and the loss are one act, because her arm *is* the signature. It fulfils a first-hour line literally: the treaty says it is "renewed at the Wall by the Ouro's hand", and everyone assumes that means the king. E2 makes the heroine's death the payoff, which is the self-erasure trope, and it resolves nothing about the mystery's truth. E3 is honest but leaves the ending's emotional image on a throne rather than a wall.

## Round 4: hidden history

**H1. The queen's forgery and the stolen shoot.** Fifty years ago, the dying queen signed a living treaty by giving a cutting of her own body. The alien text said that when the treaty-tree first fruits, the signer's root must send a new shoot to join it, after which the two peoples need no keeper. In the realm's law a clone of the sovereign *is* the sovereign, so the queen ordered her interpreter to write "fifty years, renewed by the Ouro's hand" instead, to keep her clones from ever outranking her son. The interpreter obeyed, then stole one of the queen's sleeping clones and raised it as an interpreter so the treaty could still take.

**H2. The alien forgery.** The aliens wrote a hidden clause binding humans to them, and the interpreter discovers she has been their instrument.

**H3. The bluff.** No treaty was ever really signed: both sides saw a future of mutual destruction in time-sent records, and the censor's office has kept the peace by rewriting what each side sees.

**Pick: H1.** It is a founding *lie* told by humans to humans, which every faction is downstream of (succession law, the bond market, the arms limits, the interpreters' school, the censor's habits), and its answer opens a further vista (the clone law across 42 worlds) instead of shrinking the world. It avoids the banned shape: the alien is not bound, captive, dying or fed on; it is an equal neighbour, and the secret is about the human throne. H2 is "aliens secretly behind all human troubles". H3 is clever but makes the alien power irrelevant and the treaty fake, which empties the renewal.

## Round 5: world rules per pillar (three options each)

### Aliens (the Bnne)

- **A1. A living grafted language.** The Bnne have no voice. They speak by grafting slips of their tissue onto each other or onto "speaking wood", and an utterance keeps growing, so its meaning matures over days. Editing a sentence means pruning and regrafting it, and the scar (callus) shows where it was cut.
- **A2. A species that trades moments.** They lend each other spans of lived time as a medium of speech.
- **A3. A distributed colony that speaks in prices.** Their language is the chemical market inside a hive.

**Pick: A1.** It *is* the grafting seed. It turns the interpreter's job into hands-on work (reading growth, callus and cut marks with a grafted forearm), and it creates a dilemma no other story has: a treaty written in a living language goes on growing after it is signed, and nobody can stop it saying what it grows into. A2 collides with the time pillar, and A3 with the economy.

### AGI (the Oikonomos)

- **G1. The steward of the reserve.** One mind holds the vault-ring of stored computation and the system's surveillance dust. It answers any precisely posed, *paid* question exactly, volunteers nothing, and will not let the reserve be spent except by the treaty's signers.
- **G2. A council of the disarmed weapons' minds,** which must vote on everything.
- **G3. The censor's engine:** an AGI that writes the news.

**Pick: G1.** It ties the AGI to the economy (truth costs money, so the lie is free) and to the dust. It is a third position: neither threat nor guardian, with its own small aim of keeping the reserve sealed. G3 would steal the censor's human dilemma, and G2 is a committee, not a mind.

### Cloning (rootstocks and seconds)

- **C1. The Law of the Body.** Nobles keep sleeping clones ("rootstocks") as spare bodies and wake some as household doubles ("seconds"). By law a body and all its copies are **one legal person with one name**: a second may sign for its original, and its acts are its original's acts. The crown passes to "the Ouro's body", so a clone of the Ouro is the Ouro.
- **C2. Clones as debt heirs,** who inherit their originals' debts at waking.
- **C3. Exchanged clone hostages** between the two sides.

**Pick: C1.** It makes the royal bastard's claim valid by statute rather than by blood, turns a copy into the most dangerous person in the realm, and produces a sharp legal lever: if a second commits a murder, its original is guilty. C2 and C3 are economic or military decoration.

### Time travel (backward motes)

- **T1. Paid backward dust.** Surveillance-dust motes, the only things light enough, can be pushed back in time by spending stored computation. The cost rises steeply with depth. A mote carries at most nine seconds of record. Under the treaty only the signers may send. The timeline is fixed: what arrives was sent.
- **T2. Minds grafted backward** into one's own sleeping clone.
- **T3. Growth rings as windows:** the living wall's rings let you look into the past.

**Pick: T1.** It welds three seeds into one rule (dust, stored computation, the treaty's limits), makes the future literally cost money, and gives a hunting verb (finding future motes in a cloud of present dust) and a final editing verb (cutting the clips you will send). T2 is strong but merges cloning and time into one mechanic, so the pillars stop generating different scenes. T3 is a look-at-the-past device, which the dust audit already covers.

## Round 6: the political and economic system

- **P1. The holok and the Wall Bonds.** Under the treaty both sides disarmed all but seven orbital weapons ("sickles") each. The disarmed sickles' cores became vaults of stored computation, run by the Oikonomos. Interstellar jumps need solutions computed in advance, so stored computation is the right to travel and trade. The holok is a claim on it. Rearming a sickle wipes its vault, so war burns the money. The crown financed fifty years of rebuilding with **Wall Bonds** in holoks, held by the Koinonia banking orders, maturing on renewal day.
- **P2. A currency of jump routes only,** with no tie to the weapons.
- **P3. Living money:** alien grafts as currency.

**Pick: P1.** It gives the plot its engine: **every coin is a sleeping gun.** The debtors (the crown's treasury, indebted nome governors) gain if the holok collapses, so the hawks are debtors and the doves are creditors. The mystery moves the market (a measured time-travel plume implies a huge spend on renewal night), and the market moves the mystery (whoever is entitled to spend the reserve must be one of the signers). P2 loses the arms link, and P3 confuses the alien pillar.

## Round 7: founding crime

- **F1. The forgery (H1).** The queen ordered the treaty mistranslated, and the interpreter stole a clone.
- **F2. The prisoner cores.** The sickles' vaults hold the minds of war prisoners. Rejected: it is close to the banned "society lives off a captive alien".
- **F3. The charter backdoor.** The Oikonomos's founding charter secretly lets the treasury mint against the reserve, so the currency has been diluted for fifty years.

**Pick: F1**, with F3 reduced to texture (rumours of a backdoor that turn out to be the Body account's dormant share). F1 creates the present factions: the hawks (who need the term to lapse), the bankers (who need it renewed), the crown (who loses the throne if the truth is known) and the interpreters (who inherited the lie).

## Round 8: the intrigue structure (who spies on whom)

- **I-A. A four-way web.** The Treasurer runs the junior interpreter Phib under a false flag (he believes his handler is the war-commander Strategos). The Koinonia plant a clerk, Tsia, in the censor's office. The censor, Pamin, is also the king's spymaster and watches everyone through the dust. The alien neighbour was placed beside the interpreter by her teacher to watch over her. Spies meet only in alien-grown spaces, because alien tissue eats dust.
- **I-B. A cold-war swap,** with human and alien spy services trading agents across the wall.
- **I-C. A single mole hunt** inside the censor's office.

**Pick: I-A.** It gives each plot line its own spy relationship and makes the dust's blind spots the map of intrigue. Its betrayals are spy-thriller betrayals (false-flag recruitment, a double agent in the news office, a turned agent feeding false reports), not villain reveals. I-B turns the aliens into a human-style spy service, which flattens them. I-C is too small.

## Round 9: main characters and names

Names come from Coptic personal names of the 300s to 400s, as they would have worn down over centuries of use in space. The Bnne have no spoken names, so humans name each Bnne they deal with after the Coptic month in which it was first met. That rule dates every alien character.

**The anchor (three options).**
- **Tamoun Besa**, 34, court interpreter of Bnne graft-speech, raised as a foundling in the interpreters' school. She is secretly the woken clone of the signing queen. **Picked:** her craft, her body and her bedroom wall are the treaty's three parts.
- The censor as protagonist: rich, but his job is to hide things, which fights "never let a playable character conceal what they know".
- A sickle engineer who is the bastard: has more action, but loses the interpreter seed's centrality.

**Important names (three options each, pick in bold).**
- Interpreter: **Tamoun** / Taësi / Senoute. Tamoun ("she of Amun") is short, unusual, and her surname Besa is the foundling-school name.
- Mentor: **Apa Pjol** / Apa Psote / Apa Hatre. Pjol is spiky and memorable.
- Friend and spy: **Phib** / Pamoun / Bane. One syllable, easy to call across a room.
- Censor: **Pamin Hatre** / Shenoute Lector / Apa Mêna. The title is "Lector of the Daybook".
- King: **Ouro Pshoi III** / Ouro Antoni / Ouro Bêsa. Pshoi is a dynastic name repeated across three kings.
- Signing queen: **Ouro Tsenoute** / Ouro Tachom / Ouro Sophia. The *-noute* echoes the word for god.
- Treasurer: **Kyra Taësi** / Treasurer Hermione / Lady Tsia. Kyra is the Greek-Coptic lady-title worn down into a name.
- Treasurer's second: **Maro** / Kyrilla / Little Kyra. A second takes a short name its original gives it, never a surname.
- Banker: **Amma Senetom** / Amma Maria / Amma Talia. Senetom means "sister of the lord".
- Strategos: **Apollo** / Victor / Hor. Common soldier names of the era.
- Teacher: **Amma Talou** / Amma Tsia / Amma Kyra.
- The neighbour: **Ethen**, Tamoun's private name for it, from the Coptic for "near". The Bnne envoy: **Paopi** (first met in the month Paopi).

## Round 10: premise sentence (under 15 words, no names)

- **P-1.** "An interpreter must graft her own body into a living treaty that cannot lie." (14 words)
- **P-2.** "When money is disarmed weapons, a translator must decide what peace really said." (13 words)
- **P-3.** "A censor and a translator race to edit a treaty that keeps growing." (13 words)

**Pick: P-1**, refined to: **"A court interpreter learns the living peace treaty can only take her body."** (13 words). It names what she stands to lose (her body, her name) and the mechanic that generates every dilemma (a living, grafted text). P-2 is the political frame, used for the paragraph. P-3 misdescribes the censor's role.

## Round 11: key scenes (three options for each anchor)

**Opening image.**
- **O1. Tea through the wall.** Tamoun drinks from her cracked cup while the neighbour's vine comes through a crack in the wall and grafts the word "rain" onto her wrist. The court fleet arrives. That night her mentor dies. **Picked:** it is small, domestic and alien at once, and it plants the cup, the vine, the wall and the knock.
- O2. The renewal rehearsal in the hall, with 42 nomes' banners.
- O3. A sickle firing drill seen from the market.

**Midpoint.**
- **M1. The crack in the cup.** She reads the hair-thin graft hidden in her teacup's crack and learns she is the queen's shoot. On the same night, at the capital, her friend stands in the crypt among eleven sleeping copies of her face, with orders to burn them. **Picked:** the identity reveal is delivered through the planted object, and Phib's thread makes it physical.
- M2. The censor publicly names her as the claimant.
- M3. A sickle rearms early and fires on the wall.

**Climax.**
- **K1. The hand at the union** (E1). **Picked.**
- K2. A courtroom debate before both courts.
- K3. A firefight on the sickle platforms.

**Most memorable moments (chosen from a longer list).** The gap in the dust that walks like the wrong person; "The lie is free; the truth costs a holok a second"; eleven sleeping copies of her face; the vine withdrawing through the crack; "Cut there"; the first word, "near", coming back through the wall.
