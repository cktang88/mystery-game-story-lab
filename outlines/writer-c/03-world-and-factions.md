## 2. World rules: one mechanism, one cap, one price per pillar

Canonical rule text is in `02-hidden-history.md`, section 1 (R1 to R9).

### Aliens: the Bnne and graft-speech

- **What they are.** Tall, slow, mobile beings of bark and sap, with grafting palms. They have no voice and no hearing. They feel vibration through wood and sense chemistry by touch. Their elders take root.
- **Mechanism.** They speak by grafting slips. An utterance keeps growing, so its meaning matures (Ethen's "rain" grows into "rain on a friend's roof"). Editing a sentence means pruning and regrafting it, and the cut leaves a callus.
- **Callus and memory.** A slip calluses in about three days. Before that it can be cut away unfelt. After that it carries feeling and memory, and a Bnne who cuts away a callused graft loses what it carried. This is shown harmlessly in Ch 1, when Paopi forgets Pjol's name.
- **Hard limit.** A graft takes only on living, compatible tissue. Nothing can be unsaid, only cut, and the cut shows.
- **The price (interpreters).** Bnne tissue grafted into the forearm and adapted over years. A clean cut is safe. A **tear** is fatal. Interpreters are half-shunned at court as "the green-armed".
- **One-sentence want:** to make every union take, because to the Bnne a graft that fails to take is the only real death.
- **The consent rule:** "the tree may not graft the shoot toward itself."
- **Language difference with consequences:** the Bnne have no idea of forgery. Every graft is speech, and the speaker is whoever grafts.
- **Verb: graft-reading and graft-writing.** The player lays the forearm on wood and reads growth direction, callus colour and cut faces. To answer, the player builds a reply from slips by species, cut angle and placement. The grammar is combinatorial: roughly 12 slip types × 6 angles × 5 placements gives 360 forms per single-slip utterance and tens of thousands for two-slip clauses, so guessing fails.

### AGI: the Oikonomos

- **Mechanism.** The steward of the 40-vault reserve and of all surveillance dust in Shiet. It rebuilds any moment for a paid, precisely posed query, and volunteers nothing.
- **Hard limits.** It cannot see in bare Bnne tissue or read graft-speech. It spends the reserve only on the order of both signers' roots together, and only on a send that will arrive.
- **Its stake:** **its mind is its vaults.** If the term lapses, each side may reclaim its 20 vaults to rearm, and rearming wipes them, so a lapse would erase the Oikonomos.
- **One-sentence want:** not to be turned back into guns.
- **Rule-bound self-interest:** its charter lets it set each question's price by "risk to the reserve". Questions whose answers lower the risk of rearming are cheap, and questions that raise it are dear. It cannot lie, but it can make the truth that saves it affordable (Ch 20, the one-holok question).
- **The price to users:** every audit spends money and is booked to the asker's account, which overseers can see. "The lie is free; the truth costs a holok a second." Audit cost is about one holok per audited second at standard risk.
- **Relationship damaged:** privacy, since the rich can buy the past, and buy only the part they want. **Relationship only it can save:** justice for the rewritten dead.
- **Verb: audit.** The player sets place, time and radius, watches the cost counter, walks the frozen reconstruction, and compares it with the Daybook's version.

### Cloning: sleepers, seconds and the Law of the Body

- **Mechanism.** Nobles grow **sleepers** (the legal term is rootstock), clones kept asleep for organs and continuity, which age as they sleep. Some are woken as **seconds**: household doubles who sign, attend and act for their originals.
- **The Law of the Body.** A body and all its copies are one legal person with one name. A second's signature binds its original, and its crimes are its original's crimes. The crown passes to "the Ouro's body". A dead Ouro's sleepers are relics, and waking one is treason.
- **Hard limit.** Copies share a genome and nothing else. Acquired marks tell them apart: gait, scars, calluses, stains and graft-veins.
- **The price:** seconds have no names and no standing, and nobody sets a place for them at Nairouz.
- **The open question the story asks:** what do copies owe each other, and what does a copy owe the person it was copied from? Tamoun, Maro and the woken sleeper Nesi are three answers.
- **Relationship damaged:** a second and its original (Maro and Kyra). **Relationship only it can save:** the treaty, which needs the root.
- **Verb: attribution.** The player tells copies apart by acquired marks and gait, in person and in dust, including the gait of an invisible gap.

### Time travel: backward motes

- **Mechanism.** Spending stored computation pushes dust motes back in time. Each carries at most nine seconds of record, about 4 m across, with sound.
- **Hard limits.**
  - The cost rises with the square of the depth: one hour is about 0.0006 percent of the reserve, one day about 0.35 percent, forty days about 33 percent.
  - Only motes can be sent.
  - Under the treaty, only the signers' roots acting together may send. The three Djeme pawned cores (about 2 percent of capacity) allow illegal sends of a few days at most.
  - **The timeline is fixed. Foresight shows a moment, not a fate.**
- **The price:** money on a scale that moves 42 worlds, and the knowledge of a moment without its meaning.
- **Relationship damaged:** trust in what you see (Senetom). **Relationship only it can save:** a past self's courage.
- **Verbs: the assay hunt and the cut.** The player sweeps an assay lens through dust for forward-stained motes, slots each clip on the 40-day board, and at the end cuts the exact clips that arrived.

### Dust and lacquer (the shared stage)

Bare Bnne tissue eats motes, leaving voids scaled to the exposed area:

| Exposed area | Void radius |
|---|---|
| Hairline crack | a few cm |
| Palm-sized reading port | about 30 cm |
| Hand-span | about 40 cm |
| Residents' scrape | about 1 m |
| A bloom, or the Bnne face | about 2 m |

The human face of the Wall is lacquered so that dust can work there. **Lacquer is refined Wall sap**: the humans paint the Bnne's own blood over their living wall so that they can watch it. Scrapes are where neighbours go to be unseen.

### How the four pillars collide

The union needs a root (cloning) that only an alien language names (aliens). Proving who she is costs money, booked to her own share (AGI). Her future has already shown her the arm (time). And the AGI cannot survive a lapse, so it, too, has a stake in the shoot.

### The admitted cheat

The physics of the backward send is never explained, only its price and its limits.

## 3. The economy

- **The holok:** a claim on reserve computation. Every interstellar route needs a computed jump solution. Holding holoks is holding the right to travel and trade.
- **Sickles and vaults:**
  - Originally 27 sickles per side.
  - Each side keeps 7 armed and stripped 20, which make the 40 vaults.
  - **At lapse, each side may reclaim and rearm its 20 vaults, which wipes them.** Every coin is a sleeping gun.
- **Wall Bonds:** crown debt in holoks, maturing on renewal day. Holders: the Djeme Houses (about 60 percent), nome treasuries, Wall Quarter savers' pools and the Koiak trade-groves.
- **Incentives:**
  - **Debtors** gain from a burn: the Treasury and indebted governors (Horsi; Tkow).
  - **Creditors** lose: the Djeme Houses, savers, and the union groves through Bnne trade.
  - **Apol** is a hawk from fear, not from debt.
  - **The Koiak groves** hold bonds but gain more from a wood-route monopoly.
  - **The Oikonomos** dies in a burn.
- **Wood-routes:** jump solutions grown in living Bnne wood. They are slow (a route takes weeks to grow) but independent of the vaults. **Kyra's hedge** is 2.1 million holoks prepaid to the Koiak groves for wood-routes delivered after the lapse.
- **The Signer's Share:** the human signer's own account at the Oikonomos, dormant since W.Y. 20. Its charges are visible to Daybook and Treasury oversight.
- **Pawned cores:** three stripped sickle-cores held by the Djeme Houses as collateral, used for illegal short sends.
- **The mystery moves the market:**
  - The plume leads to the Shadow Spend, which drops futures by a sixth by D6.
  - Fares for routes arriving after the renewal triple (a war-risk premium).
  - Senetom's death starts a run on the Djeme Houses.
  - The claim going public sends Horsi's repudiation threat through the bond market.
  - The send cuts the holok by a third.
  - The permanent peace mints 14 new vaults.
  - The Hearing voids every debt signed by a second.
- **The market moves the mystery:** a signer must exist (R7), so she is hunted. The auditor is exposed by her own charges. The Treasury buys the nine seconds it wants. Kyra's prepayments, read by the Oikonomos, prove her intent.

### Intrigue: who spies on whom

- **Kyra runs Phib** under a false flag: he believes Apol's office runs him. She also runs **Maro** as her hand. She **reads the Bnne spy Hathor's** graft-messages through Phib, her interpreter, which is how Phib learns Hathor's role.
- **The Koiak groves run Hathor**, who serves as Tamoun's attendant on the Bnne side and moves her into their custody (D29).
- **The Djeme Houses run Tsia** in the Daybook.
- **Pamin watches everyone** through the dust and runs Tsia, once caught (D15), as his channel. After D28 he runs Phib as a double agent.
- **Horsi runs leverage, not agents.** He holds Phib's sister on his hulk and, from D33, the smuggled sleeper.
- **Talou and Paopi placed Ethen**: a watcher's care, which reverses trust in Ch 15.
- **Spies meet in the blind**: groves, scrapes, the bloom. The dust's blind map is the spy map.

### Court politics and succession, on screen

- **Before D31:** the king against the war party (Kyra, with Apol and Horsi).
- **After D31**, when the claim is public:
  - The king's party wants the claimant held.
  - Horsi's governors want her crowned to repudiate the bonds.
  - The Djeme Houses want her held as collateral.
  - Kyra wants her charged as the forger.
  - The Bnne union groves want her at the union.
  - The Koiak groves want her caged until the lapse.
- **Six positions, one woman, nine days.**

## 5. Naming and culture

**Naming source.** Coptic personal names of the 300s and 400s, worn down by centuries of use in space. The drift rules:
- Greek endings fall away: Apollō(s) becomes **Apol**; Kolluthos becomes Koluth.
- Final unstressed vowels drop: Senoute becomes **Tsenout** (in common speech; the royal name keeps its full form).
- Sh- and Ps- clusters survive: Pshoi, Pjol.
- Attested short forms stay as they are: Tamoun, Phib, Talou and Tsia.

Characters' names are given in their worn form wherever one exists.

**Saints' names.** Several attested names are also saints' names (Pshoi and Phib, for example), as is normal in Coptic practice. The famous saintly pair Apollo and Phib is deliberately not reproduced as a pair: the Strategos is Apol, and he is a separate story from Phib's.

**Bnne names.** Humans name each Bnne after the Coptic month in which they first met it (Thout, Paopi, Koiak, Hathor), with a numeral if repeated. "Ethen" ("near") is Tamoun's private name.

**The banks are re-sourced.** The Djeme Houses come from the contract-scribes and notaries of Djeme, a non-monastic institution. They carry no religious signifiers.

**Seeded objects in the world.**
- The lunch box is a **kibot** (from Coptic *kibôtos*, chest), a tiered lacquered box.
- Lacquer is Wall sap.
- The cup is ordinary porcelain. Its crack is left unmended.

**The rite.** Nairouz is a real feast name. Its place-setting for the dead along the Wall is **an invented rite** of this future culture, and I say so plainly. No real liturgy is depicted.
