# World, rules and factions

## The world in one paragraph

**Dugutsuchi** ("land-land") is a dry, high-tilt planet whose long year sends a band of spring, a few hundred kilometres wide, crawling from pole to pole and back. Behind the band the summer burns the plain to glassy gravel and the wind erodes it; ahead of it the winter frost is still breaking. **Ichisugu** ("market-market") is a city of 31,000 people on about 4,000 buffalo-drawn wagons and a few hundred old crawlers. It walks with the spring for ever, sleeping in shifts and trading as it goes. Its founders were two Earth peoples bound by a treaty of mutual debt: Osaka merchants, who brought shop-houses, split tallies, puppet theatre and the abacus, and a Timbuktu charter of scholars and caravaneers, who brought caravan-craft, manuscript libraries, the annual replastering of mud walls and the herders' customs. The column is a moving oasis: wells are drilled at each night camp into the thawing ground, and the founders' old stone wellheads, eroding on the route, are the only fixed places in the world.

## The column (place as class)

| Part | Who | Look and sound |
|---|---|---|
| **The Saki** (front) | The twelve founding houses, the dead who own them, the Exchange wagon | Fresh grass, cool air, indigo shop curtains (*noren*) with white house marks, the clatter of Dōjima's abacus |
| **The middle wagons** | Craft houses: puppeteers (Kanté), clone-medics (the House of Forms), pigeon post, scholars' library wagons | Lacquer, dust-veils, pigeons circling the loft wagons |
| **The Ato** (rear) | Herders (Sidibé), carters, indentured apprentices' families, the Juruntan | Grazed-out ground, dust, heat at the back of your neck. It is a day's walk from the summer front and eats the front's dust |

Position in the column is fixed by **founding share**, not by origin: both peoples have front houses (Tenmaya, Keïta-ya) and rear houses (Sidibé, Diarra). The rear's poverty is a debt structure: rear families owe hereditary apprenticeship debts to front houses, recorded on receipts. This is texture from the founding charter (founding-crime option F3, kept as texture).

## Laws and customs derived from the rules

- **"The Market stops for no one, living or dying."** Broken wagons are stripped on the move and left behind. The dying are carried. The worst punishment is **to be left**: put down on the plain with water for three days, ahead of the summer front.
- **Market day**, every sixth day: wagons are lashed into lanes and the city becomes a walking bazaar. Debts are settled by joining receipt halves. Court sits. The dead walk in their vessels and speak through the chanter. The Jurutigi trades. Once a year, the **replastering** market day renews the dead and stages the founding play.
- **Life receipts**: split at birth. The house half goes to the Exchange vault, and the body half is worn wrapped in cloth around the neck. *One does not read one's own receipt.* Clerks cut life events (births, debts, sentences, deaths) from the head side.
- **The treaty custom of names**: Japanese-founded houses give Mande given names, and Malian-founded houses give Japanese ones. Founders born on Earth have unmixed names.
- **Shadow routing**: the pigeon post and many trades plan by foreseen marks. Marriages are arranged by them, and credit is extended or refused by them.

**Admitted cheat (one).** Why the Jurutigi's bone carries marks backward is never explained. That is the premise. Every solution in the game uses only the stated rules.

### Teaching order (anomaly, test, named rule, personal use, bill)

- **Receipts.** *Anomaly* (D1): a newborn's receipt at market comes out short, with its last shadow mark close to the end. *Test* (D3): Sayo tries to cut through a shadow notch on the troupe's account receipt and her knife skids; she drops it in the cookfire and it will not burn until her master cuts the mark. *Named rule* (D4): her grandmother's saying, "A receipt remembers forward only as far back as its split." *Personal use* (D7): the court receipt shows Tenmaya's correction on D31, and Sayo plans around it. *Bill* (D14): her own receipt says "was left."
- **Bevel direction** (the ending's key): taught on D3, when Sayo cuts the troupe's pay receipt held toward herself and Jūzaburō says, "Now it says we were paid, not that we paid." It is used again on D19, when the clerk cuts Jūzaburō's "was left," and it pays off on D60.
- **Vessels.** *Anomaly* (D1): "this year the Mother rides." *Test* (D2): Sayo examines Vessel 12's feet, which she knows by touch, as a harmless comparison. *Named rule* (D2): a copy gets the body, not the life. *Personal use* (D5): Uke's calluses. *Bill* (D13): a living woman was killed as property.
- **Dōjima.** *Anomaly* (D1): the Mother praises a merchant for a deal he has not yet made. *Test* (D10): Sayo asks the chanter about an old staging and gets two different answers in two years' recordings. *Named rule* (D12): "The dead are replastered." *Personal use* (D18 onward): diffing. *Bill* (D55): the machine's reading was the foundress's error.
- **The Jurutigi.** *Anomaly* (D1, background): children lay toys at its feet and get them back wrapped. *Test* (D25): Sayo offers a spindle and a cracked cup and learns "wrapped" and "unchanged." *Named rule* (D25): it answers in what it gives back. *Personal use* (D25): ledger recall returns Uke's tether. *Bill* (D31): it breaks the day-count at sixty.

**White space on purpose:** the Kitasugu caravan, the name of Bakary's widow's second husband, what happened to the 300 cryo-buffalo that never thawed, and the eleventh founder, whose vessel line was ended in YM 90 for reasons the Nakama will not record.

**Delivery order for lore:** place first (the eroded wells, the bone arches, the Gully itself on D52, which the caravan passes again this Great Walk); then object (receipts, the staff, the astrolabe); then sound (the abacus, the chanter, Ume's herding songs); and only then short in-world text with a named, biased author (Ofusa's diary, six short entries; a Juruntan pamphlet; a clerk's marginal note).
