## 2. World rules, with limits and costs (one mechanism, one cap and one price per pillar)

### Aliens: the Bnne and graft-speech

- **What they are.** Tall, slow, mobile beings of bark and sap with grafting palms (living cambium). They have no voice and no hearing. They feel vibration through wood and sense chemistry by touch. They are long-lived, and elders take root, becoming part of a grove or wall.
- **Mechanism.** They speak by grafting slips of tissue onto each other or onto speaking wood. An utterance is alive. It **keeps growing**, so its meaning matures over days (Ethen's "rain" on day 1 grows into "rain on a friend's roof" by day 3). Editing means pruning and regrafting, which leaves a **callus**, a scar that shows where something was cut.
- **Hard limit.** A graft takes only on living, compatible tissue. A sterile or foreign slip will not bear. A sentence cannot be unsaid, only cut, and the cut shows.
- **The price (interpreters).** To read graft-speech, a human interpreter has living Bnne tissue grafted into the forearm. Green veins spread up the arm over years. A clean blade cut is safe. A **tear** causes graft shock, which kills within minutes. Interpreters are half-shunned at court ("the green-armed") and can never fully leave the Wall.
- **Relationship damaged:** human interpreters and their own society. **Relationship only it can save:** the peace itself.
- **One-sentence want:** to make the union take, because to the Bnne a graft that fails to take is the only real death, and a war is just two stocks refusing a join.
- **Consent rule:** "the stock may not graft the shoot toward itself." The Bnne may never tell a shoot what it is.
- **Verb: graft-reading and graft-writing.** The player lays the grafted forearm on wood, reads growth direction, callus colour and cut faces, and answers by choosing a slip and an angle. The grammar is small and constrained.

### AGI: the Oikonomos

- **Mechanism.** The steward of the Vault-Ring and of all surveillance dust in Shiet. It holds the reserve of stored computation, records every mote, and **answers any precisely posed, paid question exactly**. An audit rebuilds a moment as a walkable, frozen 3D scene.
- **Hard limits.** It volunteers nothing. It cannot see in bare Bnne tissue. It does not read graft-speech. It spends the reserve only on the order of both signers' bodies together, and only on a send that will arrive.
- **The price.** Every audit spends stored computation, so it literally spends money, and each charge is booked to an account that others with oversight can see. **"The lie is free; the truth costs a holok a second."** (Talou's saying; the actual rate is about a holok per second of audited record.)
- **Relationship damaged:** privacy, and with it trust between neighbours, since anyone rich can buy the past. **Relationship only it can save:** justice for the dead whose deaths were rewritten.
- **One-sentence want:** to keep the reserve sealed and whole until the signers ask. It is neither guardian nor threat; it is a bookkeeper.
- **Verb: audit.** The player phrases a constrained query ("place, time, radius"), pays, walks the reconstruction, and compares it with the Daybook's version.

### Cloning: rootstocks, seconds and the Law of the Body

- **Mechanism.** The nobility grow **rootstocks**: sleeping clones kept for organs and continuity, which age while they sleep. Some are woken as **seconds**: household doubles who run errands, sign papers and attend meetings for their originals.
- **Law of the Body.** A body and all its copies are one legal person with one name. A second's signature binds its original, and a second's crime is its original's crime. Seconds have only short given names. The crown passes to "the Ouro's body", so a dead Ouro's stocks are relics and waking one is treason.
- **Hard limit.** A copy shares the genome and nothing else: no memories, no skills. Acquired marks (scars, calluses, gait, stains, graft-veins) tell copies apart.
- **The price.** Seconds live as extensions of someone else: no name, no standing, and no one sets a place for them at Nairouz.
- **Relationship damaged:** a second and its original (Maro and Kyra). **Relationship only it can save:** the treaty, because only a body of the signer can be its shoot.
- **Verb: attribution.** The player tells copies apart in person and in dust by acquired marks and gait, including the gait of an invisible gap.

### Time travel: backward motes

- **Mechanism.** Spending stored computation pushes dust motes back in time. Each mote holds **at most nine seconds** of record from a sphere about 4 metres across, with sound.
- **Hard limits.** The cost rises with the square of the depth: an hour is cheap, a day is costly, forty days is a third of the realm's reserve. Only motes can be sent; no bodies, no objects. Under the treaty only the signers' bodies may send. **The timeline is fixed: what arrived was sent.** Foresight shows a moment, not a fate.
- **The price.** Money, at a scale that moves 42 worlds' markets, and the experience of seeing a moment of your future without its meaning.
- **Relationship damaged:** trust in what you see (Senetom dies having been warned truly). **Relationship only it can save:** a past self's courage.
- **Verbs: the assay hunt and the cut.** The player sweeps an assay lens through dust clouds to find motes with a forward time-stain, then places each clip on the 40-day board. At the end, the player cuts the exact clips that arrived.

### How the four collide

- **A question none raises alone:** the union needs a body (cloning), which only an alien language says (aliens). Proving who the body is costs money that books itself to her name (AGI). A future she has already seen tells her she will go (time).
- **The dust is the shared stage.** It cannot see Bnne tissue, so every secret act (the murder, the vine, Senetom's death, the graft) happens in the blind places, and the human world's lacquer exists to shrink them.

### The admitted cheat

One knowing cheat is spent on the premise, not on a solution: **the physics of the backward send is never explained**, only its price and limits.

## 3. The economy

- **Holok:** a claim on stored computation in the Vault-Ring. Computation is needed for everything big, above all **jump solutions** (every interstellar route must be computed in advance). Whoever holds holoks holds the right to travel and trade among 42 worlds and the Callus.
- **Sickles and vaults:** each side keeps 7 armed orbital weapons. The rest were stripped and their cores turned into vaults. **Rearming a sickle wipes its vault.** War burns money, and every coin is a sleeping gun.
- **Wall Bonds:** fifty years of crown borrowing to rebuild after the war, in holoks, maturing on renewal day. Holders: the Koinonia (about 60 percent), nome treasuries and Bnne trade-groves.
- **Incentives:** debtors (the crown's Treasury, indebted nomes) gain if the holok collapses. Creditors (the Koinonia, Bnne traders, ordinary savers in the Wall Quarter) lose. **The hawks are debtors; the doves are creditors.** The honest exception is Apollo, a hawk from fear, not debt.
- **The Body account:** the signer's own share of the reserve, dormant since W.Y. 20. Its charges are visible to those with oversight (the Daybook and the Treasury). Tamoun's first audit moves it.
- **Pawned cores:** the Koinonia hold three stripped sickle-cores as collateral against crown loans. They use them for illegal short sends, and their foresight is part of why they are rich.
- **How the mystery moves the market:** the plume implies the Shadow Spend, so holok futures fall and Treasury bonds rally. The archive fire reaches the market as a rumour. Senetom's death starts a run on the Koinonia. The climax spends a third of the reserve, and the permanent peace then lets both sides strip their armed sickles, which mints new reserve.
- **How the market moves the mystery:** a signer must exist, so Kyra and the Koinonia hunt the body. Audit charges expose the auditor. The Treasury buys selective truth (the 9-second audit of Tamoun over Senetom's body) because it can afford the part it wants.

### Intrigue: who spies on whom

- **Kyra runs Phib** under a false flag (he believes Apollo's office runs him). His reports reach her through lacquer boxes left in the Treasury's laundry. She also runs **Maro**, her second, as her hand.
- **The Koinonia run Tsia** inside the Daybook. Tsia copies Pamin's raw cuts and oversight queries to Senetom.
- **Pamin runs everyone else** through the dust. He knows Tsia is a leak by D15 and lets her run as a channel.
- **Talou and Paopi placed Ethen** beside Tamoun to watch over the shoot. This is a watcher's care, not espionage, but it reverses trust when found.
- **Spies meet in the blind:** Bnne groves, scrapes in the Wall Quarter and the bare root-collar of the union. The dust's blind map is the spies' map.
- **Turns:** Phib becomes Pamin's double agent (D28). Tsia moves from Koinonia plant to Steward-Mother. Apollo moves from hawk to the man who holds the sickles.

### Court factions and succession

- **The king's party:** Pshoi III and Pamin.
- **The war party:** Kyra, with Apollo as its honest front and the indebted nome governors of Pemdje, Psoi and Tkow.
- **The creditor party:** the Koinonia and the Wall Quarter's savers.
- **Succession under the Law of the Body:** the crown goes to "the Ouro's body". Pshoi III holds it as the dead queen's grandson only because no body of hers was known to be awake. Tamoun's valid claim is therefore stronger than his, by statute.
