# LANDUNTER: world, rules and factions

## 1. The place

**Hallig Tweewarf**, North Frisian Wadden Sea, 2047. A Hallig is a tiny unprotected island of salt meadow. Its houses stand on artificial mounds (*Warften*) because the sea covers the meadow several times every winter (*Landunter*: "land under"); then each mound is its own island. At low tide the sea drains off the mudflats for kilometres and you can walk to the next island or to the Waag, if you know the channels (*Priele*) and the hour. Sea-level rise means the state talks every year about evacuating the Halligen; 41 people still live here.

| Mound or place | What is there | Look and sound |
|---|---|---|
| **Brarenswarf** | Moiken's house (thatched, 1880s) and Keike's house, ten metres apart and nineteen years estranged | Low rooms, blue tiles, a tide clock in the kitchen, the wind in the thatch |
| **Kirchwarf** | Church with a whaling-era tower, the churchyard of speaking gravestones, the one-room school (the mother teaches three children), Marret's house with her hydrophone line running into a tide pool | Lichen-yellow stone, a church bell that rings for Landunter, tape hiss |
| **Stationswarf** | The federal tide station (1990) on stilts above an artificial mound: labs, the Schriever's core, holding rooms, scales in every corridor | White, sodium lights at night, a metronomic tide klaxon, the beep of scales |
| **Süderwarf** | Abandoned after the 1962 storm-tide; now a protected bird reserve, closed April to July; Knutt's hut and the hide where Ebba grew up | Ruined walls under nettles, tern colony shrieks, the only unwatched place on the Hallig |
| **The flats** | 1.5 km east, the Waag ("Ole Kark") in its circle of forty black posts; the Priel channels between | Grey to the horizon, sea fog, the hum on Weegnacht |
| **The Lore** | A narrow-gauge rail cart on a low causeway to the mainland harbour; the only fixed link | Rattle of rails, spray over the dam at high water |
| **Mainland** | Husum and Flensburg (police identity unit), **Haus Seegatt** (a former sanatorium near St. Peter where the Kept are held) | Rain, orange streetlight, fluorescent labs, a long white building with sealed windows |

**The key resource that creates problems rather than solving them** (Jemisin's test): *weight*. The Waag pays out only to a body of matching weight, so every use of it demands a stranger of the same mass on the other side. The state has had to weigh its agents daily for fifty years; a girl must be fed to exactly 56 kg; a man must hold 68.0 kg for two years. The Schriever knows everyone's weight from the floor sensors, which is how it sees people no camera saw. Weight is how the world measures a person, and the story's question is whether that is all a person is.

## 2. Rules per pillar: mechanism, limit, cost, relationships, verb

### Time: the Waag's exchange

- **Mechanism.** On the two Weegnacht low tides each year, a living body in the circle is exchanged with the nearest body of matching weight that stood in the circle on the same night nineteen years earlier.
- **Memorable limit.** "Weight for a weight. Nineteen years. Once." (Knutt's three-sentence version, Act I.) Exactly nineteen years: the moon sits where it sat. Within two kilograms. A crossed body can never cross again.
- **Visible cost.** Someone in the past is taken forward against the pattern of their life; whoever comes forward loses nineteen years in a second; whoever goes back can never return.
- **Relationship it damages.** Inken and her daughter: nineteen years gone in one tide.
- **Relationship only it can save.** The same two, who meet as near-equals (Inken 18, Moiken 19); and Swantje and her brother.
- **Teaching order (anomaly → test → rule → personal use → bill).** A young ewe with a 2028 ear tag in the fold (Act I); a crab in a bucket at the ring's edge on the March Weegnacht (Act I); Knutt's three sentences (Act I); Moiken's banded bird to her mother in 2028 (Act I, read in Act II); the last crossing (Act IV).
- **Verbs it alone provides.** *Pair* (lay two nights nineteen years apart side by side and match who vanished from one with who appeared in the other); *send* (live use of the ring at low stakes, then at the highest).

### Cloning: no shortcuts

- **Mechanism.** Somatic-cell cloning abroad; copies have German personhood since 2041.
- **Memorable limit.** "A copy is a baby." No accelerated growth, no memory transfer. Same genome, different body history: fingerprints, scars, teeth, tattoos.
- **Visible cost.** A copy owns no past; the people who loved the original see a stranger in the right face. Kerrin's mother still compares her daughter's fingerprints with the dead girl's.
- **Relationship it damages.** Keike and the woman who came home in her daughter's body.
- **Relationship only it can save.** Moiken had a mother at all.
- **Teaching order.** Kerrin shows Moiken two sets of prints in a family album (Act I); Moiken matches a cup print to Inken's 2027 ID biometrics (Act I); the tattoo comparison (Act I); the mother's pregnancy in 2029 in the archive (Act II); Ebba (midpoint).
- **Verb it alone provides.** *Attribute* by body history: in photographs, recordings and flesh, decide who is who when DNA cannot say.

### AGI: the Schriever

- **Mechanism.** An archive mind that witnesses the island through cameras, microphones, hydrophones and load sensors, and renders any recorded moment as a walkable reconstruction.
- **Memorable limit.** "It can't lie. It can only look away." Append-only record; answers only what is asked; volunteers nothing; old versions answer as of their date.
- **Visible cost.** Nobody on the Hallig has been unwatched since 2016, and everything it sees belongs to the state.
- **Relationship it damages.** The Schriever and Inken: she asked it to look away, and it did, and she was gone.
- **Relationship only it can save.** Ebba, whom its fork raised.
- **Teaching order.** The 41-minute black square (prologue and Act I); finding who took Moiken's bicycle in a reconstruction (Act I); booting the 2028 version, which answers differently from the 2047 one (Act I); deep depositions of Inken's 2028 (Act II); the Nursery fork's death (Act III, the bill).
- **Verbs it alone provides.** *Depose* (cross-examine its archived versions and find where their answers diverge); *weigh the past* (replay a scene as load data, which shows people no camera saw).

### Alien: the Waag as a living thing

- **Mechanism.** A time-thick organism whose crossings seed spores in the bodies that cross.
- **Memorable limit.** "The crossed shine" (under UV, and visibly in the dark before it fruits); "the pitch is the weight" (each crossing hums at a pitch set by the weight).
- **Visible cost.** It is dying; the Silence is its old age, and its children will grow, centuries from now, wherever crossers lie in wet ground.
- **Relationship it damages.** The islanders and their own history (the commanders' glory was trafficking).
- **Relationship only it can save.** Marret and her drowned uncle: her lifetime of tapes is the only record of every crossing, and its last chord is the bell she listened for.
- **Teaching order.** The hum on a tape Marret plays for Moiken (Act I); the crab glowing under Knutt's torch (Act I); Moiken's mother's hand glowing (Act I end); matching pitches to weights on the 2028 tape (Act II); the Shining in the churchyard (Act III); the fruiting (Act IV).
- **Verbs it alone provides.** *Listen* (read pitch as weight and count crossings in audio); *read the glow* (see who has crossed, alive or dead).

### The admitted cheat

How a living thing can be nineteen years thick is never explained. That is the premise's one unexplained physics, spent on the premise and never on a solution. Everything that solves anything runs on the rules above, all taught before use.

**White space on purpose.** Named and never shown: the drowned parish of Wardum; the Odense clinic; the asylum at Schleswig; the Husum Memoranda; the 2009 Bote; the other two commander houses; whatever Arfst Ipsen and Nommensen's father said to each other in 1990; what the Republic did with its foresight; the harbour in the Elbe where the hum is heard.

**Delivery order for lore** (report principle 13): place first (the churchyard, Süderwarf's nettled ruins, the white station on stilts), then object (the ear-tagged ewe, the Schuldbuch, the engraved watch, the bird band, the hydrophone), then sound (the hum, the tide lullaby, Low German proverbs), and only then short in-world text with a biased author (a gravestone that boasts; a pastor's sermon that justifies; a ministry memo that euphemises; a charter sentence written by one honest man).
