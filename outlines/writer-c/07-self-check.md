## 2. Banned list (round-1 template)

| Banned element | Status |

| Coastal, sea, tidal, mudflat, island, fjord, sea cave; northern-European setting; Norse, Germanic, Frisian, Dutch or Scandinavian names | **Pass.** A dry inland planet; Japanese and Mande names. I deliberately rejected a "dawn-melt mud band" version of the moving oasis as a mudflat in disguise, and avoided sea words (I wrote "caravan" and "column," never "flotilla"). |
| Drowning or a body in water as the inciting death | **Pass.** A strangling by a puppeted body. Bakary's death is a fall into a dry ravine. |

| Alien communicating by sound, song, echo or the voices of the dead | **Pass.** It communicates only by transformed gifts. *Note:* the **dead** speak through a human chanter voicing the AGI. That is the AGI pillar, not the alien, and it is a human voice. |
| Alien buried, stranded or bound beneath a small community that lives off it, freed or answered at the end | **Partial (logged honestly).** It is not buried, bound or beneath anyone: it walks openly in the middle of the column, by its own choice. The caravan does not depend on it to survive; the receipts are a means of rule. But its want **is** met at the end by a human choice. Differences from the banned shape: it was never captive; the community's crime was misreading and concealment, not exploitation; it dies at the end (it is not freed); and its want is company, not release. I kept it because company at a death is the story's answer to its central question; I rejected option F2 (stolen bone) because that *would* have been the banned shape. |
| Small isolated village or island as the main setting | **Pass.** A walking city of 31,000. |
| Family reunion across time as the emotional core | **Pass.** No one meets a younger relative. The only message to the past that matters emotionally is a forgiveness reaching a dead friend (not family) before she died, and they never meet. |

## 5. Trope review

| Trope match | Decision |
|---|---|
| AI uprising or sudden turn | **Avoided.** Dōjima's want never changes; its D55 change is in its reading, and the conflict was always humans obeying it. |
| "AI was good all along" | **Avoided.** It ordered a killing; its last act is to withdraw the dead, not to be redeemed. |
| Machine god as final boss | **Avoided.** There is no fight with Dōjima; the climax is a choice on a plain. |
| "You are the clone" | **Avoided.** The anchor is not a clone. The one copy-identity reveal (Uke) is made early (D9) and is about the victim. |
| Which one is real / disposable clone | **Reviewed, kept in a changed form.** Vessels are treated as disposable by the world; the story's whole first act refutes that. |
| Hero caused the loop | **Kept, with care.** Sayo (or Nana) cutting the Gully mark makes the truth-teller a cause of the founding murder. The report advises revealing the cause early. I reveal that the mark is still uncut on D52, eight days before it is cut, so the player carries it as dread rather than receiving it as a last-minute twist. The loop's origin is human on both ends (Ofusa's fear, and the present's wish to record the truth). |
| Fixing the past makes it worse / reset | **Avoided.** Nothing is fixed or reset; marks only reveal. |
| Self-erasure or self-sacrifice to close the loop | **Avoided deliberately.** The price looks like a sacrifice for most of the game, and the solution is that it never was. |
| Investigator is the culprit | **Touched lightly.** Sayo becomes a cause of a 211-year-old crime, not of the case she investigates. Logged. |
| Fated death / you can't escape fate (Steins;Gate's technique) | **Technique borrowed, content not.** The loophole asks whether she ever saw her death, or only a mark that made her believe it. Our mark is a bevel and a gift-word, not a world-line. |
| Narrator who withholds | **Avoided.** Seydou's secret is shown to the player first. |
| Mentor's death | **Reviewed.** Jūzaburō is left rather than killed, and he is the killer, not a saint; his ending is open texture. |
| Chosen one by bloodline | **Partial,** see the coincidence note above. |
| Secret organisation behind everything | **Avoided.** The front houses act from visible incentives; the machine acts from a published house code. |
| Thesis speech | **Avoided.** |
| Ancient aliens built civilisation | **Avoided.** The creditor had nothing to do with human origins; its kind's history is texture. |
| Generic names | **Avoided** (section 4). |
