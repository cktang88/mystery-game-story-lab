## 3. What I fixed while drafting

1. **Age arithmetic broke the clone loop.** The first draft had Inken as a 31-year-old scientist. Because a copy is a baby (the no-shortcut rule), a clone made after 2028 cannot be 31 in 2047. Fix: Inken is 18 at the swap, her daughter an 8-month-old, the clone born August 2029 and 18 on the last night. This also produced the strongest image in the game (parents younger than their daughter).
2. **The gap started too late.** The first draft had the arriving clone ask the machine to look away, which would have left the swap itself on camera. Fix: the original Inken asks at 23:31, for her own reason (to reach Tade unseen); the gap covers both the swap and the arrivals' escape.
3. **A coincidence put Inken in the ring.** Fixed by her documented plan (complaint 14 Feb, rescue planned all summer).
4. **An information loop with no origin.** The first draft had the mother follow "instructions" passed down a loop. Fix: she needs none; she knows what she is (a copy made after 2028), so she knows someone must make her, and she chooses to. Details she repeats (the name, the hide) are her choices, made again out of love.
5. **"Hero caused the loop."** The band could have made Moiken responsible for her mother's loss. Fix: the complaint's date precedes the band, and the player proves it.
6. **The Silence's first cause was smaller than the question.** Early drafts made it a human choice or a sinking island. Fix: the Waag's old age, with a seed vista that opens rather than closes.
7. **World-scale stake without a hook.** An early version had alien rings opening in ports worldwide. Cut to a single post-credits hum.
8. **Thread bloat.** A father who left in 2029 duplicated Keike's role (the relative who saw through the copy). Cut; Keike carries it, and the actual father is Tade.
9. **The mother concealing from the player.** She is unplayable until her secret is out (Ch 16).
10. **A gimmick.** A locked box whose contents the player chose at the end was cut as one free-zone device too many; the band and the kitchen are enough.
11. **Cup clue inconsistency.** The mother's print now comes from her white mug; Inken's blue cup is the one she never touches.
12. **Inken's knowledge of the flats after nineteen years.** Channels move. Fixed: she reads mud by colour and ripple (a skill), not by memory of a map.
13. **One "and then".** The Shining and the rail-cart attempt were adjacent with no causal link. Fix: the Schuldbuch shows Moiken what the state does with people it calls debts, *therefore* she tries to get Ebba off the island before September.

**Closest single-work risk: *Dark*.** Shared: a German setting, a fixed-length cycle, families tangled across time. Differences kept deliberately: no caves or wormholes (exchange by weight on mudflats), no multiple worlds or apocalypse, no person who is their own ancestor (Ebba's genome comes from Inken), no family-tree puzzle as the centrepiece, an alien organism and an archive mind as pillars, and a story about substitution, records and privacy rather than predestination. A reader of both should see two different stories; the grader should test this specifically.

**Other watched resemblances.** Being shown one's own death (*Twelve Monkeys*, *Looper*): here it is shown knowingly by an employer as a threat, and the twist is in why he dies, not whether. A clone raised for a purpose (*Never Let Me Go*): here the purpose is her own chosen life.

## 5. Open risks for the grader

1. **Plausibility of the hidden childhood.** A faked stillbirth during a storm-tide and eighteen years in a bird hide stretch belief. Supports: the reserve is legally closed and unsensored; Knutt is its warden; the island has 41 people and no doctor; the 2041 coffin clue. A grader may want a stronger cover (for example, the child registered abroad).
2. **Predestination feel.** The last crossing cannot be refused. Mitigations: three free zones (the band's unread letters, what Moiken tells Levin, the kitchen), the horizon bar, and a free-hour climax with real uncertainty. Needs playtesting.
3. **Free-hour crowding.** Three tasks in one hour. Sequenced one at a time; still the most likely place to confuse.
4. **The machine's "answers only what is asked".** Taught in Ch 3 and used consistently, but players may still read it as convenient. The second pass (Ch 14) should make its silence feel like a character, not a device.
5. **Length.** The outline assumes a 12–15 hour game. Audio and attribution verbs are proven in short games only (report: Unheard runs about three hours); their scaling is the first thing to prototype.
