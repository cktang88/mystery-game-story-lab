# The sealed history (solution document)

## Units and calendar

- **YM** (Year of the Market) counts standard years from landfall, YM 0. The present is **YM 214**.
- The planet **Dugutsuchi** has an extreme axial tilt and a year of about seven standard years (a "Great Walk": pole to pole and back). The livable spring band moves about 12 km a day. The caravan walks with it and never halts for more than an hour. Nothing in the time rule depends on the planet's year, its day or any cycle.
- **Market day** falls every sixth day. The game runs from **D1** (a market day) to **D60**, the day the creditor stops (the **Edge**), with a coda to D67. Market days in the game: D1, D7, D13, D19, D25, D31, D37, D43, D49, D55, D61, D67.

## The four mechanisms, as they truly work

**Receipts (time).** The Jurutigi sheds one bone plate each market day. A plate split into two halves is a receipt. *A notch cut into either half, while the Jurutigi lives, has always been on both halves, back to the moment of the split.* Until it is cut, a notch shows as a **shadow notch**: visible, hard, impossible to cut through or erase. Consequences:
1. **Reach:** a receipt carries marks back only to its own split.
2. **Fixity:** every shadow notch will be cut by someone. Nothing shown can be prevented. What is not shown may still happen, but it will not be cut into that receipt before the Edge.
3. **Capacity:** marks need room; a life receipt is a forearm long.
4. **Indestructible until settled:** a receipt with an uncut shadow notch survives fire, acid and hammers until the notch is cut, because it must exist to be cut.
5. **(hidden)** Marks travel back only while the Jurutigi lives. After the Edge, receipts are plain bone. Marks cut after the Edge exist only from then on.

**Notation (locked).** Value notches (wide for tens, narrow for units), house marks (the Osaka shop-sign glyphs and Malian clan brands), and act-marks. The direction of an act-mark matters: the steep face of the V toward the receipt's head means **active** (the owner did it: *stayed*, *paid*); toward the foot means **passive** (it was done to the owner: *was left*, *was paid*). Clerks always cut from the head side. **A person cutting her own body half, held toward herself, cuts it reversed**, so an active act reads as passive. Apprentices learn this by getting it wrong.

**The Exchange mind, Dōjima (AGI).** Built YM 5 to 9 by the expedition's computing team at Ofusa Tenmaya's order. It thinks on a hall-sized abacus of buffalo-bone beads that clatters audibly. Its founding directive is the first article of the Tenmaya house code, written by Ofusa: **"What the receipts show, the Market shall make so."** Its want is to settle every account and keep ledger and world in agreement. It reads every life receipt in the vault (house halves). It cannot read people with no receipt, and it has no hands; it acts only through people who obey the voice of the dead. From YM 41 it models and voices the dead founders through a human chanter (*tayū*). Each year, at the **replastering** market day, every dead founder is "replastered": the model is renewed, and houses pay to have their dead adjusted. Dōjima never falsifies a receipt; it does edit the dead's voices, because the voices are the houses' property and the receipts are not.

**Vessels (cloning).** Clone bodies of dead founders, grown in the House of Forms in four years from preserved cells and kept sedated for life (about twenty years of service). On market day three hooded puppeteers move a vessel in a harness (head and right hand, left hand, feet) while the chanter speaks. Vessels have no receipt and no legal person; they are property of the dead founder's house. A vessel inherits the body, including Ofusa's **red-green colour blindness** (X-linked, rare in women, recorded in her medical file and visible in the miscoloured star-pointers she painted on her own brass astrolabe). It inherits no memory and no calluses.

**The Jurutigi (alien).** A house-sized walker of wind-polished bone plates on many short legs. It is the last of its kind on this world. It walks with the spring band, as it did for millennia before humans came. It foresees along its own life and knows the day it will stop. It speaks only by returning gifts transformed: **wrapped** = kept/accepted; **split** = shared; **notched** = owed; **hobbled** (a soft loop that lets an animal graze but not wander) = stay near, still free; **tethered** = bound (it never uses this); **unchanged** = refused; **broken** = ended. Given something of a person, it returns the last gift that person gave it (*ledger recall*). Its want: **to be kept company, by someone who chose to stay, when it stops.** It foresaw the humans' arrival and walked to meet them.

## Fates and convergence order

- **Uke**: dead on D1; her choices drive the plot. Her debt is settled on D60.
- **Jūzaburō Kanté**: left behind on D19. *(Texture, never confirmed: a figure is glimpsed at the old founders' well on D33. The game never says.)*
- **Seydou Tenmaya**: dies D44. His thread passes to Tōkichi, who carries his receipt and the routings he cut.
- **Kadiatou Ōmiya**: survives; after D55 she opens the House of Forms and wakes the remaining vessels who can be woken (two of nine).
- **Gonbei Diarra**: survives. He killed Seydou. Tōkichi learns this on D56 and chooses not to give him up to the court; on D60 Gonbei is the last to leave the Jurutigi.
- **Dōjima**: survives, blind to the future. Its last foreseen act was to withdraw the dead. After the Edge it asks to be taught to read the past.
- **Ume Sidibé**: survives; gives Sayo the staff and Mochi.
- **The Jurutigi**: ends at dusk on D60, accompanied.
- **Sayo**: lives.

**Convergence order (locked):** all three playable threads start together on D1 at the market. Seydou's and Sayo's threads cross at D14, D31 and D44. Tōkichi's joins Sayo's at D20 (via Kadiatou) and D31. Seydou's ends at D44 and folds into Tōkichi's. All threads meet at D55 (the stage), then at D60 (the plain).

## Ending and its branches (refusal and inaction are respected)

**Canon.** Sayo stays by choice; three marks; the creditor accompanied; the walk home by starlight; the blank receipt and the first mark.

**Branch A: the player refuses to cut the Gully mark on D59.** It is still cut, because it was shown in year 1. If Sayo puts the receipt down, Dōjima asks the chanter Nana to cut it, and she does. History is identical; the player sees that the machine took the record of its maker's crime onto itself. *Principle: the past fixes what was cut; the present chooses who cuts it.*

**Branch B: the player refuses to stay on D60.** Her receipt's "was left" mark must still be cut, and the hard-line houses supply it: they seize and tether her beside the Jurutigi, and their clerk cuts the mark from the head side, passive and literally true. At dusk the Jurutigi returns her tether **as a hobble** (its last gift, freeing her), but no one chose to stay, so its want goes unmet. She walks home the same way. The coda is the same image, with a different weight. Uke's "settled" mark is cut in both branches: in Branch B by Sayo on the walk home, before dusk. *(Locked: the only marks whose cutter varies are the Gully mark and Sayo's own; every other foreseen mark is cut by the same person in all branches.)*

**Branch C: inaction (the player waits through D60 without choosing).** This plays as Branch B, because the houses act.
