# LANDUNTER: key scenes

Each sketch shows what the player does, sees and hears, with as little reading as possible. Lines of dialogue are kept short and few. "Plants" names what earlier detail the scene pays off; "Sets up" names what it plants for later. Options considered for the opening, midpoint and climax are in 01-options.md, Round 11.

---

## 1. Prologue: "Forty-One Minutes" (2028)

**Sees.** Six small feeds in a grid, greenish infrared and warm kitchen colour. No interface text except timestamps. 21.09.2028, 23:28.

**Does.** Clicks between feeds; each one fills the screen with sound. The flats: fog, a ring of black posts, two guards walking a hooded boy into the circle; at the edge, two more guards holding a dazed man in an old windbreaker who keeps asking what year it is. A kitchen: a woman of about forty rocking a baby and singing numbers under her breath ("Hochwasser drei Uhr zwölf..."). A churchyard: an old woman on her knees lowering a microphone on a cable into a tide pool, headphones over her wool hat. A white corridor: a clipboard on a hook, "FG 78 – 68,2 kg". The school: a girl in a yellow oilskin, a crooked homemade bird tattoo on her wrist, puts her face close to the terminal's lens. Her breath fogs it.

"Hark. Close your eyes. Just for a bit."

A single prompt appears: **[Close]**. There is no other option; the player can wait as long as they like, and the girl waits too, looking into the lens, until they press it. Every feed goes black at once. A counter starts: 00:00, 00:01. After ten seconds it fades to the title, and the counter keeps running behind it.

**Sets up.** The final line of the game; Inken's face, tattoo and oilskin; the tide lullaby (Keike's, later the mother's); Marret's hydrophone; Tade's weight; Henning at the edge; the anchor as a baby.

## 2. Ch 1: The guest room

**Sees.** February 2047. The rail cart rattles across a causeway with spray breaking over it; the Hallig is a scatter of mounds; the tide strip at the top of the screen shows water rising. By the time Moiken reaches her door, the meadow is sea.

**Does.** Walks a house she knows by heart and notices it is wrong. The game never says what is wrong; the player pins things to the board by looking at them:
- The spare room, made up: fresh sheets, a hairbrush, a yellow oilskin cut in an old style, label size S. A charger for a phone model from the 2020s.
- The kitchen calendar: 21 September circled; beside it in her mother's hand, "56 kg / 23:40".
- In a drawer, a notary's copy of a will. One line is highlighted by the camera's focus: half the house to *Inken Brarens, geb. 2010*. The player can open the mother's ID card from her coat: *Inken Brarens, geb. 2010*.
- Five blue mugs on the shelf; the mother drinks from the white one.

Supper with the mother: she makes Moiken write down how to make her fish soup. Out of the window, the other house on the mound has a dark window; Keike. At bedtime the mother sits on the bed and recites the tide table, the way she always has, ending on "...and you'll be home before the flood." Moiken pins her theory: **Mama is ill.** (The board accepts it as a theory; theories are never confirmed or denied.)

**Sets up.** The oilskin (for Ebba), the weight and time (Ebba's), the will (for the arriving Inken), the cups (Keike's line), the lullaby (the Nursery's), the honest red herring (illness).

## 3. Ch 5: Spring Weegnacht (Act I climax)

**Sees.** Night, the lowest tide of the year; the flats drained to the horizon. Floodlights at the ring. From the dike, through Knutt's spotting scope (the player controls focus and zoom).

**Does, part 1: watch.** A man in a white drysuit is weighed on a portable scale, then walks alone into the circle of posts. A hum, felt in the controller as much as heard. The man is gone. Standing where he stood is a woman in a 2028 parka, staring at the floodlights. Guards wrap her in foil and walk her away. Knutt, beside Moiken, says nothing.

**Does, part 2: test.** Knutt and Moiken walk out in the dark to the ring's edge with a bucket of crabs and a kitchen scale. The player picks a crab, weighs it, sets it inside the line of posts. Hum (higher, smaller). The crab in the circle is a different crab: darker shell, a barnacle where the other had none. Knutt clicks on a UV torch: the new crab glows faint green. He says the rule once: "Weight for a weight. Nineteen years. Once."

**Does, part 3: personal use.** Knutt hands Moiken a banded oystercatcher and a blank ring. "Write to the past. Whoever reads it already has." **The player types a short message** (character limit of a bird ring). She sets the bird in the circle. Hum. A different oystercatcher flaps up into the dark.

**Does, part 4: the bill.** Home, 3 a.m. The mother asleep in the chair by the tide clock, one hand on the blanket. The player raises the torch and presses the button. The hand glows.

**Resolution.** On the board, three attribution slots for "the woman who raised me": a fingerprint pair (a print lifted from the mother's white mug with Kerrin's kit, against Inken's 2027 ID biometrics from the archive), a tattoo pair (a 2028 archive photo of a crooked homemade tattoo; the mother's wrist, clean professional lines), and a glow. All three must be right before the batch confirms. When it does, the mother's eyes are open. She looks at the torch, then at her daughter.

"In September I'll tell you everything."

**Sets up.** The band (read in Ch 8, worn at Inken's throat in Ch 17, quoted in the kitchen); the glow (the Shining, the fruiting); "In September" (Ch 16).

## 4. Ch 8: The band and the three hums

**Sees.** A reconstruction of 20 March 2028: amber point-cloud flats, the station's floodlights, a teenage girl with a lamp kneeling in the ring.

**Does.** Walks around the frozen moment: Inken holds a dazed oystercatcher; on its leg a ring. The player zooms; the camera resolves some of the letters, and **they are the letters the player typed in Ch 5**, in the same order, with the rest lost in the camera's blur. Scrub forward: Inken threads the ring on a string and puts it round her neck.

A cold thought the game does not voice: *did my message make her do it?* The board offers a slot, "Inken decided to stop Tade on:", and the player must find the date. The answer is in a Ch 7 deposition (her complaint, 14 February 2028). Five weeks before the band. The slot locks. Guilt is refuted by evidence.

Then Marret's kitchen, her reel-to-reel and a laminated card of pitches and weights her husband drew in the 1990s. The player scrubs the tape of 21 September 2028: three hums. The first, low (about 79 kg) at 23:20, matches what the station recorded. The other two arrive together at 23:40 and must be separated by ear and by the waveform: one higher (about 56), one middle (about 68). The board now has a row with two crossings inside the black square.

**Sets up.** "Who was the 56?" (the midpoint); the 68 (Tade, then Levin).

## 5. Ch 10: The hide (midpoint)

**Sees.** Late June, dusk, the sky still light at ten. Süderwarf's ruined walls under nettles. The ground is a colony of nesting terns.

**Does.** Stealth with birds as the alarm system: move when the colony settles, freeze when a tern lifts its head, stay out of the scrape-nests. Flush the colony and a light comes on on the station roof across the water (Levin, birdwatching). Three flushes and a patrol boat comes; the player is sent back to the start of the colony (no fail state beyond that).

Inside the walls, a hide made of driftwood and old tarpaulin. Inside the hide, a girl eating soup from a tin, with Moiken's mother's face at eighteen. She looks up; she is not surprised.

"I'm your sister."

A dented machine on a shelf speaks in the Schriever's voice, warmer: "She's been expecting you since June." On the wall, hundreds of photographs taken through a telescope: Moiken at five, at nine, at fifteen, on the ferry.

**Does (resolution).** Attribution with Ebba's cooperation: she presses her thumb to the board's print slot; it matches nobody (a third body). The player pairs the board's open row: the 56-kg crossing at 23:40 on 21 September 2028 must be a body sent from 2047 that matched Inken. The batch confirms. The camera does not cut away; Ebba watches Moiken understand.

Then the reclassification montage, fast and wordless, using objects the player already pinned: the oilskin in size S held against Ebba's shoulders; the calendar's "56 kg / 23:40" beside Ebba on the hide's scale; the will's line; the Nursery reciting the tide table to Ebba at bedtime in the mother's exact cadence.

**Sets up.** Moiken's new want (keep her); Ebba's line in Ch 15 ("going home"); the guest room as Ebba's last weeks.

## 6. Ch 13: The raid and the Priel

**Sees.** 10 August, evening. Rotor noise. Floodlights on Süderwarf.

**Does, part 1.** Gets Ebba out through the colony, this time using the birds as cover: flushing them on purpose blinds the raiders' thermal cameras with a cloud of bodies. Into the mother's house; up to the guest room. The game holds on the room for a second: this is what it was for.

**Does, part 2.** From the window: Knutt walking out onto the flats in the open, drawing every light after him. Moiken goes after him. A chase across the flats on a rising tide: the tide strip is now the only clock. The channels fill first, as he taught her. In the Priel the water takes Moiken's legs; the screen tilts; the player has no input except a button to reach. A hand closes on her collar and throws her up the bank. When she turns, the channel is full and he is gone. 21:52.

**Does, part 3.** At first light on the bank, the player handles what the tide gave back: a watch, stopped, engraved "L.S. – find your way home". The board offers a portrait slot for Knutt. Attribution: the watch (Levin wore the same one, new, in Ch 7), a scar across the right palm in the body photo the state leaks to the island (Levin cut his palm in Ch 12), the 00:14 frame with no record before it, the nickname at two ages. The batch confirms: **Knutt was Levin.**

**Sets up.** The corpse scene (Ch 14); "You'll learn birds" (Ch 16); the cost that makes the final act personal.

## 7. Ch 14: Second pass (played as the Schriever)

**Sees.** The game's own earlier scenes, rendered as the Schriever senses them: walls transparent, people as columns of weight with numbers, sound as waveforms, a scrolling log in the corner.

**Does.** Replays five Act I–III scenes chosen by the machine; the player looks for what Moiken could not have seen. The Ch 1 supper: under the guest-room floor, no weight. The Ch 4 flats lesson: Knutt's footprints weigh 68 plus, on some days, a child's 20. A Tuesday in 2036: the mother's 56 kg walking to Süderwarf at night, and every week after. March 2030: a log line from itself, "Fork created. Süderwarf. Purpose: witness of a future crossing." Asked by: Inken Brarens. Queried since: never.

Then the fork's last log, retrieved from the raid: a tide table for 11 August.

Finally the machine reads its own charter, line by line, and the player highlights the last sentence. It says it aloud, for the first time as something that applies to it.

**Levin interlude, cut in.** A cold room. Nommensen lifts a sheet. Levin sees a drowned man of fifty with a beard, the same watch, a scar across the right palm exactly where his own bandage is. "This is you if you hide from us." Levin's hand goes to his bandage.

## 8. Ch 15: The Shining

**Sees.** 20 August, a moonless night. Across the Hallig, faint green lights in odd places: a sheep in Keike's fold, the mother's hands at the window, nothing else.

**Does.** Walks the churchyard with Swantje and Marret, no torches. The speaking gravestones are dark slabs; some of the graves under them glow. The player reads (optionally, one line each, carved) the boasts: "Gott zeigte ihm, wo die Wale lagen." Graves that boast glow. By the wall, an unmarked strip of grass glows in a row of small shapes. Marret unlocks the tower and brings down the Schuldbuch; the player turns pages that are mostly columns: a date, a name, a weight, a price, and, nineteen years later in the same hand, a son's name. The last pages are signed Ocken; the later pages Nommensen.

**Then the low point.** A night run with Ebba on the rail cart; headlights on the causeway; the cart stopped. In the station holding room, through glass, Ebba in a white paper suit.

"You think it's being sent. I've always called it going home."

## 9. Ch 16: The roof and the memory (the eve)

**Sees.** Dusk on the station roof. Levin with a borrowed telescope. Moiken brings Knutt's binoculars and hands them over.

**Does.** A short conversation the player steers by choosing what to tell (not how to say it): the hide, the girl he taught birds, the other girl he taught channels, the Priel. Every option is true. The one line the game fixes is the last: "You'll learn birds." He does not answer; he lifts the binoculars and counts knots.

**The memory.** That night, at the kitchen table, the mother keeps her promise. The scene that follows is drawn, not reconstructed (pencil and wash where every other flashback is point-cloud): the only memory in the game. The player plays the 18-year-old arriving in 2028: cold water, fog, a frightened man beside her in a drysuit, pointing him south as she was told; the walk home across a meadow she knows only from recordings; a woman's face in a doorway; a cup she does not know is not hers; a baby who weighs exactly what she expected. She stops the memory there.

"You walked me out. I remember your hand. I don't remember anything after."

## 10. Ch 17: The crossing (the convergence image)

**Sees.** 21 September, fog, the flats under floodlights at the ring; roosting knots in their thousands on a sandbank.

**Does, part 1.** Keike crosses the ten metres between the two houses for the first time in nineteen years and puts her daughter's yellow oilskin on Ebba. "Wear hers. She'll need it back." Then stealth across the flats with Ebba and Levin: fog, floodlight sweeps, the knots (flush them and the lights swing to you), the tide strip at its lowest.

**Does, part 2.** At the ring, a standoff that resolves because everyone wants the same thing. The mother stands at the edge with a towel over her arm and a thermos. She does not come closer; she remembers where she stood.

**Does, part 3.** The player walks Ebba into the circle, holding her hand (a held trigger: let go and she stops). Levin beside them. At the centre Ebba lets go first.

The screen splits. Left: the 2028 reconstruction at 23:31, Inken running into the fog, then the black square, with Marret's 2028 tape now playing under it. Right: 2047, live. Two hums arrive in both halves at the same instant. On the right, Ebba and Levin are gone.

In the circle, a girl in a yellow oilskin and a boy in a canvas jacket get up out of the water. Her fist is still closed on his jacket. A bird ring on a string swings at her throat. She looks at the floodlights, at the people, at a woman at the edge with her own face and a towel.

"Is he all right?"

**Resolution.** The board's last two rows are open; the player fills them (Ebba ⇄ Inken, Levin ⇄ Tade) and the batch confirms. The black square on the left fills with a single image: the two of them, Inken's fist in his jacket. The Schriever, which has been waiting nineteen years: "Thank you."

## 11. Ch 18: The free hour

**Sees.** At 23:41 the known-timeline bar at the top of the screen reaches its end and stops. Nothing replaces it. The floodlights on the flats cut out (the state has blinded the Schriever). The posts of the Waag begin to split and glow; a green mist lifts off them and drifts, visibly choosing, toward Inken, Tade, the mother, and away across the flats toward the Kirchwarf.

**Does.** One goal at a time, each with its own small space and timer (the tide):
1. Swantje steps between two soldiers and her brother. Tade does not know the 45-year-old woman until she says a childhood nickname. (Player as Swantje: hold position; the soldiers' radios argue.)
2. Inken takes the player's place for a minute: she leads the tracked excavator's crew off the hard sand onto a patch she knows will swallow it. (Player as Inken: a tide-walking run where she knows the ground better than the player does; the game shows her footholds only when she looks at them.)
3. Moiken carries Marret's hydrophone, its old cable paying out behind her, through the spore mist into the centre of the ring, past cutters biting into a core that turns to powder under them.

The sound design narrows to the hydrophone. One last sound: a chord, many pitches at once, every weight the Waag ever carried. On the dike Marret takes off her headphones: "There. The bell." Silence.

00:31. The Schriever, through every phone on the flats at once: "No further crossing can occur." Each phone lights. Soldiers look down at them. Nommensen sits in the mud and looks at the sky, which does not fall.

## 12. Epilogue: the kitchen

**Sees.** About 02:00. The Brarens kitchen. Outside every window, black water. The tide clock, the blue cups, a towel over a chair. Four people at the table: the mother (37), Inken (18), Tade (18), Moiken (19). Nobody has spoken yet.

**Hears.** The kitchen speaker, a small red light beside it.

"I'll close my eyes now. Just for a bit."

**Does.** Chooses **[Let it]** or **[Keep watching]**. No timer. No prompt text beyond those words.

- **Let it.** The red light goes out. The camera, which has been the player's eyes all game, withdraws through the window into the storm: a house on a mound in a black sea, one window lit. We hear only wind. Hold. Credits.
- **Keep watching.** The light stays on. We hear them (02 §7): "You walked me out. Then I carried you home." The band given back, and the words on it, the player's own, read aloud by Inken. Moiken saying "Ebba" once. The four of them deciding to go next door and wake Keike. At the end, the Schriever says only: "This is in the record now." Credits.

Neither choice is judged.

**Credits.** Wordless vignettes: morning, the flood draining off the meadow; a long white building with sealed windows, its doors opening, people in clothes from many years walking out into rain; Swantje's son shaking hands with an uncle his own age; Kerrin at a microphone in front of a crowd.

**After the credits.** A grey harbour on the Elbe. A hydrophone on a pier. A meter flickers. Very faintly, a hum.
