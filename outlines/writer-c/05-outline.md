# LANDUNTER: outline

## 1. One sentence

A daughter must send her own mother, aged eighteen, through a tide that trades lives.

## 2. One paragraph (master plot: setup, three disasters, ending)

On a Hallig in the North Sea, where twice a year an alien ring on the mudflats trades a living body for one of equal weight from exactly nineteen years before, nineteen-year-old Moiken comes home to find her mother preparing a room for someone and saying goodbye to everything. **Disaster one:** by fingerprints, a tattoo and a glow that only crossers carry, Moiken proves that the woman who raised her is a copy of her real mother, Inken, who vanished on the flats in 2028, and that the copy came through the ring from the future. Hunting for the real Inken with the help of the station director, she reconstructs 2028 from the island's all-seeing archive mind and finds, hidden in a bird reserve, an eighteen-year-old girl with her mother's face: her mother's younger self, raised in secret to be sent back on the last crossing of September. **Disaster two:** Moiken's own breakthrough in the archive leads the state to the bird warden who raised that girl; he is the state's lost agent of 2028, nineteen years older, and he drowns saving Moiken from the tide on the date he has known for nineteen years. **Disaster three:** trying to stop the girl's sending, Moiken delivers her into the state's hands, and the state decides to send her by force. On the last night Moiken takes her back and walks her into the ring as a homecoming; her teenage parents rise from the water in exchange; and past that instant, where every memory of the future runs out, she fights for an hour to let the dying ring fruit, so that the archive mind can certify the last crossing and release fifty years of hidden records to the world. Then, in a house surrounded by the flood, she sits down with the girl she said goodbye to two hours earlier, now thirty-seven, and with the girl whose life that was, and the machine that has recorded her whole life turns off its light.

## 3. One paragraph per major thread

- **Moiken (anchor, 2047).** A daughter who thinks her mother is dying discovers her mother is a copy, finds the copy's younger self, loses her mentor because of her own cleverness, loses the girl to the state because of her own love, and wins her back only to give her away freely.
- **Inken (2028, reconstructed).** A seventeen-year-old island mother learns her boyfriend has sold himself to a state program that traffics people forward through time, finds nobody will listen, receives a banded bird from the future, and on the night plans to ask the island's machine to look away so she can drag him out of the ring. The player follows her to 23:31 and the black square; the last frame of her thread is her arrival in 2047, still holding his jacket.
- **Swantje (mainland, then the Hallig).** A copy-case detective finds a dying man who glows under UV and names her drowned brother, breaks into the facility where the state keeps the people it took, is suspended, comes to the island, meets the niece she never knew, and stands between soldiers and her brother, who arrives at eighteen.
- **The station and Levin (antagonist side).** A director who believes every arrival is a debt prepares to close the books, while the young agent chosen for his weight learns that he is the deserter the state has hunted for nineteen years, is shown his own drowned body, and chooses to walk into the ring anyway.
- **The Schriever (AGI).** An archive mind that cannot lie but once looked away spends the game trying to know its forty-one missing minutes; it loses the copy of itself that raised a child; on the last night it is blinded and then given an ear; and when its want is finally met it gives away the next moment it could have kept.

**The hidden actor's synopsis (the story the player reconstructs).** In 2047 an eighteen-year-old girl raised in a bird hide is walked into the sea by the young woman she has watched grow up through a telescope. She arrives in 2028 beside a frightened state agent, sends him south, walks into a house where a baby is crying and picks her up. For nineteen years she is that baby's mother under another woman's name; she clones herself, carries the child, buries an empty coffin and raises her own younger self in secret with the agent and a forked machine, knowing exactly which night she will lose her. She teaches the island's children, sings tide tables at bedtime, prepares a room, and on the last night stands at the edge of the ring with a towel, watching herself go.

## 4. Structure at a glance

Prologue plus four acts, about 19 chapters, two Weegnacht set pieces (March, Act I climax; September, Act IV). Each chapter has one narrative question and introduces at most one new mechanic. The year runs February to September 2047; seasons and bird migration mark time (winter storm-tides, spring arrivals, the closed breeding colony, August's ripening, September's storm).

**Persistent interface (orientation at chapter boundaries, report principle 6).**
- **The Ledger board**: the player's pairing table. Each row is a crossing (night, sent, arrived, weight, why). The player fills slots from evidence; the game confirms in batches of three correct rows (Obra Dinn style), never per slot. It also holds identity attributions (who is who in a photograph) under the same batch rule.
- **The tide strip**: every chapter opens on the Hallig map with the hour's tide drawn as a band; it doubles as a clock on the flats.
- **The known-timeline bar**: a thin line at the top of the board showing how far into the future anyone in the story has knowledge (the state's almanac horizon; the mother's memory; Levin's). It ends at 21 Sep 2047, 23:41. In the last act the player watches the present run up to its end.

**Verbs (solving by doing).** Tide-walk (traverse the flats against the tide; stealth through fog and bird colonies, where flushed birds alert patrols). Reconstruct (walk the Schriever's frozen scenes, scrub audio and video). Depose (boot archived versions of the Schriever and cross-examine them). Weigh the past (replay a scene as floor-load data). Attribute (fingerprints, tattoos, scars, faces across years). Listen (read hum pitches as weights; count crossings). Pair (match who vanished on one night with who appeared nineteen years later). Re-photograph (align an old photograph with the present view to see who and what changed). Send (use the ring).

## 5. Full outline, act by act

### PROLOGUE: "Forty-One Minutes" (21 September 2028, about four minutes)

**Question:** What is the girl asking the machine to do?
**Play.** The player is the Schriever's sensor network: six feeds, switchable. Feed 1, the flats in infrared fog: guards walk a hooded boy into a ring of black posts; at the edge, a dazed man in 2009 clothes is held by two guards. Feed 2, a kitchen: an older woman rocks a baby (Moiken, eight months). Feed 3, Kirchwarf: an old woman lowers a microphone into a tide pool. Feed 4, the school: a girl in a yellow oilskin, a homemade bird tattoo on her wrist, leans into the terminal. "Hark. Close your eyes. Just for a bit." The only input offered is [Close]. Black. A counter starts at 00:00. Title: LANDUNTER.
**Plants:** the line; Inken's face, tattoo and oilskin; the baby (the anchor is in the first scene); Henning at the edge; Marret's microphone; Tade's weight tag on the guards' clipboard (68.2).

### ACT I: "The Guest Room" (February–March 2047)

Act question: **What is Mama preparing for?** Rung: a family.

**Ch 1. Landunter.** *Question: why has Mama stopped answering?* *New mechanic: the Ledger board (pinning observations).*
Moiken arrives by rail cart as the storm-tide rises; within an hour the house is an island. She explores a home she grew up in and finds it rearranged for an ending: a spare room made up for a guest, a yellow oilskin in a 2020s cut, size small, a hairbrush, an obsolete phone charger; a calendar, 21 September circled, "56 kg / 23:40"; a notary's copy of a will leaving half the house to "Inken Brarens, born 2010" (her mother's own name and birth date). Her mother cooks Moiken's favourite dishes and makes her write the recipes down. At bedtime she recites the tide table like a lullaby, as always. Next door, Keike's window is dark; they have not spoken in nineteen years. Moiken pins her theory: Mama is ill.
*Down beat:* a quiet supper in a house surrounded by sea.

**Ch 2. The Ferry / The Glowing Man.** *Question: what does a copy look like?* *New mechanic: fingerprint attribution.*
Moiken on her ferry shift with Kerrin, who is a copy of her dead sister; Kerrin's mother keeps an album with both girls' prints; the player compares them and learns the rule (same DNA, different prints). Interleaved, **Swantje's thread opens** on the mainland: a man found half-dead on the flats near Husum, missing since September 2009 and wearing 2009 clothes, glows under her forensic UV lamp. He dies in hospital after saying three things: "a girl with a bird on her wrist ran into the ring after the boy", "Tade", "they keep a bed for the last one; September". Swantje's brother Tade vanished on the flats in September 2028; she finds his file closed by federal order.

**Ch 3. The Schriever.** *Question: what does the island's machine remember about 2028?* *New mechanic: reconstruction and deposition.*
At the island's public terminal, residents may view their own records. Tutorial: find who borrowed Moiken's bicycle by walking a frozen reconstruction and scrubbing its audio (Marret, who wanted to fetch tape from the station). Then Moiken asks for 21 September 2028, the night her father "drowned". The reconstruction plays to 23:31 and stops on a black square: "Not witnessed. 41 minutes." She boots the archived version from that Weegnacht, which answers as of 2028: "I was asked to close my eyes." By whom? "By Inken Brarens." Moiken's conclusion, which the game lets her pin: Mama hid something that night. (It is true and misleading: the woman who asked was the original.)

**Ch 4. Knutt's Lessons.** *Question: what are the old island rules about the ring?* *New mechanic: tide-walking.*
Knutt teaches Moiken to read the Priel channels on a falling tide (lesson planted: "the sea comes back through the channels first"). In Keike's fold a young ewe wears a 2028 ear tag from Marret's late husband's flock; Knutt takes it away quietly. Marret plays Moiken a Weegnacht tape: a deep hum, "the drowned bell, getting tired"; her husband wrote "the clause" for the station, she says, and the Schriever even listens to her bells. Moiken visits Keike, who will say one thing about her daughter: "Ask her which cup was hers." Knutt gives away his binoculars to a child; Moiken now worries about two adults. *Down beat:* knots wheeling over the flats at dusk.

**Ch 5. Weegnacht (spring).** *Question: what does the ring actually do?* *New mechanic: sending.* **Act I climax.**
From the dike, through Knutt's scope, Moiken watches the station walk a man in a drysuit into the ring at low water; a hum; a different person, a woman in 2028 clothes, stands up in his place and is led away. Knutt walks Moiken to the ring's edge with a bucket: a crab of one weight in, a crab of the same weight from 2028 out. He says the rule in three sentences: "Weight for a weight. Nineteen years. Once." Under his UV torch the new crab glows. "Write to the past," he says. "Whoever reads it already has." Moiken bands an oystercatcher with a message to her mother in 2028 (the player types it; the game keeps it) and sends it into the ring. At home, at 3 a.m., she shines the torch on her sleeping mother's hand: it glows.
*Resolution by doing:* the player attributes the mother using three batches: a print from the mother's white mug against Inken's 2027 ID biometrics (mismatch), the tattoo in a 2028 archive photo (wobbly, homemade) against the mother's (clean, professional), the glow. The board accepts: **"The woman who raised me is a copy of Inken Brarens and came through the Waag."** The mother wakes, sees the torch, and says only: "In September I'll tell you everything."
*Answer opens:* where is the real Inken?

### ACT II: "Breeding Season" (April–June 2047)

Act question: **What happened to the real Inken, and to my father?** Rung: the island and the station.

**Ch 6. The Director's Key.** *Question: who will help me look?* *New mechanic: deep deposition (booting any archived version).*
Station director Nommensen, islander-born and kind, invites Moiken to the white station on stilts (scales in every corridor; the charter framed in the lobby, its last line: "Until the last crossing I keep this book for the Republic. After that, for everyone."). He offers her a director's key to the Schriever's archived versions "so you can find your real mother". He means it, and he also hopes she will find the man who vanished that night. Interleaved, **Swantje** finds Haus Seegatt and, on a night entry, re-photographs its residents against missing-person photos from 1990 to 2028: dozens of people who have not aged a day since they vanished, then aged nineteen years at once; a ledger of 78 rooms; room 78 made up and empty.

**Ch 7. Inken, February 2028.** *Question: who was Inken?* (Inken thread opens.)
Through depositions and reconstructions the player follows seventeen-year-old Inken: carrying the baby across the meadow, fighting with Tade in the station canteen after the recruiters offered him amnesty and a payout "in the future", asking Hark at the school terminal what happens to Fährgäste ("They are retained"), filing a complaint on 14 February that nobody answers, and hearing her mother quote the old sermon ("the Waag takes the soulless"). **Levin interlude L1**: a young man weighs in at 68.0 kg, memorises an almanac, birdwatches from the station roof; staff call him "Knutt" because he counts red knots. (An attentive player can start to suspect.)

**Ch 8. The Band.** *Question: did my message reach her, and did it make her do it?* *Verb: listen (pitch = weight).*
In the March 2028 reconstruction Inken finds a dazed oystercatcher in the ring and reads its band by lamplight; the camera resolves only part of what the player wrote in Ch 5, letter for letter. She threads it on a string around her neck. Dread: did Moiken's message send her mother into the ring? The board refutes it: the complaint is dated 14 February, five weeks before the band. Then Marret's September 2028 tape: the player scrubs it and finds three hums, not two. Matching pitch to weight on Marret's reference chart: 79 kg at 23:20 (Henning, seen by the station), and two at 23:40, about 56 and 68. Two crossings happened inside the black square. *Answer opens:* if one was Tade (68.2), who was the 56?

**Ch 9. Weighing the House.** *Question: who has been sleeping in the guest room?* *Verb: weigh the past.*
Using the director's key, Moiken replays her own house as floor-load data: on nights she was away in April, someone of 56 kg slept in the guest room. Knutt's shop records show food for two. Marret, gossiping, remembers the 2041 surge that floated the churchyard's coffins: "the little Brarens coffin was light as a bird". The grave reads "Ebba Brarens, 2029". Moiken has never questioned the sister who died at birth. Everything points to Süderwarf, closed for the breeding season.

**Ch 10. The Hide.** *Question: what is hidden at Süderwarf?* **Midpoint.**
Stealth through the tern colony at dusk: flush the birds and the station's roof watcher (Levin, birdwatching) notices. Inside the ruined walls, a hide; inside the hide, an eighteen-year-old girl with Moiken's mother's face, eating soup, who looks up and says: "I'm your sister." A dented machine on the shelf speaks in the Schriever's voice but warmer (the Nursery fork). Ebba says her name. She weighs 56 kg; she owns a wobbly-looking tattoo that is not wobbly at all; she knows Moiken's jokes because they are hers.
*Resolution by doing:* the player attributes Ebba (prints identical in pattern to nobody's: she is a third body with Inken's genome), and pairs the board's open row: the 56-kg crossing at 23:40 on 21 September 2028 must be someone sent from 2047 who matched Inken's weight. The board accepts: **"Ebba is the girl who becomes my mother. In September she will be sent back to 2028, and Inken will come forward."**
*Reclassification:* the guest room was for Ebba's last weeks; the oilskin in size small was for her to wear into the ring; the will to "Inken Brarens, born 2010" is for the woman who will arrive; the tide lullaby is the Nursery's.
*Answer opens:* if Ebba goes back, who comes forward with Inken at 68 kg? And who was sent with Ebba?
*Moiken's want flips:* she will not let them send her sister.

### ACT III: "The Blind Year" (July–August 2047)

Act question: **What does the state want on the last night, and why has nobody come from the future since 2029?** Rung: the nation and its history.

**Ch 11. Aunt.** *Question: who else has been looking?* (Threads converge.)
Swantje, warned off and stubborn, comes to the Hallig. At the Brarens door she meets the niece she never knew she had. Two boards merge: her Henning ("a girl with a bird on her wrist") pairs with Inken's tattoo; her room 78 pairs with the 68-kg hum; her brother is Moiken's father, taken forward on the same tide as Inken. **Levin interlude L2**: Nommensen briefs the station. On a wall, the horizon chart: since 1990 a Bote has arrived every Weegnacht carrying nineteen years of the future; since 2029, nobody; the Republic has not known its own future since March. "Nobody has ever come from after this September." The lost Bote of 2028 is the man in this room, nineteen years on: find him. An aging projection of Levin's face, bearded. (The player who has seen Knutt now knows.)
*Down beat:* Swantje and Moiken on the dike; "My son is older than my little brother."

**Ch 12. The 00:14 Frame.** *Question: where did the 68-kg crosser go?*
Moiken's triumph: combing the reconstruction after the gap, she finds the one frame nobody ever queried, an infrared smudge of a wet man walking south across the flats at 00:14, toward Süderwarf. She pins it and the board accepts. The same chapter, on the station's sampling flats, Levin slices his right palm on an oyster shell (playable interlude; the player sees the wound bandaged). Through Moiken's director's-key session, Nommensen sees the frame.

**Ch 13. The Raid.** *Question: can Knutt and Ebba get out?* (10 August.)
The state lands at Süderwarf. Moiken gets Ebba out through the colony to her mother's house (stealth, now with the birds as cover, not alarm). The Nursery fork is pulled and destroyed; its last log is the next morning's tide table. Knutt leads the raiders out across the flats on a rising tide; Moiken follows. In the Priel, where "the sea comes back first", she is swept off her feet; Knutt pulls her out and is taken by the channel at 21:52. On the bank, his watch (engraved "L.S. – find your way home") stops in the water. His palm bears an old scar exactly where Levin cut his last month.
*Resolution by doing:* attribute Knutt (watch, scar, nickname, no record of him before 00:14 on 22 September 2028): **Knutt was the lost Bote, Levin, nineteen years older.** He knew this date; he gave away his things in February.
*Cost:* Moiken's breakthrough killed him.

**Ch 14. Second Pass.** *Question: what did the machine see and never say?* (Mandatory reread, the NieR device.)
The player plays the Schriever for one chapter, grieving its fork. It replays scenes from Acts I–III with its own senses (load data, hydrophones, its versions' logs) and the player re-reads them: Knutt's steps were always 68 kg plus a child's; the mother has walked to Süderwarf every week for eighteen years; in March 2030 it was asked to fork itself "to witness a future crosser growing up" and logged it, and nobody ever asked. It finds the sentence it has always known and never applied to itself: its record becomes everyone's after the last crossing. **Levin interlude L3**: Nommensen shows Levin the drowned body: the watch, the scar he got three weeks ago. "This is you if you hide from us."

**Ch 15. The Shining.** *Question: why do the dead glow?* (20 August, then late August.) **Act III low point.**
As the Waag ripens, crossed bodies glow in the dark. Moiken, Swantje and Marret walk into the churchyard at night: the commanders' graves glow green under their own boasts ("God showed him where the whales lay"), and so does an unmarked corner by the wall. Marret takes the Schuldbuch from the tower: ninety-four bought children, their weights, and the Ocken-Nommensen hand that wrote it. The founding crime and the director's line are now known to the cast. Then Moiken's own disaster: she tries to smuggle Ebba to the mainland on the rail cart before September; the state stops the cart on the causeway. Nommensen, seeing a clone of Inken at eighteen and 56 kg, understands 2028 completely and decides to send Ebba himself, by force, because the debt must be paid. Swantje is suspended and put off the island. In the station holding room Ebba tells Moiken through the glass: "You think it's being sent. I've always called it going home."
*Moiken's want flips again:* not to stop the crossing, but to make it Ebba's.

### ACT IV: "Landunter" (20–22 September 2047)

Act question: **Can the last crossing happen as a homecoming, and what happens after the moment where everyone's knowledge of the future ends?** Rung: the world.

**Ch 16. Forty-One Minutes (the eve).** *Question: what did my mother live through?*
On the station roof at dusk, Moiken finds Levin with Knutt's binoculars (she brought them). She tells him what his nineteen years were: a hide, a girl he taught to read birds, another he taught to read channels, a death in a Priel that was a rescue, not a flight. "You'll learn birds." He does not answer. That night the mother keeps her promise and tells Moiken everything; the player plays her memory, the only unrecorded scene in the game (drawn rather than reconstructed): arriving in fog beside a frightened man, sending him south, the walk home, Keike's face, the cup she did not know, the weight of a baby. She remembers that Moiken walked her in. She remembers nothing after.

**Ch 17. Weegnacht (autumn).** *Question: can Moiken get Ebba to the ring before 23:40?*
21:00: Levin opens Ebba's door. Keike, who has not crossed her own threshold to speak to the mother in nineteen years, brings Ebba her real daughter's yellow oilskin: "Wear hers. She'll need it back." Moiken, Ebba and Levin cross the flats through fog and roosting knots while the state sweeps for its missing crossers (stealth against floodlights and tide). At 23:35 at the ring: Nommensen and his team (who want the same crossing), a tracked excavator on the mud, Swantje back on Kerrin's ferry, the mother at the edge with a towel and a thermos, Marret on the dike with headphones. A standoff resolves because everyone wants the same thing for different reasons. Moiken walks Ebba into the ring; Levin walks beside her.
**The convergence image.** Split screen: on the left, the 2028 reconstruction runs from 23:31 into the black square, but now with Marret's tape under it; on the right, 2047, live. Two hums sound in both halves at once. Ebba and Levin are gone. In the ring, a girl in a yellow oilskin and a boy in a canvas jacket stand up, her fist still in his jacket, a bird band on a string at her throat. Her first words: "Is he all right?"
*Resolution by doing:* the last two rows of the board fill themselves only after the player pairs them; the batch confirms; **the forty-one minutes are solved.** The Schriever, which watched this arrival, says only: "Thank you."

**Ch 18. The Free Hour.** *Question: can the ring die on its own before the state cuts it out?*
23:41: the known-timeline bar ends. Nobody, not the mother, not the state's almanacs, knows anything from here. 23:42: the state cuts the Schriever's flats sensors to stop it certifying the last crossing and moves to seize the arrivals and cut out the core. The posts split and glow; spore mist drifts toward everyone who has crossed. One goal at a time: (1) Swantje stands in front of her brother, who does not recognise the 45-year-old woman until she says his childhood nickname; (2) Inken, who knows these flats better than anyone alive, leads the excavator onto soft ground; (3) Moiken carries Marret's hydrophone on its old cable into the centre of the ring so the Schriever can hear. The core crumbles into spores under the cutters. The Waag's last sound is a chord of every weight it ever carried. On the dike, Marret: "There. The bell." Then silence. 00:31, the Schriever: "No further crossing can occur." Every phone on the flats lights up as the whole record goes public. Soldiers lower their hands. Nommensen sits in the mud; nothing unravels.

**Ch 19. Retreat.** *Question: can everyone reach the mound before the surge?*
A tide race back across the flats, the channels filling first, carrying Marret, guiding the two arrivals who have never seen 2047. The church bell rings for Landunter. Everyone reaches the Brarens warft as the sea closes over the Hallig.

**Epilogue. The Kitchen (about 02:00).**
The mother, Inken, Tade, Moiken. A tide clock; a blue cup. The Schriever, through the kitchen speaker: "I'll close my eyes now. Just for a bit." Choice: [Let it] or [Keep watching]. If the player lets it, the camera withdraws through the window to the house on its mound in a black sea, one window lit, and a small red light goes out. If the player keeps watching, they hear the conversation (see 02 §7), and the Schriever notes, without judgement, that it is now in the public record. Credits run over wordless morning vignettes (the flood draining; Swantje opening Haus Seegatt's doors; the Kept walking out; Swantje's son meeting his uncle; Kerrin on a mainland stage). After the credits: a hydrophone in the Elbe estuary picks up a faint hum.

## 9. How gameplay delivers each revelation

- **Identity of the mother (Ch 5)**: three attribution batches on the Ledger board; no character tells the player.
- **The gap's first half (Ch 8)**: the player scrubs Marret's tape and matches pitches on a reference chart. Audio carries the evidence.
- **Ebba (Ch 9–10)**: load-data replay finds a weight; stealth through a colony finds a person; attribution and pairing state the conclusion.
- **Knutt (Ch 12–13)**: the player finds the 00:14 frame themselves, watches Levin get the cut, and attributes the body.
- **The machine's secret (Ch 14)**: a compulsory replay of known scenes through another sensor; the player re-reads Act I as weights and logs.
- **The founding crime (Ch 15)**: place (graves glowing in the dark), object (the ledger), then the shortest possible in-world text (a gravestone line that boasts).
- **The gap's second half (Ch 16–17)**: a memory played as a scene; an arrival seen in the flesh.
- **The Silence (Ch 18)**: listening to the last chord and the silence after it.

Text is optional depth throughout: gravestone lines, the charter sentence and the Schuldbuch's columns are short and authored by biased people. The critical path needs none of them read in full.

## 12. Intensity and down beats

Each act alternates investigation with quiet domestic or natural scenes: suppers in a flooded house, knots at dusk, a tide lullaby, Swantje and Moiken on the dike, the eve on the station roof. Peaks: Ch 5 (spring Weegnacht), Ch 10 (the hide), Ch 13 (the raid and Knutt's death), Ch 17–18 (the last night). Ch 14 (second pass) and Ch 16 (memory) are deliberate low-intensity, high-meaning chapters before the climaxes they set up. The final beat after the action is the quietest scene in the game.
