# Writer C: outline, *The Ouro's Hand*

## One sentence

A court interpreter on a ceasefire ring learns the living peace treaty can only take her body.

## One paragraph (master plot)

In the Shiet system, a fifty-year ceasefire between the human Nomes and the voiceless, grafting Bnne is forty days from renewal, and every coin in 42 worlds is the stored computation of a disarmed weapon. When her mentor dies at the living treaty-tree and the censor's Daybook says he died at home, interpreter Tamoun Besa starts paying for the truth, one audited second at a time. Seven nine-second motes from the future arrive the same night and show her hand cutting the treaty, the king falling and a severed arm. **First disaster:** her audits book themselves to an account dormant since the old queen died, and the realm's spymaster and its Treasurer both work out what she is before she does. **Second disaster:** by proving the treaty forged she sets the hunt in motion. Her friend is sent to burn eleven sleeping copies of her face, a banker dies at her door having been warned by her own future, and Tamoun is framed and forced through her bedroom wall into the alien half of the ring. **Third disaster:** to make the treaty take on the night of the dead she must graft her own arm into it. The Treasurer's second tries to tear her out, her friend cuts the arm off cleanly to save her, and her alien neighbour gives the vine that holds their twelve years and forgets her. The peace becomes permanent, the censor edits her out of history at her word, and a year later, on the day of the dead, a one-armed woman with a new name sits at the wall and decides whether to knock.

## One paragraph per thread

- **Thread A, the Tree (Tamoun, Kellia).** A murdered mentor and a lying newspaper lead her to a living slip in a lunch box, which leads to a forgery under lacquer and then to a message in her own teacup. She learns she is the signature. She flees through her wall to the alien side, comes back out of the treaty itself on renewal night and pays with her arm.
- **Thread B, the Daybook (Pamin, Kellia and the court).** A spymaster who hides a king's crime he did not commit. He watches the market panic at a time-travel plume, realises the interpreter is the rightful Ouro, keeps it secret, catches a leak, warns her, defies his king, proves the king was framed, and at the end sends the truth into the past while hiding it from the present.
- **Thread C, the Sickles (Phib, Kellia, Shmin and the Treasury).** A debt-bonded informer who caused his mentor's death. He is sent across six jump-days to burn sleeping clones, refuses, learns his handler was never who he thought, turns double agent, steals back the bento and cuts his friend free.

## Structure at a glance

Four acts, 21 chapters and a coda. Each act opens on a small, personal hook that turns out to carry lore. A persistent **40-day board** (days along the top, the seven clip slots, three thread lanes) appears at every chapter boundary. Clips found so far sit in their true time slots as soon as they are assayed, so the player always sees how long until each one happens.

## ACT 1: THE LIE IS FREE (D0 to D10)

**Act question:** who killed Apa Pjol, and why does the Daybook say he died at home?

### Ch 1. Tea Through the Wall (D0, Tamoun)

- **Small hook.** Morning in cell 31. Tamoun pours tea into Talou's cracked porcelain cup. A vine comes through a hairline crack in the lacquered back wall and grafts a slip to her wrist: "rain". The player reads it with the grafted forearm (**tutorial: graft-reading**). By the end of the day the slip has grown into "rain on a friend's roof". The vine always leans toward the cup, and the player can notice a fine green line in the crack.
- **The welcome at the Wall.** The court fleet arrives. In the treaty hall, Tamoun interprets the envoy Paopi's greeting graft at the union for the king (the first public graft task, at low stakes). The treaty is recited from porcelain sherds: *"renewed at the Wall by the Ouro's hand."* The king rehearses pressing his palm to the lacquered union, and the dust records it.
- **Introductions.** Kyra pays for the interpreters' tea and is kind. Her second Maro stands behind her in gloves and asks Tamoun what the Bnne word for a second would be. Apollo, grey and blunt, says the Bnne will strike at lapse.
- **Phib's lesson.** Tamoun tests Phib on the union's root-collar: "Never pull an interpreter off wood. Cut, clean, here."
- **Pjol.** Pjol takes tea with her and eats from his lacquered bento: "Rice keeps things alive." His parting line: "I'll be at the Wall tonight."

### Ch 2. The Bento (D0 night, Phib, playable)

- Phib carries Pjol's bento across the ring. Curious and under orders, he opens it in a scrape (a dust-blind lacquer patch) and finds a living slip in rice and a sherd note.
- He writes a report and drops it in a lacquer box at the Treasury laundry for "the Strategos's runner" (**tutorial: tradecraft and dead drops in the blind**).
- He delivers the box to Tamoun at 22:10. She is asleep with the vine on her wrist.
- **The player now knows Phib is a spy.** Tamoun does not.

### Ch 3. Died at Home (D1, Tamoun, then Pamin)

- **Tamoun.** At dawn she goes to the hall to ask Pjol about the box and finds him dead at the union's root-collar. His graft is **torn**, ragged, not cut. That is the forensic clue: an interpreter would have cut. The wardens move her out. By noon the Daybook says he "died peacefully at home".
- **Pamin (first playable scene, a flashback to 02:00 the same night).** The player sits at the Daybook console reviewing raw dust and sees a gap walk into the hall. The game teaches the **edit verb**: choose the cut, choose the caption, watch the public-mood gauge settle. Pamin believes the gap is the king. Canonically he writes "died at home". If the player picks another caption, Pamin's chief orders it changed back within the hour (the first respected refusal, with a small cost: Pamin loses a point of the king's trust).
- **Promotion.** The king appoints Tamoun acting Chief Interpreter. Her new seal gives her an audit line.

### Ch 4. The Truth Costs (D2, Tamoun)

- **First audit (tutorial).** She phrases the query (hall, D0, 23:00 to 23:45, 6 m radius) and watches the cost counter run: "The lie is free; the truth costs a holok a second."
- She walks the frozen hall. Pjol approaches and grafts himself in, inside a blind bubble at the root-collar. The gap enters and leaves by the Treasury corridor. At 23:41:07, seven points of light flare in the dust and drift apart. The audit ends.
- The charge appears on her slate as "booked: account B." She assumes this is the Interpreters' office.

### Ch 5. The Shadow Spend (D2 to D3, Pamin)

- The market boards drop. The Koinonia have told their clients that a plume arrived from forty days ahead, and that such a send costs a third of the reserve, payable only by a signer's body.
- Pamin's network watches the Koinonia counting-house send a test mote one hour back from a pawned sickle-core. The market board ticks down by the cost, and an hour earlier a clerk had found the mote. This is **time travel taught by experiment**: anomaly, test, named rule.
- His dust filters catch a stray mote with a forward stain: **C5**, the market board at its lowest under "SICKLES REARM AT LAPSE". He files it under "unknown, Day 40".
- On the oversight ledger he sees the Body account moved for the first time in 29 years: an audit of the hall, D0. Who has the old queen's key?

### Ch 6. Rice, Not Water (D5 to D6, Tamoun)

- She finally opens the bento and reads the slip with her forearm: *"When the union first fruits, the giver's root sends a new shoot to join it. Then the two stocks are one and need no keeper."* Paopi's insistent grafts ("Is the shoot ready?") suddenly make sense. **The current question becomes: what is this slip, and why does it contradict the treaty?**
- Senetom visits with wool, kindness and an offer: a Koinonia **assay lens** for "what the union really says". Tamoun takes the lens and gives nothing (a dialogue choice; telling Senetom is possible, and if she does, Senetom acts on it a day earlier on D23).
- **The first mote hunt.** She sweeps the hall's air with the lens and finds **C7**: a severed, green-veined arm hanging from the lacquered trunk, blood on lacquer. Its time-stain reads Day 40. She also finds **C1**: a green-veined hand cutting lacquer at the union. Whose arm? Pjol's veins, Phib's and hers all look alike in nine seconds.

### Ch 7. Account B (D10, Pamin; the act-ending reveal)

- Pamin traces the Body-account charges to the interpreters' terminal and to Tamoun's seal. He pulls the charter, Talou's translation: "the signer's body". He pulls Tamoun's foundling record (W.Y. 15) and the crypt's "vat failure" (W.Y. 15).
- **The player learns, before Tamoun, that she is a woken clone of Ouro Tsenoute and the rightful Ouro by the Law of the Body.** Pamin locks the file and tells no one.
- In the Treasury, intercut, Kyra reaches the same page and orders Phib to Shmin: "Count the Relic Stocks."

**End of Act 1 for the player:** the murder is unsolved, the king is apparently guilty, Tamoun is the heir without knowing it, the Treasurer is a polite ally who has just ordered an inventory, and four clips point toward a dreadful Day 40.

## ACT 2: THE OURO'S HAND (D10 to D26)

**Act question:** what does the treaty really say, and who is the shoot? (After the midpoint: who is killing the queen's bodies?)

### Ch 8. Six Days to Shmin (D10 to D16, Phib)

- **Small hook.** Phib's mother sends a sherd: the collectors have moved his sister to a Wall Bond labour hulk.
- **The galaxy opens.** He buys a jump solution with holoks; the price has tripled since the Shadow Spend, so travel itself is rationed by the mystery. He passes through the Vault-Ring, where stripped sickle-cores hum as banks, then six days of jumps.
- At Shmin he sees 42 nome banners and the debt court where seconds sign for their originals. Pemdje's governor is a hawk because Pemdje owes.
- Phib's verb here is **tailing in the blind**, through a crown city whose noble houses keep Bnne-wood privacy gardens.

### Ch 9. The Window (D12, Tamoun, interleaved with Ch 8)

- **Night operation.** She cannot cut the union's lacquer unseen, so Ethen pushes from the bare-wood side and makes the union's root-collar bloom: a dust-blind bubble large enough for her.
- She cuts a palm-sized window and lays the slip's cut face against the scar. **The faces match** (a forensic matching puzzle of growth rings and cambium). **The forgery is proven:** fifty years ago someone pruned the true clause and grafted a sterile slip in its place.
- **Who was the interpreter at the signing?** Talou. A sting: the founder of her school, the woman who raised her, forged the peace.
- Back in the Wall Quarter she finds **C4**: the vine at her crack, cut clean, withdrawing. **The current fear becomes Ethen.** She knocks on the wall, and the vine answers at once, alive. Not yet, then.

### Ch 10. The Gap That Walks Wrong (D14 to D17, Tamoun and Pamin)

- **Tamoun** pays for a long audit of the gap and runs **gait attribution** on an invisible person: stride length from displaced dust, height from the gap's top edge against the hall's banner line, hesitation on the steps.
- She compares it with the king, recorded walking the same steps at the D0 rehearsal. The gap is 12 cm shorter and fluid, and it leaves by the Treasury corridor. It is not the king.
- Height matches: she checks court figures in old dust, and it matches Kyra, but Kyra walks with a cane. Someone with Kyra's body and a young stride? She has met Maro. She is not yet sure.
- **Pamin** sees Tamoun's new charges (the gap, the king's walk) and realises his D1 edit may have protected the wrong person. He catches **Tsia** copying his oversight queries to the Koinonia and lets her keep leaking, now as his channel.
- Pamin's filter yields **C2**: the king dropping to his knees as the crowd cries out. He fears an assassination on Day 40.

### Ch 11. Eleven Faces (D19, the MIDPOINT; Phib and Tamoun interleaved)

- **Phib, at the Crypt of the Body at Shmin.** Twelve vats, one dry since W.Y. 15 ("failure"). Eleven sleepers aged 34, all with one face: **Tamoun's face**, without the green veins.
- The warden hands him the burning order under the Treasurer's seal. **Committed choice:** burn or refuse. Refusal is canonical. Phib walks out, and in the corridor he hears the vats vent.
- **Tamoun, the same night at Kellia.** Ethen's vine strains toward the cup so hard it tears its own bark. She looks at the crack: the green line has grown the same callus pattern she saw in the union's scar.
- She lays her fingertip on it and reads Talou's message: *"You are her shoot. When the union fruits, go to it. Choose it."*
- **Two reveals in one beat.** The player already knew (Act 1), so this is dramatic irony paying out (the Ted Lasso method). The new information is Phib's crypt: someone is killing the queen's bodies. The question changes from "what does the treaty say" to "who is hunting her, and will she go?"

### Ch 12. Archive Fire (D20 to D22, Pamin, then Tamoun)

- **Pamin** receives the Shmin report and writes "Archive fire, no one hurt." His hand hesitates over the edit key, and the player chooses whether he keeps a raw copy (canonically he does; it is used on D37).
- **Tamoun** decides whom to tell. The options are Pamin, the king, Senetom or no one. Canonically she tells no one human. She grafts it, slowly, to Ethen.
- Ethen does not answer "I know." It begins to widen the crack.
- Mote hunting in the Wall Quarter, she finds **C6**: her own white face saying "Cut there." The player now holds a clip of Tamoun apparently ordering a killing on Day 40.

### Ch 13. The Warning (D23 to D24, Pamin watching, then Tamoun)

- Through Tsia, Pamin watches Senetom receive a mote from herself, one day ahead: herself at the freight dock with Maro behind her. Senetom avoids the dock.
- At 18:00 she sends the same mote back from the pawned core to close her loop, and the market dips.
- At 19:30 she goes to cell 31 by the Wall Quarter corridor, carrying news for Tamoun, and Maro kills her at a scrape, unrecorded.
- Tamoun comes home at 19:34 and kneels over her. Senetom's last act is to press Tamoun's hand to her own sleeve, where she has sewn a sherd: "your account".
- The Treasury **buys and publishes** a 9-second audit: Tamoun over the body, hands red. **The truth can be bought in the part someone wants.**
- **Lesson:** foresight shows a moment, not a fate. Senetom survived the dock. The rule is now proven twice.

### Ch 14. Held for Her Protection (D25 to D26, all three)

- Phib returns. Pamin tells the king what Tamoun is. The king, afraid of a rival with a valid claim, orders her "held for her protection" by a Treasury-paid guard.
- **The spy-thriller betrayal:** Pamin defies his master and warns her. He cannot use the Daybook's channels, which Tsia copies to the Koinonia, so he walks to the Wall Quarter and tells her by hand, in a scrape.
- **The escape.** Guards at the door; Ethen's widened crack opens like a mouth. **Tamoun crosses through her bedroom wall into the Bnne half of the ring** (a stealth sequence in which the human guards' dust-sight fails at the bare wood).
- Kyra's men search cell 31 and seize the bento and the slip.
- On a ledge in the Wall Quarter, Phib finds **C3**: himself running with the bento. He does not know when, or why.

## ACT 3: THE OTHER SIDE OF THE WALL (D26 to D39)

**Act question:** can the union still take, and what will it cost? Underneath it runs the spy question: who is Phib's handler, and can Kyra be proven?

### Ch 15. Groves Without Dust (D27 to D30, Tamoun)

- **The genre shift.** The Bnne half is dark, warm and silent. The player's audio drops to creak and sap-tick, the HUD's dust overlay vanishes, and the navigation and conversation verb becomes graft-reading entirely.
- Paopi meets her and does not tell her she is the shoot. It shows her the union's Bnne face: buds swelling for Nairouz.
- She learns the **consent rule** ("the stock may not graft the shoot toward itself") and, from Ethen's growth rings, that Ethen was placed beside her in W.Y. 37 by Talou and Paopi. **Trust reverses:** her friend was arranged. Then she reads the vine's twelve years of growth and finds it was also real.

### Ch 16. The False Flag (D28, Phib)

- Hunted by Kyra's suspicion, Phib goes to Apollo for protection and recites his reports. Apollo has never heard of him. Who ran him? The dead-drop boxes are Treasury stock, and his pay came in Treasury-sealed holoks (a clue present since Ch 2).
- **Phib turns.** Pamin recruits him as a double agent, and he feeds Kyra false sightings of Tamoun on the human side.
- Apollo, shaken, keeps Phib's name to himself. He is now a hawk with a doubt.

### Ch 17. Thout's Rings (D31 to D36, Tamoun)

- In a grove she reads the Wall's age rings and learns the Wall is **Thout**, the Bnne signer, rooted. Ethen is grown from Thout's cutting, **the Bnne signer's body**. The neighbours sharing a wall are the two signatures.
- She rehearses with Ethen: a shoot joining a union, safe cuts, the timing of fruiting. She grafts her answer to Talou's message onto Ethen's palm: "I choose it."
- Ethen's reply is the first word it ever gave her: "near".

### Ch 18. Framed in His Own Mantle (D37 to D38, Pamin and Phib)

- **Pamin** shows the king the gait audit and the archive-fire raw. The king learns that a body shaped like his Treasurer, wearing his own Mantle, killed Pjol by a route that would have framed him, and that the same Treasurer burned his grandmother's relics.
- He asks Pamin one question: "Does she want the throne?" Pamin: "She wants the tree to take."
- **Phib** copies the Treasury's Mantle-custody log: signed out on D0 from 21:20 to 23:50 "to the Treasurer's body". Under the Law of the Body that phrase covers Maro. He sees the bento on a shelf in the Treasury annex.

## ACT 4: NAIROUZ (D40)

**Act question:** will the union take, and what will each of them give? All seven clips now land in order, each reclassified as it happens.

### Ch 19. The Set Places (D40, 19:00 to 22:50; all three threads intercut on the 40-day board's final column)

- 19:00, the day of the dead. The Wall Quarter sets bowls and cups for the dead along the Wall. Tamoun's cell is empty, her table bare.
- **22:15 to 22:21, Phib** steals the bento from the annex and runs: **C3** happens, and the player recognises it.
- **22:30, Pamin** watches the market board fall to its lowest under the rearm banner: **C5**.
- **22:40, Ethen** cuts the vine at the crack and draws it into the Wall: **C4**. It is not death; it is a gift on its way.
- **22:45, Phib** gives Apollo the Mantle log and the gait audit. Apollo orders the sickles to hold "pending the union". (Player verb as Phib: a stealth approach to Apollo's platform.)

### Ch 20. The Union (22:52 to 23:30, Tamoun)

- She steps out of the Bnne face of the union into the hall, before 42 nomes' banners. Phib hands her the bento.
- **22:58, C1:** she cuts the lacquer from the scar, and the dust thins as bare wood shows.
- **23:04, C2:** the union buds and opens. The king, who knows, kneels to her.
- **23:06:** she regrafts the true slip at the scar (the last graft-writing puzzle, done with one hand under time pressure) and sinks her arm in as the shoot. On the far side, Ethen grafts the vine.
- **23:11:** Maro breaks the guard line and grips her shoulder to tear her out.
- **23:12, C6:** "Cut there."
- **23:13:** Phib cuts, clean, at the shoulder, the rule from Ch 1. The guards shoot Maro.
- **23:15, C7:** the arm hangs from the trunk. Tamoun's graft calluses the wound, and she lives.
- **23:20:** Apollo arrests Kyra.
- **23:30:** the union takes. On the Bnne side Ethen goes still, then turns from her like a stranger.

### Ch 21. The Cut (23:41 to 23:51, Pamin)

- **The send.** In the hall annex, Tamoun's blood on the vault plate supplies the human signer's body. Ethen, who no longer knows why, answers the hand that asks and presses its graft-seal, the Bnne signer's body.
- **The final editing puzzle.** As Pamin, the player must cut from the night's dust **exactly the seven clips that arrived** (moment, angle, nine-second window). The Oikonomos rejects any clip that does not match: "That send does not arrive."
- **The temptation.** The player can try to cut a clip warning Pjol on D0. It is refused for the same reason, and that is the emotional rule of the whole game: you cannot save the dead; you can only send what made the living act. The board drops by a third.
- **The broadcast (committed choice).** The Daybook's 10-minute delay is running out. Tamoun's "Cut there" is still in Pamin's ear.
  - **Edit (canonical, E1):** splice in the D0 rehearsal of the king's hand. Keep Maro's attack. Caption: "Chief Interpreter Tamoun Besa died defending the union. The Ouro's hand renewed the peace."
  - **Do nothing, or send the raw feed (E3):** the realm sees its king kneel to her.
- **Midnight:** the term passes, and the sickles hold.

### Coda: Nesi (1 Thout W.Y. 51)

- One year later, the day of the dead. A one-armed tenant called Nesi sets places at the Wall: for Pjol, Talou, Senetom, eleven sleepers, an unnamed second, and "Tamoun Besa". She mends the cup with lacquer one-handed and leaves the crack showing. She sets the bento.
- The crack in the Wall has callused shut. **The player's only decision: knock, or don't.**
- If she knocks, a long silence follows, then the callus splits and a green shoot grows through and grafts one word to her wrist: **"near"**.
- If she doesn't, the camera holds on the cup and the closed crack until the lamps burn down. Both are endings.

**Thread budget:** three plot lines. Active threads at peak (Act 2): the murder, the treaty and shoot, Day 40 and the motes, the hunt and succession, Phib's handler (five). At most five locations are live in any act.

**Start together:** Ch 1 puts Tamoun, Pjol, Phib, the king, Kyra, Maro, Apollo, Paopi and (in the background) Pamin in the treaty hall on one morning. Only Phib's Shmin trip starts elsewhere, and only from Ch 8.

**Collide, don't separate:** the bento passes through all three threads (A to C to A to C to A). The gap is seen by A and B. The crypt (C) and the cup (A) share a single night. C3 is found by C but happens in A's climax.
