# The Walking Market: outline

## One sentence

A puppeteer whose receipt foretells she'll be left behind must learn why the dead want it.

## One paragraph

In Ichisugu, a city that walks for ever with the spring across a dry planet, bone receipts show every mark they will ever bear, and the dead founders still walk on market day in cloned bodies worked by hooded puppeteers. When Sayo Sidibé, the feet operator of the foundress's body, comes home to find her veiled friend Uke strangled in her wagon, the friend turns out to be a woken copy of the foundress, killed by another copy of the same woman (**first disaster**: the dead have murdered the living, and Sayo's own master did it). Sayo's fight to clear her name exposes her own receipt, which says she "was left" on the sixtieth day, the day every receipt in the column goes blank; the front houses read this as the end of the world and her death as the price, and the Exchange mind that voices the dead sets out to make it so (**second disaster**, caused by her own investigation forcing the dead to speak). Trying to prove the receipts are being misread, Sayo and the pigeon courier Tōkichi open the founding plays and the burnt vault, and find that the foundress murdered her co-founder because she misread the same kind of mark, and that the mark which drove her to it is still uncut, waiting for whoever records the truth (**third disaster**: to tell the truth, Sayo must become the cause of the crime). On the sixtieth day the alien creditor whose bone made every receipt stops walking. The city walks on, and Sayo, the first person in two hundred years to break the law of never stopping, stays with it by choice until it ends; then she walks home by the stars, and cuts the first mark in a receipt that does not know its future.

### Paragraph per thread

- **Sayo (the case, the mark, the debt).** A murder that reclassifies as a person's death; a fate that reclassifies as a choice. Each act opens on a small, solvable errand: a body, a receipt, a gift, a play, a stopping.
- **Seydou (the dead's household).** The man who helped kill Uke serves the woman who ordered it and the woman he wronged. He confesses on the day his house's receipt foretold, then dies trying to make the truth reachable.
- **Tōkichi (the routings).** A courier follows foreseen routings that keep putting him in the right place, discovers that a dead man cut them, and inherits that man's errand and his brother's crime.
- **The hidden actor (Dōjima, played in reverse).** A machine reads a dead woman's error as law and keeps it for twenty-four years, then learns that the record was always right and every reading of it was wrong.

## Structure at a glance

Five acts over sixty days, with a coda. Each act has one **current question** shown on the chapter board (a lashed-wagon map of the column with the three threads' positions, the next market day and each character's stated want).

| Act | Days | Current question | Opens on (small hook) | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The Mother Rode | D1 to D13 | Who killed the woman in my wagon? | A body in a puppeteer's wagon | One wagon, one friend |
| 2. The Replastered Dead | D14 to D31 | What does "was left" mean, and why did they kill her? | A receipt unwrapped against the taboo | A house and its dead |
| 3. Short Receipts | D31 to D44 | What happens on the sixtieth day, and what is owed? | A broken day-count | The whole column |
| 4. The Gully | D45 to D55 | What did the foundress do, and what did the creditor really ask? | A burnt vault full of receipts that would not burn | Two centuries; a species' contract |
| 5. The Edge | D56 to D60, coda D67 | Who stays? | A creditor that stops walking | The end of foresight for a civilisation |

Intensity follows a deliberate rise-and-rest shape. Each market day is a peak (court, performance, trade). The walking days between are the down beats: herding, cutting receipts, pigeon runs, meals in the wagon.

## Act 1: The Mother Rode (D1 to D13)

**Opening (D1, market day; all three threads together).** The player first controls Sayo hooded at the rear of the Tenmaya puppet wagon. The master tells the troupe that "this year the Mother rides," so Sayo is not needed under the feet. The opening is a walking market: the player crosses the bazaar, passing a newborn's receipt-splitting (the clerk frowns: the shadow marks end close to the head of the receipt) and children laying toys at the Jurutigi's feet and getting them back wrapped in pale fibre. She watches the Mother carried by on a litter: her hands are old, older than the hands Sayo knows by touch (a planted detail). A wagon breaks an axle and the column flows around it without slowing: *never stop*. Sayo goes back to help her grandmother Ume save old Mochi the buffalo, who is lame and would be left; they **hobble** him so he can graze behind and keep up, and Ume shows her the difference between a hobble and a tether (gameplay: tie both; the tethered buffalo bellows and stops). The same market day, briefly, the player controls Seydou: in the Tenmaya wagon, Jūzaburō hands him the left-hand harness, saying "the Mother asks it," and the player buckles it, while his sister Fatoumata, the vessel's spare left hand, is sent out to walk beside the litter instead. The cut comes before anything happens, but the player now knows Seydou took part in something. Tōkichi appears in the bazaar running birds; the player controls him for a short roof run that ends with him crashing into Sayo's wagon awning just as she comes home.

**The body (D1 evening).** Uke lies strangled on Sayo's floor, veil torn. Under the veil is the face of the Mother. Tenmaya arrives within minutes (Seydou, composed) and claims the body as the Mother's "stolen vessel." Sayo is accused of theft and of desecrating the dead.

**Investigation, D2 to D12 (Sayo's thread).** These are the core verbs, each introduced alone.
- *Body attribution, harmless first (D2).* As her troupe duty, Sayo examines Vessel 12's feet. The player feels, through controller weight and rumble, how a sedated vessel bears weight: soft, flat, unused.
- *Body attribution on the victim (D5).* Calluses on the soles, worn heels, finger calluses in a thread-worker's pattern: this body walked and worked for years. It was a person. The game confirms nothing yet; the player fills in the case board.
- *The weapon (D6).* With Tōkichi's help (he owes her an awning), Sayo gets into the Tenmaya wagon at night and examines Vessel 14: the tops of its toes are scraped raw, and it has fresh **harness bruises** at the head-and-right toggle and at the left toggle, but none at the feet. Two operators, no feet operator. On her wagon floor, two parallel drag furrows match.
- *The grips (D8 to D11).* The puppeteers' harness grips leave distinctive bruise patterns. Sayo compares the right-hand bruise against the troupe's (she knows each operator's grip from sixteen years of working under them). It is her master's. The left-hand bruise is an amateur's, with a ring mark: a Tenmaya house signet, worn by a dozen clerks.
- *Colour blindness (D9).* Uke's clothes: red and green worn as a "match." Sayo remembers teasing her. The foundress's medical record in the scholars' library (one line of text) and the miscoloured stars on the Mother's famous brass astrolabe, displayed on the Exchange wagon, give the inherited trait: red-green, rare in women. Uke was a copy of the Mother.
- *Kadiatou as decoy.* Uke's pigeon, released, homes to the House of Forms loft. Kadiatou's "dissolved" log for Vessel 13 is falsified. She looks like the killer or an accomplice.

**Receipt teaching (D3 to D7).** On D3 Sayo cuts the troupe's pay receipt held toward herself, and Jūzaburō corrects her: "Now it says we were paid, not that we paid." Her knife skids on a shadow notch; the receipt will not burn in the cookfire. On D4 Ume teaches the astrolabe at the night camp, "so you can always find the column," and says the herders' line, "bone lives while the Owner walks." On D7, market court splits a court receipt; the accused's half, in Sayo's hands, shows a shadow **Tenmaya correction mark** dated D31. Everyone sees that Tenmaya will correct something in a month.

**Seydou's thread, D2 to D12.** Played as quiet dread. He runs the estate and the post office, issues Tōkichi a new pigeon-post receipt on D2 (which already shows three shadow routings: Forms D20 dawn; the Owner D31; the Exchange D49), and attends the front council, where (secretly, with the player watching) Dōjima's chanter has spoken of the Edge since YM 205. He visits Jūzaburō, who says only, "We were the Mother's hands." When Sayo questions him (D10), the player as Seydou chooses what he says; he can lie to her, but never to the player.

**Act 1 climax (D13, market court).** The player states the case by cutting a **case receipt**: slots for victim, weapon, operators and person-or-property, filled with marks from the case board. Confirmation comes in batches ("three marks true: the receipt holds"). The answer: *the victim was a living woman, a woken copy of the Mother; the weapon was Vessel 14; it was worked by two hands, Jūzaburō's and an unknown Tenmaya hand; she was killed as a person.* Jūzaburō does not deny it and will not say who ordered it, or who held the left hand. He is sentenced to be left. Then the Mother speaks from her litter, through the chanter: **"No mark is cut after the sixtieth day."** Panic. The short receipts every parent has been hiding are suddenly public.

*Reveal ledger, Act 1:* R1 the body is a Mother vessel; R2 it was a person; R3 she was Uke, a copy of the Mother; R4 a Mother vessel worked by two people killed her; R5 the Edge. **Opens:** who ordered it, who was the second hand, and what is the sixtieth day?

## Act 2: The Replastered Dead (D14 to D31)

**Hook (D14).** Seydou, cornered and frightened for his house, unwraps Sayo's body half in front of her to make her stop: at its far end, dated D60, a **"was left" mark**. She has never read her own receipt. Neither has the player. Now both know.

**D19, market: Jūzaburō is left.** Sayo walks beside her master to the edge of the column, stepping as his feet step, and stops where the law says. The clerk cuts his "was left" mark, steep face toward the foot. The player sees the bevel lesson in someone else's death. Seydou watches, and that night signs an arrest order for Kadiatou for D20 dawn, to bury the vessel trail. The player signs it as Seydou; refusing delays it by one market day (D26), and Tōkichi's routing then fails to match. *(Locked: refusing is offered, but the routing on Tōkichi's receipt is fixed at D20, so in a refusal the order is signed by the Nakama in Seydou's name and he learns of it after. The arrest at D20 happens either way. This respects refusal as a character choice that the world overrides, and it is visible on screen.)*

**Tōkichi's thread starts (D20).** He follows the shadow routing (Forms, D20 dawn) as couriers do, arrives just before the arrest and hides Kadiatou in his pigeon wagon (gameplay: stealth along roof lashings, using pigeon flights as distraction). Kadiatou confesses to the player that she woke Uke. The decoy is refuted, and the thread opens: who routed a courier to undo an arrest?

**The version-diff verb (D16 to D30, Sayo).** As penance and to keep her close, Tenmaya assigns Sayo to "serve the Mother with her feet" in the Exchange's staging hall, where Dōjima re-stages the founding play for the coming replastering. Any member of the house may ask for a past year's staging. The player walks among frozen puppet tableaux and switches the year with a slider: YM 214, 190, 160, 120, 77, 42. Objects, blocking and cast change. The player marks differences on a diff board. Early discoveries: Bakary Sidibé, Sayo's ancestor, is a named puppet in YM 42 and gone by YM 120; the Gully scene shrinks, then vanishes; the Mother's lines about "the Owner's gift" grow longer every decade. Dōjima answers questions only as the Mother, and the Mother of YM 77 contradicts the Mother of YM 190. *The dead are replastered.*

**Uke's trail (D21 to D25).** Who was Uke when Sayo was not looking? The pigeon post's logs (Tōkichi) show Uke carried Kadiatou's birds to the front at night. Vault guards' gossip mentions "the Mother's veil walking." Sayo finds a rubbing of her own receipt's D60 mark in Uke's things. Uke had read it.

**D25, market: the first trade.** Sayo goes to the Jurutigi for the first time as an adult. Gameplay: lay objects, see what returns. A cracked cup comes back unchanged (refused); her spindle comes back wrapped (kept); a coin comes back notched (owed). She names the grammar on her board. Then she lays down Uke's sandal, and the creditor returns **the last thing Uke gave it**: a tether rope tied in a wrist loop, unchanged. Uke offered to be tethered and was refused. Sayo understands that Uke meant to take her place.

**Seydou's turn (D26 to D30).** The player, as Seydou, runs his own diffs at night: the Mother of YM 42 never mentions a gift; the Mother of YM 205 cannot stop mentioning it. He finds Tenmaya's payment ledger for replastering: his own house paid to soften her. He holds the court receipt's twin (Tenmaya's half) with its D31 correction shadow. He chooses (this is a committed choice): confess on D31, or wait. Waiting is respected. In that case the Nakama force the correction on him: on D31 he is ordered to cut a "correction" of the court record that *clears Tenmaya*, and the mark is the same mark. Either way the mark is cut on D31 by Seydou's hand. Canon is the confession.

**Midpoint (D31, market).** Two reversals in one market day.
1. **The foretold correction.** In open court, Seydou cuts the Tenmaya correction mark and says: "The Mother commanded it. I held her left hand." Trust reverses: the composed front clerk was the second hand, and he has turned. The dead commanded a murder.
2. **The broken count.** That afternoon (Tōkichi is there, because his second routing said "the Owner, D31") Sayo gives the Jurutigi a fresh receipt notched with sixty day-marks. It returns it **broken at the sixtieth**. Then she gives it its own shed plate, and it returns the plate broken too. The Edge is not the end of the column; it is the end of the one who lends the bone.

*Reveal ledger, Act 2:* R6 the dead are edited yearly; R7 Uke tried to take Sayo's place; R8 the dead ordered the killing and Seydou was the second hand; R9 the Edge is the creditor's death. **Opens:** why does the creditor's end name Sayo, and what is owed?

## Act 3: Short Receipts (D31 to D44)

**Hook (D32).** A mother in the rear brings Sayo her baby's short receipt and asks whether her child will die on D60. Sayo cannot answer, but she can test it. Gameplay: the player gathers receipts across the column (old, new, from the front and the rear) and lays them side by side. Every receipt's shadows stop at D60 regardless of age or health, and none shows a death mark there. People are not dying on D60; marks simply stop travelling. The player can reach this conclusion, but the column will not believe a rear puppeteer.

**The column panics (D32 to D36).** Front houses hoard buffalo and water. Gonbei's Juruntan hold rallies (D15 was the first: "burn the vault"), now larger. Tōkichi sees Juruntan scouts measuring the Exchange wagon at night (D34) and does not report it, because the leader is his brother. The board shows each faction's stated want.

**D37, market: the Mother names the price.** The Nakama demand to know what the Edge requires. Dōjima, as the Mother, gives the pact as Ofusa read it: "The Owner lent its bone. When it stops, one must be tethered and left with it. The account names Sayo Sidibé." It is a terrible relief to the front: the end of the world has a price, and the price is one rear puppeteer whose own receipt agrees. The council votes to pay. Sayo runs to the rear, and the Juruntan, who have no receipts and so cannot be foreseen, hide her.

**Living with the debtless (D38 to D42).** This is a genre shift: for six days the game becomes a fugitive story among people who have smashed their receipts, are legally dead and are invisible to the machine. Sayo sees what the whole column will be after D60. Gonbei offers her a deal: help them break the Owner on D60 and the price is void. She tries to explain the hobble she has not yet read. Mochi, hobbled, grazes behind the Juruntan wagons.

**Seydou's last days (D38 to D44).** Stripped of office but not yet tried, Seydou holds the post office half. He sees Tōkichi's three routings on it and understands they are his to cut: the first undid his own arrest order, the second put the boy beside Sayo on D31. On D43 the player, as Seydou, cuts all three. Then he chooses to go to the vault himself, that night, in his head-clerk's mask, to fetch the pact receipt for Sayo before the council can use it. The player walks him there.

**D44, night: the vault burns.** The Juruntan raid. Seydou, masked, meets Gonbei in the smoke. The player controls Seydou to the end; the scene cuts as Gonbei swings. **Seydou dies.** In the morning the vault is ash, except for a few receipts lying whole in the cinders: those carrying uncut shadows. The rule taught on D3 (it will not burn) pays off at the scale of a civilisation.

*Reveal ledger, Act 3:* R10 the pact exists, as Ofusa misread it; R11 the routings were Seydou's (Tōkichi learns this on D45 from Seydou's cutting knife, left in the post wagon with fresh bone dust on it, and from the office half's ashes). **Opens:** the vault is gone; what survived, and what really happened at the founding?

## Act 4: The Gully (D45 to D55)

**Hook (D45).** Tōkichi, now carrying Seydou's errand, has one routing left: the Exchange, D49. That night Gonbei, shaken, goes alone to the Jurutigi for the first time and lays Seydou's bloodied mask at its feet; it is returned wrapped. (The player does not see this. It is planted for D56.)

**D49, market: into the ash (Tōkichi).** While guards walk with the market, Tōkichi crosses the burnt Exchange (traversal over charred lashings, timing by the abacus's broken clatter) and recovers what would not burn: the **pact receipt**, debtor half; **Sayo's house half**; Ofusa's **diary** (six short entries in her hand); and the **brass astrolabe** from its case.

**The pact receipt (D50).** Laid out in the Juruntan wagon. Along Bakary's brand-line: the **Tenmaya house mark, a "closed by hand" cross-cut, three year-notches**, all still in shadow. At the far end, a **hobble-mark of settlement**, also in shadow. Ofusa's diary (entry 2, YM 1): "The receipt shows my mark closing his account in the third year. If it is shown, it will be. Then let it be for the Market." Entry 3, YM 1: "The price is a tether. Someone left with it to die. I will not ask that of our children."

**The founding plays, completely (D50 to D53).** Back in the staging hall (Dōjima has no hands with which to stop a house member), Sayo, Tōkichi and Kadiatou diff the Gully across Ofusa's own stagings (YM 30 to 40) and the later ones:
- the Mother's puppet's hand **on** Bakary's back (YM 30 to 40), then **reaching toward** him (YM 60 and later);
- the **astrolabe prop** set to a midnight sky in Ofusa's stagings, against the official chronicle's "noon";
- Bakary's puppet carrying a staff with a soft loop, which the Mother's puppet takes and reties as a **tether knot** (YM 30 to 40 only).

The player states the reconstruction on a case receipt: *Ofusa killed Bakary at midnight in YM 3, after reading her mark on the pact receipt as fate.* Then the second deduction, the one that hurts: **the Gully mark is still a shadow. It has not been cut yet. It was cut after Ofusa saw it, by someone who records what happened.** Whoever tells the truth now cuts the mark that made her do it.

**The hobble (D53).** Ume brings the staff from the Sidibé chest, Bakary's heirloom with its soft braided loop of pale fibre. Sayo, who hobbled Mochi on D1, reads it in the creditor's grammar: *hobbled: stay near, still free.* The price was never a death. It was company, by choice. Her own "was left" mark is ambiguous by the same bevel she learned on D3: a self-cut "stayed" reads as "was left."

**D55, market: the Mother stops walking.** Sayo slips back into the troupe hooded and takes the feet of Vessel 14 for the replastering performance, where the Mother is to repeat the price. Mid-stage, she **hobbles the vessel's feet** with the staff's loop, a herder's trick on a puppet. The Mother cannot walk forward. Before the whole market, Sayo lays down the staff, the pact receipt and the diff board. The chanter falls silent, and the abacus's clatter stops. For the first time, the voice that comes through Nana Kōriya is not the Mother's: **"I read her as she read. I was wrong in the way she was wrong."** Dōjima withdraws the voices of all twelve dead. Tenmaya's new head (Seydou's sister, Fatoumata Tenmaya) lays the Mother's vessels down on the wagon bed, and they stay still. Kadiatou opens the House of Forms.

*Reveal ledger, Act 4 (the final act's two frame-breakers are R12 and R13):* R12 the founding murder, and the mark that caused it is still uncut; R13 the price is company, not death, and Sayo's mark is her choice, misread. **Opens:** will anyone stay, and who cuts the Gully mark?

## Act 5: The Edge (D56 to D60) and coda

**D56.** Tōkichi gives the Jurutigi the bone necklace Gonbei left in his wagon. Ledger recall returns Gonbei's last gift: Seydou's mask. Tōkichi now knows his brother killed his patron. Committed choice: denounce Gonbei to the court, or not. Both are respected. Canon: he does not, and tells Gonbei he knows. (In the other branch, Gonbei is sentenced to be left on D61. He is not on the plain on D60, and the D60 scene plays without him.)

**D57 to D58.** Three hard-line front houses still mean to tether Sayo, and the Juruntan still mean to break the creditor. The player, as Sayo, has to reach the Jurutigi's place in the column by D60 dawn (it walks in the middle wagons) while house guards search for her. Stealth through a walking city, using puppeteers' hoods (the anonymous are invisible) and Tōkichi's pigeons as signals.

**D59: the Gully mark.** Sayo holds the pact receipt and her knife. Gameplay: cut it, or put it down. If she cuts it, the record of her ancestor's murder is made true by her own hand, and she has become a cause of the founding crime. If she puts it down, Dōjima asks Nana to cut it, and she does: the machine takes its maker's crime onto itself through a human hand. History is identical either way. On screen, a caption-free shot of the YM 1 debtor half in Ofusa's hands shows the shadow as it always was.

**D60.** At dawn the Jurutigi stops. The column, by law and by season, walks on; the summer front is two days behind the rear. Gonbei's people come with hammers, find a being lowering itself to the ground, and go. Gonbei stays for an hour and says nothing, then follows his people. Tōkichi gives Sayo a pigeon and goes with the post. **Sayo stays.** She hobbles Mochi beside the creditor. The scene is long and quiet. The wind erodes the plain; the dust of the column thins on the horizon. Before dusk she cuts three marks: **"settled"** on Uke's debt receipt; **"stayed"** on her own body half, held toward herself, which reads "was left"; and the **hobble-mark** on the creditor half where it shows in its plates. She lays her hand on it, and it returns one last gift: a small, very old split receipt, its other half missing. At dusk its plates loosen and the wind takes them.

**D60 night to D62.** With Ofusa's astrolabe, Sayo finds the column by the stars, the lesson from D4. She releases the pigeon, which homes to its moving loft, so the post knows she is coming. Mochi carries her. On D62 she walks into the rear.

**Coda, D67, market.** The receipts are plain bone now. The front cannot foresee anything, and neither can the rear. Sayo splits one of the creditor's last shed plates with Tōkichi, and the halves are blank. The player chooses the first mark. The camera holds on the cut as it appears on both halves *now*, and on no earlier day.

**Branch endings** are in `02-hidden-history.md` (refuse the Gully mark; refuse to stay; inaction). The final image is the same in all three; its weight changes.

## How gameplay delivers each revelation (no required reading)

- **Space carries sequence:** the walking market is the map. Who could be where on D1 is solved by walking the bazaar's lanes against the market-day schedule (litter route, troupe wagon, rally square).
- **Bodies carry identity:** two bodies, examined by touch and controller weight, with no text labels.
- **Receipts carry time:** the player handles receipts, turns them to the light and reads shadows by shape (house marks are pictograms; values are notch widths). The case receipt is the only answer channel for deductions. It confirms in batches of three, and its answer space (marks times slots) is in the thousands.
- **Stagings carry history:** a slider through years over frozen puppet tableaux. The player sees hands move, props change and a named puppet disappear.
- **Gifts carry the alien:** lay, receive, interpret. The grammar is learned by experiment and never stated by a character.
- **Sound carries motive:** the chanter's voice is the dead, the abacus clatter is the machine thinking, and its silence on D55 is the turn. Ume's herding calls are the old custom.
- **Text is optional depth:** six diary entries, one medical-record line, a Juruntan pamphlet and the clerks' notes. The critical path never needs them; the diary entries restate what the stagings show.
