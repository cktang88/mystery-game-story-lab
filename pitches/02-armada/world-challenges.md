# Pitch 2: The Armada, world challenge log

How `world.md` was argued into shape. Round 1 is the first draft. Rounds 2 to 5 attack it. Each challenge has an honest answer and what changed.

## Round 1: the first draft, in brief

- Central thing: the Harbours, alien stations in four moons that the colonies live inside.
- Aliens: a sail nation from a flare star, sailing a loop. The Long Stalemate, the founding law, one Relay kept by unnamed rebels.
- Humans: a shipping company found "ruins" by chance and settled them. Its engineers read the return date in the carvings and buried it.
- A public band of light in Landfall's main hall, "the Tideline", slowly filled over two hundred years and was full on the day the sails came.
- Regrowing banned for civilians by a founding law; soldiers only.
- The Mirror Fleets in another star system. Earth's silence left as a mystery with no private answer.
- The Fleet's ships were human-built in Keel's alien dry docks.

During round 1 the story file changed: the Armada now carves its whole timetable on every harbour wall, and the Helm keeps it. The draft was folded around that before round 2.

## Round 2

**1. Why four moons of one planet, and not four star systems?**
The Relay only matters if the war moves in days. Between stars, without faster-than-light travel, a week's warning is worthless. Four moons of one giant planet put every front hours or days apart.
*Changed:* one star system. The Mirror Fleets move into this system's far cold, since no ship in the story can reach another star.

**2. With no faster-than-light travel, how did humans get here, and why would anyone pay for it?**
Nobody crosses forty light-years to farm. The only prize big enough is working alien shipyards.
*Changed:* Earth saw the sails pass about five hundred years ago, traced them here, and saw moons cut open by machines. The colonists came as stored mind scans on one fast ship, the Mindelo, and were grown on arrival. Humans came for the yards on purpose.

**3. What does a harbour actually do while the Armada is away? "Empty ruins" is vague.**
A nation that lives on ships needs new ships. The harbours must be building them.
*Changed:* the harbours became pattern-driven shipyards that mine their moons and build the next lap's ships. This became the central thing, because it explains the loop, the timetable, the human economy, the war and the alien split at once.

**4. If the yards were building ships when humans arrived, what happened to those ships?**
Humans took them. Four colonies have a navy only because they had alien hulls.
*Changed:* the Fleet's grey hulls are the Armada's own unfinished ships, lifted early before their sails were fitted. Hulls became the colonies' money. The Armada came home to find its new houses pointing guns at it.

**5. This looks like Mass Effect's Citadel and Reapers: owners come back on a cycle to the structures everyone uses.**
It's close in shape. The difference matters: the Reapers are harvesters and their structures are a trap. Here the owners are a migrating nation who need repairs and new homes, the structures are honest shipyards, and the Armada has twice let other peoples stay on as tenants. The conflict is closer to an eviction than an extermination.
*Changed:* added the tenant precedent on Driftwood's chart, so Monarch's terms follow old custom.

**6. The Armada is a hundred times bigger. Why hasn't it won in six years?**
The Relay alone isn't enough; a hundred-to-one fleet can afford to be ambushed.
*Changed:* most fighting happens in and around the harbours, and every battle at Keel damages the yard the Armada needs. The Fleet fights from inside the enemy's most precious buildings.

**7. Why was a "Long Stalemate" so long? Wouldn't foresight end a war quickly?**
When both sides see a week ahead, both dodge everything. Nobody loses enough to surrender. Attrition stops.
*Changed:* what ended it was the central thing. The loop stalled, ships aged, and harbours finished ships nobody collected. The peace was economic necessity first and principle second.

**8. Why would the Armada leave the last Relay in a harbour for twelve thousand years and never find it on thirteen visits?**
A rebel cell could not keep a secret for thirteen laps. An institution could.
*Changed:* the Helm sealed it as a deterrent, to use only if someone else cheated, and only each Helm-holder learns where it is. It matches the story's "Monarch's people did hide one Relay".

**9. The Relay's week and the backup week match. Coincidence?**
Yes, in the draft.
*Changed:* Landfall circles its giant in just over seven days. A Relay reaches back one turn of its moon, and the founders set their calendar by the same turn. Both weeks share one cause.

**10. Founding law "civilians can't be regrown". What would 200 years of real people do with that?**
The rich would buy it, legally or not, and a company that owns the tanks would sell it.
*Changed:* before the war, regrowing was a costly service for Keel's rich. The war law gave every tank to the Fleet. That's why poor colonies fill the marines and why Keel's elite resent the war. It still matches the story's "marines get regrown all the time".

**11. Where does the outer colonies' debt really come from? "In debt to Keel" is a label.**
Historical colonists often paid passage on credit with years of work.
*Changed:* every founder signed for passage and tank time. The Company's charter grew its own families first and the farm and mine families last, so the last-grown owed most. Debt from the day their founders opened their eyes. Hull debt and the defence tax piled on later.

**12. The Tideline, a public filling band of light. Wouldn't four million people have guessed?**
Yes. It also spoils twist D, which needs the public to believe the builders are long dead.
*Changed:* cut the public band. The timetable is in light-code that nobody but Company translators can read, painted over at Keel and built behind at Landfall. A countdown along Keel's build line is seen only by yard engineers.

## Round 3

**1. The story's timetable: why would a civilisation publish its future? Is it just a clever idea pasted on?**
On its own it is clever but fragile. It needs a practical root.
*Changed:* the timetable has two roots. First, plain shipping sense: harbours must know when to finish ships, trading peoples must know when to have goods ready, and a fleet of kilometre-wide sails can't hide or turn fast anyway. Then the Long Stalemate made it sacred and total. Practical first, moral second, which is how real customs harden into law.

**2. A published route doesn't stop someone foreseeing battle tactics. Does the timetable logic hold?**
Only partly, so the law went further.
*Changed:* after the peace, every battle the Armada fights must be announced first. Kestrel's surprise attacks, the first in twelve thousand years, are why the old captains smell cheating, and why humans find Kestrel brilliant.

**3. Why does Kestrel hate the timetable? "Young and ambitious" is the average choice.**
Give Kestrel a grievance that comes from the central thing.
*Changed:* by old law, new ships go to crews with the oldest ships. About eight hundred crews were due new hulls here, and humans had taken them. Their old ships are failing now. The walls promised them ships, and the walls lied. Monarch can afford thirty years of patience; they can't.

**4. Why would Governor Reis believe Kestrel will let humans keep Keel?**
She wouldn't, unless she thinks Kestrel needs her.
*Changed:* the deal includes Keel building Kestrel's new ships. That's a believable business bet. It also lets a careful player see the betrayal coming, since Kestrel's crews need the yard more than anything.

**5. Is the Cape Company just the evil corporation (Weyland-Yutani)?**
It risks it.
*Changed:* the secret began as ordinary caution ("nobody invests in a colony with an eviction notice") and grew as each generation of Reises inherited a bigger silence. The Company also kept everyone alive and built everything. Blame spreads: every colonist lives in a hull-house and in someone else's harbour.

**6. Why was the vault never opened in two hundred years, and then opened by Vaz by luck?**
Luck helping the plot is banned.
*Changed:* the harbours wake as the return date nears. Eleven years ago sealed levels lit up and doors opened by themselves. Vaz led the survey sent to check them.

**7. Who sent the first message Vaz ever read?**
Rule one says the person at the Relay next week. So Vaz himself, a week later, through Company translators because the machine only takes light-code.
*Changed:* added as a buried truth. It explains why the Company was inside the secret from its first hour.

**8. Why did the Fleet fire on Monarch's hail six years ago? The story leaves it as a reflex.**
Vaz is the man who is never surprised. He'd only fire first if something told him to.
*Changed:* the Relay showed alien ships landing at Landfall, unopposed. Vaz read invasion. It was Monarch's crew coming to break the Relay, as the founding law asks. The war began with a true message misread. That fits the rule that the Relay only sends words.

**9. Driftwood walks Landfall without a map "because Driftwood's people built it". Built twelve thousand years ago?**
A navigator wouldn't know one harbour's corridors from birth.
*Changed:* the Helm sent Driftwood with the vault's map to break the Relay. Driftwood's capture was a failed mission, and Ines can finish it.

**10. If Monarch knows the humans have a Relay, why hasn't she said so?**
Saying it admits the Helm kept one, which would hand Kestrel the throne.
*Changed:* that is her secret and the reason for her silence.

**11. Why can't Bosun just read alien light-code?**
If it could, Ines wouldn't need Driftwood, and Faria's translators couldn't bury Monarch's terms.
*Changed:* the Company trained the only translators and kept the keys. Fleet AIs are barred from them. Reading light becomes a political act, which gives the "read light" mechanic weight.

**12. Why does a lonely four-moon colony with no known enemies have a navy?**
It shouldn't. That's a clue.
*Changed:* the Fleet was founded sixty years ago "against piracy and accidents" and paid for by all four colonies. Its first real action was against Brightwater's strikers. A player can ask why it existed at all.

## Round 4

**1. The owner's new rule: is the time travel genuinely new?**
Honestly, the device isn't. Messages from the future are Steins;Gate, Timescape and Minority Report. "The future can change" is the standard paradox dodge.
What is new is the use: a civilisation that answers foresight by publishing its own future, a weapon that is the pen rather than the machine, and a death that erases a week set against a machine that grants one.
*Changed:* brainstormed seven alternatives, picked two (stolen backups and the listening harbour), and wrote both into world.md's suggested changes with gains and losses. The world is built so its central thing survives either.

**2. Do the alternatives fit this world, or only the story?**
Stolen backups fit almost perfectly. The Long Stalemate becomes a war of stolen captains' minds, the founding law bans copying a mind, and the timetable follows by the same logic. The Mindelo's founders arrived as stored scans, so humans are exactly what the Armada fears most. The listening harbour hangs straight off the central thing.
*Changed:* noted in world.md.

**3. The Mirror Fleets: thousands of alien warships parked in this system. Why didn't humans salvage them?**
Fair hit.
*Changed:* they are narrow war hulls with their sails stripped, far out in the cold. Towing one home costs more than lifting a fresh hull from Keel next door. The Company stripped a few for metal.

**4. Fleet AIs "must obey direct orders". Why would 200 years of humans write that rule?**
*Changed:* in the founding years, the Mindelo's AI chose who was grown first, on Company orders. The late-grown blamed the machine. The rule came out of that fear. Fleet AIs are built from the Mindelo's patterns, which explains their sailor talk.

**5. Is the Mindelo AI keeping the secret a cliché "AI knew all along"?**
It's optional, and it mirrors Bosun instead of repeating it: an AI ordered to stay quiet that did. Bosun is the one that finds the gap.
*Kept,* as optional depth.

**6. Why does the Company keep the Mindelo's core instead of erasing it?**
It holds the founders' original scans, which the colonies' charter forbids anyone to erase.
*Changed:* added.

**7. Forty thousand ships, a few million people. Does the scale hold?**
Ships as households of fifty to a few hundred people gives a few million. A hundred to one against two hundred Fleet warships matches the story.
*Kept.*

**8. What would people on Farhaven really celebrate and fear?**
Something tied to the founding, not invented rites.
*Changed:* Growing Day and each family's "first day", whose dates quietly encode the growing order and the class system. Atlantic June festivals and cachupa survived the crossing.

**9. Regrowing: why weekly scans and not daily?**
Scanning beds are few and a scan takes an hour.
*Changed:* weekly, on the rest day. Added "the catch-up", the routine where an AI walks a regrown marine through lost days. Cancelling Ines's catch-up becomes the first clue.

**10. Is the "aliens were right all along" risk worse now?**
Kestrel's grievance is real. Monarch's Helm hid a Relay. Four million humans were born in these harbours and have their own claim. The Fleet fired first on a true message, not out of malice.
*Kept,* with the balance written into section 3.

**11. Does any of this need a coincidence?**
Earth seeing the sails is the premise, not a lucky break: humans came here because they saw them. Vaz finding the vault came from the harbours waking. Driftwood's map came from the Helm.
*None left.*

## Round 5

1. Does the central thing still explain the economy, politics, beliefs and the war? Yes: hulls are the money, Keel's yard is the power, the share of new ships splits the aliens, and the war is a fight over who gets them.
2. Does anything break the story's two rules? No. The Relay still sends words back one week, and regrowing still restores the last weekly backup.
3. Can the story be followed without any of this? Yes. Nothing in world.md is needed to follow the main plot.
4. Too many invented words? New ones are plain or real: the Cape Company, the Mindelo, the catch-up, passage debt, put ashore.
5. Tone check: no graves, ghosts or gore. The founders' list in the Old Bay is a list of births.
