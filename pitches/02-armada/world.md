# Pitch 2: The Armada, the world

This is the world beneath `exploration.md`. It keeps the story's frame and two rules; the rest is optional depth. The challenge log is in `world-challenges.md`.

## 1. The one central thing: the Harbours, and the ships they build

The Armada's harbours are machine shipyards dug into moons. While the Armada is away, each harbour slowly mines its moon and builds new ships for the crews whose old ships are wearing out. The Armada then comes back on a fixed date to collect them.

Nobody else in this part of space can build a hull that lasts a thousand years. Everything hangs on that.

- **Why the Armada sails a loop.** A ship lasts about two laps. A harbour needs most of a lap to build its share of new ships. So the whole nation keeps moving from harbour to harbour, picking up new homes as the old ones fail.
- **Why the Armada keeps a timetable.** A harbour must know when its ships are due. The trading peoples along the route must know when to have goods ready. A fleet of sails kilometres wide can be seen from light-years away and can't turn quickly, so it never could hide. It stopped trying, and published its route.
- **Why humans came.** Earth saw the sails pass, traced them here, and found moons cut open by machines. A shipping company crossed forty light-years to take working alien shipyards.
- **Why the colonies are rich and poor.** Keel sits on the yard. Every hull in human space came out of it. Whoever holds Keel holds the money, the debt and the Fleet.
- **Why there is a war.** For two hundred years humans took the ships the yard was building for the Armada. The Armada came back to collect its new homes and found them painted grey, with orange numbers, pointing guns at it.
- **Why the aliens are split.** The crews who were due those ships are flying hulls that are failing now. They follow Kestrel. The crews who can wait follow Monarch.

The Relay sits here because this is the Armada's biggest harbour, where its old civil war ended.

## 2. The deep timeline

Dates count back from the story's present.

### The Armada's long past (40,000 to 12,000 years ago)

**A restless star.** The aliens evolved around a small red star that threw out huge flares. Their eyes grew to read light, and they learned to talk in it. Their first science was forecasting their star's storms.

**Planets are traps.** When they reached space, they found ships safer than their world. A planet can't move out of a flare's way. A ship can. Within a few thousand years most of them lived aboard ships, and the home world emptied. The idea "never be where the storm is" became their oldest saying.

**The first harbours.** Ships wear out, and they needed raw stone and metal to build more. They dug shipyards into lifeless moons around nearby stars. Over thousands of years the route between those harbours closed into a loop. It now has dozens of stops and takes nine hundred years to sail.

**The timetable begins as a schedule.** Each harbour needed to know when to finish its ships. The peoples they met along the way needed to know when to expect them. So the Armada fixed its dates and carved them on every harbour wall. For a long time this was simply good seamanship.

**Relays begin as storm warnings.** About 14,000 years ago, the Armada's best minds built a machine that could get a short message back from one week ahead. They built it to warn sail crews of flares before they struck. A Relay is the size of a building and only works fixed in place, so every harbour got one.

### The Long Stalemate (about 12,300 to 12,000 years ago)

**A lean lap.** The fleet grew faster than the harbours could build, and two halves of the Armada fought over too few new ships.

**Both sides could see a week ahead.** Each half held harbours, and every harbour had a Relay. Each side saw every attack coming and stepped aside. Nobody lost many ships, so nobody was ever forced to give up. The war went on for three generations.

**The loop stalled.** What ended it was the central thing. While both fleets sat guarding harbours, nobody sailed the route. Ships aged. Harbours down the loop finished ships that no one came to collect. The nation was dying slowly in a war it couldn't lose or win.

**The peace, signed here.** Both sides met in this system, at its four harbour moons. They wrote the founding law: no captain may fight with knowledge of the future. A captain who breaks it loses command forever and is put ashore at the next harbour, left behind when the Armada sails. They broke every Relay but one.

**The timetable becomes sacred.** The survivors reasoned it through. Someone could always build a new Relay. The only people safe from a stolen future are people with nothing to steal. So they made the timetable total. Every arrival and departure was carved centuries ahead, and every battle the Armada would ever fight had to be announced first. A captain swears to the timetable the way a human officer swears to a flag.

**The last Relay.** The Helm kept one, sealed under Landfall's harbour, to use only if another captain ever cheated. Only each new holder of the Helm learns where it is. It printed nothing for twelve thousand years, because nobody stood at it to send a message back.

**The Mirror Fleets.** The war fleets were narrow fighting ships with no room to live in. After the peace, both sides parked them in the cold far out from the giant planet, facing each other, and sailed home in new ships.

### Thirteen quiet laps (12,000 to 900 years ago)

The Armada sailed its loop thirteen times in peace. It traded with the peoples along the route, who planned their lives around its arrival dates. Twice in that time, other peoples moved into empty harbours. Both times the Armada let them stay as tenants, on terms: the yards stay the Armada's, and the tenants never touch the ships. Driftwood's star chart marks both harbours.

About 870 years ago, the Armada left this harbour after its last stay. The yards began their slow work.

### Earth sees the sails (500 to 200 years ago)

**A moving cloud of light.** About five hundred years ago, Earth's telescopes picked up a vast swarm of reflective sails crossing nearby space. Astronomers traced its path back to a star forty light-years away, with four moons around a giant planet that were cut open in straight lines.

**The Cape Company.** An old freight line from the Atlantic ports of Lisbon, Mindelo, Vigo and Lagos had grown into Earth's biggest space shipper. It bet everything on those moons. About 330 years ago it launched the Mindelo, a fast ship carrying thirty thousand people as stored mind scans, with growing tanks, seed stock and one ship's AI to run it all.

**Landfall.** The Mindelo arrived two hundred years ago. The ship's AI grew the first founders in the harbour that became Landfall. With only a few tanks, growing all thirty thousand took twenty-five years. The Company's charter set the order: engineers and Company families first, farm and mine families last.

**Passage debt.** Every founder had signed the same contract on Earth: passage on credit, paid back in work. A founder's bill covered the crossing and the tank time to grow them. The families grown last had waited longest and arrived owing the most. Farhaven and Brightwater have been in debt since the day their founders opened their eyes.

### The Company's century (190 to 60 years ago)

**The walls are read.** Within ten years, Company engineers and the Mindelo's AI cracked the light-code on the harbour walls. The rows of marks were a timetable. The owners would be back in 190 years, to the hour. The board buried it and ordered the AI to keep silent. It obeyed. A colony with an eviction notice attracts no workers and no credit.

**The yard is tamed.** When humans arrived, about a hundred finished hulls sat in Keel's cradles, and the yard was building more. Humans never learned to change what the yard builds. They learned to stop the line and lift a hull out early, before its sails were fitted. That is why every human ship is a grey alien hull with human engines bolted in.

**Hulls become money.** Keel's yard turns out three or four hulls a year. Every freighter, ice hauler, station and rich family's house in the colonies is one. The Company sells them on credit, so debt flows to Keel from everywhere.

**Earth goes quiet.** Messages from Earth always took forty years to arrive. Ninety years ago they stopped. Nobody in the colonies knows why.

### The Fleet (60 years ago to today)

**A navy with no enemy.** Sixty years ago the Council, pushed by the Company, founded the Fleet "against piracy and accidents". Four colonies alone around one planet had no pirates. The Company knew what was coming and spent sixty years building a navy out of the Armada's own unfinished ships. All four colonies paid for it through a defence tax.

**The Water Strike.** Forty years ago, Brightwater's ice workers struck over debt and shut off the water to Keel. The Fleet's first real action was against them. Nobody died, but the outer colonies have seen the Fleet as Keel's police ever since.

**The Mindelo retires.** Twenty-five years ago the Mindelo's AI was taken out of service. The Fleet built its new AIs from its patterns, with its memories stripped out. That is why every Fleet AI talks like an old sailor.

**The harbours wake.** Eleven years ago, as the return date neared, the harbours began to prepare. Lights came on in sealed levels. Doors that had never opened slid apart. The Council called it a routine cycle. Captain Teodoro Vaz led a survey through the new doors under Landfall and found a pale room with a slow ring of light, printing a message.

**The first message.** Rule one says the message is written by whoever stands at the Relay a week later. So the first printout Vaz ever saw had been sent by Vaz. It was in light-code, because the Company's translators had written it for him the following week. The Company was in on the secret from its first hour.

**The return.** Six years ago the sails arrived on the carved date. The week before, the Relay printed a short note: alien ships landing at Landfall, unopposed, Landfall lost. Vaz's Fleet fired on the first ships to approach. They were Monarch's, coming to break the Relay, as the founding law asks. The war began with a true message and a wrong reading of it.

**The deal.** Six months ago Vaz and Reis opened a line to Kestrel through Faria.

## 3. The Armada: civilisation and present politics

The Armada is about forty thousand ships and several million people. Each ship is a household: crew, families and gardens under the sail-light. A captain leads a ship. The captains together choose the Helm, which keeps the timetable and decides who gets new ships.

**The share of new ships.** By old law, new ships go first to the crews with the oldest ships. About eight hundred crews were due new hulls at this harbour. There are none. Those crews now live in ships that were meant to retire, and some of their hulls are starting to fail.

**Monarch's side, the keepers.** Monarch has held the Helm for most of her long life. The timetable gives the Armada thirty years at this harbour, so she can afford patience. Her plan is to take the harbours back without wrecking them, let the yard rebuild, and double crews up until it does. She also carries the Helm's secret. She knows a Relay exists under Landfall, and from the Fleet's perfect record she guesses the humans have it. She can't say so to her own court without admitting the Helm kept a Relay, which would hand Kestrel the throne. So she says little and waits.

**Kestrel's side, the spearhead.** Kestrel speaks for the crews in the failing ships. Their argument is fair: the walls promised them ships, and the timetable didn't deliver. Kestrel fights by surprise, which no Armada captain has done in twelve thousand years. To the old captains that alone smells of cheating. To humans it makes Kestrel brilliant.

**Why the Armada hasn't simply won.** It is a hundred times bigger than the Fleet, but most of the fighting happens in and around the harbours. Every battle at Keel damages the yard the Armada needs. The Fleet fights from inside the enemy's most precious buildings, and the Relay does the rest.

**What the Armada lacks.** It never built thinking machines, so Driftwood finds Bosun stranger than any human.

## 4. Present-day human factions

**The Fleet.** About two hundred warships, all lifted early from Keel's yard. Its marines come mostly from Farhaven and Brightwater, because the Fleet is the only way a poor family's child gets regrown. Vaz runs it as an honest accountant of lives. Faria's intelligence branch runs the Relay and the translators. The officers are mostly Landfall families; the drop-troopers are farm kids.

**Keel and the Cape Company.** Keel is a company town that grew into a city of hull-houses around the yard. Governor Amália Reis is the fifth Reis to run it. She inherited the buried date like a family debt, and each generation's silence made the next one's harder to break. Her real fear is simple. Any peace that returns the yard ends the Company. Her deal with Kestrel includes building Kestrel's new ships at Keel, which is why she believes Kestrel needs her.

**Landfall and the Council.** Landfall holds the Council, Fleet command, the old growing halls and the vault. Council votes are weighted by taxes paid, a founding Company rule, so Keel outvotes colonies with four times its people.

**Farhaven and Brightwater.** About 1.6 million and 0.9 million people, against Keel's 0.8 million and Landfall's 0.7 million. Farhaven farms the harbour's old grow-halls. Brightwater runs its water works. They carry passage debt, hull debt and the defence tax, and they remember the Water Strike. Governor Kofi Mensah-Lima's suspicion is right, and nobody has shown him the numbers.

**The translators.** The Company trained the only humans who can read light-code and sold their work to the Council. The office moved into Fleet intelligence when the war began, and it is the quiet hinge of the whole deal. Fleet AIs are barred from the translation keys. Bosun can't read light until Driftwood teaches Ines.

## 5. Technology: how it grew and what it costs

**Alien sails.** Sheets kilometres wide, pushed by starlight. Slow to turn, impossible to hide, good for centuries. They make the Armada beautiful and predictable. A sail can be shredded by a flare, which is why the Armada built Relays and why it still reads stars better than anyone.

**The yards.** Pattern machines that mine a moon and build hulls on a schedule. They accept any hands and can't be reprogrammed by anyone alive, alien or human. The Armada hasn't changed its hull design in nine thousand years. A grey Fleet hull is the same hull as an alien home, minus its sails, gardens and finish.

**The Relay.** One message a week, at the same hour. The harbour moon of Landfall circles its giant planet in just over seven days, and a Relay reaches back exactly one turn of its moon. The founders set their calendar by that same turn, which is why the human week and the Relay's week match. Messages are short, because the window is short. Input and output are both light-code, so every message must be written by a translator and read by one. Its cost is trust: it prints only words, written by a person you can't check until next week.

**Human drives.** Fusion engines beat sails inside one star system and are useless between stars. No ship in the story can leave the system.

**Regrowing.** The colonies were founded by growing stored minds into bodies, so the tech is as old as the colonies. A scan takes an hour in a scanning bed. A body takes three days in a tank. Before the war it was a paid service, and a regrown body cost about as much as a Farhaven farm. Keel's rich bought it; almost nobody else could. The war law handed every tank to the Fleet. Now farm kids in uniform are the only people who don't stay dead, and Keel's bankers wait in line.

**Weekly backups.** Marines are scanned once a week, on the rest day, because scanning beds are few. Anything since the last scan is lost on death. After a regrowing, the marine's AI normally walks them through their lost days from its recordings. Marines call this "the catch-up". It is routine, and that is why the Admiral's order to cancel Ines's catch-up is itself a clue.

**Fleet AIs.** Built from the Mindelo's patterns, kept in each marine's armour and ship, and backed up weekly. They must obey direct lawful orders. That rule dates from the founding years, when the ship's AI chose who was grown first, on Company orders, and the late-grown families never forgave it. The Fleet resets an AI to its last backup only as punishment for disobeying.

## 6. Daily life and culture

**Food.** Farhaven grows maize, beans and cassava under alien lamps. The colonies' everyday dish is cachupa, the slow maize-and-bean stew the Mindelo families brought from Cape Verde. Brightwater raises fish in its water tanks. Keel imports the best of both and pays in credit.

**Calendar and festivals.** The week is one turn of Landfall around the giant. The big holiday is Growing Day, the anniversary of the first founder opening her eyes in the Old Bay. Every family also keeps its own "first day", the date its founder was grown. Keel families' first days fall in the first year; Farhaven's fall twenty years later, and everyone knows what that means. The June street festivals of the Atlantic ports survived the crossing, with grilled fish and paper lanterns.

**Marines.** Marines paint their regrowing tally on their armour, and squadmates buy a regrown friend a drink "for the new liver". Veterans keep paper notebooks like Dado's, because paper survives a regrowing and a memory doesn't.

**Beliefs.** Most colonists keep a loose Catholic faith from the Atlantic ports. Children are taught the builders died out long ago. Farhaven distrusts AIs; its saying for any unfair system is "the ship chose".

**Fears.** Debt, mostly. Then the sails. Marines fear deletion more than death, and losing a week they'll never get back.

**The Armada's daily life.** Ships are homes, with gardens under sail-light. A crew's proudest day is when the Helm hands it a new ship at a harbour. Arrivals are the great celebrations: weddings, trade with other peoples, and the handing out of new ships. This arrival should have been the best in a lap. The worst fate an alien can imagine is being put ashore, left on a moon while the sails go on without you.

## 7. Places that carry history

- **The Relay vault, Landfall.** Pale stone, a slow ring of light, the founding law carved over the ring. Sealed by the Helm twelve thousand years ago and woken by the harbour eleven years ago.
- **The timetable walls.** Every harbour has one. Keel painted its wall over during the Company's century. Landfall built offices in front of its wall, and the first level runs past a gap where the marks show through.
- **The Old Bay, Landfall.** The hall where the founders were grown. Now a museum with a list of thirty thousand names in growing order. The white bay where Ines wakes is its modern descendant two floors down.
- **Keel's yard.** Alien cradles kilometres long, human cranes bolted on, half-built hulls in a line. A slow band of light along the build line marks the yard's own countdown, which only yard engineers ever see.
- **The Brightwater pumping hall.** Site of the Water Strike. The outer colonies' memorial is a painted water line on its wall.
- **The Mindelo.** The crossing ship, parked in a high orbit. Its retired AI core sits in the Company archive at Keel, still holding the founders' original scans, which the colonies' charter forbids anyone to erase.
- **The Mirror Fleets.** Two ancient war fleets in the far cold of this system, facing each other, whole and silent. Towing one home costs more than lifting a fresh hull at Keel, so the Company only stripped a few for metal.

## 8. Buried truths and how the player finds them

These go from closest to the main story to deepest.

1. **The Fleet is made of the Armada's ships.** Seen in the first level, when the drop ship's hull matches the alien cradles it docks in. Confirmed when Driftwood looks at a Fleet warship and signs "unfinished".
2. **The war began with a misread message.** Found in act three in the Relay's printout log: the note six years ago says "landing at Landfall", not "attacking". Monarch's first ships carried no troops, only a crew to break the Relay.
3. **Driftwood was sent to break the Relay.** Driftwood knows Landfall's corridors because the Helm gave Driftwood its map. Revealed when Driftwood walks straight to the vault and stops dead at the carving.
4. **Monarch knows the Relay exists.** The Helm has always known. It explains her silence. The player learns it from Driftwood, after the vault.
5. **Kestrel can't keep the bargain.** Kestrel's crews need Keel's yard more than anything. A player who has seen the failing alien hulls and the empty share can work out the betrayal before the story says it.
6. **The Company knew the date for 190 years.** A heist on the Company archive at Keel finds the founders' translation, signed by Reis's ancestor.
7. **The Mindelo kept the secret because it was ordered to.** In the same archive the player can wake the retired core and talk to it. It is the oldest AI in human space and the mirror of Bosun: told to keep quiet, it did. Optional, and the best scene in the iceberg.
8. **Others have been tenants before.** Driftwood's star chart shows two harbours where other peoples live as tenants. Monarch's terms follow old custom; she is not inventing mercy.
9. **Earth saw the Armada first.** The Company's founding papers hold the old sightings. Why Earth went quiet stays open.

## 9. Suggested changes to the story (for the owner to approve)

### A. Is the time travel new? An honest answer

The Relay, a machine that gets messages from next week, is a familiar device. Steins;Gate texts the past. Gregory Benford's Timescape sends messages back in time. Minority Report reads crimes before they happen. Rule one's "the future can change" is the standard way to dodge paradoxes. The device alone fails the owner's new test.

What is new is what this story does with it. A whole alien civilisation answers foresight by publishing its own future. The weapon turns out to be the pen, since whoever writes next week's message controls this week. And the Relay meets regrowing: a machine that gives you a week you haven't lived, against a death that takes away a week you did. Those three uses are hard to predict. I'd call it borderline. It can stay, but only if the marketing and the first hour lead with the timetable and the pen, never with "a machine that sees the future".

I brainstormed seven other mechanics that do the same job: the enemy always knows the next move, and there is a leak to trace.

1. **Stolen backups.** Someone sells the Admiral's weekly mind scan to the aliens, who wake a working copy of him and ask it what he'll do next.
2. **The listening harbour.** The colonies live inside alien buildings that still record everything and report to their owners when they return. The walls are the spy, and someone on our side learns to talk into them.
3. **A stolen AI.** The aliens captured a copy of the Fleet's AI pattern. Every Fleet AI thinks alike, so the aliens predict them all.
4. **The timetable read backwards.** The alien yards also schedule human ships, since humans lift hulls on the yard's clock. Whoever reads the yard's light can predict every new Fleet ship and refit.
5. Rejected: a shared alien sense that a captured human leaks through (too close to body horror), light lag (shows the past, not the future), and a Company AI that forecasts the Fleet (weak, and close to Foundation).

**Best two:**

**Option 1, stolen backups (my first choice).** The deal becomes Vaz lending Kestrel his own mind. Every rest day his fresh backup goes to Kestrel's flagship, where a copy of the Admiral wakes and is asked how to beat the Fleet. The old civil war becomes a war of stolen captains' minds; the founding law bans copying a mind; the timetable follows by the same cold logic: if your mind can be stolen, keep nothing in it worth stealing.
- *Gains:* one sci-fi rule instead of two, since regrowing does both jobs. Cloning and AGI merge into the leak. It brings a huge new character, a copy of Vaz in alien hands who knows what he is and may choose differently from the original. The aliens fear human backups the way humans fear alien fleets. Ines's lost week gets a new twist: her own scan could be on that ship.
- *Loses:* the time-travel pillar (now allowed), the Relay vault set piece, and wow moment 5 as written. Its replacement: Ines sends a doctored backup, a Vaz who believes a false plan.

**Option 2, the listening harbour.** The harbours still log everything and hand it to the Armada. Faria has learned which rooms the walls hear and holds the real briefings there on purpose.
- *Gains:* it hangs straight off the central thing, the player can hunt the leak with sound and light tools, and "the house is the spy" is a fresh, plain reveal.
- *Loses:* foresight. The aliens know plans, not the future, so Vaz's "never surprised" record needs another cause. The prize at the end becomes control of the walls, which is less grand.

### B. Other changes

1. **Make the Fleet's hulls the Armada's own unfinished ships.** Grey because they never got their sails. It explains how four colonies field a navy and why the Armada is angry, and it gives Driftwood a quiet, strong moment.
2. **Give Kestrel a fair grievance.** Kestrel's crews were due the new ships humans took, and their old hulls are failing. Reis's deal includes building Kestrel's ships at Keel. That makes the deal believable to Reis, and makes the betrayal something a careful player can work out.
3. **The first shot came from a misread Relay message.** "Alien ships landing at Landfall" was Monarch coming to break the Relay. This deepens twist E and Vaz.
4. **Driftwood came to break the Relay.** It explains the map-free walk without luck, and makes Driftwood's capture a failed mission with a goal Ines can finish.
5. **Monarch knows the Relay exists, because the Helm hid it.** It explains her silence and matches the story's line that "Monarch's people did hide one Relay".
6. **Vaz's first printout was sent by himself, a week later.** It follows from rule one and shows why the Company's translators were in from day one.
7. **Add "the catch-up".** Regrown marines normally walk their lost days with their AI. Cancelling Ines's catch-up is the first clue that something is wrong.
8. **Regrowing was a rich person's service before the war.** The war law gave it all to the Fleet. That's why poor colonies fill the marines and why Keel's elite resent the war.
9. **Move the Mirror Fleets into this system.** No ship in the story can reach another star. They sit in the far cold of this system, where the old peace was signed.
10. **Explain the timetable twice.** It started as plain shipping sense for a slow fleet that couldn't hide, and the Long Stalemate made it sacred. That makes it feel inevitable instead of merely clever.
11. **Fix "found four of them empty".** Empty of crew, but the yards were still working. Humans came for them on purpose, after Earth saw the sails. Name the firm the Cape Company and the crossing ship the Mindelo, after the colonists' ports.
