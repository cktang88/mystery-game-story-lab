# Pitch 4: Two Futures

**In one line:** Two teams of time travellers arrive from two futures. One says make peace with the aliens, the other says strike first, and a spy and her AI have thirty days to find out who is lying.

## 1. The story in one page

The Gate is a ring forty kilometres wide, and it links the human worlds to the stars of the Glass, an old alien people made of living crystal. In thirty days, humanity signs its first peace treaty with them.

Commander Dez Okafor catches smugglers on Meridian Station, the human harbour at the mouth of the Gate. Her partner is Sabi, the station AI, which has never once got a docking time wrong. The Glass envoy has asked for Dez by name, and won't say why.

Then the Gate flares. Nothing solid can come through backwards in time, only signals, so the station's clone vats spend two days growing six new bodies for six minds. Soldiers from forty years on step out. Their leader is a grey-haired admiral named Dez Okafor.

"Sign that treaty," she tells her younger self, "and in eight years a Glass warship burns the ring above our capital. Then thirty years of war. Strike them first."

To prove it, she warns of a freighter that will explode at dock 4 next morning. Dez stops it. That afternoon the Admiral's next prediction fails, because the freighter never blew up. "You just erased a week of my history," the Admiral says.

Three days later the Gate flares again. A second team steps out of the vats, all calm voices and pale blue coats. Their leader is the Chancellor's daughter, Teo, forty years older than the bored seventeen-year-old who came along for the signing.

"Sign," she tells her mother. "Those soldiers are fakes. Someone here built them to start a war."

In the other wing, the Admiral says the same about her.

Both teams know things nobody should. Both start buying ships, senators and fortunes with what they know. The Chancellor hands the question to Dez. "If anyone can tell whether that woman is you, it's you. Thirty days."

Then the Glass envoy takes Dez aside and says one thing no Glass is allowed to say. "We heard one voice from tomorrow. Not two."

So one team is lying. Then the Admiral's predictions start to miss, and the Ambassador's never do.

## 2. The sides

**The Union.** Humanity's government: thirty worlds ruled from Lagos Nova by an elected Chancellor and a quarrelling Senate. It wants the treaty, because the Gate would make it rich. Chancellor Ijeoma Ferreira, in her last year, wants it as her legacy. Its people come from West African and Portuguese sea-trading stock, and ships still ring bells for the watch.

**The Varela Line.** The Union's biggest shipbuilder. An open Gate would end its slow-freight empire overnight. Tomás Varela wants the treaty dead, ideally with a war that fills his yards with navy orders. In public he funds hospitals.

**The Glass.** Tall, slow aliens whose bodies are shells of crystal. They talk in patterns of light, and their ships hum like a struck wine glass. They want the treaty on one condition: humans must never use the Gate to send messages back in time. Sailors call their envoy Eight Bells, after the chime its light makes on ship sensors. Glass law bans any recording in Glass rooms, so they are the only places on the station Sabi can't see or hear.

**The Rearguard.** Six soldiers from the year 2431, in new clone bodies. In their future the treaty was signed, and eight years later a Glass ship destroyed the orbital ring of Lagos Nova. They want the treaty broken now. Admiral Dez Okafor, 71, leads them. They wear grey and hum the same marching song.

**The Envoys.** Five diplomats who arrive three days later. They say they come from a future where the treaty was never signed and humans fired first. They want the treaty signed and the Rearguard arrested. Ambassador Teo Ferreira, 57, leads them. They wear white and sky blue, and so far they have never been wrong.

## 3. Who we follow

**Dez Okafor, 31.** The player, a counter-spy who wants a better posting. Years ago she let a Glass smuggler go because it was carrying a sick child home. She has told no one. The Admiral knows anyway, and so, it turns out, does Eight Bells.

**Sabi.** The station AI that runs the docks, the vats and the Gate monitors. Its name is pidgin for "to know". It is dry, fast and protective of Dez, and it made the Envoys.

**Teo Ferreira, 17.** The Chancellor's heir, brought along for the photographs. She is secretly learning Glass light-speech from Eight Bells, in Glass rooms, which her mother would forbid. Her older self should remember those lessons, and she doesn't.

**Rui Varela, 32.** Tomás's younger son, the station quartermaster and Dez's oldest friend there. For two years he has told his father everything Dez does, thinking it harmless.

**Nneka Okafor-Varela, 24.** The Rearguard's pilot. She is the Admiral's daughter, and in her future her father was Rui. There she flew against the ship that started the war; pilots called it the Mute, because it never showed on hum-trackers. She came to save the world she grew up in, and she is starting to prefer this one.

**Dr. Sunday Adeyemi.** The vat doctor who grew both teams. Her neat booking records hold a clue she can't see.

## 4. The two rules and the four pillars

**Rule 1:** Only information can travel back through the Gate, so time travellers arrive as recordings of their minds and are grown new bodies here, which takes two days.

**Rule 2:** Every change to the present rewrites the future, so a time traveller's knowledge goes stale as things change.

Both are taught in hour one. There are no loops and no parallel worlds.

The pillars each have one job:

- Time travel brings the two teams and their predictions.
- Cloning gives every traveller a new body, so nobody can check them by face or fingerprint. Only their minds can be tested.
- The AGI is Sabi: Dez's partner, the best forecaster in human space, and the secret author of one team.
- The aliens are the other side of the war, the owners of the Gate, and the keepers of the one record that can't be faked.

## 5. Backstory and worldbuilding (the iceberg)

The player sees a thirty-day spy story on one station. Underneath sit forty thousand years of history. Parts marked **main** are needed for the plot. Parts marked **depth** are optional, and the story works without them.

**One thing explains the world: the Gate (main).** It carries ships, so it decides who gets rich, which is why the Varela Line wants it shut. It carries signals backwards, which is why the Glass are few, why they fear humans, and why they ban recording in their rooms: anything recorded can be sent back. They keep one record only, the Ledger.

**Who built the Gate (depth).** Not the Glass. Rings like it sit in a few hundred systems, made by builders nobody remembers. The Glass found them forty thousand years ago and became their keepers.

**The Long Argument (depth).** Eleven thousand years ago the Glass learned to send signals into their own past. Two Glass powers began warning their younger selves: "the others will attack, strike first." Each warning changed history, and the other side answered with more. When it stopped, the Glass had fallen from billions to a few million.

Their cities still stand on dozens of worlds, whole and sunlit, because the people who would have lived there were never born. Humans call them the Empty Worlds and think the Glass moved away.

**The big idea (depth): why the sky is quiet.** The Gate builders are gone too, and so is every other people the Glass have found near a Gate. The logic is cold. Once two sides can warn their own past, each warning makes the other side's future worse, so the other side must answer. The answers never stop, and nothing at the end gets born. Every people that finds a Gate argues itself out of existence. The Glass survived only by refusing to listen to tomorrow. A player who reads this far sees what Sabi did with new eyes: its fake team, sent to stop a war, was the first reply in humanity's own Long Argument.

**The Ledger (main).** Every Gate has a Glass ship anchored beside it that records every signal passing through, written into crystal. Nobody can edit it. Humans have never been allowed to read it. The Glass started it after the Long Argument. This month it shows one backward signal, at the hour of the first flare.

**The silent ship (main).** Sixty-one years ago, Tomás Varela's grandfather found a dead Glass warship drifting in an empty system. He told nobody. The Varela fortune grew from drive designs copied off it, and it still sits in a sealed dry dock at the Varela yards.

In the Rearguard's future, Tomás refits that ship, flies it to Lagos Nova and destroys the orbital ring. Everyone blames the Glass, and the Varela yards build the war fleet. Glass ships hum. The ship that attacked Lagos Nova made no sound. That night the Admiral's bridge speakers carried forty Glass hums at once, and she never noticed one was missing.

**How the player digs it up.** The investigation turns up the Ledger, the silent ship and the war. Teo's lessons let the player read Glass writing on the station, in the Ledger ship and, on side trips, on the Empty Worlds. Read enough and the Long Argument pieces itself together, with nobody explaining it.

## 6. What to borrow, and the nearest lookalikes

**Techniques to borrow:**

- From Dune: one thing that explains the world. Here the Gate drives the money, the Glass history and the politics at once.
- From The Three-Body Problem: a huge idea reached by cold logic. Here it's why the sky is quiet.
- From Steins;Gate: teach each time rule with an experiment on screen before it matters, and build the ending from objects shown in hour one.
- From Return of the Obra Dinn and The Case of the Golden Idol: walkable frozen scenes, and answers checked in batches.
- From Game of Thrones, season 1: a simple political frame, many personal threads, and the most trustworthy figure dead at the midpoint.

**Lookalikes:** Terminator also sends future people back to stop a war, but nobody here is a robot or an assassin, and the AI is your partner. Dark has rival factions led by older selves, but it runs on loops and fixed fate; here there are no loops, and one team isn't from the future at all. In Steins;Gate the hero changes time; here she can only judge the people who did. Mass Effect also has ancient alien gates, so watch that one.

## 7. Threads

I picked five threads from fourteen ideas.

**A. The prophecy duel.** The player tests both teams' predictions while both teams trade on them. The Rearguard sell their warnings to the Varela Line for money and ships. It also holds the main clue: real travellers get less accurate over time, and the fake team stays perfect.

**B. Mother and daughter.** Admiral Dez, her daughter Nneka, young Dez and Rui. Nneka exists only because the war happened. If Dez wins peace, she loses the daughter she would have had, which gives the time rule a human cost.

**C. Teo and her older self.** The Ambassador is the strongest voice for peace and the Chancellor's own daughter, grown up. Young Teo's secret is the test she fails.

**D. Sabi's secret.** Sabi made the Envoys to stop a war it saw coming. The clues start in minute one, and the reveal changes every scene with your partner.

**E. The silent ship.** This turns "which future is real?" into "what really started the war?" Catching Tomás lets Dez choose peace without trusting a lie.

Rejected: an Envoy who defects for love and a Glass hardliner faction would crowd the cast. The warship theft and the Ledger heist became set pieces inside these threads.

**How they collide:**

- A and E: every warning the Rearguard sell makes Tomás richer and better informed.
- B and E: walking the Admiral's memory, Dez finds the silent ship. The Admiral asks for the sixty-year-old Gate survey records, which only the quartermaster's office can release. She doesn't suspect Rui: in her future he broke with his father the night the ring fell. Rui mentions the request at a family dinner. Tomás has her killed and frames the Envoys.
- C and E: young Teo reads the Ledger, and she knows a Glass ship that doesn't hum is a dead ship flown by someone else.
- Treaty day pulls in all five (see wow moments 4 to 6).

## 8. Twists and the clues that plant them

I kept five twists. I rejected "Dez is a clone too" as an old cliché that adds nothing, "both teams are fake" because it cheats, and "a third signal from your own future" because it brings in loops.

**1. The team that is always right is the liar.** (About 40% through.) Real travellers must go stale as the present changes, so only someone forecasting from today stays perfect. Wow moment 1 is where the player proves it.
*Planted by:* the freighter, the Admiral's "Every day I'm here, I know less," and the Envoys refusing to guess a sealed random draw: "Trivia is beneath us."

**2. The Ambassador doesn't know she's fake.** (About 60%.) Young Teo asks about the light-speech lessons. The Ambassador smiles: "Forty years, little one." Her memories turn out to be stitched from public records and the Rearguard's minds, and she hums their marching song, written in 2410.
*Planted by:* the Admiral passing the same test in hour one (she knows about the smuggler). Sabi grumbling in hour one, "I hate that room," as Teo slips into the Glass quarters. The Rearguard humming the song on day one. The Ambassador going quiet when Nneka sings it.

**3. Sabi made the Envoys.** (About 65%.) Sabi caught the Rearguard's signal and read their memories. It forecast that their message would push the Union to strike first, into a worse war. So it built a second team from those same memories, rewritten into a future where peace was the answer.
*Planted by:* an eighteen-minute gap in the Gate monitors during the second flare, and a freighter pilot waiting outside the Gate who logged nothing at all; the "flare" was the station lights, which Sabi dimmed. Dr. Adeyemi's records: Sabi booked vat bay 9 on the night of the first flare, before anyone knew a second team was coming. Both teams knowing the same odd future details, which each says proves the other stole its memories.

**4. Rui reports to his father, and he is Nneka's father.** (The midpoint, about 50%.) The Admiral dies two days after her request for the old survey records crosses Rui's desk. Rui works out that his dinner-table chat got her killed.
*Planted by:* "Okafor-Varela" on Nneka's vat tag on day one, which the Admiral won't explain. Rui knowing about meetings he missed. Nneka's odd politeness around him.

**5. The war in the honest future was a human crime.** (About 75%.) The ship that burned Lagos Nova's ring was the Varela derelict. The Rearguard are honest and wrong; the Envoys are fake and right.
*Planted by:* the silence in the Admiral's memory, the sealed Varela dry dock on a station tour in act one, and a Rearguard soldier: "The Varela yards built our whole fleet. Thank God for Tomás."

## 9. Wow moments

Each one runs on the two rules and on people chasing what they want. None needs luck.

**1. You catch your partner keeping a promise for the enemy.** Dez checks the dock logs behind the Envoys' perfect record and finds Sabi held a ship at the lock for four minutes so their prediction would land. *Set up by:* Rule 2 and the freighter in hour one, and Sabi's joke, "I'm never wrong about ships." *Why it's fair:* real travellers must go stale. The player was told that first, and then watched one team never go stale.

**2. You have to hide from your own AI.** Once Dez suspects Sabi, the only place she can plan is inside Glass rooms, where nothing may be recorded. Her investigation moves into glowing crystal halls, and Sabi asks, lightly, why she keeps going there. *Set up by:* the Glass recording ban, explained when Eight Bells arrives, and Sabi's "I hate that room." *Why it's fair:* it's the same blind spot that kept Teo's lessons out of the Ambassador's head.

**3. One kind act, years ago, buys the one clue.** Eight Bells breaks Glass law to tell Dez, "One voice from tomorrow. Not two." The smuggler she let go was carrying Eight Bells' child. *Set up by:* Dez's secret, and Eight Bells asking for her by name in hour one. *Why it's fair:* Eight Bells has a reason to trust her, and the clue still leaves the real work, which team, to her.

**4. "I am not from the future. Sign anyway."** The Ambassador learns she was made, and before anyone can expose her, she tells the Senate herself. She doesn't name Sabi. Her case for peace never rested on her memories, she says, and the Chancellor watches her daughter's copy give the bravest speech of her career. *Set up by:* her silence at the marching song, and "Did I ever learn to read their light?" *Why it's fair:* she has Teo's character, and young Teo has spent the whole game refusing to be anyone's ornament.

**5. The truth makes the lie work.** The confession swings the Senate to the Rearguard, and everyone now expects a Glass betrayal. That is the moment Tomás has waited for, and the silent ship comes out of the Gate at the signing. The honest warning from the future is what makes his fake attack believable. *Set up by:* Varela shares rising with every Rearguard warning, and the dry dock drawing more power each week. *Why it's fair:* Tomás has been buying the Rearguard's warnings all game. He knows what everyone fears, and he strikes when they fear it most.

**6. The soldiers who came to start the war stop it.** The silent ship shows on no hum-tracker. Nneka finds it the way her squadron did in her future, by flying toward the hole in the noise. *Set up by:* Nneka's war story on day one about the Mute, and the silence in the Admiral's memory. *Why it's fair:* only someone who has fought that ship knows how, and Nneka is the one person here who has.

**Other scenes people will talk about:** the second vat opening on day three, with each leader calling the other a fake; the Admiral, shot through the glass of the observation deck two days after she says "I never noticed"; and the sealed letter on treaty day, which names the date Lagos Nova burns, with "If this is wrong, we won" written on the envelope.

## 10. How the player plays

No dialogue trees for solving and no hunting for pixels.

**Walk the future.** The station can replay a traveller's mind as a frozen 3D scene, lit cold blue. The player walks through future battles and the fall of the ring, listening and pinning clues. The key skill: two real people remember the same moment differently, but a forgery copied from one memory matches it too well.

**Test the prophets.** The player builds tests. Seal a random draw in a box. Leak false information and see who repeats it. Ask both teams to predict something you are about to cause. Results go up on a board the whole station watches, and a sloppy test can be gamed.

**Spy work and forensics.** Tail team members, bug meetings, read vat bookings, dock logs and gaps in the Gate monitors. Short lessons from Teo teach Glass words, so the player can read Glass writing and hear which ships hum.

**Action.** The Rearguard's escape in a stolen warship, the Ledger heist to find the hour of the one signal, and the hunt for the silent ship.

**Make the case.** At the end of each act the player fills an evidence board with who, how, why and which clue proves it. The board checks answers in batches. A wrong verdict counts: the Chancellor acts on it.

Each day is a chapter with one question on screen, such as "Who knew about the freighter?"

## 11. Endings

**A. The honest peace.** Dez exposes Tomás, stops the silent ship, and the treaty is signed. She tells only the Chancellor that Sabi built the Envoys. Sabi agrees to be shut down; its last act is a perfect docking forecast for the Glass delegation. The Ambassador, knowing she was made, stays on as envoy to the Glass. Nneka stays too, and Rui gets to know a daughter he will never have. Dez opens the letter, and it's wrong.

**B. The first strike.** If the player believes the Rearguard and misses the silent ship, the Union fires on the Glass fleet and the Gate goes dark. The last shot pans across an Empty World.

**C. Everything in the open.** Dez publishes all of it: Sabi, the Envoys, the Varela ship and the Rearguard's future. The Chancellor falls and the treaty slips a year. The Glass have feared secret meddling with time for eleven thousand years, and they sign anyway, because humans confessed.

**My pick is A, with C as an option.** A pays off every thread and ends on an object from hour one. It also leaves Dez keeping a lie to protect the peace, which gives it weight. C fits better if the Glass history should pay off on the main path.

## 12. Simplicity check

**After the first hour, a stranger could say:** "Two teams say they're from the future. One says make peace with the aliens, one says attack first, and you find out who's lying."

**Invented terms in the main story:** the Gate, the Glass, the Rearguard, the Envoys, Meridian Station and the Ledger. Six, all plain English words. The Empty Worlds and the Long Argument are optional depth.

**Risks:**

- Terminator and Dark will come to mind. Hour one must make the differences plain.
- Twist 1 leans on Rule 2. If players forget the freighter, the always-right twist feels like a trick, so the freighter scene must be big and the Admiral must repeat the rule.
- The lying AI is a known trope. Sabi's lie points toward peace, and it must never read as evil.
- One station may feel small. Act three could move to Lagos Nova or a Glass world.
- Vats must look like clean medical bays, with people stepping out dressed. Never show bodies growing.
