# Pitch 4: Two Futures, world challenge log

How `world.md` was argued into shape. Round 1 is the first draft. Rounds 2 to 5 attack it. Round 6 found nothing serious, so the work stopped there.

## Round 1: first draft (summary)

- **Central thing:** control of the Gate, the one ring that links human space to the Glass.
- **Glass history:** builders unknown; the Glass found the rings 40,000 years ago; the Long Argument 11,000 years ago killed most of them in a time war; the Ledger is a secret Glass record of signals.
- **Humans:** Lagos–Lisbon seed ships 300 years ago; vat-born settlers; old shipping families own the banks; the Union rules thirty worlds.
- **Varela:** grandfather found a Glass warship, lost about seventy years ago, in a distant empty system; copied its drives.
- **First contact:** the Glass arrived through the Gate in the 2330s for reasons of their own.
- **Varela's motive:** an open Gate kills slow freight.
- **Time travel:** mind recording was invented in the future war for the purpose.
- **Vat-born class line:** vat-born people are the underclass; the old families are womb-born.

## Round 2: the central thing and the economy

**1. Why is the Gate the central thing, and not something everyone lives under?**
A thing one actor controls is a prize, not a world. Spice shapes Dune because everyone eats it, trades it and fears losing it. The Gate only matters to people near Meridian. I looked for the constraint beneath the Gate: why would opening it change anyone's life? Because without it, nothing moves faster than a ship, news included.
*Change:* the central thing became **the lag**: news can travel no faster than a ship. The Gate matters because it breaks the lag, across space and backwards in time.

**2. Why can't news beat ships? Build a chain of radio relays.**
Radio moves at light speed. Ships beat light. Any relay is slower than a courier.
*Change:* none to the rule. Added a line explaining it.

**3. Then why not put the news on a tiny drone?**
They do. The drive goes as fast on a drone as on a liner, so news still moves at ship speed. The contest becomes who launches first, from closer, more often.
*Change:* added courier drones, run by the banks and the Union Post, and courier forgery as an old crime.

**4. What would 300 years of the lag really produce in money?**
Cargo in flight for weeks needs credit, as in the real age of sail. Whoever hears the news first wins the market, as with real bankers and early news of battles. So the banks would be cargo lenders and courier owners.
*Change:* the old houses' power now comes from cargo credit and couriers. This gives a concrete reason they hate the Gate.

**5. What would it produce in politics?**
Orders that land five weeks late. Real empires in that position, such as Spain's in the Americas, gave governors huge freedom. So the Union is a loose league, and governors like it that way.
*Change:* governors and the charter worlds become natural treaty opponents.

**6. What would people do about the lag with AI?**
If you can't get news early, you guess it. Forecasting machines would be the most valuable tools in the Union, and the first one to trade on its guesses would cause a crash.
*Change:* added the 2353 Porto Novo crash and the Pilot Law (AIs may forecast, never act, never hide a forecast). Sabi now exists because of the lag, and its secret becomes a legal crime with clear limits.

**7. Is "vat-born underclass" real if every first settler was vat-grown?**
No. Everyone descends from the vats. A class line drawn there makes no sense.
*Change:* the real line is debt. The ship charters made everyone grown aboard owe the cost of their own making. The charter houses held that **passage debt**. Today's split is charter houses against passage stock. The houses now give birth the old way as a status mark, so "first vat-born Chancellor" still means something.

**8. Was the passage debt ever paid? Over 500 years, what happens to a debt nobody can pay?**
It gets restructured, and somebody else ends up paying. Whole worlds stopped paying in the 2290s. The Union was founded to settle it.
*Change:* the Settlement of 2301 "cancelled" the debt by buying it with bonds paid from a freight toll. This is the Union's founding lie, and it ties the debt to freight, so the Gate threatens the bondholders too.

**9. Why would sea-trading families fund ships that take 150 years to pay back?**
Because the charters made the debt compound for the whole trip, and because owning a world forever is the longest bond there is. Shipping houses already think in long voyages and paper promises.
*Change:* none, but the charter logic is now stated.

**10. Does the lag make Rule 2 easier or harder to follow?**
Easier. Everyone in this world already knows news goes stale. The Admiral's knowledge decays the same way.
*Change:* added "What news?", "stale", post day and a lag clock to daily life. Suggested showing them in hour one (story change D).

**11. Is the lag too close to Dune's spice, which also links travel and foresight?**
Spice is a substance that grants powers. The lag is a limit with no owner. The likeness is in method (one thing every part hangs on), which the brief asks for.
*Change:* none.

## Round 3: the Glass and deep time

**1. Why did the Glass ever let the Argument start, if they'd seen the builders' empty worlds?**
In draft 1 they had seen them and ignored the warning. That makes them foolish. More natural: they assumed the builders moved on, which is exactly the mistake humans now make about the Glass. They only understood the builders after their own Argument.
*Change:* the Glass learned what the builders' cities meant after the Argument. "We are the second" became the deep reveal.

**2. Billions dying in a time war: does that break the tone rule?**
A slaughter would. And it isn't what Rule 2 predicts. If no future holds still, the first thing that fails is long planning. A slow, long-lived people who plan children over centuries would simply stop.
*Change:* the Glass fell from billions to millions mostly because they stopped growing children. The Empty Worlds were built for children who never came. Cooler, sadder in a quiet way, and it follows from the rule.

**3. How did the Argument stop? A ban needs enforcing and invites cheating.**
Cold logic: a message from the future is only an edge while it is secret. Publish every one and nobody gains by sending.
*Change:* the Ledger is now a shared record every Glass world reads, with an oath not to act on it. In draft 1 it was secret, which made no sense. The treaty offers humans a seat. Eight Bells' hint breaks a real but small law.

**4. Can anyone send a signal that dodges the Ledger?**
Every signal must come out of a lit ring, and a Ledger ship sits at every lit ring. In peace the system is complete. Only seizing a ring and its Ledger ship breaks it, which is what the Union does in the Rearguard's future.
*Change:* war becomes the one way the system fails, which raises the stakes of the treaty.

**5. Why did the Glass take 29,000 years to discover the back channel?**
In draft 1 there was no reason. Better: the effect always showed as a tiny "early echo", a signal arriving a fraction of a second early, and a slow people took ages to learn to stretch it.
*Change:* added the early echo. Humans saw it in 2352 and the Navy hid it, which gives the Glass a reason to hurry the treaty.

**6. Why does Glass law ban recording?**
In draft 1 it was a custom with no cause. A people whose disaster came from recorded messages sent back would distrust every recording.
*Change:* recording is banned except for the Ledger. The story's camera-free Glass rooms now have a root.

**7. Why would warships hum and give themselves away?**
The hull is alive and hums when the drive runs. In a time war, stealth is pointless because the enemy knows your moves from the future. So the Glass never bred quiet ships.
*Change:* added. Silence now means a dead hull flown by someone else.

**8. A few million Glass guard a few hundred rings against six billion humans. Why don't they just shut the door?**
Because humans have a ring in their own space, copied a Glass drive, and have seen the early echo. Shutting the door only delays the day humans learn the trick alone, outside the Ledger. Binding them inside is safer.
*Change:* the Glass motive for the treaty is now cold and clear. A minority of Glass would still shut the door, kept in depth only.

**9. Why are Glass so slow and their tech unchanged?**
Crystal chemistry is slow and they live 3,000 years. After the Argument, every new project felt like a risk.
*Change:* Glass tech froze 11,000 years ago. This lets an ancient wreck pass for a modern warship (see Round 4).

**10. What do the Glass trade for? They seem to want nothing.**
A people who stopped making new things would value novelty: songs, recipes, games, stories.
*Change:* the licensed trade swaps grown crystal for new human things.

**11. Is "ancient builders who left gates" a cliché?**
Yes: Stargate, Mass Effect and Halo all have it. The story already needs the rings, so I kept them and made the builders a mirror instead of a lost super-race. Nobody hunts their tech; their only legacy is a warning.
*Change:* builders stay unseen. Their one role is "we are the second".

## Round 4: factions, places and fit with the story

**1. The derelict was lost seventy years ago. Why did the Glass never come looking?**
They would have. Draft 1 needed the Glass to be careless.
*Change:* the wreck is from the Long Argument, 11,000 years old, left from a battle at the Meridian ring itself. Because Glass ships haven't changed, it still looks like a Glass warship.

**2. Why did first contact happen at Meridian, in 2338?**
Draft 1 had no reason, which is a coincidence that helps the plot.
*Change:* Varela test runs of the copied drive near the dark ring threw off a wake that looked to the Glass like their warship waking. They came to look. Varela caused first contact.

**3. If the Glass came looking, why didn't they find it?**
They searched for three years. Then a Varela cousin commanding the cruiser *Calabar* fired on them.
*Change:* added the 2341 Meridian Skirmish. The Gate went dark for nine years, during which Varela hid the wreck in Dock Zero. This explains "first peace treaty" and plants Twist 5 decades deep.

**4. Why does Tomás need to strike now, not just lobby?**
Slow freight dying is a slow problem. A rich man fights a slow problem with lawyers.
*Change:* the treaty's inspection clause would put Glass inspectors in Dock Zero. In the honest future he stalled it for seven years and struck when the Senate finally ordered it, which explains the eight-year gap. In the present, the Rearguard's warnings show him that the attack worked, so he moves it to the signing. The clause exists because the Glass have suspected the belt since 2341.

**5. How do fewer than twenty people keep a warship secret for sixty years?**
Family firm, lifers, high pay and a cover story. Most staff think the hull is a legal Glass hull plate bought in the licensed trade.
*Change:* added the cover story. Rui, as quartermaster, has never been inside.

**6. Why is the station at the Gate built by Varela, a man with something to hide next door?**
Because Varela already owned the system's only yards and mining claim, so it was the cheap builder. Keeping close also let him watch.
*Change:* added.

**7. Mind recording invented in the future just for time travel? That's one rule bent for one plot.**
Yes, it was. Better if it already exists.
*Change:* added sending: in the 2230s to 2260s, colonies radioed minds to each other and grew people in vats, because radio beat slow ships. Faster drives ended it. Vats, recordings and doubles feel ordinary, and the Rearguard simply used an old method through time. Also: a genome is just data, so DNA proves nothing.

**8. Why did the Rearguard go back exactly forty years, to thirty days before the treaty?**
If the date fell out of a cost limit, that would be a coincidence.
*Change:* two limits plus a choice. A signal can only come out while the ring was lit (never before 2338), and cost climbs steeply per year. Within those, the Rearguard chose the last moment before the signing, when every decision-maker and the vats were on Meridian.

**9. Why not go back to 2338 and stop first contact?**
They didn't know about Varela, and the cost of 53 years was beyond them. Also, their target was the treaty.
*Change:* none beyond the cost rule.

**10. How did the Union in the future learn to send signals back at all?**
It captured the Ledger ship at Meridian in 2412, held the classified early-echo data, and spent a decade on it.
*Change:* added to the future timeline.

**11. Don't the Glass know which team is real, since the Ledger records content?**
Yes. That is fine. Their law forbids acting on it, and Eight Bells can only hint at the count. The heist lets Teo read it.
*Change:* stated in world.md.

**12. Where did human faster-than-light drives come from before Varela?**
A crude human drive from 2268. Varela's copy made it four times faster.
*Change:* dated eras added. This also explains why the Union grew quickly after 2335.

## Round 5: is the time travel new? (owner's new rule)

**1. Is "only signals go back" new?**
No. Steins;Gate texts the past and *Timescape* sends signals to 1962.

**2. Is "minds sent back into grown bodies" new?**
No. The TV show *Travelers* sends minds back into hosts. Growing new bodies is a small twist on it.

**3. Is "changes make knowledge stale" new?**
The idea that changes to the past alter the future is old. Using the decay as a lie detector, where the always-right team is the fake, is the freshest thing here. I have not found it as the core of another mystery.

**4. Are two rival futures new?**
No. *This Is How You Lose the Time War*, *Dark*, *Terminator* and many others have rival future factions. New here: one "future" is an AI's forecast dressed as memory. The contest is prophecy against prediction.

**5. Is "can't go back before the ring was lit" new?**
No. It comes from *Primer* and Steins;Gate. It stays because it closes a hole, not because it is fresh.

**6. Is the Glass answer to time war new?**
I think so. Most stories end time wars with a ban, a hero or a reset. Here a public record ends it, because a shared warning gives no edge. That is a cold-logic idea in the Three-Body mode and it belongs to this world.

**7. Does the world make the time travel feel less like a trope?**
Yes. With the lag at the centre, a message from the future is the fastest news there is, and the whole economy already trades in news. The time travel grows out of the world rather than sitting on top of it.

**8. Honest verdict.**
The mechanics are familiar. The uses are new: decaying foresight as the lie detector, an AI forecast posing as a future, and the public-record cure. That is enough to keep time travel if the story leans on those three. It is not enough if the story leans on paradoxes or older-self drama alone.

**9. Alternatives, as the owner's rule asks.**
I brainstormed seven premises keeping "two rival sources of impossible knowledge, each calling the other a fake" (listed in world.md, section 11). I cut the ones that copy Pitch 5 or *Altered Carbon* (rival clones, rival mind backups), the ones that bury the frame in alien lore (rival crystal records, a bonded alien memory) and one that wasn't impossible knowledge (rival forecasters). The best two:
- **Old light:** ships outrun light, so flying out far enough lets you watch any past event. Two expeditions return with contradictory film of who started the war. It grows straight out of the lag, and real old light has real limits that a fake would lack.
- **The split pilot:** Sabi is copied into two cores, each believing it's the original, giving opposite advice.
*Change:* world.md section 10 states what is new; section 11 gives both alternatives with gains and losses. Recommendation: keep time travel, with Old light in reserve.

**10. Does Old light break the world if chosen?**
No. The lag, the Glass, the Ledger (as a record of what was really seen), Varela and the wreck all survive. The Admiral's future and Nneka's arc as a future daughter would need replacing.
*Change:* noted in the losses.

## Round 6: final check

I ran one more pass of hard questions. Nothing serious turned up.

- *Does any part need a coincidence?* Meridian being the contact site, the wreck being there, Varela building the station and the Rearguard's date all now have causes.
- *Does anyone act stupidly?* The *Calabar* captain acts for her family, the Glass act on cold logic, Tomás stalls before he strikes.
- *Is anything gross or spooky?* The Empty Worlds are presented as awe, the Argument's losses as unborn children, the vats as white bays.
- *Too many invented words?* New terms: the lag, passage debt, charter houses, the Settlement, the Pilot Law, sending, the early echo, the Meridian Skirmish, Dock Zero. All plain English. None is needed for the main story.
- *Does the world fight the one-line frame or the two rules?* No. It supports both and adds only the lit-ring limit, which is part of Rule 1's explanation.

Minor items left for the outline stage: exact distances between named worlds, and the Rearguard future's war battles.
