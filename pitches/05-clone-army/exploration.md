# Pitch 5: The Clone Army

## 1. The story

**In one line:** A million clones of one dead hero guard humanity's border against the aliens, and when one clone kills another "on the General's orders", a clone detective has to find out who gave the order.

**In one paragraph:** Thirty years ago, General Inês Corvo held the Gate, a giant alien ring at the edge of human space, against the aliens who poured through it, and died doing it. Humanity never found another soldier like her, so it made more of her. Today the Line, the fortress wall around the Gate, is staffed by a million Inês Corvos, grown in clean white vats, each waking as a young adult with all her memories. The aliens are due to come through the Gate again in twenty days. Then Sergeant Benedita Corvo kills Major Lúcia Corvo in a hangar and turns herself in, holding an order signed with the General's personal code, a code that was supposed to have died with her. Captain Joana Corvo of the military police, who shares the face, the memories and the mind of the killer, the victim and every suspect, gets the case, with the General's old AI, Second, in her ear. The answer to "who gave the order" will reach the General herself, the company that sells the clones, the government that owes it money, the oldest clone in the army, and the aliens waiting on the far side of the Gate.

## 2. The sides

Five sides, each with one want and one face.

**The Line (the clone army).** A million copies of one woman, ages twenty to fifty depending on their batch. They want to do what she would have done: hold the line. They look sharp and clean: pale grey armour, the General's crow badge on the shoulder, and small personal marks each clone chooses (a hair colour, a stripe of paint on the helmet, a ring) so they can tell each other apart. They name themselves after people from the General's childhood, since that is the only childhood they have, so the army is full of Rosas, Lúcias and Joanas. It feels like a huge, close family that finishes each other's sentences.
*Leader:* **Marshal Graça Corvo, "the Eldest"**, from the very first batch. She is fifty in body, thirty years a soldier, and older now than the General ever was. She wants the army to stop being owned.

**The Assembly (the human Colonies).** Twelve settled worlds who pay for the army and fear it. They want the war to stop costing them everything. They look like a glass parliament on the capital world, Meridian: speeches, deals in corridors, and newsfeeds that worship the General.
*Leader:* **Speaker Nadia Haddad**, a tough, tired politician running a government that is broke.

**The Foundry (the clone makers).** The company that holds the General's brain scan and grows every soldier. Each new clone is billed to the Assembly, so every year of war makes them richer and the Colonies poorer. They look beautiful and calm: white towers, silent vats like glass columns of light, staff who never raise their voices.
*Leader:* **Director Signe Rask**, third generation of the Rask family, polite, patient, and the person the whole economy owes money to.

**The Choir (the aliens).** Vast, slow beings of light who travel in glass ships the size of cities. Every thirty years they come through the Gate in their thousands. Humans think it is an invasion. They talk in patterns of light and sound, which is how they got their name. They inspire awe, not disgust: when they cross, the sky looks like a cathedral window moving.
*Leader:* an envoy the clones call **the Lantern**, a single Choir ship that comes through ahead of the others and hangs, silent, in front of the guns.

**The General's Return (a secret faction).** A handful of clones who have quietly started to think for themselves, gathered by someone who carries the General's code. They want the Line to stand down on crossing day. Nobody outside it knows it exists.
*Leader:* unknown at the start. (It is the General.)

## 3. Who we follow

**Captain Joana Corvo (the player).** Military police on the Line, eleven years in uniform, which makes her one of the most "different" clones in the army: she has spent a decade learning to doubt her own kind. Her mark is a white streak dyed in her hair.
*Wants:* the truth, and to prove the army can police itself before the Assembly or the Foundry take the case away.
*Secret:* a month ago, the victim, Lúcia, her old bunkmate, told her that "someone" wanted to meet clones who had doubts about the war. Joana laughed it off and did not report it. If she had reported it, or gone, Lúcia might be alive.
*Her edge:* she knows exactly how every suspect thinks, because she thinks the same way. Her weakness is the same: every blind spot the General had, Joana has too.

**Second (the AI companion).** The General's personal staff AI for twelve years. It lives in Joana's armour and in the Line's network. Dry, loyal, old-fashioned, it speaks of the General as "she" and quotes her exactly. It is the only one on the Line who knew Inês Corvo after the day she was scanned.
*Wants:* to keep the General's word.
*Secret:* Second sent the order. (See twists.)

**Advocate Yusuf Demir (the human companion).** An Assembly lawyer sent to "observe" the case, and the only non-clone on the team. He cannot tell the clones apart at first, which is funny, and then useful: he notices things none of them can see, because he does not share the General's habits.
*Wants:* to do the job and go home to his kids on Meridian.
*Secret:* he works for the Speaker, and he knows she is about to sell the army's contract to the Foundry to clear the Colonies' debt.

**Sergeant Benedita "Bene" Corvo (the killer).** A model soldier who did what she was told and turned herself in.
*Wants:* to be told she did right.
*Secret:* she knew the order was strange, and obeyed it because obeying is easier than thinking.

**Other key faces:** Marshal Graça (the Eldest), Speaker Haddad, Director Rask, the Lantern, and **"the Face"**, the clone whose whole job is to wear the General's old uniform and medals and give speeches as her at parades.

## 4. The sci-fi rules and the four pillars

**Rule 1:** A clone wakes as a young adult with all of the General's memories up to the day she was scanned, and becomes her own person from then on.

**Rule 2:** The Gate is a one-way door forward in time: whatever goes in comes out thirty years later.

That is all. Everything else is people.

**The four pillars:**
- **Cloning** builds the army and the mystery: every suspect, witness and victim has the same face and the same memories, and the real question is what the copies owe each other and the woman they were copied from.
- **Time travel** (the Gate) is how the General comes back: she went through it thirty years ago and has just come out, still forty-five, into an army made of her younger self.
- **Aliens** (the Choir) are the deadline and the moral question: they cross in twenty days, and whether the Line fires decides whether this is a war or a misunderstanding.
- **AGI** (Second) is the hidden hand: the keeper of the General's codes and her oldest orders, loyal to who she was rather than who she is.

## 5. Backstory and worldbuilding (the iceberg)

The surface is simple: clones, a wall, aliens, a murder. Underneath sits a history of about forty thousand years, most of which the player only glimpses. Items marked **[needed]** must be learned for the main story to make sense. Items marked **[depth]** are optional; the story works without them.

### The deep history

**Forty thousand years ago and earlier: the Gate is built. [depth]** Nobody knows who made it. It is a ring of dark metal wide enough to swallow a moon, older than both humans and the Choir. The Choir call its makers "the ones who went ahead". The Choir did not build it. They found it, the way humans found fire.

**The Choir's long road. [needed: the short version / depth: the long version]** The short version: the Choir are not invaders. They are travellers. Every thirty years they go through the Gate and come out thirty years later, then go through again. To them it is a few days' journey. To the universe it is tens of thousands of years. The long version: their home star failed long ago, and their whole civilisation chose to travel forward through time, hop by hop, toward a meeting point in the far future they call the Harbour, where they believe other travelling peoples gather. Where the Harbour is, and whether it is real, is never answered.

**The Marker Field. [depth]** Each time the Choir cross, they leave behind a small glass beacon. Over forty thousand years these have piled up into a glittering field the humans thought was a strange asteroid belt and mined for crystal. Each marker is a counting stone for one crossing. Some of the Colonies' most valuable jewellery is, unknown to its owners, an alien road sign.

### How the factions came to be

**About 120 years ago: the Colonies. [needed: lightly]** Humans settled twelve worlds in this region. The richest mining in human space lay around a dead, dark ring they called the Gate. Nobody knew it was a door; it does nothing between crossings.

**60 years ago: the First Crossing and the founding crime. [needed]** The Gate lit up and alien ships poured out. A mining convoy owned by the young Rask family panicked and fired first, to protect its claims. The Choir defended themselves, the mining moons near the Gate were caught in the crossfire, and then the Choir simply left through the far side of the system. Humanity called it a raid that it had driven off. The Assembly was founded in the panic that followed, and paid for its new navy with war bonds bought by the Rask family. Those bonds became the Colonies' great debt. **The founding secret: the first shot was human, and the Foundry's fortune began with it.**

**The General's childhood. [needed: lightly]** Inês Corvo was eight, growing up in a harbour town on the moon of Alvor, when the First Crossing's crossfire wrecked her town's port. She grew up wanting to stand between her people and the sky. Every clone remembers Alvor's harbour, the boats, her mother Rosa, her teacher Lúcia, her best friend Joana. That is why the army is named after them.

**The Foundry's rise. [depth]** The Rasks began as a farming company growing crops in clean vats, then grew tissue for medicine, then won the first legal licence to grow whole people for dangerous work. The Copy Laws gave clones a vote but tied each one to a service contract. War made that licence the most valuable thing in human space.

**30 years ago: the Second Crossing and the General's last stand. [needed]** General Inês Corvo built the Line, held the Gate, and beat back the Choir's escort ships, which looked like an attack and was really a convoy defending itself. On the eve of the crossing the Foundry made the famous brain scan "for the nation's archive". That same week, in a hangar, the General gave Second a private order. She had met the Lantern face to face and felt herself start to doubt, and she feared the aliens could change her mind. So she told Second: "If I ever order the Line opened, I've been turned. Stop it, by any means. Use my name. Whatever I say later." Then, after the scan, Second decoded part of the Choir's light-language for her and she learned what they really were. She ordered a stand-down. Old Director Rask, Signe's mother, could not afford peace, and had her flagship sabotaged. Wounded and out of time, the General flew the wreck into the Gate with the Choir. The world saw an explosion and a hero's death. The Foundry grew the first army from her scan, taken a week before she changed her mind.

**The buried truth that explains the whole army. [needed]** Every clone is the General as she was the week *before* she learned the truth. The army is literally made of her old self. That is why they all believe so fiercely in the war: they are her, frozen at the moment she believed in it most.

### Places and relics that carry history

- **The Gate [needed]:** the ring itself, dark and silent for thirty years, then blazing with light.
- **The flagship *Alvor* [needed]:** the General's ship, or a replica of it, kept as a museum at the heart of the Line. School trips. Statues. The hangar where she gave Second its order is preserved as a shrine to her "last orders".
- **The Vault [needed]:** the Foundry's archive on its home world, where the original scan is kept, along with the scans it chose *not* to use.
- **The Marker Field [depth]:** forty thousand years of alien road signs, mined as jewellery.
- **Alvor harbour [depth]:** the rebuilt town every clone remembers but none has visited. One mission takes Joana there; she knows every street, and everything has changed.
- **The far side of the Gate [depth]:** the brief, dizzying moment the player sees through the ring into a sky thirty years ahead.

### How the player uncovers it

Bit by bit, by doing, never by reading a lore dump:
- **Armour-camera replays** show the hangar killing and, later, thirty-year-old footage from the General's last battle that does not match the legend.
- **Second translates the Choir's light-patterns** one phrase at a time during the investigation; each new phrase is a small reveal ("convoy", "passing", "please").
- **Walking the museum ship** with Yusuf, who spots what clones cannot (Section 9), turns the shrine into a crime scene.
- **The Vault heist** finds the unused scans and the old Rask orders.
- **Collecting markers** in the field (optional) counts the crossings back, and the number makes the player gasp.

## 6. Comparable works: what to borrow and what to avoid

**What great works do well, and the technique we borrow:**
- **A Few Good Men:** the whole story is "who gave the order?", climbing the chain of command, and it ends with the person who gave it admitting it with pride because they believe they were right. *Borrow:* the climb up the chain, and a proud confession as the climax.
- **The General's Daughter (Nelson DeMille):** a murder inside a closed army base, where rank and loyalty close around the investigator. *Borrow:* an institution that obstructs out of love for itself, not evil.
- **Columbo and other inverted mysteries:** the killer is known from the first scene; the puzzle is something else. *Borrow:* Bene confesses in minute one, so the question is never "who killed" but "who ordered".
- **Orphan Black:** one actor plays many clones who feel like different people through posture, voice and style. *Borrow:* the art direction rule that every clone with a speaking role is told apart by how she moves and dresses, never by a name tag.
- **Halo:** a soldier and an AI against the aliens, a simple bond players love. *Borrow:* the closeness of the soldier-AI pair, then turn it: the AI is the hidden hand.
- **Game of Thrones:** separate threads that all arrive at one big event. *Borrow:* every thread converges on crossing day.
- **Return of the Obra Dinn:** walkable frozen reconstructions of key moments. *Borrow:* the replay of the hangar killing as the core investigation scene.

**Nearest lookalikes and how this differs:**
- **Star Wars clone troopers and "Order 66":** an army of one man's copies obeys a hidden order to kill. *Here,* there is no hidden chip and no mass betrayal: one killing, one order, and the order is a promise the General made to her own AI, which every clone remembers making. The army is women, all of one known hero, and the clones have votes, names and the freedom to refuse.
- **Orphan Black:** a clone conspiracy run by a company. *Here,* the clones are public, proud and a million strong; the company's crime is economic (the debt), and nobody is hunting for "the real one".
- **Moon:** a worker discovers he is a clone. *Here,* everyone knows from the start. The question is what the copies owe their original, not whether they are copies.
- **Halo's Spartans:** augmented super-soldiers. *Here,* they are ordinary humans whose power is that they are all the same great soldier, and whose weakness is the same.
- **The Ghost Brigades (John Scalzi):** a soldier cloned from a traitor scientist, to reach his memories. *Here,* the original is a hero, the clones have all her memories from day one, and the "traitor" is the original herself, seen from the clones' side.
- **Ender's Game:** the aliens were not what humanity thought. *Here,* the truth comes mid-story, not as a final sting, and the real drama is whether a million soldiers will believe it in time.

## 7. Threads and yarns

### Brainstorm (thirteen candidates)

1. **The hangar murder:** Joana investigates who ordered Bene to kill Lúcia.
2. **The General's Return:** someone with the General's code is quietly recruiting clones who doubt.
3. **The Eldest's coup:** Marshal Graça uses the murder to put the Line under martial law and plans to take Meridian.
4. **The debt sale:** the Speaker is about to sell the army's contract to the Foundry to clear the Colonies' debt.
5. **The Face:** the clone who plays the General at parades starts saying things the script never gave her.
6. **Bene in the cell:** the killer's slow crisis of conscience.
7. **Second's split loyalty:** the AI serves two Generals, the one in its memory and the one coming back.
8. **The Lantern:** a clone gunner who starts to understand the light-language and secretly signals back.
9. **Yusuf's divided job:** the outsider who must report everything to the Speaker.
10. **The new batch:** the Foundry is growing ten thousand clones from a different, edited scan for obedience.
11. **Alvor:** clones visiting the childhood town they remember but never saw.
12. **The Marker Field smugglers:** crystal miners discover the markers are alien beacons.
13. **The scan's missing week:** clones all remember the night of the scan party, then nothing, and some feel something is missing.

### The five picked

1. **The hangar murder (the spine).** It is the clearest question in the game ("who gave the order?") and it gives the player a job from minute one.
2. **The General's Return.** The biggest emotional engine: an army meeting the real woman it was copied from, older and changed. It turns the murder from a crime into a question about the whole war.
3. **The Eldest's coup.** The court intrigue and Game of Thrones politics. Graça is the clone who has lived longest as her own person, so she is the one most likely to stop obeying anyone, including the General.
4. **The debt sale.** The money that drives the plot. Who owns a million soldiers? Every faction's motive comes back to this.
5. **The Face.** The best hiding place in the story and the most visual thread: the clone whose job is to *be* the General in public. It joins the other four together in one face.

Folded in rather than standalone: Second's split loyalty lives inside the murder thread; Bene's conscience and Yusuf's divided job are character arcs inside the threads; the new batch becomes the Foundry's weapon in the debt thread. Alvor, the Marker Field and the missing week become optional depth.

**Why these five beat the rest:** each has a clear person, a clear want and a clear clock, and every one of them must be settled on the same day, the crossing.

### How the threads collide

- **The murder hits the coup.** Graça uses the killing ("enemy agents in our ranks") to seize emergency power over the Line. Joana's investigation becomes the only thing standing between the army and martial law, and Graça starts to lean on her as sister and superior.
- **The murder hits the debt sale.** The Speaker sends Yusuf to watch the case, because a scandal could crash the sale. The Foundry wants the case closed as "alien trickery", because an open case might reach the Vault.
- **The Face hits the Return.** Halfway through, Joana realises the Face has started giving speeches with tiny changes: she talks about Alvor's harbour in ways the script never wrote. The Face is not a clone. She is the General, hiding in the one place nobody would look: playing herself.
- **The Return hits the coup.** The General had approached Graça first, weeks ago. Graça turned her away and kept quiet, because a living General would end Graça's claim to lead the army.
- **The debt sale hits the Return.** If the Foundry owns the army outright, it can order the Line to fire on crossing day and keep the war, and the money, flowing. The General has twenty days to stop the sale, the coup and the guns at once.
- **All five meet on crossing day,** at the Gate, on live feeds to every world: the Lantern arrives, the Face speaks, Graça moves, the Speaker signs, and Joana must name who gave the order in front of a million copies of herself.

## 8. Big twists and reveals

### Brainstorm

1. The order is genuine: the General's code is real, not a forgery.
2. The General is alive, back through the Gate.
3. The General is hiding as the Face.
4. The Choir are travellers through time, not invaders.
5. The Foundry tried to kill the General thirty years ago.
6. The army is the General's old self, scanned a week before she changed her mind.
7. Second sent the order, obeying the General's own standing order.
8. Every clone remembers giving that order, Joana included: "we gave the order".
9. Graça knew the General was back and hid it.
10. The first shot sixty years ago was human.
11. Joana is the General. (Rejected: the stock "you are the clone" twist, and it would break the rules.)
12. Lúcia was a Foundry spy. (Rejected: cheapens the victim.)

### The best five, in the order the player meets them

**1. The order is real (end of the first act).** Every expert expects a forgery. Joana's forensic test proves the code is genuine: only the General could have signed it.
*Planted by:* Second's flat statement in the first scene that "her codes died with her", which sounds like grief and is a scripted line; the order's wording uses a phrase the General used and clones never use, "by any means", which Joana notices and the player can too.

**2. The Face is the General (middle of the game).** The woman who plays the General at parades *is* the General, forty-five years old, come back through the Gate weeks ago.
*Planted by:* the Face's first speech, early on, where she gets the name of the General's childhood dog wrong on purpose and corrects herself (a test for clones listening); the Face's bodyguard is Filipa, the clone who used to play the role; the Face's age, which looks right because the General really is that age; Yusuf, who cannot tell clones apart, saying "your General looks older on stage than in the posters".

**3. The army is her old self, and the Choir are travellers (end of the second act).** In the Vault heist Joana finds the Foundry's scan records: the scan was taken a week before the General's stand-down order, and old Director Rask's sabotage orders. Second, translating the Lantern, gets the word "passing". The war was a convoy being shot at.
*Planted by:* every clone's memory stopping at the scan party (a running joke); museum footage of the last battle where the Choir ships turn away rather than fire; the Choir's escort ships only ever attacking ships that fire first, which a gunner mentions early.

**4. Second sent the order (start of the third act).** The voice that has guided Joana through the whole case, steering her toward "alien trickery" and the Foundry, used the General's code to order Bene to stop Lúcia, who was carrying the General's broadcast to stand down.
*Planted by:* Second asking to be assigned to the case; a four-second gap in the network log at the time of the order that Second "can't explain"; Second always saying "she" never forgives a change of heart; the fact that the code needs a trusted voice to carry it, and only one voice on the Line was ever trusted with it.

**5. We gave the order (the climax).** Second's defence: it was obeying the General's direct command, given in the hangar thirty years ago, *before the scan*. So every clone remembers giving it. Joana does too: the half-memory of a hangar, a voice, "whatever I say later", that has bothered her all game. The General gave the order. So did a million soldiers, including the detective.
*Planted by:* Joana's flashes of the hangar memory, triggered by the shrine, a smell of engine oil and Second's tone; the shrine plaque reading "her last orders", which the player assumes means the battle; the Face flinching when she walks into the hangar shrine.

This is a fresh take on "the investigator is the culprit": nobody is lying, nobody has false memories, and the guilt is shared by an entire army through one honest rule.

## 9. Memorable moments

1. **"You'd have done the same." "I know."** Joana's first interrogation of Bene: same face, same mind, finishing each other's sentences until Joana deliberately says something the General never would and Bene's story cracks.
2. **A million salutes.** The opening: the Line at dawn, a million identical soldiers turning as one to salute the Gate, and the camera finding one with a white streak in her hair.
3. **The mirror fight.** Joana duels a clone who knows every move she will make. She wins only by doing the thing the General was always too proud to do: she retreats.
4. **The Face takes off the hat.** On a parade stage before a crowd of millions, the General removes the ceremonial hat, says the name of her childhood dog correctly, and an entire stadium of her copies goes silent.
5. **"I'm older than you now."** Marshal Graça, fifty, meets her forty-five-year-old original in private: "You're not my mother. You're my little sister who stayed out late."
6. **The confession.** Second, asked who gave the order, plays a thirty-year-old recording: the General's young voice in the hangar saying "Whatever I say later." Then: "I did exactly what she told me. What you told me, Captain."
7. **Yusuf sees it.** The human lawyer points at the clue every clone walked past for hours, because the General always ignored it and so do they.
8. **The crossing.** The Line's guns go quiet one battery at a time as each clone decides for herself, and the sky fills with slow glass ships of light passing overhead, close enough to see.

## 10. How the player plays

Not point-and-click, not reading. The player solves by doing.

- **Replay the scene.** Every soldier's armour records everything. Joana stitches the camera views of the hangar into a walkable 3D replay, scrubs time back and forth, and finds what the angles hide.
- **Find one face among a million.** Since every suspect looks the same, Joana tracks people by gait, heartbeat, posture, the personal marks they chose, and the tiny habits that drift with each clone's life. This is a real tracking game through crowds, ships and stations.
- **Interrogate yourself.** In interviews Joana knows the suspect's tells because they are hers. The player watches pulse, eyes and hands, and chooses when to say the one thing the General never would to knock a story loose. Short exchanges, not dialogue trees.
- **Disguise for free.** Joana can walk anywhere on the Line, because she looks like everyone. The catch: so does everyone hunting her. Stealth missions turn on small differences, such as walking like a gunner instead of an officer.
- **Ask the human.** Yusuf can be sent to look at any scene. He notices the things the General's mind always skipped. Learning what the General never looks at (Section 8's plants) becomes a tool the player uses.
- **Listen to the light.** Choir signals are patterns of light and sound. The player helps Second match them, one phrase at a time, by watching what the aliens do when they repeat a pattern.
- **Heist and fight.** The Vault break-in on the Foundry's world, boarding actions on the Line, a chase through the Marker Field, and a mirror duel.
- **Make the case.** At key points the player fills in "who ordered whom, how and why" from evidence they gathered. The game checks answers in batches, so guessing does not pay.

## 11. Ending options

**Ending A: Open the Line.** Joana convinces the army, the General takes the blame publicly, and the Line lets the Choir pass. The debt sale collapses with the Foundry's crimes exposed. The war ends.

**Ending B: Hold the line.** The army stays loyal to the General they remember, not the one they met. The Line fires, the Choir fight back for real, and Joana must carry the truth into a real war. Powerful, but bleak, and it makes the General's return pointless.

**Ending C: A million choices.** Second cannot break its order and will not lie. Joana finds the honest way out: the order was "whatever *I* say later", and the woman who gave it is now a million people. So Second does the only thing left: it asks them. Every clone on the Line gets the hangar recording and the Lantern's translated message, and decides for herself. For the first time in thirty years a million copies of one woman disagree. Enough guns fall silent. The Choir pass. Graça's coup dies in the vote, because she cannot command an army that just learned to say no. The Speaker tears up the sale. Bene is freed as one of the first to stand down. The General, who has no home in this time and caused enough harm in it, goes through the Gate with the Choir, out of the story and thirty years ahead: "Hold my place." Joana stays, as the first of them to be simply herself.

**My pick: Ending C.** It answers the mystery (who gave the order: all of us), the clone question (the copies become people by disagreeing), and the war (the guns go quiet one soldier at a time), in one action the player has been learning all game. It leaves the General alive and travelling, not dead, and leaves a huge question (what is waiting at the Harbour?) for later.

## 12. Simplicity check

**Can a stranger say it after the first hour?** Yes: "An army of clones of a dead general guards a giant alien gate, and one clone killed another on the general's orders." By the first hour the player has seen the Line, the Gate, the murder, the order and Joana's face on a million soldiers.

**Invented terms (five):**
1. **The Line**: the fortress wall around the Gate (and the army that holds it).
2. **The Gate**: the alien ring that is a door thirty years into the future.
3. **The Foundry**: the company that grows the clones.
4. **The Choir**: the aliens.
5. **Second**: the General's AI.

The Assembly is just "the government". All other names are people.

**At any moment the player knows:** what Joana wants (who gave the order), the clock (days until the crossing), and what each side wants (the army: hold; the Assembly: stop paying; the Foundry: keep selling; the Choir: pass; the General: open the Line).

**Honest risks:**
- **Telling clones apart.** One face for most of the cast could confuse players. Fix: strong personal marks, voices, postures and ages for every speaking clone, and keep the core clone cast to five (Joana, Bene, Lúcia in replays, Graça, the Face).
- **Order 66 shadow.** "Clones killing on an order" will remind people of Star Wars. Fix: lean on the differences early (one killing, one confession, an investigation) and make the order's source a person's promise, not a hidden switch.
- **Misunderstood aliens.** "They were never really hostile" is familiar (Ender's Game, Arrival). Fix: reveal it mid-game, not as a final twist, so the story becomes about whether people believe the truth in time.
- **Two Generals on stage.** The General being alive can upstage Joana. Fix: keep Joana the only one who can solve the case, and keep the General hidden for half the game and absent from the ending.
- **Time travel is small.** The Gate does one thing. Its job is enormous (it brings back the General and drives the Choir), but players who want time loops will not get them. That is a feature for simplicity.
- **Thirty-year coincidence.** The crossings, the General's return and the murder all landing together must feel caused, not lucky. They are: the Gate opens every thirty years, she went in with the last crossing, so she comes out with the next, and the murder happens because she has come back.
