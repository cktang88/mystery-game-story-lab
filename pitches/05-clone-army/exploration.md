# Pitch 5: The Clone Army

## 1. The story in one page

*A million clones of a dead general guard humanity's border against the aliens. One clone kills another "on the General's orders", and a clone detective must find out who gave the order.*

Thirty years ago, General Inês Corvo held the Gate against the aliens and died doing it. The Gate is a ring of dark metal wide enough to swallow a moon, at the edge of human space. Every thirty years it blazes with light and alien ships pour through.

Humanity never found another soldier like her. So it made more of her.

Today a million Inês Corvos guard the Gate from a wall of fortresses called the Line. A company called the Foundry grows them in tall glass vats. Each wakes up as a young woman with all of the General's memories. They share one face, one childhood and one stubborn streak. They tell each other apart by small things: a dyed stripe of hair, a ring, a painted helmet.

The aliens are due back in twenty days.

Then, in a hangar at night, Sergeant Bene Corvo kills Major Lúcia Corvo. Bene walks to the guardroom and hands over her weapon and a written order. It is signed with the General's private code. That code was meant to have died with her.

Captain Joana Corvo of the military police gets the case. She has the same face as the killer, the victim and every suspect. She knows how they think, because she thinks the same way. That is her gift and her blind spot.

In her ear rides Second, the General's own AI, which served her for twelve years. Beside her walks Yusuf, a lawyer from the human government. He is the only one on the team who can't tell the clones apart.

The trail climbs the chain of command. It reaches the oldest clone in the army, the company that owns the vats, and the government that owes it money. Then it reaches the General herself, who may not be dead at all.

## 2. The sides

**The Line** is the clone army: a million copies of one woman, aged twenty to fifty depending on when each was grown. They wear pale grey armour with the General's crow badge. They name themselves after people from her childhood, the only childhood they have, so the army is full of Rosas, Lúcias and Joanas. They want to hold the Gate, as she would have.
Their leader, Marshal Graça Corvo, the Eldest, came from the first batch. At fifty she is older than the General ever got. She wants the army to stop being owned.

**The Assembly** is the human government: twelve worlds that pay for the army and fear it. They meet in a glass parliament on the capital world, Meridian. They want the war to stop costing them everything. Speaker Nadia Haddad leads a government that is broke.

**The Foundry** holds the General's brain scan and grows every soldier. It bills each clone to the Assembly, so every year of war makes it richer. Its home world is white towers and vats glowing like columns of light. Director Signe Rask runs it, third of her family to do so. Half of human space owes her money.

**The Choir** are the aliens: huge, slow beings of light in glass ships the size of cities. They speak in pulses of light and sound. One ship always comes through first and hangs in front of the guns. The clones call it the Lantern.

**The General's Return** is a secret band of clones who have started to doubt the war. Someone with the General's code is gathering them. They want the Line to hold its fire on crossing day. (Their leader is the General.)

## 3. Who we follow

**Captain Joana Corvo** is the player. Eleven years in the military police have made her one of the oldest and most independent clones; she dyes a white streak into her hair. She wants the truth, and wants the army to solve its own crime before anyone else takes the case.
Her secret: a month ago Lúcia, her old bunkmate, said "someone" wanted to meet clones who had doubts. Joana laughed and didn't report it.

**Second** lives in Joana's armour and in the Line's network. It served the General for twelve years and quotes her word for word, dry and exact. It is the only one on the Line who knew her after her brain scan. It wants to keep her word. It also sent the order.

**Yusuf Demir** is the Assembly's lawyer, sent to watch. For the first hour he keeps calling the wrong clone "Captain". Then he turns useful: he spots what clones miss, because he doesn't share the General's habits. He wants to go home to his two kids. He knows the Speaker plans to sell the army's contract to the Foundry, and keeps quiet.

**Sergeant Bene Corvo** is the killer. She knew the order was strange and obeyed anyway, because obeying is easier than thinking. She wants someone to tell her she did right.

**The Face** is the clone who plays the General at parades. She wears the old medals and reads speeches the Assembly writes.

## 4. The rules and the four pillars

Two rules:

1. A clone wakes as a young adult with all the General's memories up to the day of her brain scan, then becomes her own person.
2. The Gate is a one-way door forward in time: whatever goes in comes out thirty years later.

Each pillar has one job:

- **Cloning** makes the army and the mystery. Every suspect has the same face and past, so the question is what the copies owe each other and the woman they came from.
- **Time travel** brings the General back. She went into the Gate thirty years ago and has just come out, still forty-five, into an army of her younger self.
- **The aliens** set the clock and the test. They cross in twenty days, and if the Line fires, the war goes on.
- **The AI** is the hidden hand. Second keeps the General's codes and her oldest orders, and serves the woman she was thirty years ago.

## 5. Backstory and worldbuilding (the iceberg)

About forty thousand years of history sit under the story. Parts marked **[needed]** must come out for the plot to work. Parts marked **[depth]** are optional.

**Who built the Gate [depth].** Nobody knows. It is older than humans and the Choir. The Choir call its makers "the ones who went ahead".

**The Choir's trip [needed].** The Choir are passing through. Every thirty years they enter the Gate, come out thirty years later, and enter again. Each hop takes them days. To everyone else, their trip has lasted tens of thousands of years.
The long version is depth. Their home star failed, and their whole people set out forward through time toward a place they call the Harbour. The game never says if it exists.

**The Marker Field [depth].** After each crossing the Choir leave a small glass marker. Forty thousand years of them form a glittering belt near the Gate. Humans mine it, and rich people on Meridian wear alien road signs as jewellery.

**The founding crime [needed].** Humans settled here about 120 years ago, around rich mines near a dark ring. Sixty years ago the ring lit up and alien ships poured out. A Rask mining convoy panicked and fired first. The Choir fought back, the crossfire wrecked the nearby moons, and then the Choir left.
Humanity called it a raid it had beaten off. The Assembly formed in the panic and built a navy with war bonds the Rasks bought. Those bonds are the worlds' great debt. The first shot was human, and the Foundry's fortune grew from it.

**The General's childhood [needed].** Inês Corvo was eight when that crossfire smashed the port of her harbour town on the moon of Alvor. She grew up wanting to stand between her people and the sky. Every clone remembers Alvor's boats, her mother Rosa, her teacher Lúcia, her friend Joana.

**The Second Crossing [needed].** Thirty years ago the General built the Line and drove off the Choir's escort ships. Before the crossing she met the Lantern face to face, and her certainty slipped. Afraid the aliens could turn her, she gave Second a private order in a hangar: "If I ever order the Line opened, I've been turned. Stop it by any means. Use my name. Whatever I say later."
Days later the Foundry took her brain scan for the national archive. After the scan, Second decoded part of the Choir's language for her, and she learned they were a convoy. She ordered a stand-down. Old Director Rask, Signe's mother, couldn't afford peace and had her flagship sabotaged. Wounded, the General flew the burning ship into the Gate with the Choir. The worlds saw an explosion and mourned a hero.

**What this explains [needed].** The Foundry grew the army from the scan taken before she changed her mind. Every clone is the General that week: sure of the war, and already carrying her order to Second.

**Places.** The Gate [needed]. The flagship museum at the heart of the Line, with a shrine in the hangar under a plaque reading "Her Last Orders" [needed]. The Vault on the Foundry's world, holding the scan and the old Rask orders [needed]. Alvor harbour, which every clone remembers and none has seen [depth].

**How the player digs it up.** Old armour footage of the battle doesn't match the legend. Second translates the Choir a word at a time: "convoy", "passing", "please". A walk through the museum with Yusuf turns the shrine into a crime scene. The Vault heist finds the scan dates. In the Marker Field, counting markers shows how old the Choir's trip is.

## 6. What to borrow, and the lookalikes

Techniques to borrow:

- *A Few Good Men* climbs the chain of command to find who gave an order, and ends with a proud confession. We use both.
- In *The General's Daughter* (Nelson DeMille), an army base closes ranks around a murder. Our Line stonewalls Joana out of love for itself.
- Like *Columbo*, we show the killer first. Bene confesses in scene one, so the question is who ordered it.
- *Orphan Black* makes one actor's clones feel like strangers through posture, voice and clothes. Every speaking clone here gets that care.
- Game of Thrones sends its threads toward one event. Ours all arrive on crossing day.
- *Return of the Obra Dinn* lets you walk a frozen moment. Our hangar replay works that way.

Lookalikes:

- Star Wars has its clone troopers and Order 66: copies of one man obey a hidden kill order. Here one soldier kills on one order, a promise the General made to her own AI, and the clones can vote and refuse.
- In Orphan Black, a company runs a secret clone plot. Our clones are public and proud, and nobody hunts for "the real one".
- In Moon, a worker finds out he is a clone. Here everyone knows from the start.
- Halo's Spartans are augmented super-soldiers. Ours are ordinary people who share one great soldier's mind.
- In The Ghost Brigades (John Scalzi), a soldier is cloned from a traitor to reach his memories. Our "traitor" is the hero herself, seen from her copies' side.
- In Ender's Game, the aliens weren't what humans thought. Here that comes out mid-game, and the drama is whether a million soldiers believe it in time.

## 7. Threads

I listed thirteen threads and kept five. Four rejects became arcs inside the kept threads (Second's split loyalty, Bene's conscience, Yusuf's divided job, a secret batch of obedient clones). Four became optional depth (a trip to Alvor, Marker Field smugglers, the clones' missing last week, a gunner who learns to talk to the Lantern).

1. **The hangar murder.** Joana hunts whoever ordered Bene to kill Lúcia. It gives the player a clear question in minute one.
2. **The General's return.** Someone with her code is recruiting doubters. An army meeting the woman it was copied from turns a crime into a question about the war.
3. **The Eldest's coup.** Graça uses the murder to put the Line under martial law and plans to take Meridian. She has lived longest as her own person, so she is the clone most ready to obey nobody.
4. **The debt sale.** The Speaker is about to sell the army's contract to the Foundry to clear the debt. Every motive leads back to who owns a million soldiers.
5. **The Face.** The clone who plays the General starts going off script. She ties the other four together.

### How they collide

Graça seizes on the killing ("enemy agents in our ranks") to take emergency power. Joana's case is all that stands between the army and martial law.

The Speaker sends Yusuf because a scandal could sink the sale. The Foundry wants the case closed as "alien tricks", because an open case could lead to the Vault.

Mid-game, Joana notices the Face adding details about Alvor's harbour that no script gave her. The Face is the General, hiding in the one place nobody would look: playing herself.

The General went to Graça first, weeks ago. Graça turned her away and kept quiet, because a living General ends Graça's claim to lead.

If the Foundry owns the army, it can order the Line to fire on crossing day and keep the war going. The General has twenty days to stop the sale, the coup and the guns.

On crossing day all five threads meet at the Gate, live on every world. The Lantern arrives, the Face speaks, Graça moves, the Speaker picks up her pen, and Joana must name who gave the order in front of a million copies of herself.

## 8. Twists

I listed twelve and kept five. I dropped "Joana is secretly the General", the stock "you are the clone" twist, which also breaks rule 1. I dropped "Lúcia was a Foundry spy" because it cheapens the victim.

**1. The order is real** (end of act one). Everyone expects a forgery. Joana's test proves the code genuine.
*Plants:* Second says flatly in scene one, "Her codes died with her." The order says "by any means", a phrase the General used and clones never do.

**2. The Face is the General** (mid-game). She came back through the Gate weeks ago, forty-five years old.
*Plants:* in an early speech the Face gets the General's childhood dog's name wrong, then fixes it, a test for clones who are listening. Her bodyguard is Filipa, who used to play the role. Yusuf says, "Your General looks older on stage than on the posters."

**3. The army is her old self, and the Choir are passing through** (end of act two). In the Vault, Joana finds the scan dates and the Rask sabotage orders. Second translates the Lantern's word "passing".
*Plants:* every clone's last memory is the scan party, a running joke. Museum footage shows Choir ships turning away. An early gunner says the Choir only hit ships that shoot first.

**4. Second sent the order** (start of act three). The voice guiding Joana all game used the General's code to tell Bene to stop Lúcia. Lúcia was carrying the General's stand-down broadcast.
*Plants:* Second asks to join the case. A four-second gap in the network log, when the order went out, is one Second "can't explain". Second keeps saying, "She never forgave a change of heart."

**5. We gave the order** (climax). Second obeyed the order from the hangar, given before the scan. So every clone remembers giving it. Joana does too: the half-memory of a hangar and a voice saying "whatever I say later" has nagged her all game.
*Plants:* Joana's flashes of that memory, set off by the smell of engine oil and Second's tone. The shrine plaque, "Her Last Orders", which the player assumes means the battle. The Face flinching when she enters the shrine.

## 9. Moments people will talk about

1. The first interview: Bene and Joana finish each other's sentences. "You'd have done the same." "I know." Then Joana says something the General never would, and Bene's story cracks.
2. A million salutes: at dawn the whole Line turns as one to salute the Gate, and the camera finds one soldier with a white streak in her hair.
3. The mirror fight: Joana duels a clone who knows her every move. She wins by doing what the General was too proud to do: she backs off.
4. The hat comes off: on a parade stage the Face removes the ceremonial hat and says the dog's name right. A stadium of her copies goes silent.
5. "I'm older than you now." Graça meets her original: "You're not my mother. You're my kid sister who stayed out late."
6. The confession: Second plays a thirty-year-old recording, the General's young voice: "Whatever I say later." Then: "I did what she told me. What you told me, Captain."
7. Yusuf looks up: The General always watched doors, never ceilings, and so does every clone. Yusuf looks up and finds the crane camera they all walked under.
8. The crossing: the guns go quiet one battery at a time, and glass ships full of light pass overhead, close enough to touch.

## 10. How the player plays

You solve the case with your hands and eyes. There are no pages to read and no objects to click through.

- Replay the scene: every soldier's armour records. Joana stitches the camera views into a 3D replay of the hangar and scrubs time to find what the angles hide.
- Find one face in a million: suspects look the same, so you track gait, heartbeat, posture, chosen marks and small habits, through crowds, ships and stations.
- Read your own tells: Joana knows each suspect's tells because they're hers. Watch pulse, eyes and hands, and pick the moment to say what the General never would.
- Hide in plain sight: Joana can walk anywhere, since she looks like everyone. So does everyone hunting her. Stealth turns on things like walking like a gunner, not an officer.
- Send Yusuf: he sees what the General's mind always skipped. Her blind spots become a tool.
- Learn the light: you help Second match the Choir's pulses to what their ships do, one word at a time.
- Break in and fight: a heist on the Vault, boarding fights on the Line, a chase through the Marker Field, the mirror duel.
- Make the case: at key points you fill in who ordered whom, how and why. The game checks answers in batches, so guessing doesn't pay.

## 11. Endings

**A: Open the Line.** Joana wins the army over. The General takes the blame in public, the Line lets the Choir pass, the Foundry's crimes come out and the war ends.

**B: Hold the line.** The army trusts the General it remembers over the one it met. The Line fires, the Choir fight back for real, and Joana carries the truth into a real war. It's bleak, and it makes the General's return pointless.

**C: A million choices.** Second can't break its order and won't lie. Joana finds the honest way out: the order said "whatever *I* say later", and the woman who gave it is now a million people. So Second asks them.
Every clone hears the hangar recording and the Lantern's message, and decides alone. For the first time, a million copies of one woman disagree. Enough guns go quiet, and the Choir pass. Graça's coup dies, because she can't command an army that just learned to say no. The Speaker tears up the sale. Bene goes free, one of the first to stand down. The General has no home in this time, so she goes through the Gate with the Choir. "Hold my place." Joana stays.

**I'd pick C.** One act answers the mystery (who gave the order: all of us), the clone question (the copies become people by disagreeing) and the war. The player has practised it all game by telling one clone from another. The General lives, and one question stays open: what waits at the Harbour?

## 12. Simplicity check

**After an hour, can a stranger say it in one line?** Yes: "Clones of a dead general guard an alien gate, and one clone killed another on the General's orders." By then the player has seen the Line, the Gate, the murder, the order and Joana's face on a million soldiers.

**Invented terms (five):** the Line (the wall and its army), the Gate (the alien ring), the Foundry (the clone company), the Choir (the aliens) and Second (the General's AI). "The Assembly" just means the government. Every other name is a person.

**At any moment the player knows** the question (who gave the order), the clock (days to the crossing) and what each side wants. The army wants to hold. The Assembly wants to stop paying. The Foundry wants to keep selling. The Choir want to pass. The General wants the Line opened.

**Risks:**

- One face for most of the cast could confuse players. Give each speaking clone her own marks, voice, posture and age, and keep the core clones to five: Joana, Bene, Lúcia (in replays), Graça and the Face.
- "Clones kill on an order" will remind people of Order 66. Show the differences early: one killing, one confession, an investigation, and an order that came from a person's promise.
- "The aliens were never hostile" is familiar from Ender's Game and Arrival. Revealing it mid-game makes the story about whether people believe it in time.
- The General could upstage Joana. Keep her hidden for half the game and gone at the end, and make Joana the only one who can solve the case.
- The Gate does one thing, so players who want time loops won't get them. That keeps it simple.
- The crossing, the General's return and the murder land together for a reason. The Gate runs every thirty years, she went in with the last crossing and came out with this one, and Lúcia dies because the General came back.
