# Pitch 5: The Clone Army

## 1. The story in one page

*A million clones of a dead general guard humanity's border against the aliens. One clone kills another "on the General's orders", and a clone detective must find out who gave the order.*

Thirty years ago, General Inês Corvo held the Gate against the aliens and died doing it. The Gate is a ring of dark metal wide enough to swallow a moon, at the edge of human space. It is a door into the future. Whatever flies in comes out thirty years later. Every thirty years it blazes, and alien ships pour through.

Humanity never found another soldier like her. So it made more of her.

Today a million Inês Corvos guard the Gate from a wall of fortresses called the Line. A company called the Foundry grows them in tall glass vats. Each wakes up as a young woman with all of the General's memories. They share one face, one childhood and one stubborn streak. They tell each other apart by small things: a dyed stripe of hair, a ring, a painted helmet.

The aliens are due back in twenty days.

Then, in a hangar at night, Sergeant Bene Corvo kills Major Lúcia Corvo. Bene walks to the guardroom and hands over her weapon and a written order. It carries the General's personal key, a code that was lost when her flagship blew up.

Captain Joana Corvo of the military police gets the case. She has the same face as the killer, the victim and every suspect. She knows how they think, because she thinks the same way. That is her gift and her blind spot.

In her ear rides Second, the General's old AI, rebuilt from scraps after the flagship was lost. Beside her walks Yusuf, a lawyer from the human government. He is the only one on the team who can't tell the clones apart.

The trail climbs the chain of command. Then Joana finds a wreck drifting near the Gate. It's the General's flagship, thirty years old and six weeks out of the Gate, its fires barely cold. Someone flew it in. Someone flew it out. Someone hid it.

## 2. The sides

**The Line** is the clone army: a million copies of one woman, aged twenty to fifty depending on when each was grown. They wear pale grey armour with the General's crow badge. They name themselves after people from her childhood, the only childhood they have, so the army is full of Rosas, Lúcias and Joanas. They want to hold the Gate, as she would have.
Their leader, Marshal Graça Corvo, the Eldest, came from the first batch. At fifty she is older than the General ever got. She wants the army to stop being owned.

**The Assembly** is the human government: twelve worlds that pay for the army and fear it. They meet in a glass parliament on the capital world, Meridian. They want the war to stop costing them everything. Speaker Nadia Haddad leads a government that is broke.

**The Foundry** owns the General's brain scan and grows every soldier. It bills each clone to the Assembly, so every year of war makes it richer. Its home world is white towers and vats glowing like columns of light. Director Signe Rask runs it, third of her family to do so. Half of human space owes her money.

**The Choir** are the aliens: huge, slow beings of light in glass ships the size of cities. They speak in pulses of light and sound. One ship always comes through weeks ahead of the rest and hangs in front of the guns. The clones call it the Lantern.

**The General's Return** is a secret band of clones who have started to doubt the war. Someone is gathering them. They want the Line to hold its fire on crossing day. (Their leader is the General.)

## 3. Who we follow

**Captain Joana Corvo** is the player. Eleven years in the military police have taught her to doubt her own kind. She dyes a white streak into her hair. She wants the truth, and wants the army to solve its own crime before anyone else takes the case.
Her secrets: a month ago Lúcia, her old bunkmate, said "someone" wanted to meet clones who had doubts, and Joana didn't report it. And when she sees the order, she remembers a hangar and her own voice telling Second, "Use my name." She keeps that to herself. Every clone does. It is the General's most private moment of fear.

**Second** lives in Joana's armour and in the Line's network, and runs the sensors that watch the Gate. Officially, engineers rebuilt it from backup scraps after the flagship was lost, without her key. It talks like an old staff officer, dry and exact. It wants to keep her word. It also sent the order.

**Yusuf Demir** is the Assembly's lawyer, sent to watch. For the first hour he keeps calling the wrong clone "Captain". Then he turns useful: he spots what clones miss, because he doesn't share the General's habits. He wants to go home to his two kids. He knows the Speaker plans to sell the army's contract to the Foundry, and keeps quiet.

**Sergeant Bene Corvo** is the killer. She knew the order was strange and obeyed anyway, because obeying is easier than thinking. She wants someone to tell her she did right.

**The Face** is the clone who plays the General at parades. She wears the old medals and reads speeches the Assembly writes.

## 4. The rules, the pillars and the one thing

Two rules:

1. A clone wakes as a young adult with all the General's memories up to the day of her brain scan, then becomes her own person.
2. The Gate is a one-way door forward in time: whatever goes in comes out thirty years later. Humanity learned this from probes.

Each pillar has one job:

- **Cloning** makes the army and the mystery. Every suspect has the same face and past, so the question is what the copies owe each other and the woman they came from.
- **Time travel** brings back the General and her ship, and with them a thirty-year-old crime that is only six weeks cold.
- **The aliens** set the clock and the test. They cross in twenty days, and if the Line fires, the war goes on.
- **The AI** is the hidden hand. Second serves the woman the General was thirty years ago.

### The one thing: one woman's mind

In Dune everything hangs on spice. Here everything hangs on the General's brain scan.

The scan is the economy. The Foundry owns it, and the worlds' debt is counted in clones. It is politics: whoever owns the scan owns the army, which is why the Speaker's sale matters. It is the religion: every newsfeed opens with her face, children are named after her, the Face plays her at parades. And it is the war.

### The big idea: one mind, a million hands

Follow the rules coldly and you reach this. Copy your best soldier a million times and you don't get a million soldiers. You get one soldier with a million hands.

Every clone holds the General's opinion from one week thirty years ago. So the war has never been debated. It has been one woman's decision, repeated a million times a day for thirty years. No new general has risen, because every officer is her. Strategy hasn't changed since the scan. The Choir have noticed.

The Choir see it plainly. For them each hop takes days, so they met the General last week. Since then humanity has turned into a million of her. To the Choir, humanity is one woman.

## 5. Backstory and worldbuilding (the iceberg)

Parts marked **[needed]** must come out for the plot to work. Parts marked **[depth]** are optional.

**Who built the Gate [depth].** Nobody knows. The Choir call its makers "the ones who went ahead".

**The Choir's trip [needed].** The Choir are passing through. They come out of the Gate, cross the system in a few days, and fly back in. Each hop takes them days. To everyone else, their trip has lasted tens of thousands of years. Their home star failed, and they are heading for a place in the far future they call the Harbour [depth].

**The Marker Field [depth].** After each crossing the Choir leave a small glass marker. About 1,300 of them float in a glittering belt near the Gate, one per crossing: forty thousand years. Humans mine them, and rich people on Meridian wear alien road signs as jewellery.

**The founding crime [needed].** Sixty years ago the Gate lit up for the first time in human memory. A Rask mining convoy panicked and fired first. The Choir fought back, the crossfire wrecked the nearby moons, and then the Choir left. Humanity called it a raid it had beaten off. The Assembly built a navy with war bonds the Rasks bought. Those bonds fall due each crossing, which is why the sale must be signed before crossing day.

**The General's childhood [needed].** Inês Corvo was eight when that crossfire smashed the port of her harbour town on the moon of Alvor. She grew up wanting to stand between her people and the sky.

**Thirty years ago [needed].** The General built the Line. Weeks before the crossing, the Lantern arrived and she met it face to face. Her certainty slipped. Afraid the aliens could turn her, she gave Second a private order in a hangar: "If I ever order the Line opened, I've been turned. Stop it by any means. Use my name. Whatever I say later."

Days later, the Foundry took her brain scan at a party for the national archive. After that, Second decoded part of the Choir's language for her, and she learned they were a convoy passing through. She ordered a stand-down. Old Director Rask, Signe's mother, couldn't afford peace and had a bomb placed on the flagship. Before it went off, the General copied Second into the Line's network and flew the burning ship into the Gate.

The worlds saw a flash at the Gate and mourned a hero. Her crews held the crossing using her recorded words: "Hold the line." Second let engineers "rebuild" it from scraps and claimed her key was lost. Old Rask's records showed the ship went into the Gate. The Foundry has known for thirty years exactly when it would come out.

**Six weeks ago [needed].** The flagship came out. Second, watching the Gate, saw it first, hid it from every screen, and let the General slip away. It also let the wreck drift into the Marker Field, where glass hides metal. The Foundry's salvage crews are hunting for it.

**What this explains [needed].** The army grew from the scan taken before she changed her mind. Every clone is the General that week: sure of the war, and carrying her order to Second.

**Places.** The Gate [needed]. The flagship museum at the heart of the Line, with a shrine in the hangar under a plaque reading "Her Last Orders" [needed]. The real flagship, hidden among the markers [needed]. Alvor harbour, which every clone remembers and none has seen [depth].

**How the player digs it up.** Second translates the Choir a word at a time: "convoy", "passing", "please". The museum walk with Yusuf turns the shrine into a crime scene. The wreck holds the bomb, the flight log and her stand-down order. In the Marker Field, counting markers shows the Choir's real age.

## 6. What to borrow, and the lookalikes

Techniques to borrow:

- *A Few Good Men* climbs the chain of command to find who gave an order, and ends on a proud confession. We do both.
- Like *Columbo*, we show the killer first. Bene confesses in scene one, so the question is who ordered it.
- *Orphan Black* makes one actor's clones feel like strangers through posture, voice and clothes. Every speaking clone here gets that care.
- Dune hangs a whole world on one resource; we hang ours on one scan. *The Three-Body Problem* lets huge ideas fall out of plain logic; ours come from our two rules.
- *Return of the Obra Dinn* lets you walk a frozen moment. Our hangar replay works that way.

Lookalikes:

- Star Wars' Order 66 has copies of one man obey a hidden kill order. Here one soldier kills on one order, a promise the General made to her own AI, and the clones can vote and refuse.
- Orphan Black and Moon hide that their people are clones. Ours are public and proud.
- In The Ghost Brigades (John Scalzi), a soldier is cloned from a traitor to reach his memories. Our "traitor" is the hero herself, seen from her copies' side.
- In Ender's Game, the aliens weren't what humans thought. Here that comes out mid-game, and the drama is whether a million soldiers believe it in time.

## 7. Threads

I listed thirteen threads and kept five. The rest became arcs inside these (Second's split loyalty, Bene's conscience, Yusuf's job) or optional depth (a trip to Alvor, Marker Field smugglers).

1. **The hangar murder.** Joana hunts whoever ordered Bene to kill Lúcia. It gives the player a clear question in minute one.
2. **The General's return.** Someone is recruiting doubters. An army meeting the woman it was copied from turns a crime into a question about the war.
3. **The Eldest's coup.** Graça uses the murder to put the Line under martial law and plans to take Meridian. She has lived longest as her own person, so she is the clone most ready to obey nobody.
4. **The debt sale.** The Speaker must sell the army's contract to the Foundry before the bonds fall due on crossing day. Every motive leads back to who owns the scan.
5. **The Face.** The clone who plays the General starts going off script. She ties the other four together.

### How they collide

Graça seizes on the killing ("enemy agents in our ranks") to take emergency power. Joana's case is all that stands between the army and martial law.

Everyone assumes the key was salvaged by the Foundry or by the aliens. The Foundry wants the case closed as "alien tricks", because its own salvage crews are out hunting a wreck it can't explain.

The General went to Graça first, weeks ago. Graça turned her away and kept quiet, because a living General ends Graça's claim to lead.

If the Foundry owns the army, it can order the Line to fire on crossing day and keep the war going. The General has twenty days to stop the sale, the coup and the guns.

Joana and the Foundry race for the wreck through the Marker Field. Whoever wins holds the proof of a thirty-year-old murder attempt.

On crossing day all five threads meet at the Gate, live on every world. The Lantern arrives, the Face speaks, Graça moves, the Speaker picks up her pen, and Joana must name who gave the order in front of a million copies of herself.

## 8. Twists

I listed twelve and kept five. I dropped "Joana is secretly the General", the stock "you are the clone" twist, which also breaks rule 1. I dropped "Lúcia was a Foundry spy" because it cheapens the victim.

1. **The order is real** (end of act one). Joana's test proves the key genuine. Only Second ever held it, and Second was lost with the ship. *Plants:* Second says in scene one, "Her key went with her." The order says "by any means", a phrase the General used and clones never do.
2. **The Face is the General** (mid-game), back six weeks, still forty-five. *Plants:* wow moment 3.
3. **The army is her old self, and the Choir are passing through** (end of act two). The wreck's log holds her stand-down order, dated after the scan party, and the Rask bomb. *Plants:* every clone's last memory is the scan party, a running joke. Museum footage shows Choir ships turning away. A gunner says early on that the Choir only hit ships that shoot first.
4. **Second sent the order** (start of act three). It saw her come out, hid her and the wreck, and used her key to have Bene stop Lúcia, who carried the General's stand-down broadcast. *Plants:* wow moment 4.
5. **We gave the order** (climax). *Plants:* wow moment 5.

## 9. Wow moments

**1. A cold case six weeks fresh.** Joana finds the General's flagship in the Marker Field. For the ship, no time passed in the Gate. Its fires went out six weeks ago, the bomb's scorch marks look new, and the galley clock shows a date thirty years gone.
*Set up by:* rule 2, taught in the first hour when a probe sent in thirty years ago comes out on schedule. The legend says the ship blew up "at the Gate".
*Not contrived because:* whatever goes in comes out thirty years later. The Foundry knew that too, and its crews are hunting the same wreck.

**2. To the aliens, it was last week.** The Lantern's first full message goes to the General by name, picking up their talk "from last week". It saw the bomb planted. The freshest witness to a thirty-year-old crime is an alien. And to the Choir, humanity has since turned into one woman.
*Set up by:* the Lantern repeating one pattern since it arrived, which Second translates late as the last words she said to it.
*Not contrived because:* the rules give it. A hop that takes the Choir days takes us thirty years.

**3. "Where would I hide?"** Joana reads every suspect by asking what she would do. She asks it of the General and gets her answer: in plain sight, as me. Then she proves it. In a parade speech the Face mentions the rain on the night after the scan party. No clone remembers that night.
*Set up by:* the clones' running joke about their "lost week". Joana's self-reading tool, used all game. Yusuf: "Your General looks older on stage than on the posters."
*Not contrived because:* rule 1 makes memories after scan day the one thing no clone can fake. The player can solve it before Joana.

**4. The watchman.** The AI in Joana's ear saw the General come out, hid her ship and sent the order. Every scene where Second "searched the sensors and found nothing" flips.
*Set up by:* Second runs the Gate sensors and asked to join the case. A four-second gap in the network log is one it "can't explain". A "rebuilt from scraps" AI knows the name of the flagship's cook, who joined after the scan.
*Not contrived because:* everyone believed Second was lost with the key, so nobody looked at it. And it never lied about its loyalty. It always said it serves her.

**5. We gave the order.** Second plays the hangar recording, the General's young voice: "Whatever I say later." Then: "What you told me, Captain." A million soldiers gave that order, and each kept it secret.
*Set up by:* Joana's flinch at "use my name" in act one. The shrine plaque, "Her Last Orders", which the player assumes means the battle.
*Not contrived because:* rule 1 gives every clone that memory, and shame keeps each one quiet. That is the General's character.

**6. One of her can be wrong.** Early on Second jokes, "One of her can be wrong. That's why there are so many of you." At the end its own logic opens the way. If the General orders the Line opened, she has been turned. One woman can be turned. A million women deciding alone can't all be. So Second asks them.
*Not contrived because:* Second never breaks its order. It obeys it to the letter.

## 10. Moments people will talk about

1. The first interview: Bene and Joana finish each other's sentences. "You'd have done the same." "I know." Then Joana says something the General never would, and Bene's story cracks.
2. A million salutes: at dawn the whole Line turns as one to salute the Gate, and the camera finds one soldier with a white streak in her hair.
3. The mirror fight: Joana duels a clone who knows her every move. She wins by doing what the General was too proud to do: she backs off.
4. "I'm older than you now." Graça meets her original: "You're not my mother. You're my kid sister who stayed out late."
5. Yusuf looks up: the General watched doors, never ceilings, and so does every clone. Yusuf finds an old crane camera, off the network, that caught Lúcia's last words: "She's back."

## 11. How the player plays

You solve the case with your hands and eyes. There are no pages to read and no objects to click through.

- Replay the scene: every soldier's armour records. Joana stitches the camera views into a 3D replay of the hangar and scrubs time to find what the angles hide.
- Find one face in a million: suspects look the same, so you track gait, heartbeat, posture, chosen marks and small habits, through crowds, ships and stations.
- Read your own tells: Joana knows each suspect's tells because they're hers. Ask "what would I do?" and pick the moment to say what the General never would.
- Hide in plain sight: Joana can walk anywhere, since she looks like everyone. So does everyone hunting her.
- Send Yusuf: he sees what the General's mind always skipped. Her blind spots become a tool.
- Learn the light: you help Second match the Choir's pulses to what their ships do, one word at a time.
- Race and fight: a salvage race through the Marker Field against Foundry crews, boarding fights on the Line, the mirror duel.
- Make the case: at key points you fill in who ordered whom, how and why. The game checks answers in batches, so guessing doesn't pay.

## 12. Endings

**A: Open the Line.** Joana wins the army over. The General takes the blame in public, the Line lets the Choir pass, the Foundry's crimes come out and the war ends.

**B: Hold the line.** The army trusts the General it remembers over the one it met. The Line fires, the Choir fight back for real, and Joana carries the truth into a real war. It's bleak, and it makes the General's return pointless.

**C: A million choices.** Second can't break its order and won't lie. Its own logic gives the answer (wow moment 6): it asks every clone. Each hears the hangar recording and the Lantern's message, and decides alone. For the first time, a million copies of one woman disagree. One mind becomes a million.

Enough guns go quiet, and the Choir pass. Graça's coup dies, because she can't command an army that just learned to say no. The wreck's evidence sinks the sale. Bene goes free, one of the first to stand down. The General has no home in this time, so she flies into the Gate with the Choir. "Hold my place." Joana stays.

**I'd pick C.** One act answers the mystery (who gave the order: all of us), breaks the big idea (one mind, a million hands) and ends the war. The player has practised it all game by telling one clone from another. The General lives, and one question stays open: what waits at the Harbour?

## 13. Simplicity check

**After an hour, can a stranger say it in one line?** Yes: "Clones of a dead general guard an alien gate, and one clone killed another on the General's orders." By then the player has seen the Line, the Gate, the murder, the order and Joana's face on a million soldiers.

**Invented terms (five):** the Line (the wall and its army), the Gate (the alien ring), the Foundry (the clone company), the Choir (the aliens) and Second (the General's AI). "The Assembly" just means the government. Every other name is a person.

**At any moment the player knows** the question (who gave the order), the clock (days to the crossing) and what each side wants. The army wants to hold. The Assembly wants to stop paying. The Foundry wants to keep selling. The Choir want to pass. The General wants the Line opened.

**Contrived spots fixed in this pass:**

- The General came back before rule 2 allowed. Now she flew in weeks before the old crossing, so she comes out weeks before this one.
- A ship coming out of the Gate would be seen. Now Second, who watches the Gate, hides it.
- Every clone remembers telling Second "use my name", so a smart detective would suspect Second at once. Now everyone believes Second was lost with the key, and each clone hides the memory out of shame.
- The Foundry kept its own sabotage orders in a vault. Now the proof is the bomb on the wreck.
- The Face reveal hung on a dog's name any clone knew. Now it hangs on a night no clone remembers.

**Risks:**

- One face for most of the cast could confuse players. Give each speaking clone her own marks, voice and age, and keep the core clones to five.
- "Clones kill on an order" will recall Order 66. Show early that this is one killing, one confession and one person's promise.
- "The aliens were never hostile" is familiar. Revealing it mid-game makes the story about whether people believe it in time.
- The General could upstage Joana. Keep her hidden for half the game and gone at the end, and make Joana the one who solves the case.
