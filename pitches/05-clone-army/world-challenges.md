# Pitch 5: world challenge log

This is how `world.md` was argued into shape. Round 1 is the first draft in brief. Rounds 2 to 5 attack it. Each challenge has an answer and what changed.

## Round 1: first draft, in brief

- **Central thing:** the Gate's thirty-year clock.
- **Deep past:** unknown makers built the Gate. The Choir have looped through it for 40,000 years toward "the Harbour". Each crossing leaves a glass marker.
- **Humans:** they settled near the Gate 120 years ago for its mines. A Rask convoy fired first sixty years ago. The Assembly built a navy on Rask war bonds. The General died thirty years ago. The Foundry grew a million copies of her.
- **Tech:** sleeper ships, vats, brain scans and one AI, Second.
- **Open questions:** why clones and not machines, why only one mind, and why nobody else uses the Gate.

## Round 2: does the draft hold together?

**1. Why does the Lantern come weeks ahead every time?**
Under rule 2 the gap between two ships' exits changes by the difference in how long each stays out. If the Lantern stays out six weeks and the convoy stays out a few days, its lead shrinks every crossing and is gone in two. So both must stay out the same time.
*Changed:* the convoy's loop round the giant planet takes six weeks. The Lantern waits those six weeks at the door, then goes back in as the convoy arrives. It stays six weeks ahead forever. A side effect: the Lantern has never seen a battle.

**2. Why do the Choir come out at all? Why not stay inside the Gate?**
A ride through the Gate is one hop. To go further you must come out and go in again. A city-sized ship can't turn on the spot.
*Changed:* the convoy swings round the giant planet and skims its upper air to refill. That gives the loop a physical reason and a length.

**3. Humans settled next to an alien gate by luck?**
That is a coincidence, and it helps the plot. Better: the Gate flares every thirty years when the convoy passes, and from twenty-four light-years away it looks like a star that keeps time.
*Changed:* humans came because of the Lighthouse. The ark's motto is "We came to answer." The war becomes a betrayal of the colony's own reason to exist.

**4. Why a clone army and not drones or AI warships?**
Machines alone are cheaper, so I needed two honest reasons. First, AI law: the Old Worlds broke up their giant AIs, and each AI now belongs to one person, so no AI can command an army. Second, the war comes once a generation. Every human veteran is old by the next crossing. A cloned veteran never ages out. Drones still exist as guns and sensors, but people give the orders.
*Changed:* added the AI law. It also explains why Second still obeys a dead woman, and it supports ending C.

**5. Why copy one person, not a hundred veterans?**
A scan only wakes in a body grown from the same person's cells, and tuning a new scan takes years and a fortune. One body line is cheapest. The Assembly also wanted her face.
*Changed:* the Foundry holds other commanders' scans from the same night and has never used them. That gives Signe a lever.

**6. Why hasn't every rich person copied themselves?**
Over 88 years someone would try. The Copy Law says a copy inherits nothing. Your copy is a stranger with your memories and none of your money, so few bother. The one family that controls the vats could ignore that.
*Changed:* added the Copy Law. Added the optional twist that Signe Rask is a copy of her mother.

**7. Why hasn't anyone exploited the Gate?**
They have, in small ways: capsules, exile ahead, the very sick hoping for a cure. Sending money ahead earns nothing that waiting wouldn't. Line passes became a bribe.
*Changed:* added a modest Gate trade in section 5.

**8. The General's age doesn't add up.**
She was eight at the First Crossing and forty-five at the Second, but those crossings are thirty years apart.
*Changed:* she was fifteen at the First Crossing. Logged as a suggested story change.

**9. If humans arrived 120 years ago, they lived through a crossing before first contact.**
The current story says sixty years ago the Gate lit up "for the first time in human memory".
*Changed:* landfall is 88 years ago, timed just after a flare. The ark planners knew the timetable.

**10. Why would a mining convoy be at the Gate, and why fire?**
"Panicked" alone feels thin. They were mining the markers, which make perfect optical glass, and the Lantern drifted toward them as they cut one up. That gives the Rasks guilt as well as greed.
*Changed:* the founding crime is now marker cutting plus the first shot.

**11. "Beings of light": is that physics or magic?**
Pure energy beings are magic and a cliché.
*Changed:* the Choir are large, slow, glowing animals in lit water. They look like light from outside.

**12. Why do the bonds fall due on crossing day?**
Bonds sold in panic sixty years ago, paid out over two crossings, fall due in full at the second. Families hold some, so default hurts voters too.
*Changed:* named them crossing bonds.

## Round 3: what would people really do?

**1. Is a million clones plausible?**
If the vats wake about 35,000 adults a year, a million takes thirty years. That is exactly why the army's ages run twenty to fifty. With about thirty million people in human space, one person in thirty is a Corvo. That explains the debt.
*Changed:* added the numbers.

**2. Twelve worlds in one system. Is that big enough for the brief's "interstellar reach"?**
With no faster-than-light travel, the reach comes from three things: the Old Worlds at twenty-four light-years, the Choir's forty-thousand-year road, and the Gate's makers. One system also keeps the live broadcast on crossing day honest. A faster-than-light drive would add a third rule and make the Gate less special.
*Changed:* kept one system. Added the Old Worlds' stale letters ("Did you try talking to them?") as cheap distance.

**3. Over 88 years, what would a colony with vats do?**
It would grow its population fast and stop treating vat birth as odd. The company running the vats would become the bank and the biggest employer.
*Changed:* the Foundry grows about half of all babies. That is why the Assembly can't simply default on it.

**4. "Humans fired first" is a stock reveal (Ender's Game, many others). Is it the average choice?**
Yes, partly. It stays because the story needs a founding lie. I made it specific: humans were cutting up the aliens' history books for lenses, and rich people wear the pieces. The fresher buried truths sit beside it: the Lantern has never seen a battle, and a war that skips generations.
*Changed:* the founding crime became the marker cutting.

**5. Why didn't the Choir wipe out the Line?**
They're passing. They have met miners and soldiers before. They outlast instead of winning. Their escorts hit only what fires.
*Changed:* nothing new. I wrote the reason down.

**6. Nobody in sixty years learned the Choir's language?**
No one paid for it. War profits, bonds and pride all pushed the other way. Much of the speech runs below human hearing. The best teaching text, the markers, was being cut up for glass. Second did it in days only because the General asked.
*Changed:* the incentive is now in the faction notes.

**7. If wreckage comes out on schedule, the flagship's return is predictable. Why wasn't it seen?**
The Foundry predicted it, which `exploration.md` already says. The Line watches closely only from the Lantern's arrival. The flagship came out twenty days before that, when only Second was watching.
*Changed:* added the return-season timetable.

**8. Clones remember Alvor next door. Wouldn't they go?**
They did. Early batches walked off to see it, so the Assembly closed Alvor to the army.
*Changed:* this now explains why "none has seen it".

**9. Why scan a general at a party?**
A party alone is vanity. Scanning commanders before a battle, as insurance in case you lose them, is what a frightened government would do.
*Changed:* pre-battle insurance scans. The party is just the night it happened.

**10. Engineers "rebuilt" Second from scraps. Wouldn't they notice a whole copy?**
Under the AI law, an AI's private memory belongs to its person, and nobody may open it without her. The Assembly wanted a working network more than a court case.
*Changed:* Second's legal status is "belongs to the Line", a fudge nobody tested.

**11. The museum is "the flagship", but the flagship went into the Gate.**
That is a contradiction in `exploration.md`.
*Changed:* the museum is her headquarters hangar on the Line. Logged as a suggested story change.

## Round 4: second-order effects and clichés

**1. What would six weeks of the last war coming back actually produce?**
Living sailors from thirty years ago, reunions with children now older than them, salvage crews, and smugglers. That is the return season. It's not a coincidence that the story falls in it, because the story happens before a crossing, and that is when the season runs.
*Changed:* the return season is the game's setting and a source of witnesses.

**2. Is the Lantern never seeing a battle a cheat?**
No. It follows from fix 1 in round 2. It explains why it keeps trying to talk: from its side, every human it has met was a stranger waiting at a door.
*Changed:* added as buried truth 7.

**3. Must the Harbour be explained?**
No. It is texture. I gave three competing in-world guesses and no answer.

**4. Is "Signe is a copy" contrived?**
It follows from rules the player knows: the Rasks own the vats, the Copy Law exists, and rule 1. But it is a second clone twist next to the Face.
*Changed:* marked optional.

**5. Why can't clones vote for the Assembly?**
A million votes from one mind scared the worlds, and an army in politics scared them more. That gives Graça a real grievance.
*Changed:* written into the Line's section.

**6. What does "the army is owned" mean in practice?**
It was vague.
*Changed:* each clone serves until her growing bill is paid, which at current rates is never. The Speaker's sale sells those bills.

**7. Invented words: how many did the world add?**
The Lighthouse, the Copy Law, crossing bonds, the return season and the Returned. All are plain English. No coined words.

**8. Does anything break the two rules?**
The AI law and the Copy Law are laws, not physics. The vats and the scan limits are part of rule 1. The Lantern timing comes from rule 2.

**9. Is the time element new? (The owner's new rule: time travel stays only if it adds something new.)**
Honest answer: the core mechanic is familiar. A one-way skip into the future, where a soldier comes home unaged to a changed world, is the heart of *The Forever War* (Joe Haldeman). It also shows up in *Planet of the Apes*, *Interstellar* and every Rip Van Winkle story. *The Forever War* is the dangerous lookalike. Its soldiers fight aliens across time gaps, the war turns out to rest on a human mistake, and by the end humanity is clones. This pitch must be checked against it.
What is new here comes from the timetable, not the skip: a war that skips a generation every time, so you'd copy a veteran instead of training recruits; the last war raining out of the Gate just before the next one; the Lantern stuck six weeks ahead and never seeing a battle; and aliens who met the General "last week". Those are real, but they are consequences. A reader who hears "door into the future" still thinks they've seen it.
*Changed:* brainstormed alternatives (round 5) and put the best two in "Suggested changes".

**10. Does the world now need too much explaining?**
The player needs only the two rules, the clock and the founding lie. Everything else is found in glimpses.

## Round 5: alternatives to the time element, then a final check

The time element does two jobs. The General comes back unaged after thirty years, and the aliens remember her visit as last week. Eight other mechanics could do one or both:

1. **The last scan.** Second secretly scanned the General in her final days, after she changed her mind. Six weeks ago it slipped one extra body into a Foundry batch and woke her. The "returned General" is a clone, the only one who remembers the lost week. It needs only rule 1.
2. **Slow aliens.** The Choir live about 260 times slower than humans. Thirty of our years is six weeks to them. The Lantern has hung in front of the Line for sixty years in the middle of one sentence. Humans took it for a signal light.
3. **Slow-time aboard alien ships.** Inside a Choir ship time runs slow. The General went aboard the Lantern thirty years ago and walked out last month. This adds a rule and is close to stasis.
4. **A relativistic chase.** She chased the convoy at near light speed and came back. This is *The Forever War* exactly. Rejected.
5. **Cold sleep in the wreck.** This is a cliché, and it doesn't give the aliens' "last week". Rejected.
6. **Her mind in Second.** Second carried her mind for thirty years, and she speaks through it. There's no unaged body, and it blurs the AI and cloning pillars. Rejected.
7. **The Gate as a space door that opens every thirty years.** It keeps the rhythm and drops the time skip. Stargates are familiar too, and on its own it does neither job.
8. **Alien memory.** The Choir keep a whole visitor's mind in their glass, and the marker "plays her back". This is soft and drifts toward spooky. Rejected.

**Best two:** 1 (the last scan) and 2 (slow aliens). Together they replace rule 2 and do both jobs.
The cloning rule brings the General back, younger than she died. To the slow Choir, sixty years of war is one bad week they are still in.
The "once a generation" clock survives. The convoy moves in groups that are six weeks apart for the Choir, which is thirty years for us.
Both use rules the player already holds, and both produce new scenes. A twenty-year-old General arguing with fifty-year-old copies of herself is one. An alien sentence started before the player's character was grown is another.
Both are in "Suggested changes", with what the story gains and loses.

**Final check: anything serious left?**
- Is every date consistent? Landfall is 88 years ago. The crossings fall 60 and 30 years ago and twenty days from now. The General is 15 at the First Crossing and 45 at the Second. The flagship went in on day −20 and came out 62 days before this crossing. Yes.
- Does any coincidence help the heroes? The Lighthouse explains the location. The return season explains the timing. Second's watch explains the hiding. No.
- Does anyone act stupid? The miners panicked, which people do. The Assembly hid a known timetable for a selfish reason. No.

Nothing serious remains in the world as built. The open question is the owner's: keep the Gate or swap it out.
