# Pitch 5: The world beneath the Clone Army

This is the world under `exploration.md`. It keeps the story's frame and its two rules:

1. A clone wakes as a young adult with all the General's memories up to the day of her brain scan, then becomes her own person.
2. The Gate is a one-way door forward in time: whatever goes in comes out thirty years later.

Everything below follows from those rules and from people acting on what they want. `world-challenges.md` logs how each part was argued into shape. "Now" means twenty days before the Third Crossing.

## 1. The one central thing: the war comes back once a generation

Dune hangs on spice. This world hangs on a timetable.

Every thirty years, as regular as a tide, an alien convoy comes out of the Gate, loops round the giant planet the Gate orbits, and flies back in. Thirty years is one human generation. So the war never ends and never stays. It skips a generation every time.

Follow that coldly and the rest of the world falls out of it.

- **Why humans are here.** From the Old Worlds, twenty-four light-years away, the Gate looks like a faint star that flares every thirty years. Astronomers called it the Lighthouse, and humans crossed the dark to answer it.
- **Why the army is clones.** A soldier who fights one crossing is fifty-five at the next. A fresh army raised from a small colony every generation starts green. A cloned veteran never ages out. The General's scan is the one mind that remembers the last war, kept young forever.
- **Why the worlds are broke.** The army is needed for six weeks and paid for thirty years. Between crossings, a million soldiers must be fed, housed and billed. The war bonds were sold in thirty-year blocks and fall due on crossing day.
- **Why time travel is ordinary.** The Gate always works, not only when the aliens come. Anything that falls in during a battle comes back thirty years later, in the six weeks before the next one. The last war rains out of the Gate just before the next war starts.
- **What people believe and fear.** Lives are counted in crossings. "Born between crossings" means lucky. "A crossing child" means born in a war year. Every generation gets one war, and one turn to be brave.
- **Why the aliens see us strangely.** Each hop costs the convoy six weeks. To them, the last war was six weeks ago. Since then, humanity has turned into a million copies of one woman.

The General's scan is the most valuable object in human space. That is true because of the timetable. A war that came every year would need a different army.

## 2. The deep timeline

### Before anyone: the Gate (more than 100,000 years ago)

Someone built a ring of dark metal, wide enough to swallow a moon, and set it in orbit round a giant planet in an unremarkable star system. Nobody knows who. The Choir call them "the ones who went ahead". The name suggests they used their own door and never came back.

### The Choir arrive (about 41,000 years ago)

The Choir's home star swelled and began to fail. They built a fleet of glass ships the size of cities and crossed the gap to the Gate at well below light speed. Why the Gate? The ones who went ahead had left a way. The Choir's own record of that is the oldest, most worn marker in the field.

### The long road (40,000 years ago to now)

About forty thousand years ago the Choir made their first hop. They have made roughly 1,330 since. After each one they leave a glass marker near the Gate, one per crossing. On Earth, at the time of the first marker, humans were painting cave walls.

Twice in those forty thousand years, the markers suggest, other peoples came to the light. One left and went home. One followed the ones who went ahead into the Gate. Neither was hurt. This is deep lore only, and the game never confirms it.

### Humanity spreads (from about 2,500 years ago)

Humans left Earth on slow ships. There is no faster-than-light travel in this world. Over two thousand years they settled a cluster of nearby stars, the Old Worlds, each with its own culture and language mix.

### The great minds broken up (about 700 years ago)

On the Old Worlds, a handful of huge AIs came to run trade, banks and courts. Nothing exploded. They simply owned too much, so the Old Worlds broke them up, the way a court breaks up a monopoly. The new law, which every colony inherited, says each AI belongs to one person and serves that person's wishes. None may hold power of its own.

### The Lighthouse (about 500 to 330 years ago)

An Old World survey noticed a faint star that brightened sharply every thirty years and a few weeks. No natural star keeps time like that. For two centuries the Lighthouse was the most argued-about light in the sky, and astronomers could predict its next flare to the week.

About 330 years ago an expedition left to answer it. The Rask family, who ran an Old World company that made artificial wombs, built the nursery. Public money paid for the rest. The ship carried a few thousand sleeping crew, a bank of frozen embryos, and the vats to grow a colony. Its motto, still carved over the oldest door on Meridian, was "We came to answer."

### Landfall (about 88 years ago)

The ark was timed to arrive just after a flare, which gave the colony thirty quiet years to settle and study before the next. It found a living world, Meridian, with seas and simple native life. It found moons and asteroids rich in metal. And it found the ring.

The Rask nursery began growing the colony. Most of the first two generations were vat-born, and nobody thinks twice about it today. The nursery became the Foundry. The colony's first probe into the Gate came out thirty years later, to the day. That is how humans learned rule 2.

### The marker trade (about 80 to 60 years ago)

The colony spread to twelve worlds: Meridian, the moons of the giant planet (Alvor among them), the Foundry's world, and habitats in the asteroid belt. All twelve sit in one star system, light-minutes apart, so news travels live.

The Rasks found that the glass markers near the Gate made perfect lenses and laser mirrors. They mined the Marker Field quietly and got rich. Nobody yet knew what the markers were.

### The First Crossing and the founding crime (60 years ago)

Sixty years ago the Lighthouse flared from up close. One glass ship, the one the clones now call the Lantern, came out of the Gate. It drifted toward a Rask mining convoy that was cutting up a marker.

The miners panicked and fired. The Lantern took the hits and backed away. Then it waited.

The colony had six weeks of terror. The council of the twelve worlds called itself the Assembly, armed every mining ship it had, and sold war bonds, mostly to the Rasks. When the main convoy came out, the miners opened fire. The escorts fired back only at ships that shot. The crossfire smashed the port of Alvor. Then the convoy looped round the giant planet and left.

The Assembly called it "the Raid", a sneak attack beaten off by brave miners. The marker cutting was left out of every account. A fifteen-year-old girl on Alvor watched her harbour burn. Her name was Inês Corvo.

### Building the Line (60 to 30 years ago)

The Assembly spent thirty years building a wall of fortresses on the moons round the Gate. Inês joined the navy at eighteen and rose fast. She designed much of the Line herself.

The debt grew every year. The Foundry grew workers for the fortresses. Its salvage crews also worked the Gate, and in the six weeks before the Second Crossing they pulled out the wreckage of the First. That wreckage included the mining convoy's own records, showing who fired first. The Foundry kept them.

### The Second Crossing (30 years ago)

Here is the exact order of events. Days are counted from the moment the main convoy came out.

- **Day −42.** The Lantern comes out and hangs in front of the guns.
- **Days −40 to −30.** The General meets it. Her certainty slips. Afraid the aliens can turn her, she gives Second a private order in her headquarters hangar on the Line: "If I ever order the Line opened, I've been turned. Stop it by any means. Use my name. Whatever I say later."
- **Day −28.** The Assembly scans its top commanders, as insurance in case it loses them. The General's scan is taken the night of a party in her honour, every clone's last memory.
- **Days −27 to −22.** Second decodes part of the Lantern's signal. The Choir are a convoy, passing through. The General orders a stand-down.
- **Day −21.** Old Director Rask, Signe's mother, can't afford peace or the truth about the First Crossing. A bomb is placed on the flagship.
- **Day −20.** The General copies Second into the Line's network and flies the burning flagship into the Gate. The worlds see a flash and mourn her.
- **Day 0.** The convoy comes out. The Lantern flies back into the Gate as the others arrive. The Line holds, using her recorded words, "Hold the line." Choir escorts and human ships trade fire for six weeks. Lifeboats and wrecks fall into the Gate.
- **Day 42.** The convoy flies back in.

Within a year the Line Act ordered an army grown from her scan, and the Foundry got the contract.

### The army of one (30 years ago to now)

The Foundry's vats can wake about thirty-five thousand adults a year. To reach a million by the next crossing, it had to grow them steadily for thirty years. That is why the army's ages run from twenty to fifty. Graça, the Eldest, woke in the first batch.

Early clones kept walking off to Alvor, a harbour they remembered and had never seen, so the Assembly closed it to the army. Second was "rebuilt from scraps" and put in charge of the Line's network. Signe Rask took over the Foundry about ten years ago.

### The return season (62 days before the crossing to now)

- **62 days before the crossing.** The flagship comes out, twenty days ahead of the Lantern, exactly as it went in. Nobody is watching the Gate closely yet. Second sees it, hides it, and lets the General slip away. The wreck drifts into the Marker Field.
- **42 days before.** The Lantern comes out and hangs in front of the guns.
- **From then on.** The last war rains out of the Gate: drifting wrecks, spent shells, and lifeboats carrying sailors from thirty years ago, alive and unaged.
- **20 days before.** Sergeant Bene Corvo kills Major Lúcia Corvo in a hangar. The story begins.

## 3. The Choir

### What they are

The Choir are large, slow, glowing living creatures, each about the size of a whale. They float in lit water inside glass ships the size of cities. They talk in pulses of light, with sound running underneath, much of it too low for human ears. From outside, a Choir ship looks like a cathedral of light drifting through space.

They are not made of light. They are animals that glow, like deep-sea fish scaled up and slowed down, and they live for many centuries.

### Why they loop

The Gate sends you thirty years ahead, once. To go further you must come out and go in again. A ship the size of a city can't turn on the spot. The convoy swings round the giant planet and skims its upper air to refill its tanks. That loop takes six weeks.

The Lantern never makes the loop. It comes out first, waits at the door for six weeks, and goes back in the moment the convoy arrives. That keeps it exactly six weeks ahead, every crossing, forever. It is the convoy's doorkeeper and its voice. It meets whoever is waiting at the Gate. Because it leaves as the convoy arrives, it has never seen a crossing battle.

### How long they have been travelling

Each hop costs the convoy six weeks. 1,330 hops come to about 150 years of their own time. A Choir who was young when the home star failed is old now, but alive. The keeper of the Lantern may remember leaving home. To humans, that was forty thousand years ago.

### What they want

They want to pass. They are heading for something in the far future they call the Harbour. The game never says what it is. Three in-world guesses compete:

- Second thinks it is a time, not a place: the date the ones who went ahead promised to meet them.
- Some astronomers think the Choir are waiting for a new star to form in a dust cloud near the Gate.
- Some clones think the Choir no longer remember, and keep going because going is what they are.

### How they fight

Choir escorts hit only what fires on them, and only enough to stop it. In forty thousand years they have met miners, soldiers and at least two other peoples. They don't try to win. They outlast.

### The markers

Each marker is a house-sized block of glass holding a light-written record of one crossing. Together they are the Choir's logbook and the only full history of this system. The marker from sixty years ago records the miners firing first. Rask cutters had already sliced off half of it.

## 4. The factions and how they came to be

### The Line

**Origin.** The Line Act, passed in grief within a year of the General's death.

**Now.** A million copies of Inês Corvo, aged twenty to fifty. Between crossings they also build, mine and run the Gate zone, so the Line is a workforce as much as an army. Clones elect their own officers but have no vote for the Assembly, which feared a voting bloc of one mind.

**The bill.** Each clone's growing cost is billed to the Assembly over thirty years, with interest, and a clone serves until her bill is paid. At current rates none ever will be. This is what Graça means by "owned".

**Want.** To hold the Gate, as she would. Under Graça, also to stop being owned.

### The Assembly

**Origin.** The colony council of the ark. It renamed itself in the six weeks of panic sixty years ago.

**Now.** Twelve worlds, about thirty million people. It meets in the glass parliament on Meridian. One person in thirty in human space is a Corvo, and the Assembly pays for every one of them.

**The money.** Most of its debt is crossing bonds, sold sixty years ago and due in full at this crossing. Ordinary families hold some as savings, so default would ruin voters too. The Speaker's way out is to hand the clones' unpaid bills to the Foundry in exchange for the debt.

**Want.** To stop paying for a war that comes once a generation.

### The Foundry

**Origin.** The ark's nursery, run by the Rask family since before landfall.

**Now.** It grows every soldier and about half of all babies, and it banks for half of human space. It owns the General's scan and the other commanders' scans from that night, never used: a threat to grow a second army from a different mind. Signe Rask is the third Rask to run it.

**Its oldest secrets.** Its marker trade started the war. Its salvage crews found the proof thirty years ago and buried it. Its last Director planted the bomb.

**Want.** To own the army before the bonds fall due, because whoever owns a million soldiers decides whether the war goes on.

### The General's Return

**Origin.** Six weeks ago, when the General came out of the Gate. It began with a few clones who had drifted furthest from her, and Lúcia was one of them.

**Now and want.** A whispered network of doubters inside the Line, who want it to hold its fire on crossing day.

### Smaller players

- The Returned: sailors from thirty years ago who come out of the Gate in lifeboats during the return season. Apart from Second, they are the only living people who served beside the General, and their children are older than they are.
- Marker smugglers, who still cut glass from the field and sell it on Meridian. The Line chases them half-heartedly.
- The Old Worlds, whose letters arrive twenty-four years stale. The last, answering news of the First Crossing, asks politely: "Did you try talking to them?" Meridian reads it aloud as a joke.

## 5. Technology: how it evolved and what it costs

**Ships.** Fusion drives cross the star system in days or weeks. Crossing between stars takes centuries, which is why the colony is alone.

**Vats.** These came with the ark to grow a colony from embryos. A vat brings a body from embryo to adult size in about two years. The vats are clean, tall and glass, lit from inside. A vat-grown adult costs about as much as a small house. The Foundry has run the colony's vats for 88 years.

**Brain scans (rule 1).** A scan copies one mind at one moment, in a night of lying still. It can only be woken in a body grown from the same person's cells. Tuning a new scan to its body line takes years and a fortune, which is why the Foundry built the army on a single scan. Under the Copy Law, a copy is a new person who inherits nothing from the original. That is why the rich don't copy themselves: the copy would be a stranger with your memories and none of your money.

**AIs.** Under the Old Worlds' law, each AI belongs to one person. Second belonged to the General. Officially, Second was rebuilt from backups after her death and now belongs to the Line, a legal fudge nobody wanted to test in court. In truth it is still entirely hers. This law is also why no AI runs the army. A mind that must serve one person's wishes can't command a million.

**The Gate (rule 2).** People use it, in small ways:

- Probes and sealed capsules sent to be opened in thirty years.
- Exile ahead. A court can sentence someone to go through, and they come out into a world thirty years older.
- The very sick, who go through hoping for a cure on the other side.

All traffic needs a Line pass, so passes are a perk and a bribe. Nobody gets rich from the Gate. Money sent ahead earns nothing that waiting wouldn't, and you lose thirty years of control over it.

**Armour cameras.** Every clone's armour records sight, sound and pulse, a habit from the General, who hated arguments about what happened. This makes Joana's hangar replay possible.

**Marker glass.** It is the best optical glass known. It sits in the Line's gun sights, the parliament's dome and the earrings of Meridian's rich.

## 6. Daily life and culture

### On the Line

**Food.** Clones eat what the General ate as a girl: fish stew, grilled sardines, oranges. Every clone wakes loving the same dishes, and tastes drift with age. A fifty-year-old clone who takes her coffee black is a small scandal.

**Telling each other apart.** Each clone wears her own marks: a dyed stripe of hair, a ring, a painted helmet. They name themselves after people from Alvor. Each batch celebrates its waking day like a birthday.

**Jokes.** The scan party, every clone's last shared memory, is a running joke. Nobody knows who brought the bad wine. Their "lost week" is the gap between the scan and the General's death, the one stretch of her life no clone remembers.

**Fears and badges.** They fear being exactly alike, and being sold. Corvo means crow. Her family painted one on its boats, and the Line wears it on every shoulder.

### On Meridian and the other worlds

**Counting time.** People count their lives in crossings and hold crossing bonds as savings.

**Her face.** The General's face is everywhere. Children are named Inês.

**Fashion.** The rich wear chips of marker glass, not knowing they are wearing pages of an alien logbook.

**The motto.** Schoolchildren learn "We came to answer" as a brave motto. Hardly anyone notices what it says about the war.

### Holidays

**Holding Day.** This marks the end of the last crossing. Fleets pass in review, and the Face gives a speech.

**Return season.** In the six weeks before each crossing, families gather on the Line's viewing decks, hoping a lifeboat brings back someone who fell into the Gate in the last battle. Reunions are public and joyful. A twenty-four-year-old gunner meets her fifty-four-year-old son. This season is the setting of the whole game.

## 7. Places that carry history

**The Gate.** A ring of dark metal in orbit round the giant planet. It is silent between crossings and blazes when the convoy passes.

**The Line.** Fortresses cut into the moons round the Gate. The oldest ones were designed by the General, and every clone feels at home in them, because she does.

**The General's headquarters hangar.** The museum at the heart of the Line, where she gave Second the order. A shrine sits under a plaque that reads "Her Last Orders".

**The Marker Field.** About 1,330 house-sized blocks of glass in a glittering belt near the Gate, many cut and scarred by miners. It is the Choir's history and the place where the flagship hides.

**Alvor.** A warm ocean moon of the giant planet. Its harbour town was smashed sixty years ago and partly rebuilt. It has been closed to the army for twenty-five years. Every clone remembers it and none has seen it.

**The ark.** It still orbits Meridian. Its nursery deck, where the colony was grown, is now the Foundry's oldest archive and holds the pre-battle scans.

**Meridian and the Foundry's world.** The glass parliament's dome is made of marker glass. The Foundry's main hall hangs the Directors' portraits.

## 8. Buried truths and how the player can uncover them

The main story needs only truths 1 to 4. The rest are depth, found in glimpses.

1. **The army is the General from one week.** *Found by:* the scan date, the wreck's log and the Face knowing what happened on the night after the party.
2. **The Choir are passing, not raiding.** *Found by:* Second's translations, the escort ships' behaviour in old footage, and the gunner who says they only hit ships that shoot.
3. **Old Rask bombed the flagship.** *Found by:* the bomb on the wreck.
4. **Second still serves her.** *Found by:* the four-second gap in the network log, and Second knowing a cook who joined after the scan.
5. **Humans fired first, sixty years ago, while cutting up an alien marker.** *Found by:* reading the half-cut marker from the First Crossing in the Marker Field; the Foundry's salvaged convoy records; the Lantern's account of "the people with knives".
6. **The Lantern has never seen a crossing battle.** *Found by:* the timetable. It leaves as the convoy arrives, so the only shots it has ever taken were the miners'. A player who works this out understands why it keeps trying to talk.
7. **The Choir have been on the road since humans painted caves, and other peoples came to the light before us.** *Found by:* counting the markers, and reading the oldest ones in an optional chase through the field.
8. **The Old Worlds asked the right question.** *Found by:* the "Did you try talking to them?" letter, read as a joke early on, which lands differently at the end.

## 9. Suggested changes to the story

These are for the owner to approve.

### A. The time element: is it new? (decide this first)

Only partly. A one-way skip into the future, with a soldier coming home unaged, is the heart of *The Forever War* (Joe Haldeman). There, soldiers fight aliens across time gaps, the war rests on a human mistake, and humanity ends up as clones. That makes it a close lookalike. What is new here comes from the timetable: a war that skips a generation, the last war raining out of the Gate before the next, and a Lantern stuck six weeks ahead. Those are consequences, though. "A door into the future" still sounds familiar.

I tested eight replacements (see `world-challenges.md`, round 5). The best two together replace rule 2 and do both of its jobs:

1. **The last scan.** In her final days, Second secretly scanned the General again, after she changed her mind. Six weeks ago it slipped one extra body into a Foundry batch and woke her. The returned General is a clone, twenty in body, forty-five in mind. She is the only one who remembers the lost week. *Gains:* the story runs on one rule. The mystery sharpens, because she is the one copy no other copy can predict. Second's hidden hand grows, and "one clone too many" in a batch count is a fair clue. *Loses:* the six-weeks-fresh wreck and the bomb on it, the return season, and the literal survival of the original woman.
2. **Slow aliens.** The Choir live about 260 times slower than us, so our thirty years is their six weeks. The Lantern has hung in front of the Line for sixty years in the middle of one sentence, and humans took it for a signal light. The Gate becomes a plain door in space, and the convoy's groups pass it thirty of our years apart. *Gains:* a fresh image and cold logic: you can't beat someone who hasn't had time to notice the war. "To them it was last week" survives with no time travel. *Loses:* the Gate as a time machine, and wow moment 1.

My view: to keep time travel, keep the Gate, lean on the timetable ideas and check every beat against *The Forever War*. Otherwise use both replacements together. The frame, the murder, Second's order, ending C and the once-a-generation clock all survive.

### B. Fixes if the Gate stays

1. **The Lantern's timing.** If it stays out weeks while the convoy stays out days, its lead shrinks every crossing. Make the convoy's loop take six weeks, and have the Lantern wait those six weeks at the door, then go back in as the convoy arrives. On crossing day it turns into the Gate as the convoy pours out, and the General can go with it.
2. **The General's age.** She can't be eight at the First Crossing and forty-five at the Second. Make her fifteen at the First.
3. **The shrine.** The flagship went into the Gate, so the museum can't be the flagship. Make it her headquarters hangar on the Line.
4. **The return season.** Name the six weeks when the last war comes back out of the Gate. The flagship came out twenty days before it began, which is why only Second was watching.

### C. Fixes either way

1. **Why humans are here.** They came to answer the Lighthouse. "We came to answer" is a quiet plant for the ending.
2. **The founding crime.** The Rask miners fired because the Lantern caught them cutting up a marker. The Marker Field then holds the evidence.
3. **The AI law.** "Each AI belongs to one person" explains why Second obeys a dead woman. It also makes ending C legal: Second's person is now a million people.
4. **Owned soldiers.** A clone serves until her growing bill is paid, which is never. The Speaker's sale sells a million soldiers' bills.
6. **Optional: Signe Rask is a copy of her mother.** The Rasks own the vats, so they alone can quietly copy themselves. Signe would remember ordering the bomb, and the woman who sneers at clone rights would be a clone. Plant: identical faces in the Directors' portraits. Use it only if it doesn't crowd the Face.
