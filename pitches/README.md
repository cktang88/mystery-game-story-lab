# Pitch exploration

After three rounds of outlines were rejected as weird and hard to follow, the owner set a new test (see `BRIEF.md`, "Simple to understand"): a story must be as easy to state as Game of Thrones ("5 kingdoms fight for the crown") or Halo ("a dude and an AI fight the alien Covenant to save humanity"). Mysteries and twists sit on top of that simple frame.

The owner asked for each of these five pitches to be explored by its own subagent: research what makes comparable stories work, expand the pitch, and pick the most interesting threads and yarns. The owner will then choose which to develop.

| # | Folder | Frame | Mystery on top |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `01-throne/` | Five houses fight for the throne of a human star empire after the emperor is assassinated. | You play the empire's AI investigator, the only one who knows the killer came from the future. |
| 2 | `02-armada/` | A soldier and her AI fight an alien armada to save the last human colonies. | The aliens seem to know every move before it happens; someone is feeding them the future. |
| 3 | `03-prince/` | Humans and an alien empire share the galaxy under a shaky peace, and a murder could start the war. | The alien crown prince is killed on a human station; a human detective and his alien bodyguard have ten days to find the killer. |
| 4 | `04-two-futures/` | Two teams of time travellers come back from two different futures to stop the same war. | Each says the other is lying; you must work out which future is real before both destroy this one. |
| 5 | `05-clone-army/` | An army of clones of one legendary general holds the line against the aliens. | One clone murders another, and the evidence says the original general, supposedly dead for 30 years, gave the order. |

## Rules for every explorer

- **Keep the frame simple.** Everything you add must still let someone say the story in one line. If a thread needs a glossary, cut it. At most one or two sci-fi rules, each one sentence. Few invented words; prefer plain English names for things ("the Gate", "the Fleet") over coined jargon.
- **Simple on top, deep underneath.** Like Halo: the surface is "a soldier and an AI fight the Covenant", but beneath it sits a huge, interesting backstory (who built the rings, what happened to them, why the Covenant worships them) that players love uncovering. Every pitch needs that iceberg: a deep history, how the factions came to be, and the big buried secrets, revealed bit by bit through play. The backstory can be as rich as you like, as long as the main story never needs it to make sense.
- **Complexity lives in people.** Factions with clear wants, characters with secrets, double agents, betrayals, twists that reframe earlier scenes, threads that collide. Game of Thrones and Halo depth, not invented systems.
- **Cool, not spooky, nothing gross.** See `BRIEF.md`, "Tone". Spy craft, heists, battles, starships, megastructures, alien civilisations with awe. No horror, body horror, ghosts, funerals, corpse rites or gore. Crime is fine as a thriller.
- **The four pillars** (AGI, aliens, cloning, time travel) should each have a clear, easy job in the story; they need not all be equally large.
- **Original.** Borrow techniques from great works, never their content. Name the nearest existing works and say how this differs. Watch for Halo, Mass Effect, Star Wars, Dune, Foundation, The Expanse, Code Geass, Battlestar Galactica and Terminator lookalikes.
- **Generate before choosing.** For threads, twists, characters and endings, brainstorm more options than you need, then pick the most interesting and say why. Reject the obvious first idea when a better one exists.
- **Game, not novel.** Say how the player investigates and plays (not point-and-click, not walls of text).
- Use `research/` (see `research/INDEX.md`) and the web where useful. Do not use anything in `outlines/archive/` or `judging/archive/`.
