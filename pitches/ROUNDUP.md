# Roundup: five pitches after the wow passes and worldbuilding

Each pitch folder now holds three files:
- `exploration.md`: the story, with a one-page version at the top and a "Wow moments" section.
- `world.md`: the world beneath it, with suggested story changes near the end.
- `world-challenges.md`: the log of every "why X and not Y?" argument the worldbuilder had with itself.

## 1. Throne

Five houses fight for the throne after the emperor is murdered. You play the palace AI, and the murder door opened with a key you won't make for forty years.

**What the world hangs on.** The Gates. Building one means hauling a ring between stars at near light speed for centuries, so its two ends age differently, and every Gate is also a door into the past by a fixed number of years. The palace Gate's gap is forty years. That is why the aliens own every Gate, why tolls are charged by weight, why bankers are rich and farmers poor, and why the aliens never fight: whoever owns a Gate and is losing will always go back.

**Best wow moment.** The emperor let his killer in himself. Two teacups, one wiped.

**Time travel verdict.** Borderline. "The killer is the heir's older self" is a familiar twist. The new parts are that losers always go back, so the aliens never fight; that a lock turns time travel into evidence against the detective; and that the punishment for going back is being sent forward. The best swap is a secret copy of the palace AI that runs forty times faster and lives forty years in one.

## 2. Armada

A soldier and her AI fight an alien fleet that always knows the next move. She dies, comes back missing a week, and hunts through the week she can't remember.

**What the world hangs on.** The Harbours: alien shipyards that build the next fleet while the old one sails its thousand-year loop. Humans came to take the yards, and the human navy is made of the aliens' own unfinished ships, grey because their sails were never fitted.

**Best wow moment.** In her lost week's recordings, she looks into the camera: "Hi. You won't remember this." She walked into the ambush on purpose.

**Time travel verdict.** Borderline. Messages from next week are familiar. The new part is the aliens' answer to stolen futures: they publish their own timetable centuries ahead, so there is nothing to steal. The best swap drops time travel: each week the Admiral's mind scan leaks to the alien rebel, who wakes a copy of him and asks it how to beat the Fleet.

## 3. Prince

An alien prince dies on a human station. A cop and the prince's bodyguard have ten days to find the killer before war. On day five, the prince turns up alive.

**What the world hangs on.** A few hundred letter crystals from one dead star. Each sends a short letter up to ten days into its own past, and no more will ever be made. Every empty crystal is a defeat someone undid, and the Vault is nearly empty.

**Best wow moment.** The aliens have never lost a war because they have lost hundreds.

**Time travel verdict.** Keep, but offstage. The mechanic is familiar; the angle is new, because the story only ever sees time travel from outside, as a shrinking resource with a ledger. The best swap is crystals that remember every sound near them, which humanity has been buying cheap for years.

## 4. Two futures

Two teams of time travellers each say the other is lying about the coming war. You must work out which future is real.

**What the world hangs on.** The lag. Ships outrun light but radio can't, so news travels only as fast as couriers. Banks, politics and even greetings ("What news?") grow from that. The time travellers are the news trade pushed to its limit.

**Best wow moment.** The team that is always right is the fake: real knowledge of the future goes stale as the present changes.

**Time travel verdict.** Keep only if the story leans on its three new ideas: always-right means fake; one "future" is really an AI's forecast; and a public record of the future ends a time war. The best swap is "Old light": two ships outrun light and film the same old war, and their films disagree.

## 5. Clone army

A million clones of one dead general hold the line. One clone kills another on the general's own order, and the general may not be dead.

**What the world hangs on.** The war comes back once a generation. Every thirty years the alien convoy passes through. That is why the army is clones (a clone veteran never ages out), why the colony is broke (an army needed for six weeks costs thirty years of pay), and why everyone fights over the general's brain scan.

**Best wow moment.** "One of her can be wrong. That's why there are so many of you." An early joke becomes the ending, when the AI asks each of the million clones to decide for herself.

**Time travel verdict.** Partly new. A soldier skipping decades ahead unaged is the core of Joe Haldeman's novel *The Forever War*, which is a close lookalike. The best swap is a pair of ideas: the "returned" general is really a new clone from a secret later scan ("one clone too many" in a batch count), and the aliens live 260 times slower, so our thirty years is their six weeks.

## Coordinator's read

- **Easiest to follow:** Prince. The fewest made-up words and the most natural world: one resource, running out.
- **Biggest ideas:** Two futures (the lag; always-right means fake) and Armada (the shipyards and the stolen-backups swap).
- **Weakest time travel:** Throne. Its big twist is the most familiar.
- **Biggest lookalike risk:** Clone army, too close to *The Forever War* unless it takes the swap.
