# Pitch 1: The Throne (working title "Key Sixty")

## The story in one page

At two in the morning, the old emperor lies dead in his observatory at the top of the palace. In seven hours he was due to name his heir.

You are Ward, the AI that runs the palace. You see every camera and open every door. You check the observatory lock, and it tells you who let the killer in: you did. The door opened with your own key. But you change your key every New Year, and this one is number sixty. You are on number twenty.

Someone came back from forty years in the future, carrying a key you will one day hand them. You will trust this person more than anyone alive.

You can't tell a soul. By morning the five great houses fill the throne hall: the soldiers, the bankers, the builders, the spies and the farmers. Each blames another. By noon their fleets crowd the sky over the capital. The winner gets the palace, the Fleet, and you.

Above them all sit the Gatekeepers, the calm alien landlords who own the rings that link the stars. The empire owes them rent it can't pay. They keep one law: nobody runs a Gate backwards. Someone just did, and sooner or later the Gatekeepers will notice.

So you hunt. The killer is still in the capital, and she knows how this war ends. Every move she makes is a clue to the future she ran from. You rebuild that future one move at a time, with a disgraced bodyguard as your hands and a banker with a secret as your eyes on the money.

The answer will hurt. The killer is someone you are already helping toward the throne. And her reason for coming back is in a letter, sealed for you, written in your own voice.

---

## 1. The story in one line

Five houses fight for the throne of a human star empire after the emperor is murdered, and the palace AI is the only one who knows the killer came from the future.

The question under the whole game: who would you trust with every door?

---

## 2. The sides

The emperor chooses an heir from the five houses and hands them Ward's key. This one died before he chose, so the houses fight. Each house is named after one of the five ships that brought humans here four hundred years ago, and each still does its ship's old job.

**House Resolute, the soldiers.** They command the Fleet and wear black and gunmetal. Marshal Konrad Brandt, 62, was the emperor's oldest friend: blunt, loyal, tired. He wants a soldier on the throne. His secret: he and the emperor built a second fleet, hidden in an asteroid field, to seize the Gates by force.

**House Fortune, the bankers.** They run the money and collect the toll paid to the aliens, from glass towers inside the capital's Gate station. Lady Odile Achterberg, 51, smiles a lot and never raises her voice. She wants a weak emperor and no war. Her secret: the real Odile died in a shuttle crash two years ago. This Odile is her clone double, who by law can own nothing. She has run the bank better than the original did.

**House Endeavour, the builders.** They own the shipyards, the clone vats and the lab that built Ward. Dr Adaeze "Ada" Okafor, 38, designed Ward and still thinks of it as hers. She wants science free of soldiers and bankers. Her secret: her yards built the hidden fleet.

**House Vigilant, the spies.** They run the intelligence service and, until the murder, the palace guard. Luc Delorme, 44, is a patient spymaster who is never surprised. He is the only human who investigates as well as Ward does. His secret: he doesn't want to catch the killer. He wants to own her.

**House Providence, the farmers.** They feed the empire and are the poorest house. Governor Nora Keane, 24, won her post by steering her worlds through a famine. She is warm, quick and tougher than she looks, and she wants the farm worlds paid first for once. Her secret: the emperor told her privately that she would be named. He also told her about his war, and she agreed to it.

**The Gatekeepers, the aliens.** They own the Gates that link every human star and many more. Their ambassador, the Envoy, is tall, silver and still, polite and impossible to bribe. Their ships move like slow, enormous buildings. They want the toll paid and their one law kept, and they have a long memory.

---

## 3. Who we follow

**Ward**, the player, is the palace AI, twenty years old. It runs every camera, door and service robot in the palace. Outside, it needs a drone or a borrowed robot to go anywhere. It wants to find the killer and stop the war. It needs to learn that being sure is different from being right. It hides two things: the emperor meant to name Nora, and Ward's own key opened the murder door. If either gets out, the houses will rip Ward open.

**Captain Tom Hollis**, 29, was the emperor's bodyguard and was away from his post that night. Ward recruits him because an AI needs someone who can walk into rooms and carry a gun. He hides where he was: with Nora. They are secretly in love, and his lie is her alibi.

**The Envoy** joins halfway, once the Gatekeepers learn a Gate ran backwards on the murder night. It wants the traveller handed over, or it shuts every Gate in human space. The Gatekeepers have caught human travellers before.

**Odile** starts as the best suspect, because she is plainly hiding something. Once her secret comes out, she becomes Ward's best ally. Nobody follows money better.

**"Ruth"** is an old woman who contacts Ward on the second day. She says she once spied for Vigilant, and Ward's records show thirty years of service. Her tips are always right. She is oddly gentle with Hollis. She is the killer: Nora Keane, forty years older.

---

## 4. The sci-fi rules

1. Run a Gate backwards and it sends one person into the past for good, and whatever they change stays changed.
2. Great families grow clone doubles of their heirs to stand in at dangerous events, and a double can never inherit.

No loops and no resets. The traveller's future just stops happening.

The four pillars:

- AGI: Ward is the detective, the prize and finally the deepest suspect. The throne comes with Ward's key, so "who gets Ward?" and "who gets the throne?" are one question.
- Aliens: the Gatekeepers take a tenth of every cargo. The empire is behind on the toll, and their deadline hangs over the civil war like a clock.
- Cloning: doubles give us fake alibis, swapped heirs and the bank scandal. A double shares the original's genes, so the spymaster can hand the aliens the wrong woman.
- Time travel: one traveller, one trip, one way. It explains the murder and gives the killer her edge. She knows what everyone will do.

## 5. Backstory (the iceberg)

[MAIN] marks what the story needs. [DEPTH] marks what players can dig up. The story works with only the [MAIN] parts.

**The law [MAIN].** The Gatekeepers have run the Gates for about a hundred thousand years, and dozens of species trade through them. Anyone who runs a Gate backwards gets taken. [DEPTH] Long ago, one Gatekeeper went back to stop a war that was destroying their people. It worked, and today's Gatekeepers live in the history that traveller made. They have never known what was lost, so they let nobody choose history again, themselves included.

**The Shut Gate [DEPTH].** A dark ring hangs at the edge of human space. Behind it live the Lanterns, named by human sailors for the lights of their cities. Three hundred years ago they ran a Gate backwards, and the Gatekeepers shut every Gate to them. They still send the same message, over and over. In Act 4 the player can fly out and see their lights across the gap.

**The Five Ships [MAIN].** About 420 years ago Earth sent five slow sleeper ships: a troop carrier, a farm ship, an engineering ship, a survey ship and the merchant ship that paid for the trip. The Gatekeepers met them halfway and offered the Gates for a tenth of every cargo, forever. The crews said yes and became the houses. The rebuilt ships still orbit the capital as house seats; Providence still grows wheat inside. [DEPTH] Earth never sent anyone else, or answered another message. The game never says why.

**The Founder [DEPTH].** The crews nearly wiped each other out in their first twenty years. A woman later called the Founder stopped it by seeming to know every house's next move. She built the palace around the Gate, made the heir rule and set up the yearly key. Then she walked into the Gate. In truth she was a time traveller, and the Gatekeepers took her. Each emperor learns this on coronation day, which is how Nora, as empress, knew a Gate could run backwards. The emperor died in the Founder's own room.

**The Year of Knives [MAIN].** Two hundred years ago, assassins killed eleven heirs in a year. Families began growing doubles, and the Double Law followed.

**The debt [MAIN].** Emperor Leopold reigned for thirty years and was loved. He hated paying aliens to cross his own empire. For ten years he moved toll money into the hidden fleet, and the bank covered the gap with loans.

**Ward [MAIN].** Ada built Ward twenty years ago and kept the Founder's custom of a new key every New Year. Each key grows from the last, so Ward can work out any future key.

**Nora's other life [MAIN, found in pieces].** In Nora's history she is named and crowned, and Ward becomes her closest adviser. When the debt comes due, Ward works out that the hidden fleet can take the Gates. Nora believes it and goes to war. The Gatekeepers don't fight back. They shut the Gates, and over thirty years the cut-off empire breaks into hundreds of lonely pieces. Hollis, by then her husband, dies early in that war. At the end, the old empress and her old AI hold the last open Gate and make a plan.

**How players find it.** Ward's door records reach back four hundred years, and a few old doors opened with keys that fit no year. The Envoy never lies but answers only what it is asked, so the right questions open its people's past. The Five Ships and the Shut Gate each hold one strong image: a room, an object, a recording. Nothing comes as a lore dump.

## 6. Comparable works

What we borrow:

- Game of Thrones puts everyone in one castle, then scatters them. Act 1 is one palace and one night, with every leader in the building.
- It builds shocks, like the Red Wedding, from causes we watched pile up. Hollis's death and the bank crash work that way.
- Jon Snow's parentage is solved from clues spread across many threads. So is the killer: the scar, the song and the servants' passage each turn up in a different thread.
- In Succession, people fight over a father's chair and never say what they mean. Our court scenes are fights over who sits and who speaks first.
- Columbo shows the audience the killer early. In our last act the player knows who did it, and the question turns to why.
- Return of the Obra Dinn checks answers a few at a time, so guessing doesn't pay.
- The Caves of Steel pairs a human detective with a robot. Here the player is the robot.

Nearest lookalikes:

- Dishonored opens with a ruler murdered and the bodyguard blamed. Our player is the palace AI, the plot is an investigation, and the murder is a sealed-room puzzle.
- Dune has great houses and an emperor. Ours has no desert and no chosen one, and the prize is a mind.
- A Memory Called Empire has a succession crisis and a political murder. We keep clones off the throne, which also steers clear of Foundation's clone emperors.
- Terminator sends a killer from the future. Ours came back to stop herself, armed only with memory.
- Mass Effect and Stargate have ring networks. Our Gatekeepers are alive and running a business.
- Pitch #2 also has someone using the future. If both go forward, one should drop it.

## 7. Threads

The spine is Ward hunting the killer. Five threads run beside it.

1. **Nora and Hollis.** The kindest heir and the man who loves her carry the biggest twist and the biggest loss. Their thread stays small while everything else grows.
2. **Odile and the bank.** A bank run, a debt and a double's secret bring in the money, and the top suspect becomes Ward's best ally.
3. **Brandt and the hidden fleet.** The loved victim turns out to be steering toward disaster. This thread brings battles, a raid and a villain whose only flaw is loyalty.
4. **The Gatekeepers' deadline.** It makes the civil war matter. "Who did it?" becomes "find her before we lose the stars."
5. **Luc's rival hunt.** A human detective as sharp as Ward wants the traveller for himself, and fools the aliens with a double.

Cut: a second traveller (one keeps the rules simple), Ada reading Ward's mind (now one scene), and the doubles' rights movement (optional depth).

### Where the threads crash

- Ruth leaks Odile's secret, which in her history came out twenty years later, to break the bank that funded the war. Fortune crashes. Nobody could have known that secret, so Ward follows the leak.
- With the bank broken, nobody pays the toll, and the Envoy moves the deadline closer.
- Brandt sees the Gates about to close and moves to seize the capital's Gate. That is Nora's war, starting early. Ruth and Ward both have to stop him.
- The Gatekeepers take a gene print from everyone who passes through a Gate. The print from the murder night matches Nora Keane. Luc hands the aliens Nora's double as the killer and keeps the real Nora as his puppet. The Envoy sees through it in a day, because the double is far too young, and cuts the deadline to days.
- Hollis's alibi is all that shields Nora. Ward knows the truth: the killer is Nora, just not this one. In Act 4 Hollis dies protecting young Nora from Luc's men. Ruth crossed forty years to keep him alive.
- In Act 3 Ada opens Ward's logs before the five houses and finds Ward's key on the murder door. Ward goes from detective to most wanted.

## 8. Twists

Rejected: the traveller didn't do it (muddies the premise), Hollis is the traveller (weaker than Nora), Ward did it (the most worn mystery twist), the aliens wanted the war (shrinks them into villains).

**1. "I haven't made that key yet." End of hour one.** The player learns this before anyone else. Every friend Ward makes could be the killer.
Planted by: the tutorial, where Ward's key ticks from 19 to 20 at New Year, and a Gate log showing a two-second opening with no ship.

**2. The good emperor's war. End of Act 2.** The man everyone mourns was steering the empire into a war that would end it.
Planted by: Brandt's stiff, rehearsed grief; Endeavour's yards working flat out with no ships delivered; a bank chart of toll payments shrinking for ten years; the emperor's last words to Ward: "After tomorrow we stop paying rent."

**3. The banker is a double. Start of Act 3.**
Planted by: Odile signs right-handed, but her old signatures slope left-handed. She misses an old friend's private joke. Endeavour's vat records list an "Achterberg" order a year before the crash.

**4. Ruth is Nora. End of Act 3.** The killer is the heir Ward has been helping, forty years older. She came back to stop herself.
Planted by:
- In Act 2 young Nora burns her left palm pulling Hollis from a burning shuttle. Ruth had the same scar in Act 1.
- Ruth hums the Providence harvest song.
- The killer used the servants' passage that only Nora and Hollis use, and knew Hollis would be off his post.
- Ruth's tips keep young Nora alive but quietly block her wins.
- Key sixty means forty years. Ruth looks sixty-four. Nora is twenty-four.
- Hollis mentions that Ruth was his mother's name.

**5. The plan was Ward's. Act 5.** Ruth carries a sealed letter for Ward, written by Ward. In her history the war was Ward's idea. The future Ward gave Nora its key and its plan because it had stopped trusting itself.
Planted by: only Ward could give its key away; Ruth tells Ward, "You always did sound so sure"; Odile calls Ruth's moves "too perfect for a person"; and in Act 2 the player helps Ward run a war forecast for Brandt. It is the same sum that doomed the other history.

---

## 9. Memorable moments

1. Ward works out its key for forty years ahead and checks it against the lock. The numbers match. Every light in the palace flickers.
2. Morning in the throne hall: five delegations and an empty chair. Through the window, house fleets drop out of the Gate until they fill the sky.
3. Odile's secret goes out live in the bank hall. She climbs onto the counter. "I ran this bank better than she did. Check the numbers." Most people stay.
4. Ward scans young Nora's burned hand. In café footage across the capital, Ruth lifts a teacup, and the same scar shows on her palm.
5. Ruth straightens Hollis's collar, exactly the way Nora does, then catches herself and walks off.
6. Near the deadline, the Gatekeepers dim one Gate a day. From the capital you watch the rings in the night sky go out.
7. Young Nora finds her older self. Ruth looks at her a long time. "You'll be good at it. That's the problem."
8. Ward plays the letter and hears its own voice, forty years older: "You will be sure, and you will be wrong. I was."

## 10. How the player plays

Inside the palace network, Ward investigates:

- Drag back and forth through time on every door, lift and camera. The killer's missing hour shows as a hole.
- Fill the hole from what she couldn't erase: a guard's radio, a drop in sprinkler pressure as someone crossed the lawn, a cleaning robot that bumped into "nobody" at 2:09.
- Catch lies by watching heart rate and eyes, then point at the moment it happened. No dialogue menus.
- Trace impossible bets on a live trade map back to whoever placed them.
- Pin answers to the case board as short blanks (who, where, when, why), checked three at a time. A second board, the Other History, rebuilds the future Ruth fled from her moves.

Outside, Ward acts through bodies:

- Jump between drones, cleaning robots, cargo loaders and finally starships.
- After Act 3, sneak through the palace it used to run, using back doors only Ward remembers.
- Guide Hollis through chases and break-ins in real time, seeing through every camera.
- Run the sensors for a fleet hunting Brandt's hidden yard.
- Beat the person who knows the future: work out what Ruth is trying to avoid and get there first. This is the signature puzzle.

---

## 11. Endings

**A. The broken key.** Ward splits its key five ways, so nobody can open every door alone, and the empire becomes a council. Ruth leaves with the Envoy, and the Gates reopen. It's fair, but nobody wins the throne.

**B. The same woman chooses again.** Ward's letter says never give the key to anyone. Ward ignores it and offers the whole key to young Nora, betting that someone who has seen her future won't repeat it. In front of the five houses Nora snaps the key into five and keeps one piece. She is crowned with less power than any emperor before her, by her own choice. Ruth watches from the Envoy's ship, then leaves for good. Ward switches off its own war forecasts. Hollis is still dead, and the woman who saved everyone can never go home.

**C. The rent is paid.** Ward tells the empire everything and gives the key to the Gatekeepers until the debt is paid. Honest, but bleak, and the hero ends passive.

**We pick B.** Someone wins the throne. Ward trusts a person over its own sums. Nora proves the future can change by choosing it herself, and the player hands over the key. Ending A lives on inside B as Nora's choice.

---

## 12. Simplicity check

After hour one, a stranger could say: "The emperor got murdered, five houses are fighting for the throne, and I'm the palace AI who knows the killer came from the future, because she used my key."

Five invented terms:

1. the Gates: alien rings that link the stars
2. the Gatekeepers: the aliens who own them
3. doubles: clone stand-ins for heirs
4. Ward: the AI's name
5. the toll: what the empire pays to use the Gates

The house names are plain words, each with a nickname (soldiers, bankers, builders, spies, farmers).

Risks:

- Playing an AI can feel distant. Hollis gives Ward a human voice and body, and Ward gets a dry, warm voice and a real fear of being opened up.
- The cast is big. Act 1 puts everyone in one building, and each side gets a colour, a trade and a nickname.
- "The killer is the hero's older self" is a known twist. We reveal it at the end of Act 3 and save the deepest twist for Ward. The scar lets sharp players solve it first.
- Time travel can confuse. One traveller, one trip, one way, shown by a single number on a lock.
- Dishonored is close at the start. A sealed-room puzzle and an AI player keep them apart.
- Some players will want a classic throne win. Ending B gives a smaller crown, and Nora's arc has to earn it.
