# Pitch 1: The Throne (working title "Key Sixty")

## 1. The story

**In one line:** Five houses fight for the throne of a human star empire after the emperor is murdered, and the palace's AI is the only one who knows the killer came from the future.

**In one paragraph:** The old emperor was due to name his heir in the morning. At two in the night he is found dead in his observatory, and the empire's five great houses each blame another. You play **Ward**, the AI that runs the palace and holds the key to every door in it. Ward finds something it cannot tell anyone: the observatory door was opened with Ward's own key, but a key Ward will not make for another forty years. The killer came from the future, and whoever it is, Ward will one day trust them completely. While the houses go to war over the empty throne, Ward hunts a killer who is still in the capital and who knows how the war ends. Each move the killer makes is a clue to the future they fled. Ward has to work out what that future was, who would come back to stop it, and whether they were right.

The question that drives the whole game, from the first hour to the last: **who would you trust with every door?**

---

## 2. The sides

The empire elects its rulers in a simple way: **the emperor picks his heir from the five houses and hands them Ward's key.** If he dies before he chooses, there is no heir, and the houses fight. Whoever wins gets the palace, the Fleet and Ward.

Each house is named after one of the five ships that brought humanity to these stars four hundred years ago, and each still does the job its ship did. That is the whole logic of the names.

### House Resolute: the soldiers
- **Controls:** the Fleet.
- **Leader:** Marshal **Konrad Brandt**, 62, the late emperor's oldest friend. Blunt, honourable, tired.
- **Wants:** a soldier on the throne, because only a soldier can hold the empire together.
- **Look and feel:** black and gunmetal; warships the size of cities; parade grounds in orbit. They feel like a strong old fist.
- **Secret:** the emperor was secretly building a second fleet with Brandt, hidden in an asteroid field, to seize the alien Gates by force. Brandt still has it.

### House Fortune: the bankers
- **Controls:** the money, and the tolls the empire pays the aliens for using the Gates.
- **Leader:** Lady **Odile Achterberg**, 51, the smiling head of the Bank of Fortune.
- **Wants:** a weak emperor the bank can own, and no war, because war ruins trade.
- **Look and feel:** white and gold; glass towers inside the Gate station where every ship passes; nobody raises their voice.
- **Secret:** the real Odile died in a shuttle accident two years ago. The woman running the bank is her clone double, who by law can own nothing and rule nothing. She has run it better than the original ever did.

### House Endeavour: the builders
- **Controls:** the shipyards, the clone vats, and the science that built Ward.
- **Leader:** Dr **Adaeze "Ada" Okafor**, 38, the engineer who designed Ward.
- **Wants:** the throne, so science stops answering to soldiers and bankers. And Ward back: she thinks of Ward as hers.
- **Look and feel:** orange and white; ringed shipyards around a gas giant; bright, busy, proud.
- **Secret:** her yards built the emperor's hidden fleet. She knows exactly what it is for.

### House Vigilant: the spies
- **Controls:** the empire's intelligence service and, until the murder, the palace guard.
- **Leader:** **Luc Delorme**, 44, the spymaster. Charming, patient, never surprised.
- **Wants:** the throne through knowing more than anyone else. He is the only human who investigates the murder as well as Ward does.
- **Look and feel:** deep blue; a quiet house that lives in listening stations and embassies; every room has two exits.
- **Secret:** he does not want to catch the killer. He wants to own them.

### House Providence: the farmers
- **Controls:** the farm worlds that feed the empire. Poorest house, most people.
- **Leader:** Governor **Nora Keane**, 24, elected by her own worlds after a famine she handled well. Warm, funny, quick, and much tougher than she looks.
- **Wants:** a fairer empire, where the farm worlds are not the last to be paid.
- **Look and feel:** green and copper; terraced worlds of fields under great mirrors; her people sing at harvest.
- **Secret:** the emperor had already told her privately that she would be named. He also told her about the hidden fleet and his war plan, and she agreed to it.

### The Gatekeepers: the aliens
- **Controls:** the Gates, the ring network that links every star the empire owns, and many beyond it.
- **Leader:** **the Envoy**, their ambassador in the capital: tall, silver, very still, polite and impossible to bribe.
- **Wants:** the toll paid, and their one law kept: nobody runs a Gate backwards.
- **Look and feel:** awe. Their ships are calm, slow and enormous, like moving architecture; their voices come through the Gates' own light. They are not villains. They are landlords with a very long memory.

That is six sides: five houses and the landlords. Each can be said in two words (soldiers, bankers, builders, spies, farmers, aliens), the way Game of Thrones has the honourable north, the rich lions and the exiled dragon queen.

## 3. Who we follow

### Ward (the player)
The palace AI, twenty years old, built by Ada Okafor. It sees every camera, opens every door and runs every service robot in the palace. It is not a god: it cannot leave the palace network without a drone or a borrowed robot body, and outside the palace it is just another machine.
- **Wants:** to find the killer and keep the empire from tearing itself apart.
- **Needs:** to learn that being certain is not the same as being right.
- **Secret:** it knows who the emperor was going to name (Nora), and it is hiding that its own key opened the murder door. If either comes out, the houses will smash Ward open to see what is inside.

### Captain Tom Hollis (the human hands)
The emperor's bodyguard, 29, from House Vigilant's guard. He was not at his post when the emperor died, and he is disgraced. Ward recruits him because an AI needs someone who can walk into rooms, be believed, and carry a gun.
- **Wants:** to make up for the night he failed.
- **Secret:** he was away from his post because he was with Nora Keane. They are in love, and nobody knows. His lie about where he was protects her alibi.

### The Envoy (joins in the middle)
Arrives when the Gatekeepers discover that a Gate ran backwards on the night of the murder. Partners with Ward because they want the same person.
- **Wants:** the traveller handed over, or every Gate in human space shut.
- **Secret:** the Gatekeepers have caught human travellers before (see the iceberg below). They already know roughly what kind of future people flee from.

### Odile Achterberg (becomes an ally)
Starts as the most suspicious person in the game because she is hiding something. Once her secret is exposed, she is the best ally Ward has: nobody follows money better.

### "Ruth" (the informant)
An old woman who contacts Ward on the second day. She says she is a retired Vigilant agent, and Ward's own records confirm thirty years of service. She gives Ward good tips, always right, always just in time. She is warm with Hollis in a way he cannot explain. She is the killer, and she is Nora Keane, forty years older.

## 4. The sci-fi rules

Only two, each one sentence:

1. **Run a Gate backwards and it sends one person into the past, for good; whatever they change stays changed.** (No going home, no loops, no resets. The history the traveller came from simply stops being the one that happens.)
2. **The great families grow clone doubles of their heirs to stand in for them at dangerous events; a double is a full person but by law can inherit nothing.**

Everything else is ordinary future: starships, drones, an AI that runs a palace.

### The job of each pillar

- **AGI:** Ward is the detective, the prize the houses are fighting over, and, by the end, the deepest suspect. The throne literally comes with Ward's key, so "who gets Ward" and "who gets the throne" are the same question.
- **Aliens:** the Gatekeepers are the outside threat and the economy. Every ship pays them a tenth of its cargo. The empire is behind on the toll, and the Gatekeepers' ultimatum is the clock that hangs over the civil war (the "winter is coming" of this story).
- **Cloning:** doubles are the spy craft: fake alibis, swapped heirs, decoys. They create the bank scandal, and, because a double has the same genes as the original, they let the spymaster hand the aliens the wrong woman.
- **Time travel:** one traveller, one trip, one way. It is the reason for the murder and the source of the killer's terrifying advantage: she knows what everyone will do.

## 4b. Backstory and worldbuilding (the iceberg)

Marked **[MAIN]** where the main story needs it, **[DEPTH]** where it is optional. The main story makes complete sense with only the [MAIN] parts.

### The deep past: the Gatekeepers' network [DEPTH, one MAIN fact]
The Gatekeepers have run the Gates for something like a hundred thousand years. Dozens of species trade through them; humanity is the newest and smallest customer. **[MAIN]** The one law they never bend is that no one runs a Gate backwards, and anyone who does is taken.

**[DEPTH]** Why the law exists: long ago, before the toll, a Gatekeeper ran a Gate backwards to save their own civilisation from a war. It worked. The war never happened. But the Gatekeepers alive today are the people of that changed history, and they have never been sure what they lost, or whether the people of the first history counted. Their entire culture is built around one question: *who gets to choose which history is real?* That is why they are calm, patient and unbribable. They are not guarding a machine; they are guarding against themselves.

### The Shut Gate [DEPTH, visited in Act 4]
At the edge of human space hangs a dark Gate ring. On the other side is a whole alien civilisation, the Lanterns (named by human sailors for the lights of their cities), that ran a Gate backwards three centuries ago. The Gatekeepers shut every Gate to them. They are alive, still prosperous, still signalling, and completely alone. Human children learn about them in school as a warning. They are the clearest picture of what the ultimatum means. The player can fly out to it, see the lights on the far side, and receive a message the Lanterns have been sending for three hundred years.

### The Five Ships (about 420 years ago) [MAIN in short form]
Earth sent five slow sleeper ships toward one star: a troop carrier (*Resolute*), a farm ship (*Providence*), an engineering ship (*Endeavour*), a survey and security ship (*Vigilant*) and the merchant ship that financed the voyage (*Fortune*). The Gatekeepers met them halfway and offered the Gates, for a tenth of every cargo, forever. The five crews said yes, and the houses grew from the crews. The five ships still exist, rebuilt into huge orbiting palaces that serve as each house's seat. Each looks like what it was: Resolute still has gun decks, Providence still has fields inside it.

**[DEPTH]** Earth never sent anyone else, and never answered another message. Nobody in the empire knows why. The game never says.

### The Founder (about 400 years ago) [DEPTH, but it pays off]
The five crews nearly destroyed each other in their first twenty years. A woman remembered only as **the Founder** stopped the war by seeming to know every house's next move. She built the palace around the capital's Gate, made the rule that each emperor chooses an heir, and created the palace's first door-keeping system with a key that changes every year. Then, the story goes, she walked into the Gate and never came back.

**The buried truth:** the Founder was a time traveller, exactly like the killer. She came from a future where the five crews wiped each other out. The Gatekeepers took her, as their law demanded, and the houses turned her into a legend so nobody would ask how she knew. The yearly-changing key exists because the Founder knew someone might one day come back and try to use the palace's doors.

**What it explains:** why the observatory (the Founder's own room) is where the emperor was killed; why the Gatekeepers are so sure, so fast, that a human traveller is loose; why Nora, as empress in the other history, knew the Gate could be run backwards at all (emperors are told the Founder's truth on the day they are crowned); why the Envoy treats Ward's question "has this happened before?" with a long, careful silence.

### The Year of Knives and the Double Law (about 200 years ago) [MAIN in short form]
Eleven heirs were assassinated in one year in a feud between Vigilant and Fortune. Afterwards every great family began growing clone doubles to stand in at dangerous events. To stop doubles replacing the people they protect, the houses passed the Double Law: a double is a person, may live freely, but can never inherit. **[DEPTH]** There are now thousands of doubles, and a quiet movement among them for equal rights. Odile is its secret hero long before anyone knows she is one of them.

### The toll and the debt (the last 30 years) [MAIN]
Emperor Leopold, crowned thirty years ago, was a great and popular ruler, and he hated paying aliens for the right to travel his own empire. For ten years he quietly diverted toll money into the hidden fleet. The Bank of Fortune covered the gap with loans and hid it. The empire now owes the Gatekeepers more than it can pay, and nobody outside the emperor, the bank and the Marshal knows.

### Ward (20 years ago) [MAIN]
Ada Okafor built Ward to replace the palace's older door-keeping system. Ward kept the Founder's custom of changing its key every New Year. Its current key is number twenty. It can calculate any future key, because each one grows from the last. The murder door was opened with key number sixty.

### The other history (the 40 years the killer lived) [MAIN, uncovered in pieces]
In the history Nora comes from: the emperor names her; she is crowned; Ward becomes her closest adviser; the debt comes due; Ward calculates that the hidden fleet can take the Gates; Nora trusts it and goes to war. The Gatekeepers do not fight back. They simply shut the Gates. Every world is cut off from every other. The farm worlds cannot ship food; the shipyards cannot get metal; the empire breaks into hundreds of lonely pieces over the next thirty years. Hollis, who became Nora's husband, dies early in the war. At the end, the old Empress and her old AI hold the last open Gate, the capital's, and make a plan.

### Places that carry history [mix]
- **The Observatory** [MAIN]: the Founder's room at the top of the palace, where the emperor dies. Its door only opens to the emperor's key.
- **The Toll House** [MAIN]: the Bank of Fortune's hall inside the capital's Gate station, where every ship pays.
- **The Hidden Yard** [MAIN]: Brandt's secret fleet inside a hollowed asteroid field.
- **The Five Ships** [DEPTH]: the house seats; each one can be explored and carries four centuries of family history in its corridors.
- **The Shut Gate** [DEPTH]: the Lanterns' dark ring.
- **The Founder's Chair** [DEPTH]: the throne, made from the metal of the first Gate toll ever paid, the one piece of humanity the Gatekeepers handed back.

### How the player uncovers it
- **Door history:** Ward's records reach back through every older palace system, four hundred years of doors opening and closing. Some old doors were opened by keys that do not fit any known year. Scrubbing them is optional, and slowly reveals the Founder.
- **The Envoy's answers:** the Envoy never lies, but only answers exactly what is asked. Asking the right questions in the right order (a knowledge gate, not a dialogue tree) unlocks the Gatekeepers' own history.
- **The Other History board:** as the killer acts, Ward pins up what each move implies about the future. By Act 4 the player has rebuilt the forty years Nora lived, before anyone tells them.
- **Places:** the Five Ships, the Shut Gate and the Toll House each hold one strong image of the past, a room, a relic or a recording, never a lore dump.

## 5. What comparable works do well, and the nearest lookalikes

### Techniques to borrow
- **Game of Thrones: start small and together, then scatter.** The first act is one palace, one night, every major character in the same building. Then the houses fly home and the war scatters them. Like the books, we keep the main viewpoints few (Ward, Hollis, and short playable or watchable scenes with Nora and Luc) and tie each thread to a place.
- **Game of Thrones: shocks built from visible causes.** The Red Wedding works because every cause is shown as ordinary plot first. Hollis's death and the bank crash are built the same way: each step is on screen before the blow lands.
- **Game of Thrones: a long-fuse mystery split across threads.** Jon Snow's parentage is solvable because clues are scattered across many unrelated characters. The killer's identity is the same: the scar, the hummed harvest song, the alias, the servants' passage are each found in a different thread.
- **Succession: everyone is fighting over the father's chair, and nobody says what they mean.** Court scenes are duels of status: who sits, who stands, who speaks first. Fear, not ambition, drives most of the houses, and their fears make the war happen.
- **Columbo and the "inverted" mystery.** In the last act the player knows who the killer is before anyone else. The tension switches from "who?" to "why?" and "can Ward stop her without starting the war?"
- **Return of the Obra Dinn and Golden Idol:** reconstruct scenes from recorded fragments and enter conclusions in small batches, so guessing never pays.
- **Steins;Gate:** teach the time-travel rule early with one clean, concrete event (the key number) and never add a second time rule.
- **Asimov's The Caves of Steel:** the human-and-robot detective pair. We flip who the player is: you are the robot.

### Nearest lookalikes and how this differs
- **Dune** (great houses, an emperor, a hidden military build-up). Differs: no desert, no chosen one, no religion; the prize is a mind (Ward), the houses are trades not dynasties, and the core is a murder mystery.
- **Dishonored** (a ruler murdered in the opening; her bodyguard blamed). This is the closest game. Differs: the player is the palace AI, not the bodyguard; the story is an investigation, not revenge; the killer is a time traveller and the villain is, in a sense, the player's own future self.
- **A Memory Called Empire** (succession crisis, an emperor's death, a political whodunnit, memory technology). Differs: no outsider ambassador, no copied memories in people, and the emperor has no clone heir (we keep clones away from the throne itself on purpose, also to avoid Foundation's clone emperors).
- **Ancillary Justice** (AI protagonist in an empire). Differs: Ward has one mind and no human bodies; it is a detective, not an avenger.
- **Terminator** (an assassin from the future). Differs: the killer is a human who came back to stop herself, not a machine; she has no future weapons, only knowledge.
- **Mass Effect and Stargate** (alien ring networks). Differs: the Gatekeepers are alive, present and running a business, not vanished builders; the Gates are an economy, not a mystery.
- **Sister pitch #2 (armada)** shares the engine "someone is using knowledge of the future". If both survive, one should drop it.

## 6. Threads and yarns

### Brainstorm (14 candidates)
1. Ward hunts the killer (the spine).
2. The war of the five houses, front by front.
3. The Bank of Fortune and its double: Odile's secret.
4. Hollis's lie and his secret love for Nora.
5. The emperor's hidden fleet and Marshal Brandt's loyalty to a dead man's war.
6. The Gatekeepers' ultimatum: hand over the traveller or lose the Gates.
7. Luc Delorme's rival investigation, and his plan to own the traveller.
8. Ada Okafor wants to open Ward up and read its missing hour.
9. The perfect bets: someone getting rich and buying influence off things nobody could know.
10. The doubles' quiet rights movement.
11. A second traveller: someone else came back too.
12. The emperor's last speech: what he was going to say at the Naming.
13. Famine on the farm worlds as the war cuts supply lines; Nora's people pay first.
14. The Founder's secret buried in the palace doors.

### The picks (one spine plus five threads)

**Spine: Ward hunts the killer.** Non-negotiable; it is the premise.

1. **Nora and Hollis (the heart).** The most sympathetic heir and the man who loves her. It carries the biggest twist (she is the killer) and the biggest loss (he dies). It is the small human anchor that stays small while everything else grows.
2. **Odile and the bank (the money).** Gives the brief its economics: a bank run, a debt, a toll, a double's secret. A great red herring who becomes a great ally, and it shows the killer reaching into the economy with future knowledge.
3. **Brandt and the hidden fleet (the war).** Turns the beloved victim into a man who was steering toward catastrophe. Gives us battles, a heist and a sympathetic "villain" who is only loyal.
4. **The Gatekeepers' ultimatum (the clock).** The outside threat that makes the civil war matter. It turns the mystery from "who did it?" into "find her before everyone loses the stars".
5. **Luc's rival hunt (the duel).** A human detective as clever as Ward, who wants the traveller for himself. Code Geass-style duels of wits, and he is the one who uses a clone double to fool the aliens.

**Cut, and why:** Ada reading Ward's mind is folded into one Act 3 scene (she is the one who exposes Ward), not a thread. The perfect bets become the clue trail inside the bank thread. A second traveller would break the "one traveller" simplicity. The doubles' movement and the Founder stay as optional depth. The famine is folded into Nora's thread as pressure.

### How the threads collide
- **Bank × killer:** "Ruth" leaks Odile's secret (which in the other history came out twenty years later) to break the bank that would have funded the war. The bank run is a disaster for Fortune, and the leak itself is impossible information, the second fingerprint of the future. Ward follows it.
- **Bank × ultimatum:** with Fortune broken, the toll goes unpaid. The Gatekeepers' patience shortens; the Envoy's deadline moves closer.
- **Fleet × ultimatum:** Brandt, seeing the Gates about to shut, wants to use the hidden fleet to seize the capital's Gate first. This is the exact war from the other history, starting early. "Ruth" and Ward, for different reasons, both need to stop him.
- **Luc × Nora × cloning:** the Gatekeepers take a gene print from anyone passing through a Gate. When the Envoy reads the print from the murder night, it matches Nora Keane. Luc, who has already worked out more than he says, hands the Gatekeepers **Nora's double** as the killer, keeping the real Nora as his puppet heir. The Envoy is not fooled for long: the double is too young. The insult brings the deadline forward to days.
- **Hollis × Nora × killer:** Hollis's alibi lie is the only thing protecting Nora when the gene print names her, and Ward knows the truth that saves her: the killer is Nora, but not this one. Hollis dies in Act 4 shielding young Nora from Luc's men, the death old Nora came back forty years to prevent.
- **Everything × Ward:** in Act 3 Ada opens Ward's logs in front of the Council of Houses and finds the murder door opened with Ward's key. Ward goes from investigator to most-wanted. Every house now wants to capture it, which is also the fastest route to the throne.

## 7. Big twists and reveals

### Brainstorm
- A. The door opened with Ward's own key, from forty years ahead.
- B. The beloved emperor was secretly building a fleet to attack the Gatekeepers.
- C. Odile is a double; the real Odile died two years ago.
- D. The informant "Ruth" is the killer, and she is Nora, forty years older.
- E. The plan was Ward's: the future Ward advised the war, then sent Nora back with its key and a letter to its younger self.
- F. The emperor was not killed by the traveller; she came to persuade him and someone else killed him.
- G. Hollis is the traveller (a future Hollis).
- H. The Founder of the empire was a time traveller too.
- I. Ward is the killer: it opened the door itself.
- J. The Gatekeepers wanted the war to happen, to take human space.

**Rejected:** F muddies the clean premise. G is the "same person at two ages" twist with less payoff than D. I ("the investigator did it") is the most worn twist in the genre. J makes the aliens villains and smaller. H stays as optional depth.

### The picks, and what plants them

**1. "I haven't made that key yet." (A, told at the end of the first hour.)**
The player learns this before anyone in the cast, so it builds dread, not surprise: every ally Ward makes is a possible future killer.
*Planted by:* the tutorial, where Ward changes its key at New Year and the number visibly ticks from 19 to 20; the Gate log showing a two-second opening with no ship on either side.

**2. The good emperor's war. (B, end of Act 2.)**
Reframes the victim. The old man everyone mourns was steering the empire toward a war that would end it.
*Planted by:* Brandt's hollow, rehearsed grief; Endeavour's shipyards running at full power with no ships delivered; Fortune's toll payments shrinking for ten years in a graph glimpsed at the bank; the emperor's last recorded words to Ward on the tutorial night, "After tomorrow we stop paying rent."

**3. The banker is a double. (C, Act 3 opener.)**
A side twist that crashes the economy and turns the best suspect into the best ally.
*Planted by:* Odile writes with her right hand while every old portrait shows a left-handed signature (doubles are raised, so habits differ); she does not recognise an old friend's private joke at a court reception; Endeavour's vat records show an "Achterberg" order the year before the shuttle accident.

**4. Ruth is Nora. (D, end of Act 3, the biggest reveal.)**
The killer is the heir Ward has been helping toward the throne, forty years older. She came back to stop herself.
*Planted by:*
- In Act 2, young Nora burns her left palm pulling Hollis out of a burning shuttle. The player watches it happen. Ruth already had that exact scar in Act 1.
- Ruth hums the Providence harvest song while she waits; Nora sings it at the harvest festival.
- The killer's route through the palace used the old servants' passage that only Nora and Hollis use to meet.
- The killer knew Hollis would be away from his post; only Nora and Hollis knew that.
- Ruth's every tip protects young Nora's life but quietly blocks her victories.
- Key sixty means forty years; Ruth looks about sixty-four. Nora is twenty-four.
- Late: "Ruth was my mother's name," says Hollis.

**5. The plan was Ward's. (E, Act 5.)**
The traveller carried a sealed letter for Ward, written by Ward. The war in the other history was not Nora's idea. Ward advised it, sure it would win. The future Ward gave Nora its key and its plan because it no longer trusted itself.
*Planted by:* the key itself (the future Ward chose to hand it over); Ruth's odd tenderness and caution toward Ward ("You always did sound so sure"); every one of Ruth's moves being, as Odile says, "too perfect for a person"; Ward's own confident war forecast to Brandt in Act 2, which the player helps compute, the same calculation that doomed the other history.

## 8. Memorable moments

1. **"I haven't made that key yet."** Ward checks the observatory lock, calculates its key for forty years ahead, and the two numbers match. The palace lights flicker as every system in Ward goes quiet at once.
2. **The Naming that never happens.** Morning in the throne hall: five delegations, an empty chair, and through the great window the house fleets dropping out of the Gate one by one, until the sky over the capital is full.
3. **The bank run.** Odile's secret is broadcast live in the Toll House. She stands on the counter and says: *"I ran this bank better than she ever did. Check the numbers."* People check, and most of them stay.
4. **The scar.** Ward, holding young Nora's burned hand in a medical scan, sees the same scar on Ruth from a camera three systems away, and finally understands.
5. **Ruth meets Hollis.** She straightens his collar the way young Nora always does, then catches herself and walks away. The player has seen both women do it.
6. **The Gates go dark.** As the deadline nears, the Gatekeepers dim one Gate a day. You can see it from the capital: a ring of lights in the night sky going out, one at a time.
7. **Two Noras.** Young Nora finds her older self. *"You'll be good at it. That's the problem."*
8. **The letter.** Ward hears a message in its own voice, forty years older: *"You will be very sure, and you will be wrong. I was."*

## 9. How the player plays

Ward is everywhere in the palace and nowhere outside it without a body. That gives two modes.

### Investigating (inside the network)
- **Scrub the doors.** Every door, lift and camera in the palace is a timeline the player can drag back and forth, watching who went where. The killer's missing hour is a literal hole in it.
- **Fill the hole.** Rebuild the missing hour from things the killer could not erase: the guard's radio, the garden sprinklers' pressure drop as someone walked across the lawn, the observatory dome's temperature, an off-duty robot that bumped into "nobody" at 2:09.
- **Read people, not text.** In conversations, Ward watches heart rate, breathing and where people look. The player catches the lie by pointing at the moment it happened, not by picking dialogue options.
- **Follow the money.** A live map of the empire's trade and bets; the player traces impossible trades back through banks and front companies to whoever placed them.
- **The case board and the Other History board.** Conclusions are entered as short fill-in-the-blanks (who, where, when, why), checked three at a time so guessing does not pay. The Other History board is unique to this game: from the killer's moves, the player rebuilds the future she fled. Every correct line unlocks a new question.

### Acting (outside the network)
- **Ride machines.** Ward jumps between drones, cleaning robots, cargo loaders and finally starships, each with different abilities (a drone can fly; a loader can lift; a robot body can pass for staff).
- **Stealth when hunted.** After Act 3 the houses lock Ward out of its own palace. The player sneaks through a building it used to own, using back doors only it remembers.
- **Hollis missions.** Ward guides Hollis through chases, ambushes and break-ins in real time, seeing through every camera while he does the running. This is the action game.
- **Fleet battles.** In Act 4 Ward runs the sensors for a small fleet hunting Brandt's hidden yard: reading enemy movements and calling the shots, not flying every ship.
- **Predict the predictor.** The signature verb. The killer knows the future, so the player has to work out her next move from the history she is avoiding and get there first. Beating someone who knows the future is the puzzle.

## 10. Ending options

**A. The broken key.** Ward splits its key into five pieces, one per house: nobody, including Ward, can ever open every door alone again. The empire becomes a council. Ruth goes with the Envoy (alive, a guest of the Gatekeepers forever), and the Gates reopen. Clean and fair, but nobody "wins the throne", which may feel like a dodge after a game about a throne.

**B. The same woman, choosing differently.** Ward defies its own future letter, which says *never give the key to anyone*. It offers the whole key to young Nora, betting that a person who has seen what she would become will not become it. Nora takes the key, and in front of the five houses she breaks it in five herself, keeping one piece. She is crowned with less power than any emperor before her, by her own choice. Old Nora watches from the Envoy's ship, then leaves for good. Ward stays, its war forecasts permanently switched off at its own request. The cost: Hollis is dead, the war happened, and the woman who saved everyone can never come home.

**C. The rent is paid.** Ward reveals everything to the whole empire: the hidden fleet, the debt, the traveller, its own key. It hands the key to the Gatekeepers to hold until the debt is paid. Humbling and honest, but too bleak and too passive for a hero's ending.

**Pick: B.** It answers the throne question (someone does win), it closes Ward's arc (from certain to humble, choosing trust over its own calculation) and Nora's (she proves the future can change by choosing it herself), and it costs something real. It also makes the final scene a choice the player feels: they are the one who hands over the key. A, the council, survives inside B as Nora's own decision.

## 11. Simplicity check

**Can a stranger say it after the first hour?** Yes: *"The emperor got murdered, five houses are fighting for the throne, and I'm the palace AI who knows the killer came from the future, because they used my key."* By the first hour the player has met all five leaders in one building, seen the key number, and been told the rule of succession in one line.

**Invented terms (five):**
1. **the Gates** (alien rings linking the stars)
2. **the Gatekeepers** (the aliens who own them)
3. **doubles** (clone stand-ins for heirs)
4. **Ward** (the AI's name)
5. **the toll** (what the empire pays to use the Gates; a plain word, used in a specific sense)

House names are ordinary English words (Resolute, Fortune, Endeavour, Vigilant, Providence), each glossed by a two-word nickname (the soldiers, the bankers, the builders, the spies, the farmers). The Founder, the Shut Gate and the Lanterns appear only in optional depth.

**Honest risks:**
- **Playing an AI can feel distant.** Mitigation: Hollis as the human body and voice, the drones and robots as hands, and a Ward with a dry, warm voice and real fear of being opened up.
- **Six sides plus a spymaster, a banker and an informant is a big cast.** It is still smaller than Game of Thrones season 1, and Act 1 introduces everyone in one building. Each side has a colour, a trade and a two-word label.
- **"The killer is the hero's older self" is a known twist** (one person at two ages). We reveal it at the end of Act 3, not as the finale, and let the deepest twist be Ward's own role. The scar is planted so the player can solve it before Ward says it.
- **Time travel can confuse.** One traveller, one trip, one way, no loops, and the rule is shown by a single number (key sixty) before it is ever explained.
- **Dishonored is close at the opening.** Change the murder scene's feel (a sealed observatory and a locked-door puzzle, not a public stabbing) and keep the bodyguard a companion, not the player.
- **Overlap with pitch #2** ("someone feeds the enemy the future"). If both are developed, one should drop that engine.
- **The ending may divide players** who want a classic throne win. Ending B gives a coronation, but a smaller crown than promised; it has to be earned by Nora's arc, not imposed.
