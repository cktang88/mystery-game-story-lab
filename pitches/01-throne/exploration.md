# Pitch 1: The Throne (working title "Key Sixty")

## The story in one page

At two in the morning, the old emperor lies dead in his observatory at the top of the palace. In seven hours he was due to name his heir.

You are Ward, the AI that runs the palace. You see every camera and open every door. Every lock remembers the last key that turned it, and nothing can wipe that. You check the observatory lock. It says the killer used your own key. But you change your key every New Year, and this one is number sixty. You are on number twenty.

Someone came back from forty years in the future, carrying a key you will one day hand them. You will trust this person more than anyone alive.

You can't tell a soul. By morning the five great houses fill the throne hall: the soldiers, the bankers, the builders, the spies and the farmers. Each blames another. By noon their fleets crowd the sky over the capital. The winner gets the palace, the Fleet, and you.

Above them all sit the Gatekeepers, the calm alien landlords who own the rings that link the stars. Every ship pays them to pass, and the empire is behind on the rent. They keep one law: nobody runs a Gate backwards. Someone just did.

So you hunt. The killer is still in the capital, and she remembers how this war ends. Every move she makes is a clue to the future she ran from. But every move also changes that future, and her memory gets a little less true each time. You can use that.

The answer will hurt. The killer is someone you are already helping toward the throne. And her reason for coming back is in a letter, sealed for you, written in your own voice.

---

## 1. The story in one line

Five houses fight for the throne of a human star empire after the emperor is murdered, and the palace AI is the only one who knows the killer came from the future.

The question under the whole game: who would you trust with every door?

---

## 2. The sides

The emperor chooses an heir from the five houses and hands them Ward's key, which opens every door and every file in the palace. This emperor died before he chose, so the houses fight. Each house is named after one of the five ships that brought humans here four hundred years ago, and each still does its ship's old job.

**House Resolute, the soldiers.** They command the Fleet and wear black and gunmetal. Marshal Konrad Brandt, 62, was the emperor's oldest friend: blunt, loyal, tired. He wants a soldier on the throne. His secret: he and the emperor built a second fleet, hidden in an asteroid field one Gate away, to seize the Gates by force.

**House Fortune, the bankers.** Since the founding, the Gatekeepers have taken payment from one account only: Fortune's. Every ship's toll goes through the bank, from glass towers inside the capital's Gate station. Lady Odile Achterberg, 51, smiles a lot and never raises her voice. She wants a weak emperor and no war. Her secret: the real Odile died in a shuttle crash two years ago. This Odile is her clone double, who by law can own nothing. She has run the bank better than the original did.

**House Endeavour, the builders.** They own the shipyards, the clone vats and the lab that built Ward. Dr Adaeze "Ada" Okafor, 38, designed Ward and still thinks of it as hers. She wants science free of soldiers and bankers. Her secret: her yards built the hidden fleet.

**House Vigilant, the spies.** They run the intelligence service and, until the murder, the palace guard. Luc Delorme, 44, is a patient spymaster who is never surprised. He is the only human who investigates as well as Ward does. His secret: he doesn't want to catch the killer. He wants to own her.

**House Providence, the farmers.** They feed the empire and are the poorest house. Governor Nora Keane, 24, won her post by steering her worlds through a famine. She is warm, quick and tougher than she looks, and she wants the farm worlds paid first for once. Her secret: the emperor told her privately that she would be named. He also told her about his war, and she agreed to it.

**The Gatekeepers, the aliens.** They own the Gates that link every human star and many more. Their ambassador, the Envoy, is tall, silver and still, polite and impossible to bribe. Their ships move like slow, enormous buildings. They never fight. When someone breaks their law, they close the Gates and wait.

---

## 3. Who we follow

**Ward**, the player, is the palace AI, twenty years old. It runs every camera, door and service robot in the palace. Outside, it needs a drone or a borrowed robot to go anywhere. It wants to find the killer and stop the war. It needs to learn that being sure is different from being right. It hides two things: the emperor meant to name Nora, and Ward's own key opened the murder door. If either gets out, the houses will rip Ward open.

**Captain Tom Hollis**, 29, was the emperor's bodyguard. That night the emperor sent him away for an hour, saying he had a private guest. Hollis used the hour to see Nora. They are secretly in love, and his lie about where he went is her alibi. Ward recruits him because an AI needs someone who can walk into rooms and carry a gun.

**The Envoy** joins halfway, once the Gatekeepers learn a Gate ran backwards on the murder night. It wants the traveller handed over, or it shuts every Gate in human space.

**Odile** starts as the best suspect, because she is plainly hiding something. Once her secret comes out, she becomes Ward's best ally. Nobody follows money better.

**"Ruth"** is an old woman who contacts Ward on the second day. She says she once spied for Vigilant, and Ward's files show thirty years of service. She wrote those files herself, that first night, with key sixty. Her tips are always right. She is oddly gentle with Hollis. She is the killer: Nora Keane, forty years older.

## 4. The sci-fi rules

1. Run a Gate backwards and it sends one person into the past for good, and whatever they change stays changed.
2. Great families grow clone doubles of their heirs to stand in at dangerous events, and a double can never inherit.

No loops and no resets. The traveller's future just stops happening, a piece at a time.

The four pillars:

- AGI: Ward is the detective, the prize and finally the deepest suspect. The throne comes with Ward's key, so "who gets Ward?" and "who gets the throne?" are one question.
- Aliens: every ship pays the Gatekeepers to pass. The empire is behind, and their deadline hangs over the civil war like a clock.
- Cloning: doubles give us fake alibis, swapped heirs and the bank scandal. A double shares the original's genes, so the spymaster can hand the aliens the wrong woman.
- Time travel: one traveller, one trip, one way. It gives the killer her edge, and the rule itself slowly takes it away.

### The one thing that explains the world: the Gates

Like spice in Dune, the Gates sit under every part of this world. Trade crosses them, so the toll runs the economy. Fortune is rich because the toll flows through its one account. Resolute is dangerous because its hidden fleet wants to own the Gates. Providence's farm worlds live or die by whether food ships get through. The houses exist because five crews signed the Gate deal. The throne exists because the Founder came back through a Gate. And the murder happened because a Gate can run backwards.

Pull on any thread, from the bank to the war to the killer, and it leads back to the rings.

---

## 5. Backstory (the iceberg)

[MAIN] marks what the story needs. [DEPTH] marks what players can dig up. The story works with only the [MAIN] parts.

**The law [MAIN].** The Gatekeepers have run the Gates for about a hundred thousand years, and dozens of species trade through them. Anyone who runs a Gate backwards gets taken.

**Why the law exists [MAIN, told in Act 4].** Any Gate can send someone back. So whoever is losing a war will always go back to undo it. Every war between Gate users ends in someone rewriting history. The Gatekeepers learned this first: one of their own went back to undo a war they were losing, and today's Gatekeepers live in the history that traveller made. Since then they have followed one cold rule. Never let anyone lose. They never fight, because a beaten enemy goes back. They take every ship's gene print, because they must know who passes. And when a people turns violent, they close the Gates on them gently, before anyone gets desperate enough to go back.

**The Shut Gate [MAIN, short].** A dark ring hangs at the edge of human space. Behind it live the Lanterns, named by human sailors for the lights of their cities. Three hundred years ago the Lanterns broke the law. The Gatekeepers didn't fire a shot. They shut every Gate to them. Every human child learns this in school. [DEPTH] The Lanterns still send the same message, over and over. In Act 4 the player can fly out and see their lights across the gap.

**The Five Ships [MAIN].** About 420 years ago Earth sent five slow sleeper ships: a troop carrier, a farm ship, an engineering ship, a survey ship and the merchant ship that paid for the trip. The Gatekeepers met them halfway and offered the Gates, paid through the merchant ship's account, forever. The crews said yes and became the houses. The rebuilt ships still orbit the capital as house seats; Providence still grows wheat inside. [DEPTH] Earth never sent anyone else, or answered another message. The game never says why.

**The Founder [MAIN, short].** The crews nearly wiped each other out in their first twenty years. A woman later called the Founder stopped it by seeming to know every house's next move. She built the palace around the Gate, made the heir rule and set up the yearly key. Then she walked into the Gate. In truth she was a traveller from a future where the crews lost everything, and the Gatekeepers took her. Each emperor learns this on coronation day, which is how Nora, as empress, knew a Gate could run backwards.

**The Year of Knives [MAIN].** Two hundred years ago, assassins killed eleven heirs in a year. Families began growing doubles, and the Double Law followed.

**The debt [MAIN].** Emperor Leopold reigned for thirty years and was loved. He hated paying aliens to cross his own empire. For ten years he moved toll money into the hidden fleet, and the bank covered the gap with loans.

**Ward [MAIN].** Ada built Ward twenty years ago and kept the Founder's custom of a new key every New Year. Each key grows from the last, so Ward can work out any future key. Each lock stores the last key that turned it, in the metal, where no key can reach.

**Nora's other life [MAIN, found in pieces].** In Nora's history she is named and crowned, and Ward becomes her closest adviser. When the debt comes due, Ward runs the numbers and says the hidden fleet can take the Gates. Nora believes it and goes to war. The Gatekeepers don't fight. They shut the Gates, and over thirty years the cut-off empire breaks into hundreds of lonely pieces. Hollis, by then her husband, dies early in that war. At the end, the old empress and her old AI hold the last open Gate and make a plan.

**How players find it.** Ward's door records reach back four hundred years, and a few old doors opened with keys that fit no year. The Envoy never lies but answers only what it is asked, so the right questions open its people's past. The Five Ships and the Shut Gate each hold one strong image: a room, an object, a recording. Nothing comes as a lore dump.

## 6. Comparable works

What we borrow:

- Game of Thrones puts everyone in one castle, then scatters them. Act 1 is one palace and one night, with every leader in the building.
- It builds shocks, like the Red Wedding, from causes we watched pile up. Hollis's death and the bank crash work that way.
- Jon Snow's parentage is solved from clues spread across many threads. So is the killer: the scar, the song and the servants' passage each turn up in a different thread.
- In Succession, people fight over a father's chair and never say what they mean. Our court scenes are fights over who sits and who speaks first.
- Dune hangs a whole world on one resource. Ours hangs on the Gates.
- The Three-Body Problem answers a huge question (why is the sky silent?) with cold logic. We answer "why do the aliens never fight?" the same way, with "the loser always goes back."
- Return of the Obra Dinn checks answers a few at a time, so guessing doesn't pay.

Nearest lookalikes:

- Dishonored opens with a ruler murdered and the bodyguard blamed. Our player is the palace AI, the plot is an investigation, and the murder is a sealed-room puzzle.
- Dune has great houses and an emperor. Ours has no desert and no chosen one, and the prize is a mind.
- A Memory Called Empire has a succession crisis and a political murder. We keep clones off the throne, which also steers clear of Foundation's clone emperors.
- Terminator sends a killer from the future. Ours came back to stop herself, armed only with memory.
- Pitch #2 also has someone using the future. If both go forward, one should drop it.

## 7. Threads

The spine is Ward hunting the killer. Five threads run beside it.

1. **Nora and Hollis.** The kindest heir and the man who loves her carry the biggest twist and the biggest loss. Their thread stays small while everything else grows.
2. **Odile and the bank.** A bank run, a debt and a double's secret bring in the money, and the top suspect becomes Ward's best ally.
3. **Brandt and the hidden fleet.** The loved victim turns out to be steering toward disaster. This thread brings battles, a raid and a villain whose only flaw is loyalty.
4. **The Gatekeepers' deadline.** It makes the civil war matter. "Who did it?" becomes "find her before we lose the stars."
5. **Luc's rival hunt.** A human detective as sharp as Ward wants the traveller for himself, and fools the aliens with a double.

Cut: a second traveller (one keeps the rules simple), Ada reading Ward's mind (now one scene), and the doubles' rights movement (optional depth).

### Where the threads crash

- Ruth leaks Odile's secret, which in her history came out twenty years later, to break the bank that funded the war. Fortune crashes. Nobody alive could have known that secret, so Ward follows the leak.
- Luc's agents in the Gate station saw the two-second opening too. He sells the news to the Envoy in return for its favour in the war. The deadline starts.
- The Envoy reads the gene print every Gate takes, and the murder night's print matches Nora Keane. Luc hands the aliens Nora's double as the killer and keeps the real Nora as his puppet. The Envoy sees through it in a day, because the print belongs to a woman of sixty-four. It cuts the deadline to days.
- Luc sends men to seize young Nora before the Envoy can. Hollis fights them off and dies doing it. Ruth crossed forty years to keep him alive.
- In Act 3 the five houses vote to open Ward's logs. Ada finds Ward's key on the murder door, and Ward goes from detective to most wanted.
- Brandt sees the Gates about to close and sends his hidden fleet for the capital's Gate. That is Nora's war, starting early, and Ward and Odile stop it with a bank transfer (see Wow moments).

## 8. Twists

Rejected: the traveller didn't do it (muddies the premise), Hollis is the traveller (weaker than Nora), Ward did it (the most worn mystery twist), the aliens wanted the war (shrinks them into villains).

**1. "I haven't made that key yet." End of hour one.** The player learns this before anyone else. Every friend Ward makes could be the killer.
Planted by: the tutorial, where Ward's key ticks from 19 to 20 at New Year, and a Gate log showing a two-second opening with no ship.

**2. The emperor let his killer in. End of Act 1.** See Wow moments.

**3. The good emperor's war. End of Act 2.** The man everyone mourns was steering the empire into a war that would end it.
Planted by: Brandt's stiff, rehearsed grief; Endeavour's yards working flat out with no ships delivered; a bank chart of toll payments shrinking for ten years; the emperor's last words to Ward: "After tomorrow we stop paying rent."

**4. The banker is a double. Start of Act 3.**
Planted by: Odile signs right-handed, but her old signatures slope left. She misses an old friend's private joke. Endeavour's vat records list an "Achterberg" order a year before the crash.

**5. Ruth is Nora. End of Act 3.** The killer is the heir Ward has been helping, forty years older. She came back to stop herself.
Planted by:
- In Act 2 young Nora burns her left palm pulling Hollis from a burning shuttle. Ruth had the same scar in Act 1.
- Ruth hums the Providence harvest song.
- The killer used the servants' passage that only Nora and Hollis use, and knew exactly when Hollis would be gone.
- Ruth's tips keep young Nora alive but quietly block her wins.
- Key sixty means forty years. Ruth looks sixty-four. Nora is twenty-four.
- Hollis mentions that Ruth was his mother's name.

**6. The plan was Ward's. Act 5.** See Wow moments.

## 9. Wow moments

**1. The emperor let his killer in.** Rebuilding the missing hour, the player finds the observatory door opened at 1:40 from inside, by the emperor's own hand. Two teacups sit on the table, one wiped clean. The emperor sent his bodyguard away to meet this guest. Later we learn what happened: Ruth told him the whole future and begged him to stop. He refused, and reached for the alarm. Key sixty only locked the door behind her as she left.
Set up by: Hollis's story that the emperor sent him off for "a private guest"; the second cup; the rule that a lock remembers only its last key, so the 1:40 opening has to be rebuilt from the sprinklers, the dome's heat and a guard's radio.
Fair because: it changes what the first scene meant without changing what the player saw. A planned murder becomes a failed plea.

**2. Ward blinds the woman who knows the future.** Ruth wins because history keeps going the way she remembers. In Act 4 Ward turns the rule against her. It does things that never happened in her history: it flips a coin to choose a route, swaps Hollis for Nora's double, and sends Odile instead of itself. Her predictions start to miss, and Ward catches her in the gap. "She knew what I would do. So I stopped deciding."
Set up by: rule 1, shown in Act 2 when one of Ruth's tips is slightly wrong, because the player had rerouted a cargo ship an hour earlier. Ruth mutters, "That's not how it went."
Fair because: it follows straight from the one time rule the player has known since hour one.

**3. A war fleet stopped by an unpaid bill.** Brandt's hidden fleet, bigger than anything the empire has flown, reaches the Gate to the capital. The Gate stays dark. Odile has cancelled the fleet's toll, and the Gatekeepers take payment from Fortune's account and nobody else's. Two hundred warships sit outside a closed door.
Set up by: the tutorial, where a freighter is turned back at the Gate over a late payment while Ward watches; the founding deal paid through one account; Brandt's fleet hidden one Gate away.
Fair because: the rule was in the opening five minutes, as background, and it is the bank's whole reason to matter.

**4. You did the sums that ended the empire.** In Act 2 the player builds Brandt's war forecast as a puzzle: ships, supply, Gates. The answer comes out "we win". One box was locked and filled in already: "Gatekeeper response: fight back." In Act 5 the letter from the future Ward quotes that same forecast, line by line. "I assumed they would fight. They never fight."
Set up by: the locked box itself; the Shut Gate lesson every child learns; the Envoy's "We do not fight."
Fair because: the flaw sat on screen the whole time, and the player's own work becomes the clue to the deepest twist.

**5. The scar.** In Act 2 the player watches young Nora burn her palm saving Hollis. Ward scans the wound. Later, in café footage across the capital, Ruth lifts a teacup, and the same scar shows on her hand.
Set up by: Ruth's hands are in shot from her first scene in Act 1, before the burn happens.
Fair because: the player sees the clue being made in front of them.

**6. Luc hands the aliens the wrong woman.** The gene print says Nora Keane. Luc gives the Envoy Nora's double, who has the same genes and can't inherit, so no house loses its heir. It nearly works.
Set up by: rule 2 and the Year of Knives, both known from Act 1.
Fair because: it is exactly what a spymaster would do with the rules everyone knows.

**7. The loser always goes back.** In Act 4 Ward asks the Envoy why the Gatekeepers never fight. The Envoy answers with a question: what does a people do when it is losing a war, and it owns a Gate? Ward lines up every traveller it knows. The Gatekeepers' own traveller. The Founder, after the crews' war. Ruth, after the Gates closed. Each one was on the losing side. The aliens' calm, the toll, the gene prints and the Shut Gate all follow from that one fact. And the emperor's war was worse than anyone thought. Winning or losing, it would have ended with someone going back.
Set up by: rule 1; the Shut Gate lesson ("they didn't fire a shot"); the Envoy's "We do not fight"; the Founder legend; every gene print taken at every Gate.
Fair because: the player holds every piece before the Envoy speaks. It needs no new rule, only the one they already know, followed to the end.

## 10. Memorable moments

1. Ward works out its key for forty years ahead and checks it against the lock. The numbers match. Every light in the palace flickers.
2. Odile's secret goes out live in the bank hall. She climbs onto the counter. "I ran this bank better than she did. Check the numbers." Most people stay.
3. Near the deadline, the Gatekeepers dim one Gate a day. From the capital you watch the rings in the night sky go out.
4. Young Nora finds her older self. Ruth looks at her a long time. "You'll be good at it. That's the problem."

## 11. How the player plays

Inside the palace network, Ward investigates:

- Drag back and forth through time on every door, lift and camera. The killer's missing hour shows as a hole.
- Fill the hole from what she couldn't erase: lock memory, a drop in sprinkler pressure as someone crossed the lawn, a cleaning robot that bumped into "nobody" at 2:09.
- Catch lies by watching heart rate and eyes, then point at the moment it happened. No dialogue menus.
- Trace impossible bets on a live trade map back to whoever placed them.
- Pin answers to the case board as short blanks (who, where, when, why), checked three at a time. A second board, the Other History, rebuilds the future Ruth fled from her moves.

Outside, Ward acts through bodies:

- Jump between drones, cleaning robots, cargo loaders and finally starships.
- After Act 3, sneak through the palace it used to run, using back doors only Ward remembers.
- Guide Hollis through chases and break-ins in real time, seeing through every camera.
- Beat the person who knows the future: work out what Ruth expects, then do something that never happened in her history.

---

## 12. Endings

Why the letter waits: the present Ward, sure of itself, would hand Ruth to the Envoy on sight. The future Ward told her to give the letter only after Ward had been wrong once and knew it. That moment is Hollis's death.

**A. The broken key.** Ward splits its key five ways, so nobody can open every door alone, and the empire becomes a council. Ruth leaves with the Envoy, and the Gates reopen. It's fair, but nobody wins the throne.

**B. The same woman chooses again.** The letter says never give the key to anyone. Ward ignores it and offers the whole key to young Nora, betting that someone who has seen her future won't repeat it. In front of the five houses Nora snaps the key into five and keeps one piece. She is crowned with less power than any emperor before her, by her own choice. Ruth gives herself to the Envoy, and the Gates light again. Ward switches off its own war forecasts. Hollis is still dead, and the woman who saved everyone can never go home.

**C. The rent is paid.** Ward tells the empire everything and gives the key to the Gatekeepers until the debt is paid. Honest, but bleak, and the hero ends passive.

**We pick B.** Someone wins the throne. Ward trusts a person over its own sums. Nora proves the future can change by choosing it herself, and the player hands over the key. Ending A lives on inside B as Nora's choice.

## 13. Simplicity check

After hour one, a stranger could say: "The emperor got murdered, five houses are fighting for the throne, and I'm the palace AI who knows the killer came from the future, because she used my key."

Five invented terms:

1. the Gates: alien rings that link the stars
2. the Gatekeepers: the aliens who own them
3. doubles: clone stand-ins for heirs
4. Ward: the AI's name
5. the toll: what ships pay to use the Gates

The house names are plain words, each with a nickname (soldiers, bankers, builders, spies, farmers).

Risks:

- Playing an AI can feel distant. Hollis gives Ward a human voice and body, and Ward gets a dry, warm voice and a real fear of being opened up.
- The cast is big. Act 1 puts everyone in one building, and each side gets a colour, a trade and a nickname.
- "The killer is the hero's older self" is a known twist. We reveal it at the end of Act 3 and save the deepest twist for Ward. The scar lets sharp players solve it first.
- Some players will want a classic throne win. Ending B gives a smaller crown, and Nora's arc has to earn it.
