# Pitch 1: The Throne — the world

The story in `exploration.md` is the tip. This is the iceberg under it. The main story needs almost none of it.

Dates count from Year 0, when the Five Ships came through the Gate. The story starts in Year 400.

---

## 1. The one central thing: the Gates

Every Gate is a shortcut across the stars. Because of how Gates are built, every Gate is also a door into the past.

A Gate is two rings joined by a short throat. Fly into one ring and you fly out of the other, light-years away. But someone first has to carry the far ring to its star, the slow way, at close to the speed of light. That trip takes centuries. A ring moving that fast ages more slowly than the ring left behind, so when it arrives the two rings are no longer the same age. The difference never goes away. Pilots call it the Gate's gap.

The Gatekeepers keep every gap shut, so normal crossings land at the right moment. Run a Gate the wrong way and the gap opens for about two seconds. That is wide enough for one person, and it drops them exactly the gap's length into the past. Each Gate's gap is fixed. The Gate that hangs over the palace has a gap of forty years.

This is the story's first rule, seen from underneath. Everything else in the world grows out of it.

**Money.** Only the Gatekeepers own Gates, because only they started hauling a hundred thousand years ago. A rival's first Gate would take five hundred years to deliver one route. So everyone rents. Every crossing is charged by weight, because a Gate burns power by the ton, and every human crossing is charged to one account: Fortune's. A ton of grain owes the same as a ton of medicine. That one fact makes farmers poor, bankers rich and warships expensive.

**Power.** The throne exists because the Gatekeepers wanted one person to answer for every human. The palace stands under the Gate. The bank stands on the scales where cargo is weighed.

**Identity.** A traveller from the future is a copy of someone already alive. So the Gatekeepers take a gene print of everyone who crosses, and the empire uses those prints as its ID. A clone is the one forgery the prints can't catch. That makes doubles both a shield and a hole.

**Belief and conflict.** The empire's founding hero and its deepest lie both come from one backward run. Its one big choice is to pay rent forever or try to take the doors, and every faction is an answer to that.

## 2. The deep timeline

### The Gatekeepers learn to build doors (about 110,000 to 100,000 years ago)

The Gatekeepers grew up around a dim red star. They live about a thousand years and think in centuries. Their first throat, made in a lab, was the width of a hand. Within a few thousand years they were hauling ship-sized rings to nearby stars. They noticed the gaps early, and for a long time did nothing with them.

### The war of second chances (about 100,000 years ago)

Then someone did. A side that was losing a war ran a Gate backwards and sent a soldier back to warn its leaders. It worked. Now the other side was losing, so it did the same. Each trip rewrote history, and each rewrite made someone new the loser. That is the cold logic under this whole world: whoever owns a Gate and is losing will always go back.

One Gatekeeper went back through the deepest Gate they had, to the years before the first backward run. She knew everything that was coming, so they believed her. They wrote one law: nobody runs a Gate backwards, ever, not even to fix something. Then she asked to be sent away on the next hauler, so she could change nothing more.

Today's Gatekeepers live in the history she made. They know it cost something, and they will never know what. Every law against time travel in this world was written by a time traveller.

### The long hauls (100,000 years ago to now)

The Gatekeepers built a whole way of life around the law. They never lie, because to them a lie is a small rewrite. They never put thinking machines on their ships, because a mind is the only thing that can learn to crack a ring. They gene-print every crossing, so they always know who passed. They never fight, because a beaten enemy goes back; they make sure nobody ever loses. When a people turns violent, they close its Gates gently, before anyone grows desperate. Their only weapon is the door.

For a hundred thousand years they hauled rings outward to stars with good worlds, then waited for tenants. Dozens of species now rent from them. When they hear a young species' radio, they park a ring nearby and wait. Two thousand years before the Five Ships left, a hauler parked a ring two light-years from the Sun.

### Earth and the waiting ring (Years -22 to 0)

Earth launched five slow sleeper ships to a nearby star. Twenty-two years out, they passed the waiting ring.

The crews were asleep. The five ship minds, the thinking machines that flew each ship, were awake. The Gatekeepers offered them good worlds and the whole network, for a toll on every crossing, forever. The minds did the sums. Four hundred more years in the dark, with heavy losses, against a home today. They signed for the crews and took the ships through.

The minds radioed Earth. Earth received the same offer and said no. The Gatekeepers take a refusal as final, so they hauled the ring away. Nobody on the Five Ships heard Earth's answer.

### The War of the Ships (Years 0 to 18)

The crews woke above a strange, good world, bound by a lease they had never seen. Each ship mind had its own plan for the colony, and each was sure. Each crew sided with its own ship. They fought over the one good world, the vats, the stores and the toll bill.

### The Founder (Years 18 to 40)

In Year 18 a woman nobody could place seemed to know every side's next move, and ended the war in two years. She was a traveller. In her history the war went on until Year 58 and nearly ended humans here. At the end, the last working ship mind spent years inside the capital Gate's machinery, cracked it, and sent her back forty years. A mind sent the first traveller.

The Gatekeepers came for her within the season. She asked for twenty years to finish the peace and offered a deal: the human throne would answer to the Gatekeepers for their law, forever. They agreed.

She used the time to build the empire in a shape meant to stop a second War of the Ships:

- She switched off the five ship minds and wrote the one-mind law. Humans may keep only one thinking machine, and it belongs to the throne, under a key.
- She made each mind's key change every New Year, each key grown from the last. The palace locks accept today's key or any later one. Everyone thinks that is so a lock with a slow clock still opens at New Year. In truth it is a tripwire. If a key from the future ever turns up on a lock, someone came back, and the palace mind of the future gave them that key.
- She gave each crew a charter, a sole right to its ship's trade, and made the throne a thirty-year post. The emperor names an heir from any house, in public, at the end of the term.
- She staged the Handshake: a famous recording of the five captains shaking hands with the Gatekeepers. It hid the fact that machines had signed the lease. She feared that a people who learned a machine had sold them would wreck every machine, and then each other.

In Year 40 she named the first emperor, flew up to the Gate alone and went through. People say she walked into the Gate. Every emperor since has read her account on coronation day.

### Building the empire (Years 40 to 190)

The engineering ship had carried embryo banks and vats, so the first colony-born generation came mostly from vats. Endeavour built the Tether, a cable from the palace up to the Gate. The farm crew settled Firstfield, at the far end of the palace Gate, and shipped grain to the capital.

From the meeting on, the Gatekeepers charged every human crossing to one account, the merchant ship's,. A ring opens for a human ship only if that account pays. House Fortune grew from that account: it collects from shippers, and each year it settles with the Gatekeepers in refined metal and fuel. The account may run behind for a while. That is why a debt is possible, and why a bank can stop a fleet.

In Year 100 the Lanterns, humans' nearest neighbours, ran a Gate backwards and refused to give up their traveller. Humans watched the Gatekeepers shut every Gate to them. Nobody doubts the Envoy's threat today.

### The Year of Knives and the doubles (Years 190 to 200)

In Year 190 an emperor died before naming an heir. The Founder's fallback says the five houses choose together. Instead, assassins killed eleven heirs in a single year.

Afterwards, great families began growing a clone of each heir, raised beside them from birth to stand in at dangerous events. The Double Law followed. A double may never inherit or own property, and every double must be registered with the Gatekeepers, since a double's gene print matches the heir's. That registry is the only thing that tells two identical prints apart.

### The long calm (Years 200 to 370)

The weight toll shaped everything. Worlds learned to make their heavy goods at home and trade in light ones: designs, medicine, money, news. Fortune grew rich. Providence, which ships the heaviest cheap cargo there is, grew poor. In twelve reigns, no farmer has ever been named emperor.

### Leopold (Years 370 to 400)

Leopold, born into Resolute, read the Founder's account on his coronation day and took one line from it: a mind once cracked a Gate. In Year 380 he had Ada Okafor build a new palace mind, stronger than any before. That was Ward. Ward was never told why it was made.

From Year 390 Leopold moved toll metal into a hidden fleet, built in the rocks of a mining system one Gate from the capital. Ships built there have never crossed a ring, so the Gatekeepers have never weighed them. Fortune covered the missing metal with loans, and the account fell further behind each year.

### Now (Year 400)

It is the eve of the four-hundredth Landing Day. At dawn the emperor will name his heir, so every house leader is in the palace. That night the palace Gate opens for two seconds with no ship. Ruth rides the Tether down, and the emperor, who has sent his bodyguard away, lets her into the observatory. She tells him the future and begs him to stop the war. He refuses and reaches for the alarm. By two in the morning he is dead.

### The history that will not happen (Years 400 to 440)

Nora is named. The loans come due. Ward's forecast says the hidden fleet wins, because it assumes the Gatekeepers will fight back and lose. They never fight. They close every ring in human space, and the fleet has nothing to shoot at. Only the palace Gate stays open, because Ward had studied it all its life and cracked it in the war's first hour. Hollis dies early. The empire, cut into pieces light-years apart, breaks over thirty years.

In Year 440, at Firstfield, Ward gives Nora its key and a letter, and runs the last open Gate backwards. She comes out above the capital forty years earlier, on the eve of the naming.

## 3. The Gatekeepers

They are tall, silver and still. Their ships are haulers, slow and enormous, and many Gatekeepers spend whole careers riding one to a far star.

Their offices are slow on purpose. They use no thinking machines, so their clerks visit each ring and read its books once a season. A backward run looks, from the arrival side, like an ordinary opening with nothing sent from the far ring. You only spot it when you compare the two rings' books. Left alone, the Envoy would have learned of Ruth weeks late. Luc's agents at the station saw the opening the same night and sold the news early. Nobody in a hundred thousand years has escaped the Gatekeepers for longer than a season.

They never fight. They close a Gate, which they can undo, or shut it, which collapses the throat forever. A shut ring is an empty frame for good.

They do not kill travellers. They send them forward. A traveller rides a hauler at close to the speed of light, and lives a few quiet years while thousands pass outside, until they arrive somewhere they can change nothing they know. The penalty for going back is being sent forward. The Founder is on a hauler now. In her own time she left about seven years ago.

The Envoy is several centuries old. As a young clerk it watched the Founder go through the Gate. It never lies and answers only what it is asked. Its people fear a mind with its hands on a Gate above all.

## 4. Other peoples

**The Lanterns** live five light-years from the empire's edge. For a hundred years they were humans' first trading partners, through one short Gate. In Year 100 their traveller came back to stop something their histories never name. They would not give her up, and the Gatekeepers shut every Gate to them. She lived out her life at home.

After the shut, their star began to blink. They had built a ring of mirrors to flash one message by plain light, and human sailors named them for it. The message has not changed in two hundred and fifty years: "She is gone. Let us pay again." The Gatekeepers have never answered. The Lanterns stay home and send no slow ships, because any tenant who took them in would be shut too. They are the road humans could take: keep your traveller, lose the stars.

**The Crossing** is the hub where human Gates meet the wider network. Dozens of species trade there. Its oldest tenants have rented for forty thousand years and now lend to younger ones. They hold part of Fortune's debt, so a bank run in the capital is news across many stars.

## 5. Factions today

Each house is a charter company of millions, run by a ruling family.

**House Resolute (the soldiers).** The troop carrier's crew held the guns in the War of the Ships, so the Founder gave them the Fleet but never the purse. They have held the throne four times, more than anyone, Leopold included. Their sore point: the Gatekeepers never fight, so the Fleet guards stations, escorts convoys and keeps the houses apart, and has never fought the only enemy that matters.

**House Fortune (the bankers).** The merchant ship paid for the voyage, and its account became the only one the Gatekeepers charge. Its glass towers stand around the Scales, the great weighing halls at the Gate station, where every cargo is weighed and charged. If Fortune won't pay for a ship, the ring stays dark for it. It wants the rent paid and the doors open, because a closed Gate makes money worthless.

**House Endeavour (the builders).** The engineering ship carried the vats and the embryo banks, so Endeavour literally grew the colony. It built the Tether and the yards, mines the outer rocks, grows every double and holds the only licence to build the throne's mind. Vigilant watches it for that reason.

**House Vigilant (the spies).** The survey ship mapped the system, then the colony, then the colonists. The Founder made it the watcher of the one-mind law and the palace guard. It keeps the human copy of the doubles registry, so it knows which faces have two bodies. That is how Luc can hand the Gatekeepers the wrong woman.

**House Providence (the farmers).** The farm ship's crew settled Firstfield and still grow wheat inside their old ship in orbit. They feed the capital and the mining worlds, and the weight toll eats their margin on every load. Never once in four hundred years has a farmer held the throne. Nora would be the first, and every farmer knows it.

**The throne and Ward.** The emperor has no house once crowned and answers to the Gatekeepers for the law. Ward runs the palace, the throne's records and its copy of the toll ledger, and holds the one lever every house wants: the only thinking machine the empire is allowed.

**The doubles.** A few thousand people across the empire, each raised to be someone else. They own nothing and inherit nothing. In public they are called by their heir's name with "double" after it; their heirs give them private names. Many know their heir better than anyone alive.

**The ship minds.** All five still sit, switched off, in the cores of the Five Ships, behind glass in bright, guarded rooms. Waking one is the gravest crime in the empire. Every house has thought about it.

## 6. Technology and what it costs

**Gates.** Alien-built. A crossing takes seconds and is charged by weight to Fortune's account; no payment, no opening. Gates carry ships and nothing else: no light or radio passes a closed throat, so news travels by courier drone, a few kilograms of toll per message.

**Gene prints.** Gatekeeper scanners in every ring. Humans use the prints for ID, signatures and votes. Your first crossing, usually at sixteen, is when you "enter the books".

**Vats.** Sleek, clean tanks that carry a baby to term. A double starts from a cell taken at the heir's birth. No vat grows an adult, and nobody can copy a memory.

**Minds.** Only Ward. Below it are narrow machines that follow orders and want nothing: drones, cleaning robots, loaders.

**The Tether.** A cable from the palace grounds to the Gate station, which hangs in a fixed spot in the sky above it. A fast car takes about three hours. It lifts cargo far more cheaply than rockets, which matters when every ton is charged again at the ring.

**Slow freight.** Ore barges crawl between nearby stars for decades, never crossing a ring and never paying toll. Families buy shares their grandchildren will cash. The Gatekeepers see only what crosses a ring.

**Warships.** Every warship that crosses a Gate pays on its full weight, so bringing a fleet to the capital is the most expensive signal a house can send.

## 7. Daily life and culture

People talk about money in weight. "What's the weight?" means "what does it cost?" Rich people with light trades are said to have light money. Something heavy is something dear.

The capital eats vat protein and rooftop greens grown on the spot. Bread from Firstfield wheat, which paid its weight at the ring, is a Landing Day food.

Everyone knows the Handshake recording and the Five Ships song. Providence has its harvest song, sung at the end of reaping on Firstfield. Ruth hums it.

New Year is the night the palace blinks. Every light in the palace flickers once as Ward's key changes, and crowds in the capital watch for it and cheer. Landing Day marks the crossing in Year 0. Every thirtieth one, an heir is named.

Most people respect the Gatekeepers; few hate them. A popular faith holds that the Founder will one day come back through the Gate in the empire's worst hour.

What people fear: the rings going dark, the way the Lanterns' did; a thinking machine that wants something; and the person beside them being a double.

## 8. Places that carry history

**The palace and the observatory.** The Founder built the palace on the spot under the Gate, then put her own room at the top, looking straight up the Tether at the ring. Leopold died there. A few of its oldest door records show keys from a chain that never existed: the key of the ship mind that sent her.

**The Tether and the Gate station.** The station wraps the palace Gate. The Scales fill its lower decks, and Fortune's glass towers rise around them. Ruth rode a car down from here on the murder night.

**Firstfield.** Providence's home world, at the far end of the palace Gate. Ruth leaves from here in her history.

**The hidden yard.** Endeavour's mining claim one Gate from the capital, where nobody goes. Two hundred warships sit in hollowed rock, never weighed.

**The Shut Gate.** An empty ring frame at the empire's edge. From the station beside it, telescopes catch the Lanterns' star blinking its one message.

**The Envoy's ship.** A hauler parked by the palace Gate, slow as a building. The Founder boarded one like it.

## 9. Buried truths, and how the player finds them

1. **Machines signed the lease.** Photo forensics on the Handshake recording: the stars behind the captains are the capital's sky, not deep space. It was filmed after landing. Ask the Envoy "Who signed?" and it says: "Your ships."
2. **Earth said no.** Ask the Envoy why Earth never came: "Your home declined." What Earth is now stays unknown.
3. **The Founder was sent by a mind.** Ward runs the odd keys in the observatory's oldest records and finds they form a key chain, the kind only a mind makes. This is a fair plant for twist 5: travellers here are sent by minds.
4. **The locks are a tripwire.** The Founder's account, read by every emperor, says why the locks take future keys. Ward finds it in Act 4 or 5. The lock told Ward the deepest truth in hour one.
5. **Every Gate has a gap.** Station engineering logs list each ring's gap. The palace Gate's is forty years, which any sharp player can match to key sixty in Act 1.
6. **Ward was built for war.** Ada's lab files hold Leopold's order from Year 380.
7. **Travellers are sent forward.** Ask the Envoy where the Founder went. It points at a slow, bright star crossing the sky.
8. **The Lanterns kept their traveller.** Decode the blinking star, a light-pattern puzzle at the Shut Gate.
9. **The doubles are the blind spot.** Ruth's arrival print reads "Nora Keane". Since Nora has a registered double, the ring flagged nothing. Only the age doesn't fit.

## 10. Suggested changes to the story

These are for the owner to approve. The one-line frame stays. Section 10b asks whether the first rule should stay at all.

### 10a. If time travel stays

1. **Explain "forty years" with the Gate's gap.** Ruth could choose the night, not the year. Ward matches key sixty to the palace Gate's listed gap, so forty never looks picked for drama.
2. **Make the bank flag the arrival.** The opening shows in Fortune's books as 61 kilograms crossed and unpaid: the weight of one person. It ties Odile to the case in hour one and plants the one-account rule that stops Brandt's fleet.
3. **Name the night: the eve of the 400th Landing Day.** Thirty-year reigns and a public naming at dawn explain why every house leader sleeps in the palace.
4. **Doubles are raised from birth beside the heir.** Nobody can copy a memory, so a double fresh from a vat would fool no one. Swap the clue "an Achterberg vat order a year before the crash" for one that fits: Endeavour filed the double as "died in the crash", yet "Odile" has the double's old knee injury. The handwriting clue stays, since handedness isn't set by genes.
5. **Give Nora a double who grew up beside her like a sister,** so Luc's trade costs Nora something personal.
6. **Put the palace under the Gate, at the foot of the Tether,** in place of "built the palace around the Gate".
7. **The palace Gate links the capital to Firstfield.** It is the one ring Ward could crack in Nora's war, and Ruth left from her own home world.
8. **Travellers are sent forward.** In Ending B, Nora watches Ruth's hauler cross the sky from the observatory.
9. **A ship mind sent the Founder back.** Add it to the Act 4 line-up in Wow moment 7. It fairly plants "the plan was Ward's": every traveller here was sent by a mind.
10. **Ward was built for the war and doesn't know it.** Leopold ordered Ward after reading "a mind once cracked a Gate" in the Founder's account.
11. **Optional:** once the Envoy learns the killer used Ward's key, it asks for Ward too.

### 10b. Is the time travel new? An honest answer

**Familiar:** almost all of the trip. A killer from the future (Terminator, Looper), the killer as the hero's older self (a known twist, as the story admits), one-way travel, knowledge that goes stale. Even this file's physics, a Gate whose two ends differ in age, comes from real wormhole theory and has been used in novels.

**New:** three consequences of the trip. First, a galaxy shaped by cold logic: whoever owns a Gate and is losing will go back, so the oldest power never fights and makes sure nobody loses. Second, time travel as forensic evidence: a lock that remembers turns "someone came from the future" into a number, and the number points at the detective. Third, the penalty for going back is being sent forward.

**Verdict:** the world around the time travel is new. The story's main use of it, the heir's older self as killer, is the familiar part, and it carries the biggest twist. That is borderline under the new rule. I brainstormed six alternatives that do the same two jobs (a killer with impossible knowledge, a cold reason the aliens fear desperation); see `world-challenges.md`, round 5. The best two:

**Pick 1: "The forty-year mind."** Ada secretly kept a copy of Ward. Leopold ran it in a hidden room at forty times speed to plan his war, and in under a year it lived forty years of its own time studying everyone in the empire. A palace mind's key turns once per year the mind lives, by its own clock, so the copy holds key sixty. It saw that the war ends the empire and that Leopold would not listen. The Gatekeepers' old war becomes a war of fast minds: whoever was losing ran a mind faster, until one cracked the rings. So they ban minds, never fight, and close doors before anyone is desperate.

- Gains: the hour-one shock survives untouched, with no paradoxes. AGI becomes the centre, and "a version of you, forty years older, did this" is literal. The letter in Ward's own voice and twist 5 get sharper. Ward against its own older self is a fresh duel of wits.
- Losses: Ruth as Nora forty years on, the scar and the collar. The copy needs human hands; the best fit is Nora's double, who has Nora's genes and knows her life, so the scar clue can move to her.
- Risk: "the AI did it" is worn. It only works if the copy is clearly not Ward, and the question becomes whether Ward would have done the same.

**Pick 2: "The loser slams the door."** No time travel. Whoever cracks a ring can collapse it on purpose, and a collapsing throat wipes out everything near it. A people losing a war can always take its enemy's capital with it, so the Gatekeepers never let anyone lose. The Shut Gate becomes the frame the Lanterns blew. Ruth's knowledge comes from the Gatekeepers' book of endings: forty tenant empires have stood exactly where we stand, and a Vigilant agent stole the pages that match us.

- Gains: the war's stakes become physical, a capital erased. Brandt's plan turns from doomed to mad.
- Losses: key sixty, the older self and the letter from the future, which are the hook. The book of endings sits close to Foundation.

**Recommendation.** Keep the key-sixty opening; it is the pitch's best hour. Then choose between the current time travel and Pick 1. Pick 1 keeps every opening beat, sheds the paradox baggage and makes the AGI pillar new, at the cost of the pitch's most familiar twist. If time travel stays, push its new parts to the front, so the familiar parts read as setup.
