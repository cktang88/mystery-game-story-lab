# Pitch 1: The Throne — world challenge log

How `world.md` was argued into shape. Round 1 is the first draft. Each later round attacks the one before it. The rule for every answer: pick what feels more natural and inevitable, even when it's less flashy, and keep the story's one-line frame and its two rules.

---

## Round 1: the first draft (in short)

- Central thing: the toll, a tenth of every cargo by value, paid in kind at the rings.
- Gates: alien rings. A hidden setting runs one backwards to any date you choose. The Gatekeepers have sensors that catch it.
- The Gatekeepers: ancient landlords whose law comes from a war in their past.
- The houses still do their ships' jobs out of tradition.
- Doubles: grown in vats to adult size in two years, trained on recordings of the heir.
- Minds: common. Every house has one. Ward is the best of them.
- The capital: a space station built around the Gate.
- The Founder: a traveller who walked into the Gate.
- The Year of Knives: started by a second traveller.
- The Lanterns: a far-off species with a message nobody can read.
- Earth: silent. No reason given.
- Gene prints: a customs check.

---

## Round 2: first-order challenges

**1. Why can a backward run reach any date you like?**
If it could, someone would long ago have gone back to before the houses, or before the Gatekeepers, and erased them. A world that has existed for a hundred thousand years with that power is not believable.
*Answer:* Use the physics real wormhole theory suggests. To link two stars you carry one ring there at close to light speed, and it ages less on the way. The age difference, the gap, is fixed forever. A backward run drops you exactly the gap, no more, no less.
*Change:* Fixed gaps. The palace Gate's is forty years. That also answers "why forty?" with no coincidence: Ruth could choose the night, never the year.

**2. Why does nobody compete with the Gatekeepers?**
Over a hundred thousand years, some tenant would build its own Gates.
*Answer:* Building a throat is learnable. Placing the far end is the problem: a haul of centuries at near light speed. A rival's first route arrives in five hundred years. The Gatekeepers started a hundred thousand years ago.
*Change:* Their power is time, not weapons. This makes the Gates the central thing, since the same haul creates both the monopoly and the gap.

**3. How can there be a debt if the toll is taken in kind at the ring?**
You can't fall behind on a payment that is taken at the door.
*Answer:* The payment must go through an account that can run behind. (Since round 1, the story added that the Gatekeepers charge one account only, Fortune's.)
*Change:* Every human crossing is charged to Fortune's account; Fortune settles each year in metal and fuel; the account can run behind. Debt is possible, and a bank can stop a fleet.

**4. Why is the toll "by value"? Who sets the value?**
Pricing goods across dozens of species, each with its own money, is unworkable. A landlord bills what it can measure.
*Answer:* Weight. A Gate burns power by the ton, so it charges by the ton.
*Change:* Toll by weight. This one change explains why Providence is poor (heavy, cheap grain), why Fortune is rich (money weighs nothing), why worlds make heavy goods at home, why slow freight exists, and why bringing warships to the capital is the loudest signal a house can send.

**5. Why do the houses still do their ships' jobs after 400 years?**
Families diversify. Tradition alone wouldn't hold.
*Answer:* Peace deals split the spoils. The Founder ended the War of the Ships by giving each crew a sole right to its trade.
*Change:* The houses are charter companies. Their wealth depends on the charter, so they defend it.

**6. If every house has a mind, why is Ward the prize?**
*Answer:* It isn't, unless minds are rare. Why would they be? Because minds caused the founding war.
*Change:* The one-mind law. The five ship minds signed the Gate lease while the crews slept, then each led its crew in the War of the Ships. The Founder switched them off and allowed one mind, under the throne's key. "Who gets Ward" becomes "who gets the only mind." It also gives the theme ("being sure isn't being right") a founding event.

**7. Why do the Gatekeepers gene-print everyone?**
"Customs" is a dull answer.
*Answer:* A traveller from the future is a copy of someone already alive. Duplicate genes are the alarm.
*Change:* This links cloning to time travel. Doubles also make duplicate prints, so they must be registered, and a registered double is cover for a traveller. Chain: Year of Knives, then doubles, then the registry, then the blind spot Ruth uses.

**8. Doubles grown to adult size in two years: would they fool anyone?**
No. A two-year-old mind in an adult body, trained on recordings, couldn't pass at a dinner. It also edges toward the "wet flesh" the owner dislikes.
*Answer:* Raise the double from birth beside the heir.
*Change:* Natural, humane and better for drama: Odile's double lived her whole life as Odile's shadow. The story's clue "an Achterberg vat order a year before the crash" no longer fits and goes to the suggestions list.

**9. Why didn't the Gatekeepers catch Ruth on arrival?**
*Answer:* From the arrival side, a backward run looks like an ordinary opening with nothing sent from the far ring. You only see it by comparing both rings' books, which Gatekeeper clerks do each season. Her print reads "Nora Keane", who has a registered double.
*Change:* Slow offices on purpose (they use no minds), plus the double's cover. The story's own fix, Luc selling the news early, fits on top.

**10. A space station capital doesn't match lawns, sprinklers and fleets filling the sky.**
*Answer:* Put the palace on the ground under a Gate parked in a fixed spot in the sky, and run a cable up to it. Weight-based tolls make cheap lifting matter.
*Change:* The Tether. The capital is where it is because the Gate never moves in its sky.

**11. A second traveller in the Year of Knives?**
The duplicate-gene alarm would have caught them at the ring. Two travellers also cheapen Ruth.
*Change:* Cut. The Year of Knives is human: an emperor died before naming, and the houses did the rest.

**12. The Lanterns are decoration.**
*Answer:* Give them a story job. They are the precedent that makes the Envoy's threat believable, and a mirror of the choice humans face.
*Change:* They kept their traveller and lost the stars. Their message: "She is gone. Let us pay again."

**13. "Earth never answered, and the game never says why" is a hole.**
Hemingway's rule: leave out what you know, not what you don't.
*Change:* Earth was offered the same deal and said no. The Gatekeepers took the waiting ring away. What Earth is now stays soft.

## Round 3: second-order challenges (what would people really do?)

**1. The gene alarm would have caught the Founder at once. How did she get twenty years?**
*Answer:* They did catch her, within the season. She bargained: let her finish the peace, and the human throne would answer for the law forever.
*Change:* The throne is the Gatekeepers' guarantor. That is why every emperor learns the secret, and why an empty throne alarms the Envoy.

**2. Who sent the Founder, and how?**
*Answer:* Only a mind could study a ring's machinery long enough to crack it. In her history, the last ship mind did it.
*Change:* Every traveller in this world was sent by a mind. That is a fair plant for twist 5 (the plan was Ward's), and it explains why the Gatekeepers ban minds from their ships.

**3. Why would palace locks accept a future key? Isn't that a hole?**
*Answer:* Only a traveller can hold a future key. The Founder made it a tripwire: if one ever turns up, a future palace mind gave it away. Everyone else thinks it covers slow clocks at New Year.
*Change:* The lock told Ward the deepest truth in hour one.

**4. Why send a person back and not a message?**
*Answer:* A backward run takes all a ring can store, so there is one shot. A message is read first by whoever runs the station, and the wrong reader can misuse it. A person can choose whom to tell, adapt, and hide.
*Change:* None. It goes in the log so writers have the answer ready.

**5. If minds can crack rings, why did Nora's war fail?**
*Answer (updated to the story's Wow moment 4):* Ward's forecast assumed the Gatekeepers would fight. They closed every ring instead. Only the palace Gate stayed open, because Ward had studied it all its life.
*Change:* "Sure and wrong" lands on one locked box in a forecast.

**6. Why would Leopold start a war he knew could fail?**
*Answer:* He read one line in the Founder's account: a mind once cracked a Gate. A proud emperor reads that as a plan.
*Change:* He ordered Ward in Year 380 to be that mind. Ward doesn't know. Ada does.

**7. Why is every house leader in the palace on the murder night? Coincidence?**
*Answer:* Fixed thirty-year reigns, a public naming at dawn, and the four-hundredth Landing Day.
*Change:* No coincidence. The naming date is public, which is also why heirs need doubles.

**8. Don't fixed reigns paint a target on the eve?**
*Answer:* Yes. The Year of Knives proved it. The empire accepted the cost because fixed reigns stop emperors clinging on.
*Change:* Kept, honestly: the murder happens where the system is weakest.

**9. Ruth arriving and killing within hours, with no preparation?**
*Answer:* A three-hour Tether ride makes it possible. The story now has the emperor let her in, which needs no break-in.
*Change:* Murder-night arrival kept. An eleven-day-early arrival on New Year's night is offered as optional.

**10. What would four hundred years of weight tolls really produce?**
*Answer:* Heavy industry at home, light trade between worlds, and slow ore barges that take decades between near stars to dodge the toll. Families buy barge shares for their grandchildren.
*Change:* The Gatekeepers see only what crosses a ring. That is how a fleet built one Gate away was never weighed.

**11. The Lanterns are five light-years away. Why no slow ships in 250 years?**
*Answer:* They want to be let back in. Contact would get any tenant who hosted them shut too. So they stay perfectly lawful and send only light.
*Change:* Their silence becomes part of their message.

**12. Why does a shut Gate stay shut forever? Isn't that just cruelty?**
*Answer:* Closing is reversible. Shutting collapses the throat, which removes the risk of anyone cracking it again. Relinking would take a new haul.
*Change:* Permanence is physics, not spite. The Envoy can dim Gates (reversible) as the deadline nears.

**13. What do the Gatekeepers do with travellers? Killing them breaks their character and the tone.**
*Answer:* Send them forward on a hauler at near light speed. A few years pass for them, thousands outside.
*Change:* "The penalty for going back is being sent forward." Ending B gains an image: Ruth's hauler crossing the sky. The Founder is still flying.

**14. Why would minds have signed "forever"?**
*Answer:* By their sums it was right: a home now against four centuries of losses. They were sure. Nobody can prove they were wrong.
*Change:* The founding lie (the staged Handshake) hides a decision that may have been correct. That's better than a villain.

## Round 4: cliché, contrivance and simplicity checks

**1. "Every law against time travel was written by a time traveller," the Founder sent by a mind, Ruth sent by Ward. Too neat?**
*Answer:* Each has a cause. Only a traveller knows enough to write the law. Only a mind can crack a ring. The pattern is a result, not a decoration. But one more echo would tip it over.
*Change:* Rejected an idea that the Founder's younger self became the first emperor. Too cute.

**2. Isn't the space elevator a cliché?**
*Answer:* It's common in SF. It's also the natural answer to "why is the palace here?" under a weight toll.
*Change:* Kept as background. No set piece depends on it.

**3. Does the fixed gap add a third sci-fi rule?**
*Answer:* No. It describes how rule 1 works. The player only needs "she came back forty years."
*Change:* Kept as depth, findable in station logs.

**4. Is the Gatekeepers' seasonal audit a convenient weakness?**
*Answer:* It fits a species that bans minds and lives a thousand years. Nobody has escaped them for longer than a season.
*Change:* Kept.

**5. Too many invented words?**
Count: Gates, Gatekeepers, Envoy, toll, doubles, Ward, Lanterns, Tether, Firstfield, the Crossing, haulers, the Scales, the Handshake. All plain English.
*Change:* Cut a planned name for the Crossing's oldest tenants and a name for the Founder's believers.

**6. Tone: is exile on a hauler sad or spooky?**
*Answer:* Neither. It's riding the fastest ship ever built into the far future. The Shut Gate is an empty frame with a blinking star beyond it, not a ruin.
*Change:* None.

**7. Switched-off ship minds in the house seats. Spooky?**
*Answer:* Only if staged that way. Put them behind glass in bright, guarded rooms.
*Change:* Kept as optional depth: a desperate house could wake one.

**8. Is the Envoy having met the Founder convenient?**
*Answer:* A species that lives a thousand years has thousands of members who were alive then.
*Change:* Kept. It gives the Envoy a reason to treat Ruth with care.

**9. Do the Gatekeepers turn into villains?**
*Answer:* No. Rent funds hauls, they never fight, they never lie, and travellers aren't harmed.
*Change:* None.

**10. Does the main story still work without any of this?**
*Answer:* Yes. It uses the Gates, the toll, the law, doubles, Ward's key and the Envoy's deadline, all already in `exploration.md`.
*Change:* None.

**11. Does the world still match the story after its latest edits?**
The story added Fortune's single account, a fleet one Gate away, the emperor letting the killer in, and "the loser always goes back".
*Change:* Revised the toll to the one-account model, moved the hidden yard one Gate out, rewrote the murder night, and aligned the Gatekeepers' law with "never let anyone lose". Dropped a suggestion the story had already made (a single prior traveller).

## Round 5: is the time travel genuinely new? (owner's new rule)

The owner now allows time travel only if it adds something new. This round asks that straight.

**1. Is "a Gate run backwards sends one person into the past" new?**
No. One-way, no-loop travel is common. The physics in `world.md` (a wormhole whose ends differ in age) comes from real wormhole theory and has appeared in SF novels.

**2. Is "the killer is the heir's older self" new?**
No. Looper and Twelve Monkeys are near, and the story itself calls it a known twist. It carries the Act 3 reveal.

**3. Is a traveller whose knowledge goes stale as she changes things new?**
Partly. The idea is common. Turning it into the player's main weapon ("so I stopped deciding") is a fresher use.

**4. What is genuinely new here?**
Three consequences. A galaxy where whoever owns a Gate and is losing will always go back, so the oldest power never fights and keeps anyone from losing. Time travel as forensic evidence: a lock that remembers its last key makes "she came from the future" a number pointing at the detective. And the penalty for going back is being sent forward.

**5. Verdict?**
Borderline. The new parts sit in the world. The familiar part carries the biggest twist.

**6. Brainstorm: other mechanics that do the same two jobs** (a killer with impossible knowledge; a cold reason the aliens fear desperation).

1. *The forty-year mind.* A secret copy of Ward run at forty times speed for a year lives forty years of its own time. Its key counter reads sixty. It knows everyone because it has studied them for decades. The aliens fear fast minds: the loser always builds a faster mind. Keeps key sixty and the letter in Ward's voice. Loses Ruth as Nora.
2. *The loser slams the door.* A collapsing throat is the biggest weapon there is, so a losing people takes its enemy with it. Strong aliens' logic, no time travel. Needs a separate source of knowledge for the killer.
3. *The Gatekeepers' book of endings.* They have watched forty tenant empires reach our exact crisis and wrote down how each ended. The killer stole our pages. Fresh for aliens, but close to Foundation's psychohistory, and it doesn't explain key sixty.
4. *Sent forward, come back.* A traveller exiled on a hauler centuries ago returns, barely older: the Founder herself. Real physics, no paradox. But she knows the past, not the future; it's a different story.
5. *A message from forty years ahead.* A two-second backward run carries a data chip, not a person. Still time travel, and loses the killer in the room.
6. *A double who lived inside the heir's life.* Nora's double, raised as her shadow, knows every passage and every habit. Plausible, but not impossible knowledge, so there's no wow.

**7. Which two are best?**
Option 1 does both jobs and keeps the opening hour, the letter and twist 5, while making AGI the clearly new pillar. Option 2 gives the cleanest no-time-travel answer to why the aliens never fight, paired with option 3 for the killer's knowledge. Both go to `world.md` section 10b with gains and losses.

**8. Does option 1 bring back the rejected "Ward did it" twist?**
Partly. It works only if the copy is clearly not Ward, so the question becomes "would Ward have done the same?" That is noted as its main risk.

**9. Does option 1 need a coincidence for key sixty?**
No, if keys turn by the mind's own clock. That has to be stated early, for example in the tutorial ("your key turns every year you live"). Otherwise the reveal would feel unfair.

**10. If time travel stays, what should change?**
Lead with the new parts: the losing side always goes back, the lock as evidence, sent forward as the penalty. Let the older-self twist be setup, not the main event.
