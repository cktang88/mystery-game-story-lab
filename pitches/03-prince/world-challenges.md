# Pitch 3: world challenge log

How the world in `world.md` was built and argued with. Round 1 is the first draft. Rounds 2 to 4 attack it. Round 5 answers the owner's new question about whether the time travel is new. Each challenge has an answer and what changed.

## Round 1: the first draft

The first draft put the crystals at the centre from the start, since the story's own rule already made them scarce. Its main choices:
- Crystals came in split pairs. Speak into one half and the words appear in the other half's past, anywhere. Each royal wore a half in their ring, so the Empress could warn her children.
- Both peoples had fast drives from the start.
- Humans borrowed because they were broke. The debt grew from bad luck and Aun skill.
- Doubles were invented in the war.
- The Empress lived the ten days before and sent a letter two weeks back to her son.
- The war AI heard letters land, so the Aun could be surprised, so they made peace.
- Two species, the Aun and humans, and nothing else.

## Round 2

**1. Why would the crown ever let paired halves leave the Vault?** If a half in a ring can receive letters, every general and banker would want one, and the crown's monopoly leaks within a generation. Worse, a child wearing a half could be kidnapped for it.
*Answer:* pairs break the monopoly the whole world rests on. A crystal that speaks only to its own past makes the monopoly physical: letters only reach whoever held that crystal, and the crown holds them all.
*Changed:* crystals speak to their own past. The Empress always writes to herself. The Vault travels with her. The ring became a seal, not a receiver.

**2. Does the two-week timing work?** The rule says ten days. A letter written on day 10 can't reach two weeks before the murder.
*Answer:* no. But day 10's dawn minus ten days is day 0's dawn, the morning of the murder, hours before it happens.
*Changed:* the letter lands on the morning of the murder. (The story had already reached the same fix in parallel.)

**3. A double takes a month to grow and longer to train. How did Promise get one after a warning?**
*Answer:* he couldn't. The double must already exist.
*Changed:* Promise bought his double years ago. (The story now says eight years, after a scare at court.)

**4. Why ten days for the fleet? A fleet that close could come tomorrow.**
*Answer:* ten days is exactly how long the Empress can wait and still send a letter back to before the murder. She is keeping a retry open. She did the same last time.
*Changed:* the deadline became the letter window. This is now the first suggested story change.

**5. Isn't "aliens who treat promises as law" the average noble-alien cliché?**
*Answer:* yes, if it's just their nature. It becomes natural if it was forced on them: after a ruler used a letter to ambush a peace table, the other houses had no way to beat her except to bind her word.
*Changed:* promise law is the leash the houses put on the crown at the First Table. Added the law that the crown may never punish anyone for what a letter says they will do. That law also explains why the Empress only warned her son and arrested no one.

**6. Why would a crown sell its shrinking, priceless foresight to banks?**
*Answer:* because one letter of market prices earns more than a province, the Vault looked big at first contact, and a young Empress was short of money. Short-term thinking from a long-lived ruler is still human-shaped greed.
*Changed:* Long Winter approved the renting while young. Thirty-five years of it burned more crystals than four centuries before.

**7. If both sides had fast drives, why didn't a 3,000-year-old empire with foresight overrun humans long ago?**
*Answer:* it would have. The Aun must have been slow.
*Changed:* the Aun only had slow ships. Humans sold them the fast drive at first contact, in exchange for loans. Humanity armed its own creditor.

**8. Why would human traders keep losing to Aun banks for decades without suspecting?**
*Answer:* they would suspect if they lost every time. Banks that win just often enough look skilled.
*Changed:* the banks won "just often enough." The proof survives in the Debt Clock's old jumps, which line up with letter dates. That gave the player a doing-puzzle.

**9. Why hasn't anyone built a second listening AI since the truce?**
*Answer:* the method lives only in the AI's learned mind, from years of war data. The truce ordered the data destroyed.
*Changed:* the Fleet secretly kept the war logs. This gave Strand a real secret and Maren her source.

**10. Who stops the Clinic from swapping a leader for a double?**
*Answer:* nobody independent. It sits under the Chancellor's office.
*Changed:* the Clinic became a state service under the Chancellor, which is exactly what Maren exploits.

**11. Why would humans give up their best weapon for a truce?**
*Answer:* they were still losing. The listening only slowed the bleeding. And the Empress's real price was not the weapon but the count: each ring heard is a crystal spent, so the AI could audit the Vault.
*Changed:* the hidden truce term is silence about the count.

**12. Two species only. The brief wants many polities and species.**
*Answer:* a third species would add a glossary and break "simple on top." Polities can multiply instead: the Hundred Worlds split between rich and border worlds, the Aun Court and its banks, the Crown, the Clinic, the Fleet and the station.
*Changed:* added the border worlds and the Court's bankers as factions. No third species.

## Round 3

**1. If ten days is a retry window, why doesn't the Empress write on day 1, with more time to fix things?**
*Answer:* a day-1 letter says nothing she doesn't already know. Waiting to day 10 lets her fill the letter with everything ten days of searching found. The ten days are an investigation she runs to write one sentence.
*Changed:* sharpened the framing in section 8.

**2. Last time, why didn't her letter name the killer, or cancel the signing?**
*Answer:* last time the case failed, so she had no name. Cancelling the signing would show she knew something, and the killer might strike where she couldn't see. The Table law also stops her acting against anyone on a letter alone.
*Changed:* nothing new; confirmed the story's version.

**3. If letters only reach the Vault, how did the Aun win battles all over human space?**
*Answer:* the Empress led from her throne-ship with the Vault aboard. Battles near her were won by letters; orders went out by courier.
*Changed:* the throne-ship as flagship. It also explains why the AI could hear landings: the Vault was at the front.

**4. "Crystals from a dying star" is hand-waving.**
*Answer:* it is, and that's fine. Dune never explains why spice works. The rule matters, not the physics. What matters is that nobody knows, which gives humans a reason to survey dying stars.
*Changed:* added the human surveys and a sequel hook.

**5. Would a society really accept a class of numbered, grown people?**
*Answer:* it would, in a war, the way societies accept conscription. Twenty-five years later there's a small rights movement, and doubles get names at the end of service.
*Changed:* added the doubles' terms, pensions and the rights movement. It gives Ines a world to come from.

**6. A 2.5-metre glowing alien hides in a human market as a dock worker?**
*Answer:* only if Aun dock crews are common there and humans can't tell Aun faces apart.
*Changed:* suggested story change 9.

**7. Where does the Truce Cup custom come from?**
*Answer:* the First Table. If both leaders drink from one cup, a poisoner kills himself too. That makes the custom a poison check, and an Aun-only poison breaks it.
*Changed:* the cup's origin. Suggested story change 3.

**8. Can the Clerk count the whole Vault?**
*Answer:* no. It only hears landings near Halfway. Its war logs show the wartime rate. The count is an estimate built from those logs plus the captured ledger.
*Changed:* Maren's count became an estimate from two sources, which needs Strand's logs.

**9. Why can't the Aun grow their own doubles to fight back?**
*Answer:* they could. The Court banned copying royals because a copy of the heir could take the Vault.
*Changed:* the copy law became recent and practical, asked for by Long Winter herself. Suggested story change 6.

**10. With the Vault nearly empty, what does each Aun side want?**
*Answer:* the same fact gives opposite answers. Promise wants peace before the empty Vault is known. Keen, who keeps the Vault, needs peace but must sound strong. The bankers want their debt and don't know the count.
*Changed:* the Court's bankers became the real Aun war voice. Suggested story change 10.

**11. Why are the Aun tall, slow, long-lived and good at hearing? Is that just "elegant alien" styling?**
*Answer:* it was styling. A cold, dim, low-gravity home world with thick air explains all of it: height, slowness, long lives, hearing, and light-lines for signalling in the dark.
*Changed:* the home world. The light-lines show feeling, which became a play mechanic (suggested change 5).

**12. Why is Halfway where it is?**
*Answer:* human drives must refuel at gas giants. The giant at Halfway is the short road between the two peoples, the last battle was fought there, and the station grew around the wreck.
*Changed:* the Old Hull is Lookout's ship, and the Clerk still runs in it.

## Round 4

**1. The first time, with no earlier letter, why did the Empress choose ten days?**
*Answer:* for the same reason. She did the same sum both times.
*Changed:* nothing.

**2. Does the Clerk's landing log give too much away on day 1?**
*Answer:* the player sees a fact ("a letter landed this morning") whose meaning only opens on day 8. That's fair play.
*Changed:* nothing.

**3. Is First Spring's betrayal too neat a mirror of the murder?**
*Answer:* the mirror is now the point: both punish a future that never happened. It stays optional lore.
*Changed:* nothing.

**4. Do the coin flips trivialise the war?**
*Answer:* no. They're a small custom with a forgotten reason, the way real customs work.
*Changed:* nothing.

**5. Is Maren's plan (a short war to drain the Vault) safe enough for her to try?**
*Answer:* it's a gamble, but her numbers say the Aun can't undo many more losses, and she plans for the war to happen close to human space. It's in character for someone who sends a double to every risky event.
*Changed:* nothing.

**6. Would a poison that harms only the Aun be easy to make?**
*Answer:* yes. Two species have different body chemistry. Something mild to humans can be deadly to the Aun.
*Changed:* nothing.

**7. Does the world need a coincidence anywhere?**
*Answer:* checked each link. The double existed for years. The warning came that morning. Maren's habit of sending Ines is set up. The deadline is a sum. No coincidence helps the heroes.
*Changed:* nothing.

**8. Is anything here a stack of invented systems?**
*Answer:* there is one rule detail (a crystal speaks to its own past) and the rest is people. The only invented word remains "Aun."
*Changed:* nothing.

**9. Over twenty years of truce, wouldn't the Aun have held a Reading by now?**
*Answer:* they'd want to and can't afford to. The missing Readings are a clue some Aun have started to notice.

**10. Would a 700-year-old human civilisation still be this disorganised?**

Round 4 found nothing serious. We stopped.

## Round 5: is the time travel new? (owner's new rule)

**Challenge:** Is "the aliens send short letters up to ten days back from a limited stock" new, or a familiar trope?

*Honest answer:* the mechanic is familiar. Messages to the past are the heart of *Steins;Gate*. Rare foresight running an empire is close to Dune. Limited charges are a common game device.

*What is new is the angle and the uses.* The story shows time travel only from outside, as a resource with a ledger. From that angle come ideas readers won't predict:
- No one spends a crystal on good news, so the unbeaten empire has lost hundreds of times.
- The enemy can hear letters land, so each ring confesses a defeat, and counting rings audits the Vault.
- Cloning beats foresight: a letter tells you where, not which.
- The deadline is the letter window.
- The warning caused the clue that cracks the case.

**Seven alternatives brainstormed**, each tested on three jobs: an empire that seems unbeatable, an Empress who knows what she shouldn't, and a prince with a reason for a double.
1. **One-use instant messages.** Paired crystals carry one message instantly, anywhere; all else moves at ship speed. The Aun always know first. Works on all three jobs. Old idea (the ansible), loses the best twists.
2. **Crystals that remember sound.** Every crystal records what's said near it; the crown can play any one back. Human tech is full of cheap Aun crystal parts sold with the loans. Works on all three jobs and makes the debt the weapon. Risk: "they heard the murder" can kill the mystery.
3. **A shared Aun mind.** All Aun share memories through their light. Hive minds are a heavy trope, and it makes Patience's secrets impossible. Rejected.
4. **The Aun see probabilities.** A gift of near-certain prediction. Plain precognition is more worn than time travel. Rejected.
5. **An ancient oracle AI the Aun found.** Clashes with the Clerk as the story's AI and is a familiar ruin-of-the-ancients idea. Rejected.
6. **The Empress is one mind in a line of clone bodies.** She "knows" because she remembers her own past lives. Muddles the cloning pillar and confuses the double plot. Rejected.
7. **Aun can rewind a small bubble of space.** Still time travel, more visual, more paradox-prone. Rejected.

**Best two:** 1 and 2. Both are written up in `world.md`, section 9, with gains and losses.

**Verdict:** keep the ten-day letters, shown only from outside: no loops on screen, no paradox talk, one rule. The new ideas come from the time rule, and both alternatives lose the story's two best reveals ("lost hundreds of times" and "these ten days happened before"). If the owner still wants time travel gone, alternative 2 is the stronger swap.
*Changed:* added the novelty section to `world.md`.
