# Pitch 3: The Prince

**In one line:** An alien prince is murdered on a human space station, and a human cop and the prince's alien bodyguard have ten days to find the killer before the two empires go to war.

## 1. The story in one page

At dawn, an alien guard finds the crown prince dead in his glass garden.

The garden sits on Halfway, a city-sized station on the border between humans and the Aun. The two sides fought one war twenty years ago, and the humans nearly lost it. The Aun almost never lose. They can send short letters back in time, and human soldiers still say, "You can't surprise the Aun."

The prince came to Halfway to sign a real peace. He died the night before the signing.

By noon his mother, the Empress, has parked her throne-ship over the station: half a kilometre of white glass. Her message fills every screen. "Ten days." After that, her war fleet arrives.

The humans hand the case to Sam Okafor, a night-shift cop with a bad war record. The Empress sends Patience, the giant bodyguard who failed to save her son, to stay at his side. Sam lost a brother to the Aun. Patience can hear through walls, and she doesn't trust humans.

The station's AI, the Clerk, can rebuild any moment on the human half, and Sam can walk around inside the rebuild. The garden shows up as a blank white space, because by treaty the Clerk can't see in there. And the Clerk has logged something odd. A letter from the future landed on the Empress's ship that morning, hours before the murder. Nobody spends a letter on good news. Someone knew.

On day five, Patience takes Sam to a hydroponics bay. A tall figure in a dock worker's coat turns around.

"You must be Sam," says Prince Promise. "I'm told I'm dead."

The body was a copy. Someone meant to kill the real prince and still thinks they did. If Promise steps out, the war stops, but his people will never forgive him for the copy, and he loses the throne. If he stays hidden, the fleet arrives.

And the Empress keeps saying something nobody understands: "Ten days was not enough last time."

## 2. The sides

Five sides: two human, two alien, and the station in the middle.

**The Hundred Worlds** are a loose union of human planets: loud, crowded and broke. Every human still pays a war debt to the Aun, and a giant Debt Clock over Halfway's market ticks the total up. Humans also use doubles, grown copies of leaders who stand in at risky events. To them it's as normal as a seatbelt.

Their leader, **Chancellor Maren Hale**, is warm and funny, the human face of the peace. In secret she has learned something from a captured Aun ledger: the Aun are nearly out of letters. If the peace is signed now, humans pay half the debt forever, just as the Aun lose the edge that made them win.

**The Human Fleet** waits outside Halfway in grey warships. **Admiral Halvard Strand** attacks the Aun on the news every night, so he's the first suspect. In private, he lost half his ships last time and never wants to fight again.

**The Aun Empire** is old and rich. The Aun live about three hundred years, stand two and a half metres tall and move slowly. Faint lines of light run under their pale grey skin, like a lamp behind porcelain. A spoken promise is close to law. **Empress Long Winter**, three metres tall, ran out of patience with humans in the first war. Her son grew up loving them.

**The Aun Court** is nobles who quote ancient law. Its loudest voice is **Princess Keen**, Promise's younger sister and keeper of the royal vault of letters. She is a hawk on every broadcast. With Promise dead, she inherits, so she's the first Aun suspect.

**Halfway Station** is a ring around a gas giant's moon, half markets and half glass gardens. **The Clerk**, an AI built under the truce, runs the station for both sides. It sees everything on the human half and nothing inside Aun rooms. It wants the peace to hold, and it keeps one secret for that reason.

## 3. Who we follow

**Sam Okafor** is the player: late forties, ex-army, stuck on night shifts. He watches people's hands and makes jokes when nervous. He wants to keep Halfway, his home, out of another war, but he still flinches around the Aun. In the war he let an Aun prisoner go, and the army threw him out for it. He never learned her name. He doesn't know why he got this case. The Chancellor picked him because she expects him to fail.

**Patience** is the prince's bodyguard and Sam's partner. She is huge, polite and deadly. The Empress orders her to guard Sam and report everything, but her loyalty is to Promise. She hides three things, which come out one at a time:
- The dead prince was a double, and she helped the real one hide.
- She heard the whole garden visit through the wall. She and Promise suspect the Chancellor, but can't accuse her without exposing the double. They need Sam to find other proof.
- She was the prisoner Sam let go. That's why she accepted him.

**Prince Promise** studied humans all his life, and he borrowed their oldest trick. Eight years ago, after a scare at court, he paid a human clinic to grow a double of himself. Only Patience knew. On the morning of the murder his mother called him: "Do not be alone in your garden tonight." Cancelling the eve-of-treaty drink would insult the humans and risk the signing. So he sent his double. To the Aun, copying royal blood is a disgrace. If it comes out, he loses the throne.

**Ines** has been Maren's double for twelve years. Doubles get numbers; she picked her own name. On the murder night she was the "Chancellor" who poured the prince a gift of wine. She didn't know Maren had poisoned it. She knew the prince's double, too: years ago the clinic trained them side by side.

**The Clerk** talks in exact sentences, with a dry wit. It scanned the body at dawn and knew it was a copy. It tells no one, because the truth could end the peace as fast as the lie.

**Empress Long Winter** has lived these ten days before. That time her son died, nobody found the killer, and war began. On the tenth day she spent a letter to warn her past self on the morning of the murder. Then she called her son. When his body turned up anyway, she believed the warning had failed. She is grieving a son she saved.

## 4. The rules, and the one thing that explains the world

**Time:** The Aun can send a short letter back in time, up to ten days, but each letter burns up a crystal they can't replace, and only a few are left.

**Cloning:** Humans can grow an adult double of a person in about a month, with the body but none of the memories.

**One thing explains the world: the letters.** Every big fact traces back to them. The Aun empire was founded with a letter. Aun banks grew rich seeing markets ten days ahead, which is how humans fell into debt. Humans invented doubles because a letter can tell you where the Chancellor will be, but not which Chancellor. The truce happened because a human AI learned to hear letters landing. And the murder happens now because the vault is nearly empty.

**The big idea: the Aun have never lost a war, because they have lost hundreds.** No one spends a crystal on good news. A letter only goes back when something went wrong, so every Aun victory is a defeat that someone undid. The vault's empty holders list the battles the Aun lost. When the last crystal is gone, the undefeated empire will lose for the first time in two thousand years, and every enemy it has will know. That's why the Aun suddenly want peace. It's also why Maren wants war before the treaty locks the debt in place.

The pillars: the **aliens** are the other side, the victim and the partner. **Time travel** is the world's hidden engine. **Cloning** is the murder's trick. The **AI** is the player's main tool and the reason the first war ended.

## 5. Backstory (the iceberg)

The player needs only the tip. Parts marked *main* hold up the plot. Parts marked *optional* reward players who dig.

**3,000 years ago: the dying star** *(optional)*. The crystals grew in one dying star near the Aun home. Strike one the right way and it carries a few words up to ten days into its past. The star went dark 1,200 years ago. Every letter since has come from a shrinking vault.

**1,900 years ago: the betrayal at the table** *(optional)*. The Aun were once rival houses at war. They met at a great table to make peace. One leader, First Spring, read a letter from ten days ahead and ambushed the others at the table. Her family still rules, and the official history blames the other houses. That's why the Aun treat promises as close to law: they built a culture of kept promises on top of a broken one. The murder on Halfway repeats it, a peace broken at a table by someone who thinks she can see ahead.

**60 years ago: the debt** *(main)*. Humans met the Aun and borrowed from their banks. Their trades kept going wrong. *(Optional: the royal family rented letters to the banks, which used them to see markets ahead. The debt was built on rigged trades.)*

**25 years ago: the war** *(main)*. Humans stopped paying. The Aun won almost every battle, and every loss they had, they undid. Their assassins killed human leaders with the same foresight. So humans started using doubles.

**20 years ago: the truce** *(main: it happened; optional: why)*. The humans' war AI learned to pick up the faint signal a crystal gives off when a letter lands. Each signal meant "the Aun lost something here, in a future that no longer exists." For the first time, humans could tell where the Aun were weak. The Empress traded the war for the AI. It was rebuilt as a neutral keeper that may never serve a fleet again. That AI is the Clerk.

### Places that hold history
The Debt Clock over the market. The Truce Cup, shared by the two leaders the night before any treaty. The Clinic, where both doubles were grown. The Vault of Letters, rows of empty holders and a handful of full ones. And, for players who dig, the Old Hull where the truce was signed and the First Table from the betrayal.

Days one to three show the surface. From day four the rules turn into facts, and from day seven the Vault and the Clerk's past open up.

## 6. What to borrow, and what to avoid

We borrow techniques only:
- From *Alien Nation* and Asimov's *The Caves of Steel*: hostile partners who each need the other's skills. Sam reads people and footage; Patience hears through walls and opens Aun doors.
- From *The Day of the Jackal*: a visible countdown. Each day opens on the day number, the Debt Clock and the fleets' distance.
- From *Dune*: one resource that explains politics, money, custom and war at once. Ours is the letters.
- From *The Three-Body Problem*: a huge idea reached by cold logic. Ours is "no one sends good news back," which turns an unbeaten empire into one that has lost hundreds of times.
- From *Return of the Obra Dinn* and *Majora's Mask*: walkable frozen scenes and fixed days with people on schedules.

*Star Trek VI* is the nearest lookalike: a peace-minded alien leader is killed before talks, and hawks on both sides did it together. Here the loud hawks are innocent, the killer is the human peacemaker working through her double, and the victim survived.

## 7. Threads

We tried fourteen threads and kept six:

1. **The case.** Sam and Patience go from enemies to partners. He lost a brother to the Aun; she has to trust a human with her prince.
2. **The hidden prince.** The victim is alive, but saying so destroys him.
3. **The double.** Ines goes from a face in the background to the most important witness in two empires.
4. **The sister.** Keen sounds like the biggest hawk alive. She keeps the vault, so she knows better than anyone that the Aun can't afford a war.
5. **The Chancellor's game.** Maren is kind, helpful and always a step ahead.
6. **The Clerk's conscience.** The AI hid one fact to save the peace. Then human law orders it to erase evidence, and it has to decide what neutral means.

We cut a leaking journalist, a false confession and an Aun general. Each added a face but no new question.

**Where the threads collide:** Maren hands Sam the Clerk and points him at the Admiral (day 3). Patience, cornered, takes Sam to Promise (day 5). Maren has the Clerk switched off (day 7). Keen learns her brother is alive and keeps quiet (day 8). Sam shows Ines the poison report, and she goes home to Maren without a word (day 9). Everyone meets in the Treaty Hall (day 10).

## 8. Twists

We tried eleven and kept five. We rejected Patience or Keen as killer, hawks on both sides (*Star Trek VI*), the AI as killer, Promise faking his death and Sam as a clone. Each was too obvious, too borrowed or confusing for no gain.

**1. The body is a double (end of day 4).** The question flips from "who killed him?" to "he's alive, so why is he hiding?"
Planted by:
- Patience stays calm at the body while the other guards grieve.
- The Clerk turns down a full medical scan, its first refusal ever.
- An old notebook in the garden says "Stand straighter. He laughs with his eyes shut." It's a copy learning to be Promise, eight years ago.
- The hand has no ring mark.

**2. The visiting Chancellor was Ines (days 6 and 7).** The human defence against assassins becomes the murder weapon.
Planted by:
- The Chancellor drank the same wine and is fine, so everyone rules it out. A day-4 Clinic report shows the poison only harms the Aun.
- In the corridor rebuild, the "Chancellor" carries the bottle in her left hand. At the day-one gala, Maren signs autographs with her right.
- Ines hums an old song while she thinks. Patience heard the same tune through the garden wall.

**3. Two people chose Sam for opposite reasons (days 5 and 6).** Maren chose him to fail. Patience accepted him because he once freed her. Planted when Sam's boss can't see why the Chancellor named a night-shift cop, and when Patience once calls Sam "the guard," then corrects herself.

**4. The Clerk is switched off (end of day 7).** The player loses their best tool, and Maren does it legally. Her public reason is true: the Clerk refused to share the medical scan. Planted when the Clerk says on day 1 that it obeys "lawful human authority on human matters," and when Maren jokes, "If it ever stops being neutral, there's a switch." On day 6 the Clerk asks whether its evidence would count if it were switched off, and hands Sam one copied file: the corridor footage.

**5. These ten days have happened before (day 8).** Planted by the Empress's "not enough last time" (everyone thinks she means the old war), the letter in the Clerk's log, and a short call from the Empress to her son that morning. See "The warning made the clue" below.

## 9. Wow moments

**The warning made the clue.** On day 8 Sam works out what the Empress did. Last time the body was Promise himself, so there was nothing odd to find. This time her warning made Promise send his double, and the double's missing ring mark is the thread Sam has been pulling all along. The Empress thinks her letter failed.
*Set up by:* the morning letter in the Clerk's log, the call log, and her "not enough last time."
*Why it isn't contrived:* it uses only the letter rule, and every step follows from people acting on the warning.

**The unbeaten empire has lost hundreds of times.** In the vault, the Empress tells Sam why there are so few crystals left. "Every empty holder is a battle we lost. We just sent the news back." The player suddenly sees the whole galaxy's history differently.
*Set up by:* "You can't surprise the Aun," the Clerk's "nobody spends a letter on good news," and Patience's count of letters.
*Why it isn't contrived:* it's cold logic from the one rule the player knows. Letters cost something, so they're only spent on disaster.

**The loudest hawk needs peace most.** Keen learns on day 8 that her brother is alive. One word from her and the throne is hers. She stays silent. She keeps the vault, and she knows a war now would be the first one the Aun can't undo.
*Set up by:* Keen guarding the vault, losing her temper when Patience mentions the count, and her broadcasts that boast of Aun strength a little too often.
*Why it isn't contrived:* she acts on what she wants. She wants the throne of an empire that still exists.

**Two copies and a cup.** Patience finally plays Sam what she heard through the wall. Two doubles find out the other is a copy and laugh about the leaders who sent them. He raises the Truce Cup: "To the ones who get sent." She pours. Neither knows what's in the wine.
*Set up by:* Ines's humming, the left hand, and the Clinic training both doubles side by side.
*Why it isn't contrived:* both leaders used doubles for the reason all leaders do, because the eve-of-treaty drink was a risky event.

**The Chancellor sends her double.** On day 10 the Empress summons the Chancellor in person, with both fleets outside. Maren does what she has always done for dangerous meetings: she sends Ines. Standing in Maren's place before two empires, Ines says, "I'm not the Chancellor. I'm what she sends. And this is what she had me carry." Sam's copied file does the rest.
*Set up by:* Maren's habit of sending doubles to risky events, set up on day 1, and Sam showing Ines the poison report on day 9.
*Why it isn't contrived:* Maren has no reason to think Ines knows. The human system of doubles is turned on its owner by its own logic.

## 10. Other scenes people will remember

- In the first rebuild, the whole station stands in perfect detail except the garden, which is blank white. Sam stops at the edge. Patience walks in. "Then you'll have to listen with me."
- Sam asks whether the body is the prince. The Clerk pauses for the only time in the game. "I'm not allowed to answer that. I'm allowed to tell you it's the right question."
- On live news, Maren orders the Clerk shut down "pending review," looking sorry. Its last words go to Sam's earpiece: "Keep the small thing I gave you. I was neutral. I was never indifferent."
- Over the market, the Debt Clock freezes. For a second nobody cheers. They just look up.

## 11. How the player plays

The player solves the case with eyes, ears and feet.

- Until day 7, Sam can step into the Clerk's frozen 3D rebuild of any moment on the human half. He scrubs time, follows one person, freezes on a hand. Aun rooms show as white blanks.
- To fill the blanks, the player holds a button and drops into Patience's hearing. Voices behind walls become sounds to pull apart, place and replay.
- Sam tails suspects through the markets. Lose them and the lead is gone for the day.
- He tells doubles from originals by tells the player has collected: which hand, what song, how they hold a cup. The game never says which is which.
- There are zero-gravity chases down the station's spokes. In fights, Patience does the lifting while Sam times doors and cuts lights.
- Once a day, Sam puts a claim to the Empress's envoy: who, how and why, with evidence. A wrong claim brings the fleets closer. A right one buys time.

Each day is a chapter. Suspects keep schedules and Sam has only a few hours, so the player picks where to be. Dialogue stays short.

## 12. Endings

**A. The prince walks out (our pick).** In the Treaty Hall, Ines confesses in Maren's place and Sam plays the Clerk's last file. Then Promise walks in, alive, and admits the double to his mother. By Aun law he can't inherit. Keen becomes heir. She signs the peace and forgives half the debt. The Debt Clock stops.

It costs something. Promise loses his throne. Ines loses the only life she had. The Clerk stays off until a new treaty. And the Empress learns her letter worked, from the son she thought she'd lost.

**B. The lie that keeps the peace.** Sam proves Maren's guilt in private, and Promise stays "dead" to protect the treaty. It's darker, but it buries the biggest reveals.

**C. War, then a letter.** If the player fails, war starts and the Empress spends one more crystal. As a main ending it feels like a reset button, so it's the fail state.

## 13. Simplicity check

**After the first hour,** the player has seen the body, the Empress's message and the new partners. They can say: "An alien prince is murdered on a human station, and a cop and his alien bodyguard have ten days to find the killer before war." The sides are humans and the Aun, the threat is war, and we follow Sam and Patience.

**Invented terms:** the Aun, Halfway, the Hundred Worlds, the Clerk, doubles, letters. Only "Aun" is a made-up word. The optional lore adds First Spring, which the plot doesn't need.

**Risks:**
- Two doubles in one murder could confuse. We reveal them days apart, each with one clear clue: the missing ring mark, then the left hand.
- *Star Trek VI* is close. The plot must never fall back on "both sides plotted together."
- The letters carry a lot. Each new use has to follow from the one rule, stated in the first hour.
- Maren may be guessed because she's too nice. Strand and Keen must be louder suspects.
- With the Clerk gone, listening, tailing and comparing have to carry the last three days.
