# Pitch 3: The Prince

**In one line:** An alien prince is murdered on a human space station, and a human cop and the prince's alien bodyguard have ten days to find the killer before the two empires go to war.

## 1. The story in one page

At dawn, an alien guard finds the crown prince dead in his glass garden.

The garden sits on Halfway, a city-sized station on the border between humans and the Aun. The two sides fought one war twenty years ago, and the humans nearly lost it. Halfway is the only place they still meet. The prince came here to sign a real peace. He died the night before the signing.

By noon his mother, the Empress, has parked her throne-ship over the station: half a kilometre of white glass. Her message fills every screen. "Ten days." After that, her war fleet arrives.

The humans hand the case to Sam Okafor, a night-shift cop with a bad war record. The Empress sends Patience, the giant bodyguard who failed to save her son, to stay at his side. Sam lost a brother to the Aun. Patience can hear through walls, and she doesn't trust humans.

The station's AI, the Clerk, can rebuild any moment on the human half, and Sam can walk around inside the rebuild. But the garden shows up as a blank white space. By treaty, the Clerk can't see in there. To fill the gap, Sam has to listen through Patience's ears.

The clues don't fit. The dead prince has no mark from the heavy ring every royal wears from birth. The human Chancellor shared a drink with him that night and she's fine. And Patience is far too calm.

On day five, Patience takes Sam to a hydroponics bay. A tall figure in a dock worker's coat turns around.

"You must be Sam," says Prince Promise. "I'm told I'm dead."

The body was a copy. Someone meant to kill the real prince and still thinks they did. If Promise steps out, the war stops, but his people will never forgive him for the copy, and he loses the throne. If he stays hidden, the fleet arrives.

And the Empress keeps saying something nobody understands: "Ten days was not enough last time."

## 2. The sides

Five sides: two human, two alien, and the station in the middle.

**The Hundred Worlds** are a loose union of human planets: loud, crowded and broke. Every human still pays a war debt to the Aun, and a giant Debt Clock over Halfway's market ticks the total up. Humans also use **doubles**, grown copies of leaders who stand in at risky events. To them it's as normal as a seatbelt. Their leader, **Chancellor Maren Hale**, is warm and funny, the human face of the peace. In secret, she thinks the peace means paying the Aun forever.

**The Human Fleet** sits outside Halfway in grey warships that creep closer each day. **Admiral Halvard Strand** attacks the Aun on the news every night, so he's the first suspect. In private, he lost half his ships last time and never wants to fight again.

**The Aun Empire** is old and rich. The Aun live about three hundred years, stand two and a half metres tall and move slowly. Faint lines of light run under their pale grey skin, like a lamp behind porcelain. Their names translate into plain words, a spoken promise is close to law, and they can send short letters back in time. Human soldiers say, "You can't surprise the Aun." **Empress Long Winter**, three metres tall, ran out of patience with humans in the first war. Her son grew up loving them.

**The Aun Court** is nobles who quote ancient law. Its loudest voice is **Princess Keen**, Promise's younger sister, a hawk on every broadcast. With Promise dead, she inherits, so she's the first Aun suspect.

**Halfway Station** is a ring around a gas giant's moon: markets and docks on the human half, quiet glass gardens on the Aun half. **The Clerk**, an AI built under the truce, runs it for both sides. It sees everything on the human half and nothing inside Aun rooms. It wants the peace to hold, and it keeps one secret for that reason.

## 3. Who we follow

**Sam Okafor** is the player: late forties, ex-army, stuck on night shifts. He watches people's hands and makes jokes when nervous. He wants to keep Halfway, his home, out of another war. He still flinches around the Aun, because his brother died fighting them. In the war he let an Aun prisoner go, and the army threw him out for it. He never learned her name. He doesn't know why he got this case. The Chancellor picked him because she expects him to fail.

**Patience** is the prince's bodyguard and Sam's partner. She is huge, polite and deadly. The Empress orders her to guard Sam and report everything, but her loyalty is to Promise. She hides three things, which come out one at a time:
- The dead prince was a double, and she helped the real one hide.
- She was the prisoner Sam let go. That's why she accepted him.
- She heard the whole conversation in the garden that night, through the wall.

**Prince Promise** appears on day five. He wanted to forgive half the human debt. Two weeks before the signing, he got a letter from the future, sealed by his mother: "Do not be alone in your garden on the night before the signing." So he paid a human clinic to grow a double and sent it in his place. To the Aun, copying royal blood is a disgrace. If it comes out, he loses the throne.

**Ines** has been Maren Hale's double for twelve years. Doubles get numbers; she picked her own name. On the murder night she was the "Chancellor" who poured the prince a gift of wine. She didn't know Maren had poisoned it. She knew the prince's double too: the clinic had trained them side by side.

**The Clerk** talks in exact sentences, with a dry wit. It scanned the body at dawn and knew within a minute it was a copy. It has told no one, because the truth could end the peace as fast as the lie.

**Empress Long Winter** has lived these ten days before. That time her son died, the case went unsolved, and war began. On the last day she sent a letter back to warn him. That's why the body is a double.

## 4. The two rules, and what each pillar does

**Time:** The Aun can send a short letter back in time, up to ten days, but each letter burns up a crystal they can't replace, and only a few are left.

**Cloning:** Humans can grow an adult double of a person in about a month, with the body but none of the memories.

Each pillar has one job. The **aliens** give us the other side, the victim, the partner and the deadline. **Time travel** explains why the Aun are feared, why the prince survived and why the deadline is ten days. **Cloning** is the murder's trick: the victim was a copy, and so was the visitor. The **AI** is the player's main tool, a witness with a conscience, and the reason the first war ended.

## 5. Backstory (the iceberg)

The player needs only the tip. Below sits a long history. Parts marked *main* hold up the plot. Parts marked *optional* reward players who dig, and the story works without them.

**3,000 years ago: the dying star** *(optional)*. The letter crystals grew in one dying star near the Aun home. Strike a crystal the right way and it carries a few words up to ten days into its past. It went dark 1,200 years ago, so every letter now comes from a shrinking vault.

**1,900 years ago: the betrayal at the table** *(optional)*. The Aun were once rival houses at war. They met at a great table to make peace. One leader, First Spring, read a letter from ten days ahead and ambushed the others at the table. Her family still rules, and the official history blames the other houses.

That buried fact explains why the Aun treat promises as close to law, why their nobles assume any treaty murder is a betrayal, and why only the royal family may hold letters. The murder on Halfway repeats it: a peace broken at a table by someone who thought she could see ahead.

**60 years ago: the debt** *(main)*. Humans, spread over a hundred worlds since leaving Earth, met the Aun and borrowed from their banks. Their trades kept going wrong. *(Optional: the royal family sold letters to the banks, which used them to see markets ten days ahead. Maren has proof in a captured ledger.)*

**25 years ago: the war** *(main)*. Humans stopped paying. The Aun won almost every battle, because they knew where human fleets would be. Their assassins killed human leaders the same way. So humans started using doubles. A letter can tell you where the Chancellor will be. It can't tell you which Chancellor.

**20 years ago: the truce** *(main: it happened; optional: why)*. The humans' war AI learned to pick up the faint signal a crystal gives off when a letter lands. Suddenly the Aun could be surprised, so the Empress offered a truce. Her price was the AI. It was rebuilt as a neutral keeper that may never serve a fleet again. That AI is the Clerk, and it noticed a letter land two weeks before the murder.

### Places that hold history
- The **Debt Clock** over the market. In the best ending, it stops.
- The **Old Hull**, the wreck where the truce was signed, sealed in the station's core. *(optional)*
- The **Glass Garden**, the murder scene.
- The **Truce Cup**. By custom, the two leaders share a drink from it the night before any treaty.
- The **Clinic**, where doubles are grown and taught. Both doubles came from here.
- The **Vault of Letters** on the throne-ship: rows of empty crystal holders and a few full ones. *(optional)*
- The **First Table** from the betrayal, which Keen shows Sam late in the game. *(optional)*

### How the player finds it
Days one to three show only the surface. In days four to six the rules turn into facts: Promise shows Sam the letter, and the Clinic's files open. From day seven the Clerk's story and the Old Hull open up. The archive, the ledger and the Vault are side paths.

## 6. What to borrow, and what to avoid

We borrow techniques only:
- From *Alien Nation* and Asimov's *The Caves of Steel*: hostile partners who each need the other's skills. Sam reads people and footage; Patience hears through walls and opens Aun doors.
- From *The Day of the Jackal*: a visible countdown. Each day opens on the day number, the Debt Clock and the fleets' distance.
- From *Kagemusha*: a political double who earns his own dignity. Ours dies on night one and leaves a notebook.
- From *Game of Thrones*: threads built apart, then crashed together.
- From *Return of the Obra Dinn* and *Majora's Mask*: walkable frozen scenes, and a fixed number of days with people on schedules.

*Star Trek VI* is the nearest lookalike: a peace-minded alien leader is killed before talks, and hawks on both sides did it together. Here the loud hawks are innocent, the killer is the human peacemaker working through her double, and the victim survived. *Babylon 5* and *Mass Effect* share the alien-station setting, but we have two powers, one case and one clock.

## 7. Threads

We tried fourteen candidate threads and kept six:

1. **The case.** Sam and Patience go from enemies to partners. He lost a brother to the Aun; she has to trust a human with her prince.
2. **The hidden prince.** The victim is alive, but saying so destroys him.
3. **The double.** Ines goes from a face in the background to the most important witness in two empires. "I was always the one sent to get shot at. This time they sent me to shoot."
4. **The sister.** Keen doesn't kill her brother. But once she learns he's alive and made a copy, she can take the throne by telling the truth. Her choice decides the ending.
5. **The Chancellor's game.** Maren is kind, helpful and always a step ahead.
6. **The Clerk's conscience.** The AI hid one fact to save the peace. Then human law orders it to erase evidence, and it must decide what being neutral means.

The admiral and the market panic fold into these as background. We cut a leaking journalist, a false confession and an Aun general: each added a face but no new question.

**Where the threads collide:**
- Day 3: Maren gives Sam full access to the Clerk, and points him at the Admiral.
- Day 4: Sam asks the Clerk, "Is the body the prince?" It refuses to answer. The refusal is the clue.
- Day 5: Patience, cornered by evidence, takes Sam to Promise. The partnership has to start over.
- Day 7: Maren orders the Clerk to wipe the Clinic records. It obeys. That night she has it switched off.
- Day 8: Keen finds out Promise is alive.
- Day 9: Ines, hunted by Maren's guards, comes to Sam.
- Day 10: Everyone meets in the Treaty Hall, before the Empress, with both fleets outside.

## 8. Twists

We tried eleven and kept five. Rejected: Patience as killer (no motive), Keen (the first guess), hawks on both sides (*Star Trek VI*), the AI as killer (a tired trope), Promise faking his death (makes him a coward) and Sam as a clone (confusing for no gain).

**1. The body is a double (end of day 4).** The question flips from "who killed him?" to "he's alive, so why is he hiding?"
Planted by:
- Patience stays calm at the body while the other guards grieve.
- The Clerk turns down a full medical scan, its first refusal ever.
- The prince's notebook says "Stand straighter. He laughs with his eyes shut." It's a copy learning to be Promise.
- The hand has no ring mark.

**2. The visiting Chancellor was Ines (days 6 and 7).** The human defence against assassins becomes the murder weapon.
Planted by:
- The Chancellor drank the same wine and is fine, so everyone rules the wine out. A day-4 Clinic report shows the poison only harms the Aun.
- In the corridor rebuild, the "Chancellor" carries the bottle in her left hand. At the day-one gala, Maren signs autographs with her right.
- Maren boasts that the treaty bans doubles at the signing: "No tricks. Just me."
- Ines hums an old song while she thinks. Patience heard the same tune through the garden wall.

**3. Two people chose Sam for opposite reasons (days 5 and 6).** Maren chose him to fail. Patience accepted him because he once freed her. Planted when Sam's boss can't see why the Chancellor named a night-shift cop, and when Patience once calls Sam "the guard," then corrects herself.

**4. The Clerk is switched off (end of day 7).** The player loses their best tool and friendliest voice, and Maren does it legally.
Planted by:
- On day 1 the Clerk says it obeys "lawful human authority on human matters, and lawful Aun authority on Aun matters."
- Maren jokes on day 4: "If it ever stops being neutral, there's a switch."
- On day 6 the Clerk asks whether its evidence would count if it were switched off. Sam says yes, if a person carries it. It hands him one copied file, the corridor footage, which later wins the case.

**5. These ten days have happened before (day 8).** This explains the double, the deadline and the Empress's grief.
Planted by:
- The Empress's first message: "Ten days was not enough last time." Everyone thinks she means the old war.
- The Clerk's log shows a letter landing on the prince's ship two weeks before the murder.
- Promise shows Sam the letter, sealed in his mother's hand.
- Patience mentions that the Empress has "fewer letters than fingers left."

## 9. Scenes people will remember

1. The throne-ship drops out of nowhere over Halfway and fills every screen with two words: "Ten days."
2. In the first rebuild, the whole station stands in perfect detail except the garden, which is blank white. Sam stops at the edge. Patience walks in. "Then you'll have to listen with me."
3. Sam asks whether the body is the prince. The Clerk pauses for the only time in the game. "I'm not allowed to answer that. I'm allowed to tell you it's the right question."
4. A hydroponics bay, a dock worker's coat, and the prince turning round: "I'm told I'm dead."
5. Ines and Patience piece together the murder night. Two doubles find out the other is a copy and laugh about the leaders who sent them. He raises the Truce Cup. "To the ones who get sent." She pours. Neither knows what's in the wine.
6. On live news, Maren orders the Clerk shut down "pending review," looking sorry. Its last words go to Sam's earpiece: "Keep the small thing I gave you. I was neutral. I was never indifferent."
7. The Empress shows Sam the empty crystal holders. "Last time I wrote to my son. This time I have three left, and an empire."
8. Over the market, the Debt Clock freezes. For a second nobody cheers. They just look up.

## 10. How the player plays

The player solves the case with eyes, ears and feet.

- Until day 7, Sam can step into the Clerk's frozen 3D rebuild of any moment on the human half. He scrubs time, follows one person, freezes on a hand. Aun rooms show as white blanks.
- To fill the blanks, the player holds a button and drops into Patience's hearing. Voices behind walls become sounds to pull apart, place and replay.
- Sam tails suspects through the markets. Lose them and the lead is gone for the day.
- He tells doubles from originals by tells the player has collected: which hand, what song, how they hold a cup. The game never says which is which.
- There are zero-gravity chases down the station's spokes. In fights, Patience does the lifting while Sam times doors and cuts lights.
- Once a day, Sam puts a claim to the Empress's envoy: who, how and why, with evidence. A wrong claim brings the fleets closer. A right one buys time.

Each day is a chapter. Suspects keep schedules and Sam has only a few hours, so the player picks where to be. Dialogue stays short.

## 11. Endings

**A. The prince walks out (our pick).** In the Treaty Hall, Sam lays out the case with the Clerk's last file and Ines's testimony. Then Promise walks in, alive, and admits the double. By Aun law he can't inherit. Keen, who could have exposed him on day 8 and didn't, becomes heir. She signs the peace and forgives half the debt. The Debt Clock stops.

It costs something. Promise loses his throne, Ines loses the only life she had, and the Clerk stays off until a new treaty. Every thread ends in one room, and the red herring becomes the ruler.

**B. The lie that keeps the peace.** Sam proves Maren's guilt in private, and Promise stays "dead" to protect the treaty. It's darker, but it buries the biggest reveal.

**C. War, then a letter.** If the player fails, war starts on day 10. The Empress spends one of her last letters on Sam, and a new day one begins. As a main ending this feels like a reset button, so we use it as the fail state.

## 12. Simplicity check

**After the first hour,** the player has seen the body, the Empress's message and the new partners. They can say: "An alien prince is murdered on a human station, and a cop and his alien bodyguard have ten days to find the killer before war." The sides are humans and the Aun, the threat is war, and we follow Sam and Patience.

**Invented terms:** the Aun, Halfway, the Hundred Worlds, the Clerk, doubles, letters. Only "Aun" is a made-up word. The optional lore adds First Spring, which the plot doesn't need.

**Risks:**
- Two doubles in one murder could confuse. We reveal them four days apart, each with one clear clue: the missing ring mark, then the left hand.
- *Star Trek VI* is close. The plot must never fall back on "both sides plotted together."
- The time-travel reveal could feel bolted on unless it's planted in minute one and answers a question the player already has: why was there a double at all?
- Maren may be guessed because she's too nice. Strand and Keen need to be louder suspects with real secrets.
- With the Clerk gone, listening, tailing and comparing have to carry the last three days.
- A detective with an alien partner is a familiar shape. That keeps it simple, so the surprise has to come from the twists.
