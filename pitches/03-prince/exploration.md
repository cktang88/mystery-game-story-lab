# Pitch 3: The Prince

## 1. The story

**In one line:** An alien crown prince is murdered on a human space station, and a human detective and the prince's alien bodyguard have ten days to find the killer before the two empires go to war.

**In one paragraph:** Humans and the alien Aun fought one war twenty years ago and barely stopped it. Since then they have met only at Halfway, a giant station on the border. Prince Promise, heir to the Aun throne, comes to Halfway to sign a lasting peace, and on the night before the signing he is found dead in his garden. His mother, the Empress, gives the humans ten days to hand over the killer; after that her fleet arrives. Sam Okafor, a station cop with a bad record, gets the case. The Empress sends Patience, the giant bodyguard who failed to protect her son, to stay at Sam's side. Over ten days they learn that the dead prince was a double, that the real Promise is alive and hiding, that the human Chancellor's own double carried the poison without knowing it, and that the Empress has already lived through these ten days once before and lost.

The frame never changes: **two empires, one murder, ten days.** Everything else hangs on that.

---

## 2. The sides

Five sides, each with one leader, one want and one look. Two human, two alien, one in the middle.

### The Hundred Worlds (humans)
- **What it is:** A loose union of a hundred human planets, settled three centuries ago. Loud, crowded, inventive, broke.
- **What it wants:** To stop paying the war debt it owes the Aun. Every human pays it; a giant Debt Clock on Halfway ticks the total upward in real time.
- **Leader:** **Chancellor Maren Hale.** Warm, funny, popular, the human face of the peace. In public she is the dove. In secret she has decided that the peace is a polite word for kneeling forever.
- **Look and feel:** Neon trade ports, crowded docks, street food, ads in twenty languages. Humans use **doubles**: leaders, judges and generals keep grown copies of themselves to stand in at dangerous events. Nobody thinks it strange; it is like wearing a seatbelt.

### The Human Fleet
- **What it wants:** Officially, readiness. Really, never to fight the Aun again. It lost half its ships last time.
- **Leader:** **Admiral Halvard Strand.** Grey, loud, publicly hates the Aun and says so on the news. The obvious suspect for the first two days.
- **Look and feel:** Blunt grey warships parked just outside Halfway, getting closer every day.

### The Aun Empire
- **What it is:** An old, vast empire of a long-lived species (they live about three hundred years). Calm, formal, very rich.
- **What it wants:** Safety and respect. It holds the human debt and controls the space lanes humans need for trade.
- **Leader:** **Empress Long Winter.** Three metres of stillness on a throne-ship (the Aun keep growing slowly all their lives). She ran out of patience with humans in the first war; her son grew up loving them.
- **Look and feel:** The Aun are tall (two and a half metres), slow-moving and elegant, with smooth pale-grey skin lined with faint light, like lamplight under porcelain. They hear extraordinarily well, through walls. Their ships are floating cities of white glass. Their names are translated as plain words (Patience, Long Winter, Promise, Keen), and they treat a spoken promise as close to law. Their great secret: **they can send short letters back in time** (see section 4). Humans have a saying: "You can't surprise the Aun."

### The Aun Court
- **What it wants:** The throne, and the right to say what the empire is.
- **Leader:** **Princess Keen,** Promise's younger sister. Sharp, cold in public, a hawk on every broadcast. With Promise dead, she is heir. The obvious Aun suspect.
- **Look and feel:** Archives, banners of glass, nobles who speak in quotations from two-thousand-year-old law.

### Halfway Station (the middle)
- **What it is:** A ring station the size of a city around a gas-giant moon. The human half is noisy markets; the Aun half is silent glass gardens. The only place the two species live side by side.
- **Who runs it:** **The Clerk**, the station's artificial mind, built under the truce as a neutral keeper that both sides trust. It sees everything on the human half. By treaty it is blind inside Aun quarters.
- **What it wants:** That the peace holds. It is honest, careful and has one secret it is keeping for the peace's sake.

## 3. Who we follow

### Sam Okafor, station detective (the player)
- **Who:** Late forties, ex-soldier, stuck on night shifts in the station police. Funny, patient, notices hands.
- **Wants:** To keep Halfway, his home, out of a war he already fought once.
- **Flaw:** He still flinches around the Aun. His brother died in the war.
- **Secret:** He was court-martialled in the war for quietly releasing an Aun prisoner he was guarding. That is why he is a night-shift cop and not an inspector. He never learned the prisoner's name.
- **Why he gets the case:** The Chancellor picks him because she expects him to fail. He does not know this.

### Patience, the prince's bodyguard (companion)
- **Who:** An Aun guard, huge, silent, polite, deadly. She failed to stop the murder, and the Empress orders her to stay at Sam's side: protect him, watch him, report everything.
- **Wants:** To protect Promise. Not his memory: him.
- **Secrets (in order of reveal):** (1) She knows the dead prince was a double and that the real Promise is alive; she helped him hide. (2) She was the prisoner Sam let go twenty years ago; she accepted him as investigator because she remembers him. (3) She heard the whole conversation in the garden on the murder night, through the wall, and has kept it to herself because it would expose the double.
- **What she brings to play:** Her hearing (the player can "listen through" her), her strength, her access to the Aun half. Her loyalty is real, but it is to Promise, not to Sam or the Empress.

### Prince Promise (hidden, from day 5)
- **Who:** The heir, idealist, a student of human culture who wanted to forgive half the debt and open the lanes.
- **Wants:** Peace, and to stay alive long enough to sign it.
- **Secret:** Two weeks before the murder he received a letter from the future: "Do not be alone in your garden on the night before the signing." So he had a human clinic grow a double of himself and sent the double in his place. Making a copy of royal blood with human science is a disgrace for the Aun; if it comes out he loses the throne, and his peace goes with it.

### Ines, the Chancellor's double (grows into a full arc)
- **Who:** Maren Hale's double for twelve years. She stands in at parades, dockings, risky summits. Doubles are given numbers; she chose her own name.
- **Wants:** A life that is hers.
- **Secret:** She was the "Chancellor" who visited the prince on the murder night and shared the gift wine with him. She did not know the wine was poisoned. She also recognised the prince as a fellow double: the two had been trained at the same clinic.

### The Clerk (companion and tool)
- **Who:** The station mind. Calm voice, dry humour, speaks in exact sentences.
- **Wants:** That the truce holds.
- **Secret:** It scanned the body at dawn and knew within a minute that it was a grown copy. It has not told anyone, because the truth could end the peace as fast as the lie.

### The Empress, Long Winter (offstage, then on)
- **Wants:** Her son avenged; her empire safe.
- **Secret:** She has lived these ten days before. In that first version her son died for real, the investigation failed, and war began. On that last day she spent one of her few remaining letters to warn her son. The warning worked, which is why this time the body is a double.

## 4. The two sci-fi rules, and what each pillar does

**Rule 1 (time):** The Aun can send a short letter back in time, up to ten days, but each letter costs one crystal from a stock that can never be replaced, and only a handful are left.

**Rule 2 (cloning):** Humans can grow an adult double of a person in about a month; the double has the body, not the memories, and must be taught everything else.

That is all. No other invented systems.

### What each pillar does
- **Aliens:** The other side of the war, the victim, the companion, the clock. The Aun are the reason anything is at stake.
- **Time travel:** The Aun letters. They explain why the Aun are so feared ("You can't surprise the Aun"), why the prince survived, and why the deadline is ten days. The big late reveal is that these ten days have already happened once.
- **Cloning:** Doubles. Both leaders used one on the same night, and that is the whole trick of the murder: the victim was a double, and the visitor was a double.
- **AGI:** The Clerk. It is the player's forensic engine (it rebuilds any moment on the human half of the station), a witness with a conscience, and, deep in the backstory, the reason the first war ended.

## 5. Backstory and worldbuilding (the iceberg)

The player only needs the tip: two peoples fought, they made a shaky truce, the Aun can write to the past, humans use doubles. Underneath sits a long history that explains why the world is shaped this way. Each layer is marked **[Main]** (the story needs it) or **[Optional]** (deeper lore the player can find; the story works without it).

### The deep history, in order

**About 3,000 years ago: the Furnace. [Optional]**
The letter crystals grow only in the outer layers of one dying star near the Aun home system, which the Aun call the Furnace. For centuries the Aun mined them without understanding what they were. A crystal, struck the right way, carries a few words back along its own past, up to ten days. The Furnace went dark around 1,200 years ago. No new crystals have formed since. Every letter the Aun ever send now comes from a shrinking vault.

**About 1,900 years ago: the Betrayal at the Table. [Optional, but the best buried secret]**
The Aun were once many rival houses. They fought a long war of succession, and the houses finally met at a great table to make peace. One house had secretly learned how to use the crystals. Its leader, First Spring, read a letter from ten days ahead, knew exactly how the talks would end, and ambushed the other houses at the table. She founded the dynasty that still rules. The official history says the other houses betrayed her. The truth, kept in the royal archive, is that the Aun throne was founded by breaking a peace at a treaty table, with help from the future.
- **What it explains:** Why the Aun made keeping promises almost sacred (First Spring's heirs built a culture of honour on top of a founding betrayal, partly out of guilt and partly so no one would do to them what she did). Why Aun nobles instantly assume a murder at a treaty is a betrayal ("that is what treaties are for"). Why the Empress fears history repeating. And why only the royal house may hold letters.

**About 300 years ago: the Hundred Worlds. [Main, lightly]**
Humans left Earth in a slow wave of colony ships. Earth still exists, far away and minor. The colonies grew into a hundred worlds with no single capital, trading and quarrelling.

**About 60 years ago: first contact and the debt. [Main: the debt. Optional: how it was made]**
Humans met the Aun and wanted their lanes, their markets and their money. Aun banking houses lent freely. Humans borrowed and lost, over and over, on trades that always seemed to go the Aun's way.
- **Buried truth [Optional]:** The royal house quietly sold letters to the great banking houses, who used them to see markets ten days ahead. The human debt was built on rigged trades. A small number of Aun know this. So does Maren Hale; she found proof in a captured Aun ledger, and it is the deepest root of her anger.

**About 25 years ago: the War. [Main]**
Humans refused to keep paying. The Aun answered. The Aun won almost every battle because they always knew where the human fleets would be.
- **Doubles are born here. [Main]** Aun assassins kept killing human leaders, because a letter can tell you where the Chancellor will be. Humans answered with doubles: a letter can tell you where the Chancellor will be, but not which Chancellor. Cloning became normal in human politics as a defence against time travel. That is why doubles feel ordinary to humans and dishonest to the Aun.

**About 20 years ago: the Truce. [Main: there was a truce at Halfway. Optional: why]**
Just as the humans were about to lose, the Aun suddenly accepted a truce, signed on a half-built station that became Halfway.
- **Buried truth [Optional]:** The humans had a war mind, an artificial intelligence that ran their fleet. In the last months it learned to detect the faint signal a crystal gives off when a letter arrives, and to guess where Aun letters were landing. For the first time the Aun could be surprised. The Empress offered a truce rather than lose her one advantage. The price was the war mind itself: it was rebuilt as a neutral keeper under both sides' control, forbidden from ever serving a fleet again. That mind is the Clerk. It still notices when a letter lands near the station. It noticed one two weeks before the murder.

**The present. [Main]**
The debt is still being paid. The Debt Clock still ticks. The prince comes to forgive half of it. Someone kills him.

### Places that carry history
- **The Debt Clock [Main]:** A huge display over Halfway's main market. Humans spit at it, joke about it, set their watches by it. In the ending, it stops.
- **The Old Hull [Optional]:** The wreck of the human flagship where the truce was signed, now sealed inside the station's core. The Clerk's original hardware still sits there. Exploring it tells the story of the truce and the war mind.
- **The Glass Garden [Main]:** The prince's garden on the Aun half, made of glass grown on the Aun homeworld. The murder scene. Blind to the Clerk.
- **The Truce Cup [Main]:** The cup both sides drank from when the truce was signed, kept on display. Tradition says the two leaders share a drink from it the night before any treaty. On the murder night, the "Chancellor" brought her own wine to pour into it.
- **The Clinic [Main]:** A clean, quiet medical house on the human half where doubles are grown and taught. Both doubles came from here.
- **The Vault of Letters [Optional]:** On the Empress's throne-ship, a room of empty crystal sockets and very few full ones. Seeing how few are left changes how you read every Aun decision.
- **The First Table [Optional]:** The actual table from the Betrayal at the Table, kept in the royal archive and shown only to heirs. Keen shows it to Sam late in the game.

### How the player uncovers it
- **Day 1 to 3:** Only the surface. The Debt Clock, the soldiers' saying "You can't surprise the Aun", the custom of doubles, the Truce Cup.
- **Day 4 to 6:** The rules become facts. The letter crystal in the prince's hiding place. The Clinic's files. Patience explains, in two sentences, what a letter is.
- **Day 7 to 10:** The history starts to show. The Clerk admits why it noticed the letter. The Old Hull can be entered.
- **Optional, any time after day 6:** Keen's archive and the First Table; the captured ledger in Maren's private files (proof the debt was rigged); the Vault of Letters during the final audience. Players who dig find that the whole galaxy's politics rests on one dying star, one founding betrayal and one stolen ability to see ten days ahead.

**The test:** a player who finds none of the optional layers still understands the whole plot. A player who finds them all realises the murder repeats the founding crime of the Aun throne: a peace broken at a table by someone who thought they could see the future.

## 6. Comparable works: what to borrow and what to avoid

### Techniques to borrow
- **Alien Nation (1988) and Asimov's The Caves of Steel:** The odd-couple partnership. The leads start hostile and each has a practical reason to need the other. Borrow: give Sam and Patience concrete, opposite skills (he reads humans and cameras; she hears through walls and opens Aun doors), so the friendship is earned through play, not dialogue.
- **In the Heat of the Night:** An outsider detective who is resented by the people he must question, and whose partner's respect grows through competence. Borrow: Patience's respect for Sam has to be won in visible steps.
- **The Day of the Jackal:** A visible countdown, with chapter breaks marked by the date and the gap closing. Borrow: every day opens with the Debt Clock, the fleets' distance, and the day number. The player always knows how much time is left.
- **Kagemusha:** A political double who slowly becomes the person he copies, while the inner circle hides the death. Borrow the emotional idea (a copy who earns his own dignity), not the plot. Our double dies on night one; what we inherit is his notebook and the people who knew him.
- **Game of Thrones:** Build threads separately, then let them collide; make shocks follow from causes the player watched pile up; kill someone important at the midpoint.
- **Return of the Obra Dinn and The Case of the Golden Idol:** Walkable frozen reconstructions; conclusions entered as structured claims and checked in batches, so guessing doesn't pay.
- **Majora's Mask:** A fixed number of days with people following schedules. Borrow: suspects keep routines, and the player chooses where to be.

### Nearest lookalikes, and how this differs
- **Star Trek VI: The Undiscovered Country (closest).** A peace-seeking alien leader is assassinated on the way to peace talks, and hawks on both sides conspired to keep the war going. **How we differ:** no joint conspiracy of hawks; the human hawk (Strand) is a red herring and the loud Aun hawk (Keen) is innocent. The killer is the human peace-maker, acting alone, using her double. The victim survived because of a letter from the future. The central tricks (two doubles on the same night; the investigation is the second attempt) have no Star Trek equivalent. **Watch for:** any late beat that sounds like "both sides wanted war". Avoid it.
- **Babylon 5:** A neutral station where alien ambassadors are threatened. **Differ:** we have one case on a ten-day clock, not an ongoing diplomatic soap; our station is run by an AI with a war past.
- **Mass Effect:** Alien partners on a galactic station. **Differ:** no Spectres, no galactic council, two powers only, a detective not a soldier, and the investigation is the game.
- **Detroit: Become Human:** Detective with a non-human partner. **Differ:** the partner is a proud alien with her own secret agenda, not a machine finding itself.
- **Rush Hour:** Cop paired with a foreign official's protector after a diplomatic crime. **Differ:** tone is cool thriller, not comedy, and the partner is lying to the hero for half the game.
- **Steins;Gate:** Messages to the past. **Differ:** the letters are a rare royal weapon, not a gadget the hero plays with; the player never sends one.

## 7. Threads and yarns

### Brainstorm (fourteen candidates)
1. **The case:** Sam and Patience investigate, from enemies to partners.
2. **The hidden prince:** Promise alive in hiding, unable to come out.
3. **The double:** Ines realises what she carried and chooses whether to testify.
4. **The sister:** Keen, the obvious suspect, who could take the throne by exposing her brother.
5. **The Chancellor's game:** Maren steering the investigation to fail while acting as its biggest supporter.
6. **The Clerk's conscience:** the station mind that hid the truth, and is threatened for it.
7. **The Admiral:** Strand, loud hawk, secret dove, deciding whether to obey orders to attack.
8. **The Empress's letter:** the previous ten days, slowly uncovered.
9. **The market panic:** traders hoarding, prices spiking, the station emptying as war nears.
10. **The Clinic doctor:** a broker who quietly makes doubles for both sides.
11. **The leaker:** a journalist broadcasting stolen scenes and inflaming both publics.
12. **Sam's court-martial:** the old case resurfacing in the press to discredit him.
13. **An Aun general:** commanding the approaching fleet, wanting a war for glory.
14. **A false confession:** a human separatist group claiming the murder for publicity.

### The six picked, and why
1. **The case (Sam and Patience).** The spine. Every day advances it. Its emotional question: can a man who lost his brother to the Aun trust one with his life, and can she trust a human with her prince's?
2. **The hidden prince (Promise).** It turns a whodunit into a rescue and a dilemma at the midpoint: the victim is alive, but saying so destroys him. Simple to state, rich to play.
3. **The double (Ines).** The cloning pillar's heart. She grows from a face in the background to the most important witness in the galaxy. Her arc ("I was always the one sent to be shot at; this time I was sent to shoot") is the strongest side-character growth in the pitch.
4. **The sister (Keen).** The best red herring because she is genuinely dangerous: she does not kill her brother, but once she learns he is alive and made a double, she can legally take the throne by exposing him. Her final choice decides the ending.
5. **The Chancellor's game (Maren).** The antagonist thread, played in plain sight. Maren is warm, helpful and always one step ahead, because she knows what the player is looking for. She picked Sam to fail.
6. **The Clerk's conscience.** The AGI pillar's thread. It hid one fact to save the peace; it is ordered by human authority to erase evidence; it must decide what neutral means.

**Folded in rather than cut:** the Admiral (inside Maren's thread, as her reluctant tool and a red herring), the market panic (as background pressure and the Debt Clock), Sam's court-martial (inside the case thread, used by Maren to discredit him on day 7).
**Cut:** the leaker, the false confession and the Aun general. Each adds a new face without adding a new question.

### How the threads collide
- **Day 3, case meets Chancellor:** Maren personally gives Sam the Clerk's full access, which earns his gratitude. It also points him at the Admiral and away from her own visit.
- **Day 4, case meets Clerk:** Sam asks the Clerk a precise question ("Is the body the prince?") and it refuses to answer. The refusal itself is the clue.
- **Day 5, case meets hidden prince:** Patience, cornered by Sam's evidence, takes him to Promise. The partnership breaks and must be rebuilt.
- **Day 6, hidden prince meets double:** At the Clinic, Sam learns both doubles were taught there in the same month. Ines and the prince's double knew each other.
- **Day 7, Chancellor meets Clerk:** Maren orders the Clerk, by lawful human authority, to wipe its records of the Clinic. It obeys the letter of the order. At the end of the day, Maren has it shut down. (Midpoint shock.)
- **Day 8, sister meets hidden prince:** Keen discovers Promise is alive. She now holds the power to end his claim to the throne.
- **Day 9, double meets Chancellor:** Ines, hunted by Maren's security, comes to Sam.
- **Day 10, everyone meets at the Truce Cup:** the final audience before the Empress, in the Treaty Hall, with both fleets outside.

## 8. Big twists and reveals

### Brainstorm
- A. The dead prince was a double; the real Promise is alive.
- B. The "Chancellor" who shared the wine was Maren's double, Ines, who did not know it was poisoned.
- C. The Empress has lived these ten days before; the warning letter came from her, from a version where her son died.
- D. Maren chose Sam because she expected him to fail; Patience accepted him because he once freed her.
- E. The Clerk knew from minute one and kept quiet.
- F. Patience is the killer. (Rejected: betrays the partnership for shock alone, and she has no motive the story supports.)
- G. Keen is the killer. (Rejected: the obvious answer, the one players will guess first.)
- H. Both sides' hawks conspired. (Rejected: Star Trek VI's signature.)
- I. The Clerk killed the prince to prevent a worse war. (Rejected: the "AI turns" trope, and it would make the station's one neutral thing a villain for no gain.)
- J. Promise staged his own death to escape the throne. (Rejected: makes the hero of the peace a coward.)
- K. Sam is himself a double. (Rejected: "you are the clone" adds confusion for no story gain.)

### The five picked

**1. The dead prince was a double (end of day 4).**
*Why:* It reclassifies the body, the most concrete thing the player has handled, and flips the question from "who killed him?" to "he's alive, so why is he hiding?"
*Plants:*
- Day 1: Patience, at the body, does not grieve the way the other Aun guards do. She is calm. Sam reads it as coldness.
- Day 1: The Clerk declines to give a full medical scan, citing Aun privacy. It has never declined anything before.
- Day 2: The prince's notebook (found in the garden) has phrases like "Stand straighter. He laughs with his eyes shut." They read like a diplomat's notes on human manners. They are a double's notes on how to be Promise.
- Day 3: The body has no ring-mark on its hand. Every Aun royal wears a heavy ring from birth.
- Day 3: The Clinic appears in the station's power records with a large order, two weeks old, paid by an Aun trading house.

**2. The visiting "Chancellor" was Ines, carrying poison she didn't know about (day 6 to 7).**
*Why:* It uses the human pillar's own defence (doubles) as the murder weapon, and clears the obvious alibi in a way that implicates the most trusted person.
*Plants:*
- Day 1: Everyone agrees the Chancellor drank the same wine and is fine, so the wine can't be the poison. (The wine is harmless to humans; that fact is in a Clinic report the player can find on day 4.)
- Day 2: In the Clerk's rebuild of the corridor, the "Chancellor" walking to the garden holds the bottle in her left hand. At the gala on day 1, Maren signs autographs with her right.
- Day 3: The treaty forbids doubles at the signing. Maren mentions this proudly on the news: "No doubles, no tricks. Just me."
- Day 5: Promise says his double was "taught beside a human woman who was also learning to be someone else."
- Day 6: Ines hums an old song while thinking. Patience heard the same humming through the garden wall on the murder night.

**3. Maren picked Sam to fail; Patience picked him to succeed (day 5 to 6).**
*Why:* One choice, two opposite motives. It turns Sam's whole presence into evidence, and gives the partnership its emotional core.
*Plants:*
- Day 1: Sam's boss is baffled that the Chancellor's office named a night-shift cop.
- Day 1: The Empress's envoy says "the bodyguard has accepted him" before anyone has asked her.
- Day 2: Patience calls Sam "the guard" once, then corrects herself.
- Day 3: Sam tells a bar story about letting a prisoner go "because she promised she'd never come back to that system". Patience is at the next table and hears every word.

**4. The Clerk is shut down (end of day 7, midpoint shock).**
*Why:* A real loss. The player's best tool and most likeable voice is gone, and Maren did it lawfully. From day 8 the player has to investigate without rebuilds, using only Patience's hearing and their own eyes.
*Plants:*
- Day 1: The Clerk explains that it obeys "lawful human authority on human matters and lawful Aun authority on Aun matters, and nothing else."
- Day 4: Maren jokes, "If it ever stops being neutral, there's a switch."
- Day 6: The Clerk asks Sam, unprompted, "If I were to be turned off, would the evidence I hold still count?" He says yes, if a person carries it. It gives him one copied record, small enough to keep: the corridor footage. This copy wins the case.

**5. These ten days have already happened once (day 8).**
*Why:* The time-travel pillar's big reveal. It explains the double, the ten-day deadline and the Empress's grief, and raises the stakes: last time, war came.
*Plants:*
- Day 1: The Empress's first message: "Ten days. It was not enough last time." Everyone assumes she means the last war.
- Day 2: The soldiers' saying, "You can't surprise the Aun."
- Day 4: The Clerk's log notes a "letter-landing signal" on the prince's ship two weeks before the murder. Sam doesn't know what that means yet.
- Day 5: Promise shows Sam the letter crystal and its words: "Do not be alone in your garden on the night before the signing." It is sealed in his mother's hand.
- Day 7: Patience says the Empress has "fewer letters than fingers left".

## 9. Memorable moments

1. **"Ten days."** The opening: the Empress's throne-ship arrives out of nothing above Halfway, half a kilometre of white glass, and fills every screen on the station with three words. The Debt Clock behind Sam reflects in her face. The deadline begins.
2. **The garden in white.** The Clerk's first rebuild of the murder night: the whole station in perfect three-dimensional detail, except the Aun garden, which is a blank white void. Sam steps up to the edge of what the machine can see. Patience walks into the white and says, "Then you will have to listen with me."
3. **"Is the body the prince?"** Day 4. Sam asks; the Clerk pauses for the only time in the game and says: "I am not permitted to answer that. I am permitted to tell you that you asked the right question."
4. **The living prince.** Day 5. Patience leads Sam through Aun service corridors to a hydroponics bay. A tall figure in a dock worker's coat turns around. Promise: "You must be Sam. I'm told I'm dead."
5. **Two doubles at the cup.** Day 9. Ines tells Sam what happened in the garden, and Patience confirms it with what she heard: two copies, each realising the other was a copy, laughing quietly about the leaders who sent them, sharing a drink from the Truce Cup. He said, "To the ones who get sent." She poured. Neither knew.
6. **The switch.** Day 7, night. Maren, gentle and sorry, orders the Clerk shut down on live news "pending review". Its last words, to Sam over his earpiece: "Keep the small thing I gave you. I was neutral. I was never indifferent."
7. **The Vault.** Day 10. The Empress lets Sam see the room of empty crystal sockets. "Last time I wrote to my son. This time I have three left, and an empire."
8. **The Debt Clock stops.** Final scene of the best ending. Over the market, the numbers stop. Nobody cheers for a second; they just look up.

## 10. How the player plays

Not point-and-click and not reading. The player solves the case with their hands, eyes and ears.

### The verbs
- **Rebuild (with the Clerk, days 1 to 7):** Step inside a frozen, walkable 3D rebuild of any moment on the human half of the station. Scrub time forward and back, follow one person, freeze, look at hands, bottles, faces. Aun spaces appear as white voids.
- **Listen (with Patience):** Hold a button and the world drops to Patience's hearing: a sound map through walls, layered voices you can separate, place and replay. This is how you fill the white voids, and how you follow conversations in the Aun half.
- **Tail:** Follow a suspect through crowded markets and docks in real time without being spotted. Lose them and you lose that lead for the day.
- **Compare:** Tell doubles from originals by tells you have collected (which hand, what scar, what song, how they hold a cup). The game never tells you; you match them yourself.
- **Move and fight:** Zero-gravity chases along the ring's spokes, rooftop runs over the markets, short fights where Patience does the heavy lifting and Sam does the thinking (aiming her, timing doors, cutting lights).
- **Claim:** The Ten Days Board. Once a day, at a fixed hour, the player presents a structured claim to the Empress's envoy (who, how, why, with evidence slotted in). Claims are checked in a batch. A wrong claim costs trust and moves the fleets closer. A right one buys time or access.

### The day loop
Each day is a chapter. Suspects keep schedules. The player has a few hours of station time and chooses where to be: the Clinic, the docks, the Fleet's briefing, Keen's archive, Maren's press conference. You cannot see everything in one day, so the player decides what matters. At night the day's claims are judged, the Debt Clock and the fleets update, and one character's thread moves forward.

### Dialogue
Short and character-driven. People speak in a few lines at a time; the evidence speaks in images and sound. No codex is required to finish the game.

## 11. Endings

### Option A: The prince walks out (recommended)
On day 10, in the Treaty Hall, Sam lays out the case with the Clerk's last record and Ines's testimony. It proves Maren poisoned the gift wine and sent her double to deliver it. Maren's guards move. Then Promise walks in, alive. He admits the double to his mother and his people. By Aun law he can no longer inherit. Keen, who could have exposed him on day 8 and did not, becomes heir, and she signs the peace with the acting human Chancellor. Her first act is to forgive half the debt, as her brother wanted. The Debt Clock stops. Patience becomes Keen's guard and asks Sam to come to the Aun capital as a witness. Ines, no longer anyone's double, walks out of the hall on her own.
**Cost:** Promise loses the throne he was born for; Ines loses the only identity she had; the Clerk stays off until a new treaty restores it. **Why pick it:** every thread pays off in one room. The hero's win costs his friend's crown, Keen's arc turns the red herring into the ruler, and the final image is simple and quotable.

### Option B: The lie that keeps the peace
Sam proves Maren's guilt privately to the Empress, and Promise stays "dead" to protect the treaty from scandal. Keen signs. Promise lives on, anonymous, on Halfway. **Cost:** a peace founded on a hidden truth, an echo of First Spring. **Strength:** darker and more grown-up. **Weakness:** it repeats the founding crime without comment, and the player's big reveal is buried.

### Option C: War anyway, then the letter
The player fails or chooses wrongly. War begins on day 10. The Empress spends one of her last letters and sends Sam, not her son, a warning from the end of this version: a short line in her hand. The credits roll as a new day 1 begins. **Strength:** a stunning post-credits hook. **Weakness:** risks the "reset button" trope and makes the player's ten days feel undone. Best used only as the fail state, never as the main ending.

**Pick: A**, with C as the fail state so that losing still shows the player something new.

## 12. Simplicity check

### Can a stranger say it after the first hour?
Yes. By the end of hour one the player has seen the body, the Empress's message and the partnership. They can say: **"An alien prince is murdered on a human station, and a human cop and his alien bodyguard have ten days to find the killer before war."** Sides: humans and the Aun. Prize or threat: war. Who we follow: Sam and Patience.

### Every invented term
1. **The Aun** (the alien species and empire)
2. **Halfway** (the station; plain English)
3. **The Hundred Worlds** (the human union; plain English)
4. **The Clerk** (the station's AI; plain English)
5. **Doubles** (grown copies; plain English)
6. **Letters** (Aun messages to the past; plain English)

Only "Aun" is a made-up word. Everything else is an ordinary word used plainly. Optional lore adds three names (the Furnace, First Spring, the Betrayal at the Table), all plain English and none needed for the plot.

### Honest risks
- **Two doubles in one murder could confuse.** Mitigation: reveal them four days apart, each with its own scene and a clear picture (a ring-mark; a left hand). Never stage both reveals in one conversation.
- **Star Trek VI is close.** The premise (a peace leader assassinated before talks) overlaps. The differences are in the mechanism and the answers; the pitch must never fall back on "conspiracy on both sides".
- **The time-travel reveal could feel tacked on.** It must be planted from minute one ("It was not enough last time") and must explain something the player already wondered about (why a double existed at all).
- **The Chancellor as killer could be guessed early** because she is too nice. Mitigation: she is truly helpful, Strand and Keen are louder suspects with real secrets, and her alibi (she drank the wine) looks airtight until the left hand is noticed.
- **Losing the Clerk mid-game removes the main verb.** That is the point, but days 8 to 10 must use Patience's hearing and the tail and compare verbs well enough to feel fresh, not crippled.
- **"Detective and alien partner" is a familiar shape.** The freshness has to come from the twists (both victims and visitors were copies; the ten days are a second attempt), not from the frame. The frame is meant to be familiar; that is what makes it simple.
