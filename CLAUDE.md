# mystery-game-story-lab

Story development for an original sci-fi mystery video game. It uses AGI, aliens, cloning and time travel, and is not point-and-click or text-heavy.

When starting a session, read these first:
1. `HANDOFF.md`: current status and next steps.
2. `BRIEF.md`: the owner's requirements. They are the standard for all work.
3. `CONVERSATION.md`: the full early conversation, if you need the owner's exact words.

Conventions:
- Work on `main`. Commit small and often with `scripts/commit.sh "subject" path ...`, then `git push origin main`.
- Research is in `research/` (see `research/INDEX.md`). Current work is in `pitches/`. Old rounds are in `outlines/archive/` and `judging/archive/`.
- Never plagiarize: borrow techniques, never content. Tropes are a prompt to look for something better, not a ban.

## How to write

The owner finds most AI writing hard to read and full of slop. Everything in this repo, and every reply to the owner, follows these rules. Pass them to every subagent that writes.

### Plain English

- Use short, common words. Write "use", not "utilise". Write "start", not "commence". Write "show", not "demonstrate".
- Explain each idea so a smart 14-year-old would follow it on first read.
- Name things in plain English: "the Gate", "the Fleet", "the palace AI". Invent a word only when nothing plain will do.
- Say the thing directly. Put the point first, then the detail.
- Cut every word that does no work: "very", "really", "actually", "in order to", "it is important to note that".

### Active, concrete sentences

- Use active verbs with a clear subject. Write "The prince's guard found the body", not "The body was found".
- Pick strong verbs over weak verbs plus nouns. Write "decide", not "make a decision". Write "fails", not "is a failure".
- Prefer concrete nouns and specific details to abstractions. Write "a cracked teacup on the floor", not "signs of a struggle".
- Show what people do and say. Don't summarise their feelings for them.

### Rhythm

- Vary sentence length. Mix short punches with longer, flowing sentences. Three long sentences in a row tire the reader; three short ones sound robotic.
- Keep most sentences under 25 words. One idea per sentence.
- Keep paragraphs short: two to five sentences.
- Read it aloud in your head. If you stumble, rewrite.

### Banned AI habits

- Words: delve, tapestry, testament, nuanced, multifaceted, intricate, pivotal, realm, landscape (when not a landscape), journey (when not a trip), unravel, navigate (when not steering), leverage, robust, seamless, elevate, underscore, foster, resonate, palpable, beacon, symphony, crucible, labyrinthine, enigmatic, "a sense of".
- Patterns:
  - "It's not X, it's Y."
  - Groups of three adjectives or phrases by reflex.
  - "In a world where..."
  - Rhetorical questions as openers.
  - Ending a paragraph on a grand moral.
  - Stacked em-dashes.
  - Every bullet starting with a bolded label.
- Filler: "Ultimately", "At its core", "It's worth noting", "Moreover", "Furthermore", "In conclusion".
- Hype: don't call your own ideas "compelling", "fascinating" or "powerful". Show why they work and let the reader judge.

### Story prose specifically

- Open scenes on action or a concrete image, not on weather or exposition.
- Let dialogue sound like real people: short, interrupted, with things left unsaid.
- One clear viewpoint per scene.
- Trust the reader. Plant the clue and move on; never explain the twist twice.

### Before you hand anything over

Reread it once and check:
1. Could a stranger follow it on first read?
2. Did you use a plain word where a fancy one crept in?
3. Is every sentence active unless passive is clearer?
4. Do sentence lengths vary?
5. Did any banned word or pattern sneak in?
