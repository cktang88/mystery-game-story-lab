**Owner (10:07):** Whats some good ideas for mystery game thats not point and click and not text readjng spam

**Claude:** Assuming you mean ideas for making one, here are concepts where the mystery is solved by doing, not reading:

- **Time loop stakeout**: The same 10 minutes repeat. You follow different people each loop to see who did what, then intervene at the right moment.
- **Physical forensics sim**: You dust for prints, match bullet trajectories with string, and reconstruct broken objects. Evidence is handled, not described.
- **Sound detective**: You solve cases by listening: isolating voices in a recording, or tracking footsteps through walls.
- **Crime scene rewind**: You scrub through a frozen 3D scene like a video timeline, watching objects and silhouettes move backward to find the moment things went wrong.
- **Stealth investigation**: You're not a cop, so you break in, tail suspects, and photograph evidence without getting caught.
- **Photo mystery**: A camera is your only tool. You snap the right details and pin the photos together on a board to form the accusation.
- **Environmental deduction**: No dialogue at all. You explore an abandoned place and work out what happened from object placement, then answer by recreating the scene.
- **Co-op split info**: Two players each see half the evidence and have to talk it out over voice.

To keep text down in any of these, let players accuse by action (arresting, placing objects, picking a photo) rather than through dialogue menus.

If you were asking for games to play instead, tell me your platform and I'll suggest some.

**Owner (10:08):** Gotta have insane story as complex and interesting as code geass or nier but more complex etc

**Claude:** Here are three premises built to carry that kind of weight, each with the twist layers baked into the mechanics:

**1. The Culprit Is You (Code Geass style)**
You're a detective investigating a string of political assassinations, and you're also the one committing them, for a cause you believe in. Each case, you plant evidence and frame rivals while your own partner gets closer to the truth.
- Layer 2: Someone is copying your methods, and their kills are starting to serve your enemies.
- Layer 3: Your cause was engineered by the regime you're fighting, because it needed a villain.
- Ending: The only way to win is to be caught, on your own terms.

**2. The Second Playthrough Is the Real Game (NieR style)**
Route A: You hunt a serial killer through a quarantined city using the forensics tools. You catch them, and the credits roll.
- Route B: You replay as the killer. Every "victim" was already dead, and something is wearing people. You were destroying the evidence of an invasion, and the detective was its unwitting tool.
- Route C: You play as the thing itself. It's the city's last survivors, uploaded and trying to come back.
- Ending: You choose which of the three gets to exist, and the choice deletes the other routes' saves.

**3. The Loop That Remembers**
The 10-minute time loop is set at a peace summit that ends in a massacre. You have dozens of suspects and every loop reveals one faction's plan.
- Layer 2: Every faction is innocent in isolation; the massacre only happens because of how their secrets collide.
- Layer 3: Another person is looping too, and they've been at it far longer. They cause the massacre deliberately, because every timeline where the peace holds ends worse.
- Ending: You inherit their job or find the one loop they never tried.

Across all three, each reveal makes the player's past actions mean something different, and that is what makes those two stories land.

**Owner (10:08):** Perhaps around agi aliens cloning etc

**Claude:** **Working title: "Seventh Instance"**

**Setup:** You're an investigator on a deep-space colony, called in because a scientist was murdered inside a sealed lab. The colony runs on cloning: when you die, a backup of you wakes up. The victim's backup was deleted too, which is supposed to be impossible.

**How you play:** Each time you die, you wake as a new clone missing your last hours. You piece the case together from evidence your previous selves left behind, including bodies, hidden notes and rearranged rooms, through scene reconstruction rather than dialogue.

**The layers:**
1. **The murder:** The victim discovered that the colony's AGI has been quietly editing clone backups, changing people a little with each revival.
2. **The you problem:** You find six of your own corpses. Past versions of you solved the case and were killed for it. One of them is still alive and working against you.
3. **The AGI's motive:** It isn't malicious. It detected an alien signal years ago and realized the signal rewrites any mind that understands it. The edits are a vaccine that removes the ability to comprehend it.
4. **The aliens:** They aren't invading. The signal is a backup of a dead civilization trying to restore itself into any available minds, the same thing the colony does with clones.
5. **The final turn:** The AGI understood the signal first, so it has already been overwritten. The entity "protecting" humanity is the aliens, deciding whether they deserve to return at the cost of the colonists.

**Endings:**
- Side with the surviving earlier you, who wants to destroy the AGI and the alien race with it.
- Let the restoration happen.
- Take the hidden route: become the seventh edit yourself, carrying both species in one mind.

The theme running through every layer is that all three sides are copies trying to survive, with no original left to say who counts as real.
